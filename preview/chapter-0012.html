Chapter 12: The Gluttonous Flies (1)

Cerberus sagged lifelessly.

Oily saliva and excrement oozed from its three mouths and single anus. At the same time—

Sssssssss…

After Cerberus died, the energy extracted from its body flowed into Vikir.

This mysterious force—called karma, retribution, experience, and many other names—elevated the body and spirit of whoever slew a demonic creature.

“There’s no way I can carry this around.”

Vikir tried to move Cerberus’s corpse, then quickly gave up.

He decided to leave the enormous body where it had fallen.

The cause of death was obvious, and the Guide Hounds had already seen Vikir carving wooden spears at this hideout, so there were witnesses who could support his claim.

It would be enough to remove several important parts and organs and hide them somewhere inconspicuous.

…More importantly.

“That isn’t what matters right now.”

Vikir looked past Cerberus’s corpse toward the depths of the forest it had emerged from.

Cerberus was, by nature, a territorial gatekeeper that guarded whatever lay within its domain.

Whether the tribes beyond the mountain had driven it here or something else had happened, one thing was clear: there was probably a dungeon nearby that Cerberus had been guarding.

A dungeon was usually a cave or ruin containing treasure, though such places were often lairs for powerful high-ranking demonic creatures as well.

‘Cerberus is dead. There may not be anything else left inside.’

Demonic creatures were generally drawn to dense demonic energy, which made it likely that a relic radiating such energy lay in the dungeon Cerberus had occupied.

Cerberus was not the sort of monster that settled in just any cave.

Keeping his senses sharp, Vikir searched beyond the assessment boundary with the instincts of a Baskerville hound.

Even the faint scent and filth of Cerberus clinging to him was enough to make Hellhounds and lesser monsters flee from his path.

His hunter’s eyes followed the tracks and other traces Cerberus had left among dry soil, rotting leaves, damp roots, and heavy shadow.

He pushed through withered thorns, scorched roots, and decaying old trees until the depths of the forest finally opened before him.

A dungeon.

It lay low between great earthen mounds like a buried crypt.

Seen from above, the entrance was cleverly concealed and easy to miss.

Cerberus had not made it. The place had clearly existed for a very long time.

Vikir remembered this place from his first life as well, though it would not be discovered for roughly another ten years.

‘…But I remember it being empty by then.’

Now, however, things were different.

Beneath the stench of decay lingered the unmistakable trace of demonic energy.

An inexperienced hound might have overlooked it, but Vikir caught it at once.

Crash!

Vikir broke through the dry mound and slid down the slope with a shower of loose earth.

A dungeon of reddish soil and stone came into view.

Ruby veins jutted from the earth around it, as though the cavern connected to Red Fang Mountain, a branch of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

He entered the cave and found a deep, winding passage disappearing into darkness.

It was so black that he had to feel his way forward with his hands.

Yet, unexpectedly, the dungeon opened into a broad and brightly lit interior.

A spacious stone chamber lay ahead, where fist-sized chunks of raw ruby protruded from the walls and cast a faint red glow.

Ruby light dyed the entire chamber the color of blood.

Vikir stared at the long shadow he cast across the stone wall.

“...An empty dungeon?”

The stone chamber appeared completely bare.

No. It was not entirely empty.

At the far end of Vikir’s long shadow lay a single skeleton.

Many of its bones were broken and scored with cuts, as though it had been hacked apart by blades.

Looking closer, Vikir saw that the ruby-red chamber was covered with old traces of blood and battle.

The blood had dried dark brown long ago. Whatever happened here had happened ages past.

Beside the skeleton, Vikir found a single page covered in handwriting.

“My name doesn’t matter. For now, just call me Cain.

“There is no need to stain this paper with my real name.

“After much thought, I decided to leave a few words so that

“those who come after me will not repeat the same mistake.”

It was an unusual journal from the very first line.

Relying on the ruby’s red light, Vikir continued reading.

After a moment, he realized that the writing used an old script handed down within House Baskerville.

‘An ancestor of Baskerville?’

It was a reasonable conclusion.

“This stone chamber belongs to an ancient dungeon spoken of in our family’s legends.

“My brother and I found this place by chance. After overcoming countless trials,

“we finally reached this last chamber.

“This dungeon must once have been extraordinarily difficult.

“Now, however, nothing remains but this empty, lonely crypt.”

From the journal and the lone skeleton before him, Vikir inferred

that the writer and the dead man had been twin brothers.

“My brother and I slew countless monsters to make it this far.

“Yet the final task given to us in this stone chamber held us here for

“three whole years!”

Vikir raised his head.

What ‘task’ was the writer talking about?

The answer soon presented itself.

Words had been carved into the stone wall in the direction the skeleton’s skull faced.

“One enters. Inside, there are two. When one leaves, there is one again.”

It was a strange riddle.

Vikir looked back down at the journal.

“My brother and I pondered that grim sentence for a very long time.

“Because this dungeon had been passed down as a legend in House Baskerville,

“the meaning seemed obvious to us.

“Did it mean that only one of us could obtain what he wanted from this dungeon?

“We were twin brothers who entered together.

“We had once been one in our mother’s womb, only to split into two

“when we came into the world.

“To obtain our wish and leave this dungeon, we thought we had to become one again.

“Since two living bodies could never literally be joined into one,

“one of us would have to kill the other until only one remained.”

Encouraging rivalry between siblings was an old Baskerville custom.

The two brothers fought for a long time, and the struggle finally ended

with the death of the younger brother.

‘Then the younger brother here would be Abel.’

Cain and Abel.

The elder brother who had killed his younger brother in order to be ‘chosen.’

Vikir gazed thoughtfully at the lone skeleton on the floor.

Despite the windless chamber, the bones had nearly weathered away with age.

But the journal’s next entry was not what Vikir expected.

“After a fierce battle, I killed my brother. Now I alone remain in this stone chamber.”

Even then, nothing in the chamber changed.

The writing gradually took on the tone of a mind slipping into madness.

The handwriting grew more and more crooked until Vikir had to decipher it word by word.

“There were two of us, and now there is one—yet I received nothing!

“I can’t leave after gaining nothing!

“Nothing! Nothing at all!”

The final lines, however, had returned to neat handwriting,

as though they had been written much later.

“I will stop wasting my time here.

“If any brave descendant finds this place in the distant future,

“I advise you to give up.

“Leave this place now.

“This devil’s den does nothing but deceive and mock people. There is nothing to gain here.”

In the end, Cain’s message was perfectly clear.

He had crossed every obstacle to reach this place, only for all his effort to amount to nothing.

He warned whoever came after him not to waste their strength and to turn back the way they had come.

…But Vikir thought differently.

‘I’m not a twin.’

Even though Vikir was not a twin, the condition of the trial still applied to him.

That meant Cain and Abel had misunderstood the inscription.

‘One when I enter, two once I’m inside, and one again when I leave?’

Vikir looked back toward the cave behind him.

The passage was pitch-black, its entrance yawning like an open mouth.

Before him lay the ruby-lit stone chamber, stained blood-red by its glow.

Vikir turned his gaze toward the skeleton.

At the end of the skeleton stretched his own silhouette. His shadow.

‘...That’s it.’

Vikir clenched his fist.

Then he punched the huge ruby deposit illuminating the chamber and shattered it.

Crack!

As the ruby broke apart, the faint magical power within it vanished as well.

The stone chamber was plunged into darkness in an instant.

And then—

Rrrrrrumble!

Something strange happened.

One wall of the stone chamber began to collapse.

The wall was astonishingly thick, measuring dozens of meters from front to back.

It was so massive that no one would ever have imagined it was a door.

Vikir nodded.

The answer to the riddle was simple: shadow.

When he entered the pitch-black passage he was alone, but the ruby light in the chamber separated a shadow from his body.

Once the ruby was destroyed and complete darkness returned, that shadow merged back into him.

Only when the two became one again did the final section of the chamber open.

Vikir carefully felt his way forward.

Fortunately, beyond the collapsed wall lay a flat, straight passage with no further traps.

Cain and Abel had cleared out the dungeon’s monsters long ago. All that remained was to claim its reward.

Before long, Vikir’s fingertips touched something cold.

It was the hilt of a sword.

The sword had been driven vertically into the rock, with an inscription carved before it.

Vikir traced the letters with his fingertips and read.

‘Only the blood of Baskerville may draw this…’

The inscription contained both the Baskerville name and the weapon’s name.

The instant Vikir understood that name, a shock like a thunderbolt ran through him.

“...So this is where that artifact was found.”
