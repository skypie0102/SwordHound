Chapter 4: The Baskerville Dog (1)

Eight years had passed.

Vikir Van Baskerville was now eight years old and, like the other Baskerville children, attended lessons.

“All right, one last question,” the tutor said. “How are swordsmen ranked?”

Vikir answered calmly.

“The ranks are determined independently of skill in sword technique.”

“A swordsman who cannot imbue a sword with mana is a Sword Beginner.”

“One who can imbue the tip with mana, but whose aura remains thin and gaseous, is a Sword Expert.”

“When the aura flowing from the sword becomes dense and viscous like a liquid, the swordsman is a Sword Graduator.”

“When that aura becomes solid and can change shape at its wielder’s will, the swordsman is a Sword Master.”

Of course, these divisions existed for convenience.

Combat depended on countless factors: physical condition, terrain, climate, humidity, gravity, experience, even the density of mana in the air.

One could calculate them, but by the time the calculation was finished, the outcome might already have changed.

Eight-year-olds did not yet make such complicated calculations.

As Vikir answered without hesitation, the children around him raised their hands and shouted, competing to be heard.

“If a Beginner fights an Expert, the Expert wins!”

“If an Expert fights a Graduator, the Graduator wins!”

“A Master is stronger than a Graduator!”

“And the head of our family is a Master!”

Vikir listened in silence.

One child’s words pierced him.

‘The head of our family is a Master!’

Hugo Le Baskerville, head of the Baskerville family.

Measured by a mage’s standards, he possessed seven circles’ worth of mana.

But he was a swordsman. He converted that mana into aura and channeled it through his sword, reaching the rank of Sword Master, revered throughout the world.

The tutor looked at the children and nodded.

“Correct. Lord Hugo is a Sword Master. Only seven people in the Rok Empire have reached that level: the heads of the seven great families.”

Vikir already knew the lesson.

He also knew of several more Sword Masters, both inside and outside the Empire.

That information was not yet known to the others.

Vikir fell into thought.

The tutor had been watching him for some time.

‘Extraordinary.’

A truly overwhelming talent.

The hundred-day-old infant who had stayed beneath the Styx for more than seven minutes had become a small legend within the family.

But his gifts were not merely physical.

Overshadowed by his remarkable body, his intellect was no less exceptional.

Teach him one thing and he learned a hundred.

‘The lord will be pleased today.’

The tutor intended to report all of this to Hugo.

Usually, Hugo showed little interest in any children but his eldest and second sons.

He had behaved like a livestock dealer, occasionally visiting to record his stock’s growth and value. Yet over the past few years, his interest in Fang Castle had grown markedly.

Because of Vikir, of course.

Hugo’s visits to Fang Castle, where children under ten were raised together, had once been rare. His increased attention had left the tutors feeling the weight of their responsibilities.

‘Thank you, young master. I’ll do all I can to help you.’

Vikir had brought them a larger budget and greater standing among the family’s knights. Everyone regarded him favorably.

Vikir himself paid none of it any mind.

◆◆◆

After class, the children returned to their rooms throughout the castle.

Vikir took stock of himself.

‘Hugo is a Master.’

Unfortunately, it was true.

The solid aura that only Masters could produce far surpassed the density and strength of the viscous, liquid aura he had wielded as an advanced Sword Graduator in his previous life.

Grind—

Vikir clenched his teeth.

He would not live or die so meaninglessly in this life.

‘I must regain all my former strength before the coming-of-age ceremony.’

He was eight now.

Without anyone knowing, he had already reached the advanced stage of Sword Expert.

In his previous life, he had barely managed that in his twenties.

He had first reached Sword Expert at fifteen, when a faint aura appeared on his sword.

Even that would have made him a genius outside the family. Among the Baskervilles, it was average, perhaps slightly below.

Baskerville children generally approached advanced Sword Expert around twenty and Sword Graduator around thirty.

Those who could coat the tip of a sword in liquid aura rose to senior positions. The others remained below them.

Even the Baskervilles’ elite, geniuses among geniuses, usually reached advanced Sword Expert around twenty.

Vikir had reached it at eight.

At this age in his former life, he had only just begun to sense mana.

His present progress surpassed even the family’s most exceptional children.

As Vikir reflected on the difference—

“Hey.”

A voice called from behind.

He turned and saw three nine-year-old boys.

Highbro Le Baskerville, Middlebro Le Baskerville, and Lowbro Le Baskerville.

Male descendants of the direct line bore Le in their names; female descendants bore La.

These three were direct-line descendants of the Baskervilles.

Their pedigree was beyond question.

Predictably, Highbro, the eldest and the one in front, began provoking him.

“Where are you going, you half-blood trash?”

“Where are you going?”

“Where are you going?”

The boy in front led, and the two behind echoed him.

An ordinary nine-year-old’s provocation.

The Baskervilles judged people by ability so strictly that such childish behavior would fade as they grew older.

Vikir watched the triplets closely for a different reason.

‘…Hugo Le Baskerville’s trident.’

In another ten years, they would become troublesome enemies.

Before his return, these three hounds had relentlessly pursued and stabbed him when he broke through the encirclement.

‘Come to think of it, they bullied me from childhood.’

They had stolen so much of his food that he had grown slowly as a child.

During assessments, they had secretly tripped him and struck him with blunt blades.

The resulting injuries had left him limping and nearly cost him his life on several missions.

Flash—

A sinister red light flickered in Vikir’s eyes.

Oblivious, the triplets, only just nine, giggled and surrounded him.

“Hey, trash. They say you stayed in the Styx for seven minutes and strangled two vipers in your cradle. Stop boasting! Eight years old and already lying!”

“Lying!”

“Lying!”

Highbro, the most vicious of the three, stepped forward.

“Can you do this?”

A small, faintly glowing sphere appeared above his palm.

Baskerville children were born with exceptional sensitivity to mana and skill at controlling it.

They could already condense it into a sphere.

He did not seem able to channel it through a sword yet, but even this would mark him as a genius outside the family.

It was enough to earn praise even among the Baskervilles.

“This is talent. Come on, you try! Let’s see how much mana you can draw out.”

“Let’s see!”

“Let’s see!”

The triplets kept pressing him.

“…”

Vikir looked at the ball of mana Highbro had formed.

A sphere the size of a child’s fist. Yes, that was impressive enough for his age.

But Vikir had done as much before he was eight months old.

When he had strangled two snakes in his cradle.

Now he had added eight years of practice since his return to thirty years of experience from his former life. How far had his control of mana advanced?

Vikir glanced around.

There was little to worry about in Fang Castle, where only children lived.

The few guardian knights and tutors had all gone to the outer castle.

Flash—

Vikir released his power.

He raised his palm high and gathered mana.

The triplets stared in astonishment.

“…!”

“…!”

“…!”

Nothing appeared above Vikir’s palm.

For a moment, the triplets looked blank. Then they burst out laughing.

“Ahahaha, you little shit! Don’t tell me you still can’t even sense mana at your age!”

“You can’t?”

“You can’t?”

They laughed at him until tears ran down their faces.

…But—

At that same moment, in a watchtower not far from Fang Castle…

A knight on duty stared in bewilderment at the unexpected sight beyond the window.

“What? Why are there two suns above Fang Castle?”
