Chapter 11: Hounds of Hell (4)

An unpleasant growl rang out, like bronze beasts grinding their bodies together.

<Cerberus>

Danger Level: A+

Size: 7 m

Discovery Location: Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, Seventh Ridge

AKA: “Hell’s Guard Dog”

It showed no interest in souls entering hell, but any soul that tried to escape was immediately torn to pieces.

It dwelled in the deepest reaches of the underworld, the final destination of all souls, and stood at the pinnacle of hell-type canine demonic creatures.

Gas, oil, and flame spewed from deep within its three throats. This was Cerberus, the guard dog of hell.

Even Vikir, a seasoned hunter, had seen this super-elite monster only once from a distance before his regression.

One day, the gates of the Demon Realm had suddenly opened and demonic creatures poured out across the continent.

On the day that marked the beginning of the long catastrophe, a single Cerberus rampaging at the front killed hundreds of warriors selected from the Seven Great Families.

The losses had been even worse because everyone was still inexperienced at the beginning of the war.

Greater disaster had been prevented only when Camus Morgue—the youngest female head of the Morgue Family at the time, later known as the Ironblood Empress—personally intervened.

And now that very same kind of Cerberus stood before Vikir.

“…”

Even before a monster powerful enough to make a hundred Hellhounds meaningless, Vikir remained calm.

“So Cerberus has already made it this far? Why is a creature that should stay around the Seventh Ridge all the way down here?”

As he said, Cerberus had originally been encountered in the highlands of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Yet this one had descended all the way to the lowlands around the First Ridge.

What could have driven it this far down…?

[Grrrrr!]

It was an awkward situation even for Vikir.

“There’s no other choice.”

Vikir decided to reveal every card he had been keeping hidden.

There was no longer any room to conceal the skills he had possessed before his regression.

Fortunately, he had crossed well beyond the boundary, and he could sense no one watching nearby.

There were no Guide Hound Guardian Knights here, so he did not need to hide his strength while fighting.

Flash!

Vikir drew his shortsword and channeled mana into it.

A dense gaseous aura welled from the tip—the hallmark of an Advanced Sword Expert.

Low Sword Expert = 1st-Circle Mage

Intermediate Sword Expert = 2nd-Circle Mage

Advanced Sword Expert = 3rd-Circle Mage

Low Sword Graduator = 4th-Circle Mage

Intermediate Sword Graduator = 5th-Circle Mage

Advanced Sword Graduator = 6th-Circle Mage

Sword Master = 7th-Circle Mage

This was the general equivalence used between swordsmanship and magical ranks.

At the tip of eight-year-old Vikir’s sword burned a dense gaseous aura that looked ready to condense into liquid at any moment.

It signified the Advanced Sword Expert level, roughly equivalent to a 3rd-Circle Mage.

In his previous life, Vikir had not reached this stage until around age twenty.

Vikir drew up his mana and moved his hand swiftly.

The shortsword carved strange trajectories through the air.

Sharp, fang-shaped arcs appeared as though three teeth had been summoned into the air.

Baskerville Style, Third Form: a sword technique that draws three fangs and drives them into the opponent’s neck.

In his previous life, Vikir had mastered the Baskerville Style through the Fourth Form and could produce four fangs.

Back then, he had also possessed the mana and aura of an Advanced Sword Graduator, so his present level was still far below his former peak.

But Vikir possessed other weapons now.

His small body and quick mobility, together with a body hardened by the blessing of the River Styx, gave him advantages his former self had not possessed.

His strengths and weaknesses were both clear.

Vikir swung his sword and formed three fangs.

His child-sized hands and arms were too short to form the fourth fang he had wielded in his previous life, but the gaseous aura and three clear fangs extending from his blade were still enough to block Cerberus’s claws.

“…?”

Vikir sensed that something was wrong.

Cerberus had been a terrifying opponent even at his old strength. How could he contend with one now in a child’s body far weaker than his former self?

That meant something was wrong with Cerberus as well.

Just as he suspected—

“…!”

Vikir’s keen eyes caught something.

One side of Cerberus’s rib cage was visibly caved in.

Its movement was slightly unnatural; several ribs were clearly broken.

Clear arrow wounds also marked the places where its fur had been torn away.

“Who did this to it?”

Vikir let out an incredulous laugh.

Then he remembered the barbarian tribes living beyond Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Those tribes had long clashed with both the iron-blooded Baskerville Family and the prestigious mage family Morgue.

If the barbarians had driven Cerberus down here, then the monster was already in poor condition.

Clang! Crash! Bang!

Vikir repeatedly gathered his dispersing gaseous aura to block Cerberus’s claws and teeth.

Every collision between blade and fang rang like metal striking bronze and sent sparks flying.

[Grrr! Bark! Grrr!]

Cerberus attacked with all three heads, trying to bite Vikir, but each attempt failed.

“I’ve had plenty of practice dealing with triplets.”

Indeed, the swordsmanship and trident tactics of Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro had been inspired by Cerberus’s three-headed fighting style.

Vikir had been tormented by those three through countless practical evaluations in his previous life, so their coordination was painfully familiar.

He remembered most of their countless variations as well.

The triplets of his previous life had been skilled and clever enough to reproduce Cerberus’s attack patterns almost instinctively. Drawing on those memories, Vikir faced the real thing.

Whoosh!

Baskerville Style, First Form—the first fang knocked Cerberus’s bite aside.

Clang!

Baskerville Style, Second Form—the second fang drove halfway into Cerberus’s neck.

Thrust!

Baskerville Style, Third Form—the third fang struck the second and drove it even deeper into Cerberus’s neck.

Vikir’s Baskerville Style resembled the orthodox family swordsmanship, yet it was subtly different.

Those who had survived the Age of Destruction possessed something swordsmen of the peaceful era could not imitate.

Traditional sword theory had been completely reinterpreted through the Age of Destruction, with every unnecessary flourish stripped away.

A sword that moved only to kill its opponent.

Extreme practical experience was layered on top of that.

If ordinary swordsmanship was well-marbled beef, Baskerville swordsmanship was dry chicken breast, and Vikir’s current swordsmanship was hard, dried jerky.

At its limit, only killing intent remained.

Vikir’s sword possessed an unmistakable purpose.

Boom!

The shortsword slammed into Cerberus’s injured side.

The gaseous aura burst, and Cerberus screamed in pain.

But Cerberus was the guard dog of hell. Damage at this level was nowhere near enough to bring it down.

It drove a claw into the opening beneath the path Vikir’s shortsword had just traced.

Thud!

To break the enemy’s bones, one had to be willing to sacrifice some flesh.

Vikir’s body buckled under the impact.

Even so, his body—tempered by the River Styx—managed to withstand Cerberus’s claw.

“…But I doubt I could take that twice.”

Vikir frowned.

His organs had not ruptured, but several ribs were definitely broken.

And to make matters worse—

Snap!

The shortsword finally shattered under the strain of Vikir’s aura.

“Damn.”

It hurt, but an experienced hunter always found the best route even in a situation like this.

“Of the Thirty-Six Stratagems, fleeing is the best.”

One of the Thirty-Six Stratagems had been devised by the head of the Leviathan Family, the greatest strategist among the Seven Great Families.

The thirty-sixth was simple: when all else failed, retreat was the best policy.

Whoosh—

Vikir spun around and kicked off the ground with all his strength.

Cerberus, sensing victory, immediately gave chase.

[Grrrroar!]

With its speed, Cerberus would have caught him almost instantly if not for the severe wounds along its side.

Vikir fled through the sea of trees, silently grateful to the nameless barbarian tribe beyond the mountains.

Soon the boundary he had crossed earlier came into view.

Fortunately, there were no Guide Hounds nearby.

Vikir vaulted over the barbed wire marking the boundary and kept running.

Cerberus smashed through the wire and wooden stakes, thrusting all three heads toward Vikir’s back.

[Bark! Bark! Bark!]

What could be more relentless than a hound chasing its prey?

Cerberus opened its mouths in triumph.

The distance vanished. In another moment it would tear into its prey’s flesh and crush his bones.

Thump!

If only the ground had not suddenly given way beneath it.

Crack—!

The earth collapsed.

Cerberus charged onto a patch of flat ground disguised beneath dry straw and plunged into the pit below.

Thrown off balance by the trap, Cerberus planted its feet and tried to leap back out.

But that was impossible.

Thud! Thud! Thud!

Dozens of upward-pointing wooden stakes drove into its body.

[GRAAAH!]

A tearing scream burst from all three of Cerberus’s heads.

The bottom of Vikir’s pit bristled with countless sharpened wooden stakes.

Most shattered against Cerberus’s tough hide, but some managed to pierce through.

They drove into its body and opened serious wounds.

Most of those wounds clustered around the inner flank where the earlier arrow injury had already torn deep.

“Welcome to the Cradle of Needles.”

Vikir gave himself a brief, dry smile.

He had originally built the trap in case other monsters such as Hellhounds chased him.

The stakes had only been meant to slow their movement a little.

Against Cerberus, however, the trap had produced an unexpected harvest.

Rattle…

Cerberus snapped the stakes embedded in its flank and mouths, its entire body trembling violently.

Rage shook it, but something else was making its body tremble even more.

Bloody Beans!

Vikir had boiled Bloody Beans and soaked the wooden stakes in the liquid, letting the cacao compounds seep into their tips.

Cerberus was a canine demonic creature too. Through the open wounds, a large dose of chocolate entered directly into its body.

But as expected of a top-tier monster, it did not collapse immediately.

Foam bubbled from its mouths, burning excrement leaked from behind, and the heavy scent of chocolate and coffee filled the air.

Even then, all three heads remained upright, staring fixedly at Vikir.

Vikir emptied his pouch and drew out every Bloody Bean he had left.

Rattle! Rattle! Rattle!

The beans flew toward Cerberus’s three open mouths.

Almost every bean Vikir threw landed inside one of the mouths of the Cerberus slowed by the stakes.

As a result, Cerberus’s movements grew slower and slower.

Crack! Snap!

At last Cerberus broke the remaining stakes embedded in its body and drove Vikir into a corner of the pit.

By then, the pouch of Bloody Beans was empty.

Cerberus was a wreck, but it still had enough strength left for one final leap at its prey’s throat.

“…”

Knowing that, the cornered Vikir watched it with extreme care.

Before long, Cerberus moved.

Vikir lunged forward as well, gripping one of his wooden spears.

Snap!

The spear thrown by the hunter cut through the air and grazed Cerberus’s body.

It seemed to slip briefly into the wound on its side before popping helplessly back out.

Without the existing arrow wound, it would have bounced off entirely.

Cerberus bared its fangs and charged the seemingly defenseless Vikir.

Its six eyes burned even brighter at the thought that this infuriating prey was finally within reach.

But—

Stagger!

Cerberus’s charging body suddenly lurched.

Startled by the loss of balance, it steadied itself and took another step.

Stagger—

The second step was off as well.

The third was no better. On the fourth its legs bent at a strange angle; on the fifth it collapsed.

On the sixth it dragged itself forward once more, and on the seventh it fell flat, unable even to crawl.

Seven steps.

That was all Cerberus managed while approaching Vikir.

And then it was over.

Thud!

Its three foam-covered heads struck the red earth and never moved again.

Only then did Vikir sit down with his back against the cliff.

“…So the work I hid away finally paid off.”

The hunter’s gaze shifted to the wooden spear he had thrown moments earlier.

It had only grazed Cerberus.

But this spear was different from the others.

Two small, sharp thorns had been fixed to its tip.

A dark aura still clung faintly to those dried thorns.

They were venomous snake fangs whose poison could make even seven steps impossible.

The Bloody Mamba’s venom still lingered within them.
