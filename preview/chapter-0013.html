Chapter 13: The Gluttonous Flies (2)

‘Only those of Baskerville blood can draw this blade—Beelzebub.’

The inscription contained both the Baskerville family name and the sword’s name.

“...So this is the artifact that was found here.”

By the faint light of the shattered ruby fragments, Vikir traced the weapon’s shape.

Three rounded, ruby-red nodes formed its guard, while a long black awl-like blade projected from the center.

A rough green sheen glimmered around the hilt, giving the whole weapon the appearance of a giant fly.

Vikir knew exactly what this artifact was.

He remembered seeing an illustration of it in a book of myths during his first life.

‘Beelzebub, the Gluttonous Fly.’

Explaining the sword’s full origin would take a long time.

In short—

Ancient myth told of seven unprecedented demon constellations known as the Seven Calamities, against which the peoples of the continent once united.

Although those seven beings were defeated, remnants of them endured in material or conceptual forms, each retaining a fraction of the power it had possessed in life.

One such remnant was this sword: Beelzebub.

The founding heads of the Seven Great Families had each brought down one of those demon constellations.

That was why Beelzebub’s remains had once been kept by House Baskerville, their demonic aura drawing powerful monsters into the surrounding lands.

‘So the old legend was true. And later, this sword fell into the demons’ hands.’

The forgotten artifact would one day reappear on a battlefield and send countless humans to their deaths.

Its wielder changed repeatedly even among the demons, and in time the number of demons slain by the weapon may have exceeded the number of humans.

…But this time, things were different.

Beelzebub had come into Vikir’s hands first.

In his previous life, the demons had taken it before Baskerville even understood what had been lost. That would not happen now.

Bzzzzzz—

The sword resonated with Vikir’s mana, the droning of fly wings filling his ears.

When Vikir reached out and touched it, Beelzebub began sinking into his palm as though returning to a part of his own body.

Fssht!

Black fissure-like lines spread along the artery side of his wrist.

Beelzebub settled quietly inside Vikir’s body, ready to spring from his hand as a hidden spare weapon whenever he willed it.

However—

Beelzebub was no ordinary sword. It had been fashioned from the remains of a demon constellation.

Its supernatural abilities did not end with hiding inside its wielder.

Grrrrr—!

The instant Beelzebub settled within him, a sudden wave of weakness swept over Vikir. He was ravenously hungry.

“...What is this?”

For once, Vikir was genuinely taken aback.

In his first life, he had often gone hungry for an entire week and then eaten everything he needed in a single meal.

On campaigns, he had sometimes remained hidden for a month while surviving on little more than water.

A stomach trained under conditions that harsh should have endured ordinary hunger without complaint.

Even in a child’s body, he should not have been this weak.

Grrrrr—!

His stomach twisted again as though throwing a violent fit.

Vikir realized that this extreme hunger was anything but normal.

“Are you asking me for something?”

That was exactly it.

Beelzebub answered with the agitated buzzing of fly wings.

Sniff—

His sense of smell seemed sharper as well. Somewhere nearby, an irresistible scent reached him.

It was the scent of a demonic creature, mouthwatering in a way it had never been before.

Vikir crawled out of the dungeon and into the rat-infested forest.

The closer he drew to the assessment boundary and his former position, the stronger the scent became.

The first thing he found was the corpse of a Hellhound.

Before Vikir even summoned it, Beelzebub thrust its stinger from his hand on its own.

Normally, a hound that bared its fangs without its master’s command deserved punishment.

This time, however, Vikir made an exception.

The hunger Beelzebub shared with him was simply too intense.

‘It must have been starving for a very long time.’

The Hellhound’s corpse around Beelzebub’s stinger—and even the soil beneath it—began to dry and shrivel.

‘Could Beelzebub’s insane hunger be why so much of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain is filled with dead trees and parched earth?’

If so, just how much food would he have to provide to satisfy the thing from now on?

“...”

Vikir shook his head and cleared the thought away.

Beelzebub’s power was valuable enough to justify its appetite.

Before long—

the Beelzebub Vikir remembered began to reveal its true ability.

Gulp. Gulp. Gulp. Gulp—

With Beelzebub buried in its neck, the Hellhound’s corpse was drained of blood at an astonishing speed.

Blood and bodily fluids flowed through Beelzebub’s hollow stinger and into Vikir.

The gnawing hunger inside him eased, his cramped stomach gradually settling.

Flash!

One of Beelzebub’s three eye-like nodes suddenly glowed a vivid red.

‘It acquired a skill.’

Vikir’s intuition was correct.

Beelzebub, the Gluttonous Fly, possessed the power to steal the distinctive traits and specialties of those whose blood it consumed.

When Beelzebub had been alive, the scope of that absorption had been nearly limitless, leaving countless victims stripped of achievements they had spent their entire lives building.

As a mere artifact, however, it could no longer exercise that boundless power.

It could store only three stolen skills at a time.

By drinking the Hellhound’s blood, Vikir’s Beelzebub had obtained its first skill.

<Beelzebub, the Gluttonous Fly> / Stinger

- Slot 1: Bleeding — Hellhound (B+)

- Slot 2: None

- Slot 3: None

The ability manifested only when Vikir willed it.

Anyone pierced by Beelzebub’s stinger would experience the Hellhound’s deadly bleeding power.

Even a shallow wound that would normally stop bleeding could begin to seep again, and the bleeding would persist far longer than an ordinary injury.

Meanwhile—

Sluuuurp—

Beelzebub kept drinking until the Hellhound’s corpse was nearly mummified.

It had acquired the skill long before finishing, so the continued feeding was clearly nothing more than hunger.

Vikir felt his own hunger ease somewhat as well.

Grrrrr—!

Even so, he was still hungry.

The delicious scent that had brushed his nose continued to drift from farther away.

Vikir quickly left the forest.

Soon, the familiar boundary came into view.

The Cradle.

Beyond it lay the area where Baskerville’s children were undergoing their practical examination.

After slipping across the boundary, Vikir spotted the pit he had dug beneath a distant rock.

Fortunately, none of the Guide Hounds had noticed his return yet.

The Cradle covered a vast area; even the Guide Hounds could not watch every corner of the examination grounds at once.

Besides, because Vikir had spent so long sitting quietly in his pit, he had likely been classified as a safe subject and was now watched only loosely.

Vikir crossed back to his hideout and brushed away the thin covering of soil and fallen leaves.

A mouthwatering slab of meat soon appeared beneath them.

Cerberus lay exactly where it had died.

Bzzzzzz—

At the rich scent of flesh and fat, Beelzebub quivered with almost delirious excitement.

The buzzing of fly wings grew louder.

Then Beelzebub drove its stinger into Cerberus’s corpse and began greedily drinking from its flesh and organs.

But Vikir stopped it.

“Whoa, whoa. This is evidence for the practical examination. If you ruin it too badly, it’ll be difficult to explain during the autopsy.”

Beelzebub ignored him and tried to push its stinger in deeper.

Vikir’s expression hardened.

“No.”

Smack!

Vikir slapped the back of his own hand hard.

Of course striking his own hand hurt. And because Beelzebub was fused with him, pain Vikir felt was pain Beelzebub felt as well.

Keeeeeng…

Only then did Beelzebub withdraw its stinger from Cerberus’s corpse.

Vikir watched fresh blood and new power course through the artery-like lines on the back of his hand.

<Beelzebub, the Gluttonous Fly> / Stinger

- Slot 1: Burn — Cerberus (A+)

- Slot 2: Bleeding — Hellhound (B+)

- Slot 3: Rapid Regeneration — Brown Rat Norvegicus (F)

Was it because Cerberus was the stronger demonic creature?

Cerberus’s power had taken Slot 1.

The Hellhound’s bleeding ability had been pushed down to Slot 2.

And, absurdly enough, Slot 3 now held the power of a Brown Rat Norvegicus.

Apparently, the remains of a rat Cerberus had eaten were scattered beneath its corpse.

‘So if it takes in a stronger ability, it can push out or replace an older one.’

Vikir watched the artery carrying the rat’s power pulse more faintly than the other two.

For now, he decided to leave it alone. Even a weak ability was better than an empty slot.

More importantly, obtaining Cerberus’s Burn ability was an enormous gain.

Anyone pierced by Beelzebub could now be afflicted by Cerberus’s hellish burning power, a flame-like injury that would not simply fade on its own.

The victim could be left suffering searing pain for an agonizingly long time.

Considering that burning was among the worst pains a person could endure, it was a far more vicious effect than the Hellhound’s bleeding.

“I’ve taken everything worth taking from this mountain.”

There was little left for Vikir to do.

He only had to wait out the practical examination, hand Cerberus’s corpse over to the Guide Hounds, and collect the highest evaluation.

On top of that, he would probably receive a reward for the achievement.

“...”

Vikir closed his eyes and examined the state of his body.

His mana reserves were already comparable to those of a Fourth-Circle Mage.

He could manifest three Baskerville fangs with his sword—perhaps even four now.

He stood at the very top of Sword Expert, already peering into the threshold of Sword Graduator.

‘Conservatively, I’m at the highest level of Sword Expert. Generously, I could be called a Low Sword Graduator.’

In actual combat, however, his young body dragged his output down by roughly half a stage, so Sword Expert remained the fair classification.

In his first life, no matter how much mana or combat experience he accumulated, the swordsmanship he had been taught could never produce more than four fangs.

For comparison, the present Hugo could produce seven fangs, while the Hugo Vikir remembered from before his regression could produce nine.

Baskerville Style, Ninth Form.

That swordsmanship was reserved for the direct line—the eldest son chosen to inherit the family and the sons selected to support him.

It was a refined and exalted art on an entirely different level from the Fourth Form Vikir had learned.

But now things were different.

“...Tenth Form.”

Baskerville Style, Tenth Form. Ten fangs.

It was an ultimate realm no Baskerville of the present age had reached.

A legendary secret text preserved the teachings of the first patriarch, who in ancient times had subdued one of the demon constellations of the Seven Calamities.

Vikir knew where that treasure was.

‘I don’t even have to go far.’

It was lying in a place very close at hand, rotting among miscellaneous books so ordinary that almost anyone could read them.

And who would give it to him?

Hugo Le Baskerville, patriarch of House Baskerville.

Hugo himself would eventually place it in Vikir’s hands.

He would never imagine the true value of the seemingly worthless book he was giving away.
