Chapter 10: Hounds of Hell (3)

Vikir recalled.

It had happened only a few days earlier.

Hugo Le Baskerville had given him a reward—something like one—for placing first on the written exam.

‘Is there any snack you want?’

‘Chocolate!’

‘Barrymore, take him to the food storage and give him some chocolate. Don’t let him take too much—only what he can carry.’

That was when Vikir had been given the key to the food storage.

Accompanied by Butler Barrymore, he went down to the cellar and obtained a pouch of cacao beans.

‘Long ago, the patriarch personally led the family elders to suppress the barbarians on the western frontier, then reclaimed the vast jungle there as farmland. They say a single local “Bloody Bean” can produce one hundred liters of chocolate.’

Bloody Beans. Intensely flavored cacao beans so concentrated that a single one could make one hundred liters of chocolate.

Vikir took one Bloody Bean from the pouch at his waist.

The Hellhound, slowed greatly by having to go around the water, was charging toward him again.

It ran with its mouth wide open, as canine creatures tended to do—as if inviting him to throw something inside.

To Vikir, a seasoned hound himself, that was exactly what it looked like.

Flick!

Vikir snapped the Bloody Bean from his thumb straight into the Hellhound’s open mouth.

Then—the reaction was immediate.

Yelp!

The Hellhound swallowed the object that had flown into its mouth.

It immediately began convulsing.

Ghack! Krrk! Yelp!

Foam gathered around its mouth, and thick saliva streamed out.

Watery stool leaked from it as its entire body shook with spasms.

Its heart hammered as though it might burst, and the blood vessels in its eyes swelled red.

‘As expected.’

Vikir nodded.

Every hunter who had survived the Age of Destruction knew this fact.

Canine-type demonic creatures were vulnerable to chocolate.

Compounds found in cacao beans were effectively poisonous to dogs.

Even a hound from hell was no exception.

‘Let’s see. What were the symptoms? Mucus vomiting, difficulty breathing, incontinence, diarrhea, elevated body temperature, increased heart rate, loss of appetite, convulsions, seizures, severe agitation… and then death?’

Vikir gripped his blunt shortsword.

He followed the Hellhound as it staggered deeper into the sea of trees.

‘The stomach and heart are hit first. Then the liver.’

But a Hellhound’s stomach, heart, and liver were protected by ribs harder than steel.

His current blunt shortsword could not penetrate those ribs or slip between them effectively.

So the best target was an organ not fully protected by the ribs and already weakened by the chocolate toxin.

‘…The answer is the kidneys.’

The kidneys lay low in the abdomen, not fully covered by the ribs, and were already being overworked by the chocolate toxin.

Vikir held the shortsword horizontally and drove it in with all his strength.

The blade was too blunt to cut or pierce, but the impact was enough to damage the muscle and the kidneys beneath.

Thud! Krrk…

Each blow further damaged the kidneys, allowing the toxin to hit even harder.

The Hellhound writhed in pain, expelled the last of its burning waste, then collapsed with more than a meter of tongue hanging from its mouth.

Vikir finished off the Hellhound, which could no longer resist.

Hellhounds were canine demonic creatures that carried hellfire within their bodies.

If not killed completely, their tenacious vitality could flare back up like an ember in ashes.

‘Even a fire that looks extinguished should be checked once more.’

Vikir raised his shortsword and extinguished the Hellhound’s last spark of life for good.

Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud!

With a blunt shortsword, beating it to death was the only option.

Not long afterward—

something remarkable happened.

Ssssss…

As the Hellhound died, an invisible energy seeped from its corpse and merged into Vikir’s body.

People called it many things: “experience,” “effort,” “karma,” or “retribution.”

When this mysterious energy accumulated within the body, it translated into physical growth.

Vikir had single-handedly defeated a high-ranking monster that children his age could scarcely imagine facing, and his body grew stronger as a result.

The blessing of the River Styx combined with the experience gained from killing the Hellhound, strengthening him further.

Vikir’s body had become highly resistant to toxins, magic, and physical force.

‘…Hmm. Are my bones thicker? I think I’ve grown a little taller too.’

The shortsword in his hand felt much lighter than before.

The sword itself could not have lost weight, so his grip strength had clearly increased.

Whoosh. Whoosh.

A sword that other eight- and nine-year-olds struggled to wield with both hands could now be swung easily by Vikir with one.

Even so, he still had a long way to go before recovering the strength of his previous life.

‘At this rate, I can recover all of my old strength before the coming-of-age ceremony.’

Vikir was only eight years old, yet he had already reached the Advanced Sword Expert level.

Other Baskervilles normally reached that stage only after their coming-of-age ceremony.

Given another seven years, he was confident he could become even stronger than he had been before his regression.

‘And if I take first place on the practical exam, there should be a reward.’

Vikir looked down at the Hellhound’s corpse with satisfaction.

Bringing the still-warm corpse back to the family would guarantee first place in the practical evaluation.

Crossing beyond the boundary could be an issue, but the blame would fall on the Guide Hounds supervising the area; for the examinee, the feat itself would be praised.

An eight-year-old had entered the Forbidden Zone—somewhere even the Guide Hounds struggled to approach—and single-handedly killed a B+ danger-class monster.

He could already imagine Hugo’s expression.

Heave!

Vikir grabbed the Hellhound by the tail and began dragging it away.

A demonic creature’s corpse obeyed different rules of weight than ordinary matter because its soul remained bound to the Demon Realm.

That made the Hellhound’s corpse much heavier than it looked, but Vikir’s strength was far beyond that of an ordinary eight-year-old.

Creak—scrape—crunch!

Vikir kept moving.

The thorn-covered uphill path looked to him like a flower-strewn road.

Now all he had to do was return the Hellhound’s corpse to the Guardian Knights and claim first place.

A faint smile appeared on his lips as he thought about the reward to come.

It would be a treasure he had never dared dream of possessing in his previous life—one whose true value no one in this era even understood.

…Just then.

Vikir stopped—not because of one presence, but several.

Grrrrr…

Growls rose from deep within the sea of trees behind him.

From caves woven through the tangled roots, the low howls drew closer.

‘…Damn.’

Vikir set the Hellhound’s corpse down for a moment.

He had forgotten.

Hellhounds were pack hunters.

Before long—

yellow-red flames of sulfur began appearing throughout the darkness of the sea of trees.

Eleven Hellhounds had surrounded Vikir before he knew it.

But—

“…”

Vikir only smiled faintly, showing no fear.

The Hellhounds facing that smile instead lowered their bodies and growled uneasily.

Did dogs recognize their own kind?

Even after living through a ruined world and undergoing regression, the bloodlust rooted deep in Vikir’s soul had not washed away.

Just as the Bloody Mamba had frozen at the sight of Vikir in the Cradle, the Hellhounds of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain hesitated before him when he fully released his killing intent.

Vikir spoke.

“Come on, boys. I still have plenty of beans.”

The Bloody Beans at his waist rattled.

Killing just one Hellhound had strengthened his body. If he killed every Hellhound gathered here, the growth he might gain was impossible to guess.

As Vikir and the pack faced each other in a tense standoff—

Whine…

the ferocious Hellhounds suddenly tucked their tails and began backing away.

“…?”

Vikir was slightly surprised.

Once a Hellhound bared its fangs, it never turned its back.

Yet now they were not merely showing their backs—they were clearly fleeing in fear.

No matter how fierce Vikir’s killing intent was, he was still trapped in the body of an eight-year-old. It was not enough to drive off more than ten Hellhounds.

Then why?

What outside factor could make them flee with their tails tucked?

Vikir soon learned the answer.

Grrrrr…

The sound of saliva and sulfurous fire boiling together.

The master of this territory—the presence that had frightened all the Hellhounds away—had appeared.

Six eyes burned in the darkness.

Three heads emerged from a cave among the roots.

“…Something like this lives here too?”

It was a top-tier monster—one formidable enough to surprise even Vikir.

<Cerberus>

Danger Level: A+

Size: 7 m

Discovery Location: Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, Seventh Ridge

AKA: “Hell’s Guard Dog”

It showed no interest in souls entering hell, but any soul that tried to escape was immediately torn to pieces.

It dwelled in the deepest reaches of the underworld, where all souls ultimately arrived, and stood at the pinnacle of hell-type canine demonic creatures.

A three-headed dog symbolizing hell itself had appeared.
