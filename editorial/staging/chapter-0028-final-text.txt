Vikir made his way to City Hall at the center of Underdog City.
Past the offices where hundreds of employees worked, the Magistrate’s office stood at the far end of the complex.
“The new Deputy Magistrate arrives today. Make sure everything is spotless.”
“I wonder what kind of person he is.”
“I don’t care who he is as long as he understands how things actually work here.”
“Don’t expect too much. I heard he’s only fifteen.”
Employees swept, scrubbed, and cleaned throughout the building.
A banner hung high on the wall welcoming Vikir Van Baskerville, the new Deputy Magistrate and acting Magistrate.
Vikir walked past the workers without drawing attention to himself.
His formal first day was supposed to be tomorrow, so no one expected him to appear yet.
“Whose child is this? Did one of the staff bring him?”
“Kid, you’re not supposed to be in here.”
“This section is temporarily closed because an important official is arriving.”
“Go play outside. There’s a place for employees’ children.”
Several employees stepped in front of Vikir.
“Move.”
Then they noticed the appointment letter in his hand and bowed in confusion.
“…Deputy Magistrate, sir. You arrived early.”
The employees flattened themselves in panic as though they had seen a ghost.
Vikir entered the office and sat in the large chair behind the desk.
His first order after taking practical control of the city administration was immediate.
“Bring me wine and women.”
◆◆◆
Chihuahua Baskerville.
He had worked at Underdog City Hall for decades, and a sick feeling settled in his stomach.
‘A drinking party on his very first day.’
And the new Deputy Magistrate was only fifteen.
After the previous officials had been dismissed or suspended for bribery, watching a newly appointed child immediately begin indulging himself was almost unbearable.
‘And in a government office, of all places. What kind of brat is this?’
Chihuahua Baskerville rubbed his forehead.
He had entered Baskerville from outside the bloodline and earned the family surname through exceptional administrative ability.
Without a Le or Van middle name, he had risen only as far as chief secretary despite years of diligent work.
“I already have more work than I can finish, and now some spoiled brat rolls in to make things worse… I still hoped a Baskerville appointment might mean something.”
Even after hearing that a fifteen-year-old was coming, Chihuahua had tried not to abandon that last bit of hope.
Now the clink of glasses, women’s laughter, and entertainers’ songs drifted through the window.
Chihuahua twisted his goatee in irritation and dropped the file he had been holding.
“Fine. Let it all collapse. Let Underdog rot if that’s what they want—!”
He never finished the complaint.
Creak—
The office door opened and a young man stepped inside.
Vikir Van Baskerville, the newly appointed Deputy Magistrate.
He had finally come to the administrative offices in person.
“You’re the chief secretary.”
Chihuahua straightened and gave the proper formal greeting.
“Yes, sir. Chihuahua Baskerville.”
“You don’t have a middle name.”
Chihuahua’s expression tightened slightly.
Having no family middle name at all marked him as someone who had entered Baskerville from outside rather than through blood.
Outsiders were commonly required to complete years of contracted service before receiving the Baskerville surname and formal recognition; Staffordshire and Chihuahua had both followed that route.
Strictly speaking, they were retainers admitted into the house, not blood relatives.
Newly appointed nobles and deputies often used that distinction as an excuse to discriminate against them.
Chihuahua had endured that same atmosphere for years.
But—
“Good.”
Vikir’s reaction was the exact opposite of what Chihuahua expected.
While Chihuahua stared, Vikir recalled a name from his first life.
‘Chihuahua Baskerville. Yes, I remember him.’
He looked thin and somewhat sly because of his goatee, but in truth he was remarkably conscientious.
As an outsider, Chihuahua had suffered constant discrimination, and many of his achievements had been claimed by Magistrates and Deputy Magistrates above him.
That was something Vikir understood personally.
Vikir trusted blood relatives very little; a competent outsider with a proven record was far easier for him to rely on.
“I skimmed your personnel record on the way in. You were born in Underdog City, correct?”
“Y-yes, sir. Born and raised here.”
“Then you understand this city far better than a newcomer like me.”
“…Yes, sir.”
Chihuahua answered cautiously, then immediately regretted admitting it.
Past experience told him what usually came next: ‘If you know so much, why don’t you do it yourself?’ ‘If you’re so capable, why are you under me?’ or some other way of turning expertise into insubordination.
But Vikir surprised him again.
“I know how difficult the work is for people who actually operate in the field. I have no intention of casually interfering with their working principles, customs, or methods.”
Chihuahua went blank.
He had never expected to hear that from someone born inside Baskerville’s power structure.
He had assumed the new official would arrive with privilege, condemn every existing custom, and replace everything for the sake of showing authority.
‘At least he may not be a disaster.’
Even if Vikir did nothing useful, merely avoiding unnecessary damage would already be an improvement.
While Chihuahua quietly relaxed, Vikir began turning through the documents on the desk.
Rustle, rustle.
Chihuahua expected the boy to glance at them and lose interest.
Instead, Vikir read through the files with unsettling concentration.
After finishing, he closed the stack.
“The legislative structure itself is serviceable. The judicial system, however, has a surprising number of distortions.”
“What? You read all of that already?”
Chihuahua stared at the hundreds of pages in disbelief.
But Vikir already knew Underdog City far better than a new arrival should.
In his first life, the city had repeatedly appeared in intelligence work, infiltration operations, and criminal investigations.
“Illegal gambling houses, brothels, slave auctions, private moneylending, and other unauthorized financial operations are widespread. Underdog’s underground economy must be enormous.”
Chihuahua’s eyes narrowed.
Vikir continued.
“Those interests were probably part of what corrupted the previous Deputy Magistrates. Once officials begin compromising with that system, breaking the cycle becomes extremely difficult.”
“….”
“So tell me. As someone born in Underdog, what do you think?”
The direct question left Chihuahua speechless.
Vikir had absorbed a mountain of documents, identified the city’s central problem, and then asked an outsider-born subordinate for his judgment instead of looking down on him.
And he had done it before his formal first day had even begun.
The situation was so far outside Chihuahua’s expectations that he could only stammer.
Vikir gave a faint smile.
“Destroying an underground economy is actually simple once you find the center connecting all the scattered lawlessness.”
In his first life, Vikir had spent years fighting Underdog’s illegal organizations.
He remembered where major crimes occurred, which organizations controlled them, and where their people hid.
He even remembered the identities and locations of officials who took bribes.
‘The black ledgers seized from those organizations in my first life are finally useful.’
Making and enforcing law was precisely the authority of a Deputy Magistrate.
Vikir went straight to work.
“I will preserve every existing law. I am adding only one new law.”
“One new law?”
“Yes. It will be short—only a few lines.”
A cold light entered Vikir’s eyes.
“Within one week, I will dry up the roots of Underdog City’s underground economy.”
The declaration was brief and forceful, another step toward the Academy.
A war on crime was about to begin, and one new statute would make it possible.
“But to create and enforce that law, I need a proper justification.”
Listening to Vikir, Chihuahua found himself slowly drawn into the strange confidence surrounding the boy.
He could not help asking.
“A justification? Why would suppressing illegal activity need one?”
“I am young, and officially I am still the new Deputy Magistrate who begins work tomorrow.”
Vikir looked back at Chihuahua with a small smile.
“The least I can do is give them a warning first. Isn’t that professional courtesy?”
“…?”
Chihuahua tilted his head, unable to understand what Vikir meant.
Vikir did not explain.
Instead, he stroked his chin and looked down from the office window at the celebration below.
Eat, drink, and make merry—the welcoming banquet for the new Deputy Magistrate was already in full swing.
The party had become lavish even though Vikir himself was not attending it.
While everyone else celebrated in his name, the supposed guest of honor sat upstairs planning what would happen tomorrow.
“…Right. If you want to catch flies, you put out something that draws flies.”
The low murmur carried an edge that did not belong to an ordinary fifteen-year-old.
Chihuahua looked at Vikir and had a sudden thought.
Meeting this boy might change the course of his entire life.
