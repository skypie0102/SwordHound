It was late at night, before the moon had risen.
Vikir visited the dungeon beneath City Hall.
The nearly empty prison reeked of decay.
Prisons were eerie places by nature, and the darkness of an almost vacant one made it worse.
All the guards had gone home early. Unusually, not one remained for the night shift.
Every prisoner had been executed, pardoned, or transferred elsewhere, leaving the cells empty.
Vikir walked toward one of the deepest cells.
The darkest and foulest-smelling place in the dungeon.
Solitary confinement.
The most secluded cell, behind the only door still securely locked.
Only one prisoner remained there.
She had once served as secretary to Baron Gambino, one of the great figures of the underground economy.
A woman with short greenish-blond hair crouched in the corner in prison clothes, heavy shackles binding her wrists.
Vikir pulled up a chair and sat before the bars.
Scrape—
The iron chair legs grated loudly against the stone floor of the empty dungeon.
“Your name is… Sen Rose Cindywendy. Correct?”
Vikir asked, but she did not answer.
She simply stared at the floor with empty eyes.
“Why did you keep me alive?”
Cindywendy’s question sounded hollow.
The Freak Show. And the Pit Bull Knights.
On the night when everything that moved was being slaughtered, Cindywendy had clenched her teeth and tried to escape.
She could not die there.
Even if she survived, losing her wrists and ankles would have been little different from death.
There were things she still needed to do; she could not die or become crippled in a place like that.
Almost everyone facing death must have felt desperate, but Cindywendy believed she had more reason than most to survive.
Yet the fangs of the fighting dog blocking her path had been merciless.
When a Pit Bull Knight stepped in front of her, it was practically a death sentence.
Cindywendy gritted her teeth as the blood-red blade descended toward her throat.
But.
Clang!
The sound that rang out was not the sound she expected.
The Pit Bull Knight’s sword had failed to reach her throat.
Vikir—the newly appointed Deputy Magistrate—had intercepted the blade with a bundle of chains.
The Pit Bull Knight looked surprised that Vikir had stopped his strike, but Vikir’s next order was brief.
“This woman is an exception. Capture her alive. Do not harm a single finger.”
That was how Sen Rose Cindywendy had ended up imprisoned here.
Vikir watched her silently for a while.
Finally, he spoke.
“If you answer my questions truthfully, I will release you.”
…!
Cindywendy’s attention sharpened at once.
But she soon lowered her head again and muttered bitterly.
“I lost the will to escape once I was locked in here. I have no reason to do so now.”
Whether she liked it or not, Vikir had already begun questioning her.
“Are you the reason Baron Gambino, once an insignificant underworld figure, expanded his power so dramatically in recent years?”
“….”
“I looked into you. You have an extraordinary talent for making money. If that ability had flourished somewhere other than the black market, you could have become an exceptional merchant or financier.”
Cindywendy gave a bitter laugh.
It was a laugh that seemed to ask what any of that mattered now.
Vikir held her gaze.
Then he spoke in a low, even voice.
“I give you my word.”
“…?”
“Answer my questions and I will let you leave. But—”
Vikir paused, tilted his head toward the bars, and met Cindywendy’s eyes.
A sharp red gleam pierced her green irises.
“If you refuse to answer, you will regret this day.”
There was something strange in his voice, an oppressive weight beyond ordinary fear.
Without fully understanding why, Cindywendy opened her mouth.
“If it is something I can answer, I will.”
“Good.”
Vikir asked directly.
“What do you think?”
“About what?”
“Do you think I am enforcing the law incorrectly?”
Cindywendy answered calmly.
“Why should you care what a lowly criminal woman like me thinks?”
“I am asking this lowly criminal woman what she thinks.”
“Then I’ll speak plainly. I think you are doing it wrong.”
Vikir nodded.
“Why?”
“In every theory of rulership, virtue is one of a ruler’s central qualities. Harsh rule of law may work for a while, but over the long term…”
“Virtue? What is that?”
“A virtuous ruler does not ride while others walk because he is tired, does not shelter himself from the heat while others endure it, and does not surround every journey with armed men. When such a ruler dies, the people mourn, children stop singing, and even laborers stop humming. That is virtue.”
“Is my lack of virtue such a serious flaw?”
“It is serious. At this rate, you will not live long. Harsh laws may let you establish authority by crushing petty villains, but over time resentment and anger will accumulate.”
“Should I fear the resentment of ordinary people?”
“Not only ordinary people. House Baskerville will dislike it as well. Underdog may be only one city, but its people have begun to fear you, the Deputy Magistrate, more than Baskerville itself.”
“Hmm. True.”
“The same problem will remain when your superior arrives or when a successor replaces you. You changed the law, established your authority, and accumulated achievements at frightening speed. Local factions are grinding their teeth over it. Other cities have their own underworld powers as well. Considering how many interests you have offended, I doubt you will live long if you continue like this.”
“Then what do you think I should do?”
“You are as precarious as morning dew. If you want to live, resign as Deputy Magistrate, return your authority to House Baskerville, and let the Patriarch and Imperial Family take credit for what happened here. Then go somewhere appropriate for your age—training in Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, perhaps, or the Imperial Academy—and prepare for the future.”
“And?”
“Keep a low profile. Conceal your strength and devote yourself to learning. Advise your superiors to discover and promote overlooked talent, respect the elderly, care for orphans, recognize unrewarded service, and honor the virtuous.”
“And what would I gain from doing that?”
“Peace of mind, for one thing. By then, the Patriarch of House Baskerville will have absorbed the blame that should have fallen on you. Even if he takes the immediate credit as well, you are only fifteen. Your name has already spread as far as the Imperial Capital, even if half the stories about you are exaggerated.”
Vikir gave a rare smile at Cindywendy’s lengthy advice.
Her recommendations aligned closely with his own plans for the future.
“Good.”
Vikir nodded.
At the same time,
Clang!
the iron cell door swung open.
Vikir tore the lock apart with his bare hands and removed Cindywendy’s restraints.
“As promised, you are free.”
“….”
Cindywendy looked up at the open iron door.
Then she turned toward Vikir.
“You are really letting me go?”
“I am. I keep my word.”
“You will regret it.”
Life returned to Cindywendy’s eyes.
Vikir smiled faintly.
“I hope you can make me.”
“…?”
Cindywendy tilted her head in confusion.
After a moment, Vikir spoke again.
“You gave me useful advice, so I will return the favor.”
Cindywendy stopped just as she was about to leave the cell.
Vikir spoke to her back.
“There are seven famous native families in Underdog: House Montblanc, House Pierre, House Louis Vuitton, House Chanel, House Ferragamo, House Hermès, and House Prada.”
“….”
“But a few years ago, there were eight, not seven.”
Vikir did not miss the slight tremor that passed through Cindywendy’s body.
“There was originally an eighth family: House Messinadnaro, the wealthiest of Underdog’s merchant houses.”
“….”
“But they were annihilated overnight. Every member of the family was said to have died. Do you know why?”
“….”
“They were accused of stealing House Baskerville’s sword manual. Their eight-year-old son had suddenly begun boasting that he had learned excellent swordsmanship.”
“….”
“At the boy’s birthday banquet, the head of House Messinadnaro asked him to demonstrate the swordsmanship he had learned.”
“…Enough.”
Cindywendy raised one hand to stop him.
But Vikir continued.
“In front of everyone, the child demonstrated his new swordsmanship. The guests were shocked, because what he showed was the Baskerville Style—swordsmanship taught only within House Baskerville.”
“…Enough. Stop.”
“House Baskerville guards its swordsmanship as a highest-level military secret. When the report reached the family, the Patriarch released the hounds. From that day on, House Messinadnaro vanished from the world.”
“Shut up, you bastard!”
Cindywendy screamed.
But Vikir did not stop.
“Later, it became clear that House Messinadnaro had been innocent. The children of the other seven houses had set the trap. They manipulated a young Baskerville into stealing the manual, had the Messinadnaro boy taught from it, then made sure the ignorant child demonstrated the technique before adults. The entire incident was staged.” Cindywendy whispered, “…Please stop.”
“But there was one survivor of House Messinadnaro, which everyone believed had been annihilated: a one-year-old girl. The seven wretches secretly spirited her out of the massacre.”
“…Ugh. Ugh!”
Cindywendy staggered and leaned against the wall.
Her eyes were red and bloodshot.
Vikir finished the story.
“That girl was subjected to every kind of cruel, degrading, and humiliating abuse simply because she was bright and beautiful. The details are not something I care to repeat.”
“I don’t want to hear any more. Goodbye.”
A bloody tear slid from one of Cindywendy’s eyes.
She pushed herself away from the wall and staggered toward the exit.
Then,
“I warned you. If you do not listen, you will regret this day for the rest of your life.”
Vikir remained where he was.
“There is a sequel to this story.”
“…?”
Cindywendy stopped.
Vikir shrugged once.
“Much later, a new Deputy Magistrate came to the city.”
“….”
“He dragged the old evils of Underdog into the light and punished them.”
“….”
“And the event began with the seven wretches who had driven House Messinadnaro to ruin.”
Cindywendy turned to face Vikir.
Vikir met her gaze directly.
“The new Deputy Magistrate tortured those seven heirs to death.”
“….”
“The torture was so terrible that even an interrogator with thirty years of experience vomited.”
“….”
“And while they were dying, the seven confessed every crime they had committed—including what they did to House Messinadnaro. They admitted their guilt and apologized.”
Cindywendy suddenly screamed.
“Apologized? How dare they think they can apologize!”
“To the sole survivor of House Messinadnaro. To the girl who now uses the name ‘Sen Rose Cindywendy’—whose real name is ‘Messinadnaro Sen Cindywendy.’”
Cindywendy staggered backward, stunned.
“Ugh!”
She vomited.
Her vision spun.
The dungeon was pitch black.
The rotten smell lingering in the cell seemed to grow stronger.
Cindywendy braced herself against the wall with one hand and held her forehead with the other.
She spat onto the floor and asked,
“How am I supposed to believe you?”
“….”
“How can I believe you when you say you captured them, tortured them, uncovered the truth, and even made them apologize?”
Cindywendy screamed.
Vikir gave no answer.
Instead,
Click!
Vikir struck a spark from the rod in his hand and lit a torch.
The dungeon cell was suddenly flooded with light.
And then,
“…!”
Cindywendy’s eyes widened.
Seven headless corpses knelt in one corner of the room.
Their fingers had been worn away, and the blood from their hands had stained the floor, walls, and even the ceiling.
The entire chamber was red.
Looking closer, the red covering every surface was made from countless overlapping lines of writing.
-Please forgive me. I deserve to die, but spare my family…
-We destroyed House Messinadnaro. Please forgive our sins…
-We sincerely apologize to Cindywendy. We are the ones who wronged you…
-We were wrong, wrong, wrong, wrong, wrong…
-Mercy, mercy, mercy, mercy, mercy…
-I don’t want to die. I don’t want to die…
-I’m scared. I’m scared. I’m scared…
The corpses smelled of rot.
And the apologies they had scratched out before death, wearing their fingers down against the stone, covered the room.
The blood used to write them had stained the entire chamber red.
“….”
Cindywendy stood in the middle of the red room and stared at Vikir in disbelief.
Then Vikir stepped toward her.
“But House Messinadnaro’s revenge is not finished.”
“….”
“One family still remains.”
His meaning was obvious.
Their greatest enemy—House Baskerville—still existed.
Vikir spoke.
“Resent House Baskerville. You have every right to.”
“….”
“And beyond that, I will speak on behalf of every Baskerville.”
Vikir slowly bowed before Cindywendy.
“I’m sorry.”
At the same time,
drip, drip, drip,
tears began to fall from Cindywendy’s eyes onto the stone floor.
“….”
“….”
For a while, neither moved.
Then Cindywendy spoke.
“You are a Baskerville too… so why are you helping me?”
Vikir did not answer.
Sharp-eyed Cindywendy understood the meaning of his silence.
Whether from inside or outside the family, their hatred of Baskerville pointed in the same direction.
In that sense, Vikir and Cindywendy understood each other well.
Then,
Cindywendy walked out of the prison.
She turned toward Vikir, who still stood inside, and spoke in an unusually calm voice.
“No matter what you do with your life, I will make sure you never lack money.”
Vikir had gained a new sponsor.
