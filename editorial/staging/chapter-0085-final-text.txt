Ahmen descended from the altar after days of ritual.
His body was soaked with sweat from the sacrifices he had performed without rest.
When he stepped outside and saw the village strangely empty, he laughed to himself.
“…They must all be sick by now.”
In truth, Ahmen had known the Red Death was coming.
He knew because he had helped House Leviathan release it into the jungle.
His dealings with outsiders had been going on for a long time.
He had helped Leviathan test medicines and poisons on unsuspecting natives and had also aided Bourgeois merchants in exploiting the tribes.
In return for access to test subjects, Leviathan supplied Ahmen with new poisons and antidotes that could strengthen his authority as shaman.
His method was simple: secretly poison someone, then later cure them and claim spiritual power.
Disease and medicine became tools of control.
If someone resisted him, he could make that person sick.
The poison would often affect friends and family as well.
In a close-knit tribe like Ballak, that multiplied the fear.
Only when a victim seemed close to death would Ahmen intervene.
He would perform a dramatic ritual, administer the antidote, and turn the grateful patient and family into devoted followers.
His authority would rise because everyone believed the ritual had saved them.
Recently, however, Ahmen had felt that authority slipping.
The younger generation, especially Aiyen, had grown skeptical because they had rarely needed his treatments.
The arrival of Vikir made the problem even worse.
Young hunters began leaving without blessings, and more rituals were dismissed as superstition.
Then Leviathan approached Ahmen with a proposal.
“We intend to conduct a large-scale plague experiment through the waterways.”
Ahmen swallowed hard when the cloaked messenger explained the plan.
The messenger supplied both the Red Death pathogen and an antidote.
Ahmen was to spread the disease at the proper time, then distribute the antidote later.
Leviathan wanted population-control results and clinical data from the Black Mountain tribes.
Ahmen wanted the resulting crisis to restore and expand the shaman’s political authority.
So he secretly contaminated the river and allowed the Red Death to spread.
Once the tribe was desperate enough, he planned to emerge with the antidote, end the crisis, and claim savior-like power.
Even the young hunters who mocked him would be forced to acknowledge him.
‘…I even made sure my own granddaughter was infected.’
Sacrificing Aheul’s health was meant to make his innocence look unquestionable.
That had been Ahmen’s calculation.
…?
As he crossed the village, confusion began to replace confidence.
The settlement was almost completely deserted.
Sick people, their families, and frightened friends should have been waiting for the shaman who claimed he could save them.
Yet no one had come to greet him.
“Are they all dead already?”
Ahmen frowned. If the plague had killed everyone, his plan had failed.
They were supposed to become desperate, not die before he could recruit their loyalty.
He adjusted the sack containing Leviathan’s antidote at his waist and hurried across the square.
The surrounding shelters remained empty.
Only a frog croaked in a drained puddle below.
Then—
Ahmen stopped.
Thick smoke rose from one corner of the village.
Every familiar face seemed to be gathered there.
The people of Ballak were eating together.
They laughed, talked, and pulled meat from enormous piles of food.
“????”
Ahmen’s mouth fell open.
How could people he expected to be dying look completely healthy?
He rubbed his eyes, but the sight did not change.
Worse, Aheul herself was standing by a pot and stirring food.
Ahmen stared at his fully recovered granddaughter in disbelief.
Then he turned and ran.
He crossed the village boundary and sprinted toward the same river he had contaminated.
“…Hah… hah!”
He reached the headwaters and stopped in fresh horror.
The water that should have carried the Red Death was running clear.
The pale sandbar remained clean, and carp still swam against the current.
Monkeys moved through the trees while children from neighboring tribes played nearby without showing any sign of illness.
There was no trace of the disaster Ahmen expected.
“No… how…?”
He grabbed at his hair.
Then—
“Why? Surprised that so many people are alive?”
The words struck him like a blade.
Ahmen spun around.
A boy stood behind him.
Vikir’s red eyes were fixed on the shaman.
◆◆◆
Meanwhile—
Vikir had already brought Ballak’s warriors to the river, including Aiyen and Ahun.
Aiyen remembered Vikir’s prediction clearly.
“As soon as the ritual ends, Ahmen will check the village. When he sees everyone healthy, he’ll run straight back to the water source.”
Because Ahmen himself was the one who had released the Red Death.
Vikir remembered the outline of the betrayal from his previous life.
He had not remembered Ahmen’s name, but he remembered that Ballak’s shaman had collaborated with outsiders during the plague.
“The enemy’s scheme killed an enormous number of Black Mountain natives in my previous life.”
Driven by greed, Ahmen had waited for the epidemic to reach its worst point.
He intended to appear at the perfect moment and become the jungle’s savior.
But in the previous timeline he had miscalculated.
The plague had spread too widely and too quickly for his antidote plan to control.
The resulting deaths became notorious even in Imperial records.
As always, ordinary and vulnerable people paid the price for the ambitions of powerful men.
Poisons and medicines could be legitimate tools in a shaman’s craft, but Ahmen had long since crossed every acceptable boundary.
“…What is this?”
Ahmen glared at Vikir but did not dare move.
Ballak’s warriors had already surrounded him.
Young and old alike stared at the shaman with murderous anger.
Those were not the worshipful eyes Ahmen had expected.
“How did you cure the plague?”
Ahmen could barely form the question.
Then—
Someone stepped forward.
Ahmen’s face went pale.
Akwilla, the Night Fox and chieftain of Ballak, looked down at him.
She held an official notice obtained from the Empire during the previous night.
“House Quovadis has declared a crusade against House Leviathan over the artificial development and release of the Red Death. They are demanding the truth behind the plague.”
“I-is that so? Why tell me?”
“Strange, isn’t it? A plague Leviathan was studying somehow appears here in the depths.”
“I-I know nothing. I only just finished a ritual for the tribe’s recovery…”
Akwilla raised one hand and cut him off.
She exchanged a glance with Vikir.
“Enough. I want to see what’s inside the sack at your waist.”
