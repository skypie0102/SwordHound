Chirp—.
A small bird called nearby.
Vikir opened his eyes to morning sunlight.
The light stung against his battered body.
When he tried to rise, pain tore through him as though every part of him had been shattered.
Through sheer will he managed to sit upright, but standing was another matter.
‘Where am I?’
Vikir searched his memory for the previous night—or however many days ago it had been.
He remembered throwing every ounce of strength he had into the darkness of the swamp.
His mana had been wrung dry and his aura burned nearly to nothing.
He had combined his Intermediate Sword Graduator power with Beelzebub and struck with everything he had.
‘Failure.’
Madam Eight-Legs, the gigantic demonic beast hidden in the darkness, had exceeded even Vikir’s estimate.
At the decisive moment, he would have died if the sudden explosion of light had not made the creature recoil.
And an attack with that much power had exacted a terrible price from Vikir himself.
Muscles and blood vessels throughout his body had ruptured.
The bones supporting them had suffered even worse.
The fingers and forearms that held his sword were crushed by comminuted fractures, while twisted broken ribs had pierced into his insides.
His legs had suffered several different kinds of severe fractures as well.
Hardly any part of his body—bone, muscle, blood vessel, or skin—had escaped damage.
‘At least I survived.’
Vikir exhaled in relief.
The protection of the River Styx and Beelzebub’s absorbed **Tough Life** had kept him alive.
Misjudging Madam Eight-Legs had been costly, but at least he had successfully fallen off Hugo’s radar.
House Baskerville would soon receive word of his death.
Adolf, Camus, Staffordshire, and the triplets had all witnessed his apparent sacrifice, so establishing the story would not be difficult.
And he had not supposedly died through incompetence, but while saving Morgue’s future head from barbarians and an S-rank demonic beast.
‘…That should give Baskerville considerable leverage in its future relationship with Morgue.’
For a knightly house, the story would also improve Baskerville’s reputation both inside and outside the family.
Such a feat might even earn praise from the Imperial Court.
Even dead, Vikir would not be remembered as a disgrace; his supposed death brought the family honor and political profit.
If he later returned alive, he could expect even better treatment.
‘I need to recover quickly and rebuild the strength of my previous life.’
Vikir drew on the power absorbed from Murciélago as his battered body began to mend.
The recovery was not instantaneous, but bone and flesh were repairing themselves at an extraordinary pace.
Then,
Vikir noticed the rope around his neck.
A lasso hung from him almost like a dog collar.
Besides Styx and Tough Life, this rope was another reason he had survived.
Just before Madam Eight-Legs’s attack could crush him completely, the lasso had flown from behind and yanked him away.
The perfectly timed pull had nearly broken his neck, but that was preferable to death.
Vikir gripped the rope at his throat.
Its other end passed through the tent and out the entrance.
Only then did Vikir properly examine the shelter.
It was a conical animal-hide tent supported by a few wooden poles, with dry leaves scattered across the floor.
Rustle—
A glass vial lay nearby; on closer inspection, it was a potion bottle bearing Baskerville’s crest.
The smells of dry soil, fallen leaves, animal hide, and medicinal herbs mingled in the tent.
Vikir crawled out on hands and knees.
Every movement burned, but he needed to understand his surroundings.
The view outside soon came into focus.
Animal-hide tents of many sizes, drums sounding through the settlement, and flames rising from tall piles of wood.
A large number of tribespeople moved among them.
Men and women alike had black or silver hair, brown skin, and powerful physiques.
Their clothes were made from deer or jaguar hides and cloth dyed with plant sap.
Tattooed and scarred adults butchered demonic-beast carcasses while children played nearby with birds.
The settlement felt cheerful and energetic, but also raw and unrefined.
Vikir immediately understood that he was in a Ballak village.
The heart of a people who had long been enemies of House Baskerville.
He should have been on guard, but his current body was too damaged for meaningful resistance.
Vikir considered what to do next.
Ballak warriors passed him without paying much attention to the lone outsider.
Women and children watched him warily, but did nothing more.
Then,
“Move! Hurry up!”
A harsh shout sounded nearby.
Vikir turned and saw several Ballak warriors dragging prisoners bound with rope.
The captives staggered in a line and came from several different peoples.
Most were black-haired, brown-skinned men from other tribes.
Every one of them showed signs of harsh captivity and abuse.
Vikir recognized several faces.
He did not know their names, but some were clearly warriors of Baskerville and Morgue.
Some he had seen at the fortress; others had entered the jungle with him.
Bound together like fish on a line, they briefly met Vikir’s eyes as he stood wearing only the rope at his neck.
“….”
“….”
Then the two sides passed one another.
Vikir silently watched them go.
The Ballak guards jabbed the captives forward with spearpoints.
“Climb. Unless you want the wolves to tear you apart.”
They drove the prisoners toward an impossibly tall tree.
Long, blade-like thorns covered its trunk.
The Ballak forced the captives to climb the thorn-covered tree without protection.
Blood covered them as they struggled upward.
If anyone refused, the guards set fire to the base.
Oil had been spread over the trunk, so the flames climbed faster than most prisoners could.
A captive who remained on the tree risked bleeding from the thorns, burning in the rising fire, or falling to the ground below.
Woof! Woof! Woof!
Wolves howled beneath the tree, driving the prisoners upward.
Those who refused to climb could instead be stabbed by spears or attacked by the wolves.
This brutal **Tree of Thorns Punishment** had been devised by an old Ballak shaman.
Vikir could only watch the execution in silence.
Then,
“Hah. Be grateful you don’t look like them.”
Someone spoke beside Vikir.
Ahun—the Ballak warrior who had fired the paralytic thorn at Camus.
He glared at Vikir as though he wanted to kill him on the spot.
“If you weren’t the captain’s pet, I’d have boiled you already.”
“….”
“Don’t stand there. Go find your master.”
Ahun kicked Vikir hard from behind.
“….”
Vikir fell, then looked back at Ahun.
Ahun flinched under the stare.
“What are those eyes…?”
There was not the slightest agitation in Vikir’s calm gaze.
Neither the captives’ brutal deaths nor the pain of his own ruined body disturbed his expression.
Ahun felt a chill when he realized how little any of it seemed to shake Vikir.
Slowly, Vikir rose and began following the rope attached to his neck.
Ahun could only stare after him.
◆◆◆
‘That was Ahun, wasn’t it?’
He was probably still angry about the injury Vikir had given him during the raid.
Vikir had not forgotten the face of the man who had just kicked him.
But in his current condition he could do little about it, so he would endure it for now.
The protection of Styx and the power taken from Murciélago were steadily restoring his body.
In the meantime, Vikir decided to find the owner of the lasso that had saved his life.
He followed the rope for some time until it led to a secluded place far from the village.
A small waterfall spilled between rocks into a clear pool fed by a narrow stream.
Several crayfish crawled along the bottom.
The rope led directly toward the water.
“…?”
Vikir stopped.
The line ran to the center of the pool and disappeared beneath the surface.
Then,
Splash!
Someone rose from the water.
Droplets scattered from silver-and-black hair beneath pointed ears.
Aiyen emerged from the pool without her usual clothing.
The far end of Vikir’s rope was tied securely around her wrist.
“….”
Vikir looked directly at her face.
Without the charcoal, her sharp brows, clear eyes, straight nose, and red lips were plainly visible.
The black ash was gone, but Vikir knew those eyes.
They belonged to the same Ballak girl he had encountered at the illegal slave auction during Underdog’s purge.
Their positions were now completely reversed, and Vikir held her gaze.
“….”
“….”
A brief silence passed.
Aiyen broke it first.
She tugged on the rope around Vikir’s neck and grinned.
“Down.”
