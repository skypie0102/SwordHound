Camus had dropped a bombshell.
“Are you the thief who stole our ruby mine?”
The eight-year-old girl's fearless question froze the room in an instant.
Servants and attendants from both families stiffened where they stood.
“….”
“….”
Even Adolf Morgue, normally unflappable, stared in confusion.
Hugo also remained silent, looking genuinely stunned.
‘What exactly did I just hear?’ The thought was almost visible through his usual poker face.
The patriarch's office became so quiet it might have been empty.
Camus broke that silence herself.
“Give us back the ruby mine.”
She marched forward and held out one small white palm.
Hugo continued staring at her, still momentarily at a loss.
No one had ever made such an accusation directly to his face—much less an eight-year-old child.
Adolf was the first adult to recover.
“Camus! What is this sudden rudeness?”
“You said it yourself, Uncle. You said this man stole our ruby mine.”
“W-when did I ever say that!?”
“Before we got out of the carriage. You said my research allocation of rubies would be reduced starting this month because of that useless old mustached thief.”
“W-when did I say something like that!?”
“If you mean exactly when… about one hour and forty-two minutes ago. I heard you clearly.”
Camus's confidence faltered only slightly.
By then, cold sweat had begun forming on Adolf's skin.
He did not even need to look toward Hugo to feel the temperature in the room drop.
“…A useless old mustached man. And a thief?”
The voice from in front of him was cold.
Hugo narrowed his eyes at Adolf.
Adolf produced a handkerchief, covered his mouth, and coughed.
“Ahem. There appears to be some misunderstanding. I will correct it later.”
“What does a puppy know? I have no interest in hearing excuses for how Morgue raises its children.”
Hugo's words were barbed, and his mood had clearly soured.
Adolf turned toward Camus with an inward groan.
“Camus, you cannot speak so rudely to an elder of a neighboring house.”
“But Uncle, you said—”
“Even if people around you say something unkind, you should not simply repeat it. Rumors are often exaggerated… and occasionally understated.”
Adolf and Camus stared at one another.
Hugo abruptly found the entire scene tiresome.
He remembered a report Butler Barrymore had delivered not long ago.
‘First report: regarding the bloodshed with House Morgue at the Red Fang Mountain ruby mine…’
‘We will have an opportunity to discuss it soon enough. Morgue will approach us first.’
The ruby mine was, in fact, an old dispute.
In the so-called Joint Security Zone along the Baskerville–Morgue border lay a mine producing high-quality rubies.
The problem was simple: the accessible entrance lay on Morgue land, while the underground vein extended beneath Baskerville territory.
Because of the angle of the deposit, the only practical surface route into the vein began from Morgue's side.
But following the ruby underground inevitably meant tunneling beneath Baskerville land.

Morgue had therefore tried several ways to work around the territorial problem.
The most common was to dig covert tunnels and mine the rubies in secret.
But Baskerville's hounds, with their exceptional senses, repeatedly detected the intrusion and raided the mining sites.
Blood had been spilled more than once, but nothing was publicly reported, allowing the two houses to remain cordial on the surface.
Underneath that courtesy, resentment was already boiling.
Adolf's visit for the annual exercise was therefore also a visit to discuss the mine and the bloodshed surrounding it.
Hugo had prepared for exactly that conversation.
“Give me back my rubies! I can't continue my research properly without them!”
What he had not prepared for was an eight-year-old pressing the demand so relentlessly.
Hugo put a hand to his forehead.
“Acting representative Adolf, what exactly is the purpose of this official visit?”
“Ahem. I apologize, my lord. But I am in a difficult position, because the child is not entirely wrong.”
“…What?”
“Her wording is crude, but the underlying point is valid.”
Hugo and Adolf met one another's eyes.
“….”
“….”
Hugo began releasing the pressure of a Sword Master's presence, intending to dominate the room, but Camus stood directly in front of him.
He restrained the pressure enough that it would merely make the child step back.
“What are you doing? Is the Sword Star of the Empire persecuting an eight-year-old girl?”
Adolf immediately seized the opening.
He swept Camus beneath his cloak as though protecting her.
Hugo's face tightened.
Arguing with an eight-year-old would damage his dignity, yet if he said nothing Adolf could keep using the girl's innocence as political cover.
The entire situation irritated Hugo immensely.
Was it really necessary to drag the long-simmering ruby dispute back into the open?
For Baskerville the issue was sovereignty and pride; for Morgue it was practical wealth and research.
Rubies were important magical materials to Morgue, while Baskerville had comparatively little need for them.
Hugo, who disliked entanglement with other houses, would have preferred to ignore the ruby vein altogether.
Training hounds and expanding through the hostile mountain frontier already gave him enough problems; he had no desire to quarrel over red stones used in magical experiments.
“Please return the ruby mine. Without those rubies, continuing my studies will be difficult.”
“Camus, the adults are talking. Stop interrupting.”
Hugo could barely stand listening to Camus and Adolf play off one another.
‘No matter how I look at it, this is political theater…’
But because the person in front of him was a child, he could do little more than seethe.
Even the head of Ironblood Baskerville could be trapped by appearances.
Just then—
a new voice made Hugo's attention sharpen.
“Does Morgue not teach you not to covet what belongs to others?”
The voice was clear and youthful, unmistakably that of another eight-year-old.
Vikir had arrived.
