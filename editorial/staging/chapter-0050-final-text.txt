Vikir wrinkled his nose.
“…Pass?”
He had no idea what that was supposed to mean.
But there was no time to dwell on it.
Vikir searched his memories, trying to identify the Ballak girl before him.
Information about the Ballak tribe from his previous life flashed through his mind.
# Ballak # frontier warrior tribe # Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain highlands # population roughly 300–500 # wolf-riding hunter-gatherers # bows as primary weapons # extremely hostile to the Empire # men and women both fight # every tribesperson is highly militant…
After sorting through the fragments, Vikir began to form a picture.
Few individual Ballak were known to the Empire, but several names had become famous even beyond the mountains.
The first was Adonai, the legendary Archer God, one of the greatest warriors in Ballak history.
He had been counted among the Empire’s Three Calamities Beyond the Border and had led Ballak during one of its strongest eras.
According to the records, he once declared war on the Empire almost single-handedly and killed 542 Sword Graduators during the Hundred-Day War.
The number of ordinary soldiers who died was beyond counting.
The Empire suffered staggering losses in those hundred days, and afterward Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain was treated as extralegal territory where all-out war was to be avoided.
Imperial histories minimized the story, but the feat remained extraordinary.
‘…But this is not Adonai’s era.’
Vikir thought for a moment.
Adonai belonged to a generation far earlier than his own.
He was so old that even Vikir had known him only from historical accounts.
Adonai vanished around the time House Baskerville first expanded into the western frontier and was presumed to have died then.
Hugo Le Baskerville had not even been born.
So who from the modern era was famous enough to fit?
‘If I think of someone more recent… there was the Night Fox, who fought Baskerville and Morgue for ten days, killed thirty-two elites, and disappeared.’
When Vikir was young, Baskerville and Morgue had once launched a joint campaign against the tribes of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.
One Ballak archer distinguished herself so greatly in that war that the Empire called her the Night Fox.
Very little was known about her except that she was a woman.
Over ten days, she killed sixteen Sword Graduators of Baskerville, ten Fourth-Circle mages of Morgue, and six Fifth-Circle mages.
Vikir had been too young to witness that disaster directly, but many of the instructors he later knew had died because of it.
‘And the same person is active in this life.’
The archer who recently scarred Swordmaster Hugo’s nose and wounded Sixth-Circle Master Adolf in the chest was almost certainly the Night Fox.
‘…But this girl is far too young to be her.’
The Night Fox should now have been at least in her early thirties, perhaps closer to her mid-thirties.
Even an archery genius would need years of training to threaten a Swordmaster and a Sixth-Circle mage.
The Ballak girl in front of him looked only in her late teens.
Her talent was extraordinary, but she was still far below the Night Fox’s known level.
‘She was never mentioned in the histories, and I have never heard her name.’
Then what was she—perhaps a descendant of the Night Fox?
Imperial records contained little about Ballak, and even Baskerville, which fought them regularly, knew surprisingly little.
If the Night Fox had children or close blood relatives, this girl could be one of them.
‘I’ve practically become a researcher of barbarian tribes.’
Vikir processed all of that in only a few moments.
Just then,
Whoosh—
a whisper of wind came from somewhere.
It was too faint to sound like an arrow.
But the instincts Vikir had honed through the Age of Destruction would not permit even a hair-thin opening.
Snap!
Vikir swept his cloak through the air and knocked the projectile aside.
It was a cactus thorn coated in paralytic poison.
The same kind that had struck Camus earlier.
“…What!? How did he block that?”
A shocked voice came from the darkness.
Ahun was hiding beneath a tree root with a blowpipe at his mouth.
Vikir considered retaliating, then withdrew his aura instead.
The priority was getting Camus to safety—and, in doing so, settling the enormous debt Morgue would owe him.
“Hold on a little longer. I’ll get you back to your uncle soon…?”
Vikir stopped mid-sentence.
Camus was staring blankly up at him from his arms.
Her face was flushed, her breathing quick, and her heartbeat abnormally fast.
“Were you poisoned with something besides the paralytic?”
Vikir asked, sensing that something was wrong.
“Poisoned?”
“…Maybe.”
If that was true, it was troublesome.
Vikir began retreating quickly.
Aiyen drew her bow at once, but even she could not easily stop Vikir once he committed to withdrawing.
Whirr—!
Rope snares came flying again.
The Ballak women relentlessly cast their lassos toward Vikir.
But,
Slash-slash!
Vikir released his aura and cut every rope apart.
With so many witnesses present, he could show only low-level Sword Graduator aura and swordsmanship, but even that was enough to overwhelm ordinary Ballak warriors.
However,
Ping! Ping!
Aiyen’s arrows still demanded caution.
Fortunately, she was not shooting for Vikir’s vital points.
Her shots precisely targeted his legs while lassos repeatedly crossed their paths.
Carrying Camus in his arms made the retreat much more difficult.
Camus clutched at Vikir’s robe, clearly aware that she was slowing him down.
“…I never thought I’d become the embarrassing heroine who only gets in the hero’s way.”
“Calling yourself the heroine is rather self-important.”
Vikir adjusted Camus in his arms and continued backing away.
Just then,
Boom!
A tremendous explosion raised a wall of ice between Vikir and the Ballak warriors.
An enormous frozen barrier suddenly enclosed the pursuing tribespeople.
Adolf Morgue.
A high-ranking member of the Mage Council and leader of the powerful Light Hall faction.
He stood among the highest-ranking mages of the Empire.
His power had already passed deep into the Sixth Circle and was reaching toward the Seventh.
“Stand back, nephew-in-law.”
Adolf traced formulas through the air and calculated rapidly.
Volume, density, latent heat, remaining mana, and the strain on his magical tools were all combined into the spell.
The result was terrifying.
Creak, creak, creak!
The waterlogged ground began freezing over.
Hail and snow filled the air like the arrival of an ice age, and the ankles of the fleeing Ballak froze to the ground.
The Ballak warriors and their wolves were tough enough that the sudden freezing did not simply shatter their limbs.
But being pinned in place was another matter.
“Bite them all to death.”
Baskerville’s hounds fell upon the immobilized Ballak warriors.
Morgue’s mages joined them, and screams rose across the frozen ground.
By then, Adolf had taken Camus from Vikir.
“Niece, are you all right?”
Adolf anxiously examined Camus.
At the same time, detoxification magic formed in his hands.
Flash!
CURE POISON.
It was high-level magic beyond the reach of ordinary mages.
Adolf removed much of the paralytic poison from Camus’s body.
“That was an enormous dose. I cannot purge all of it here. You may need extended rest, and once we return we should have the Holy House of Quovadis examine you.”
Even Adolf could not immediately eliminate every trace of the toxin.
Once enough of the paralysis faded, Camus forced herself upright.
Then,
Thump!
She lunged on still-unsteady legs and wrapped her arms around Vikir’s neck.
“You saved me again!”
Behind her, Adolf’s face tightened with frustrated disappointment.
“Niece, your uncle is standing right here.”
“Why are you only getting here now? I’m telling Mother everything!”
“….”
Adolf looked even more dejected.
Vikir gently pushed Camus back and addressed Adolf.
“Lord Adolf. I thought Camus might have been exposed to another poison as well. From what she said earlier…”
“Another poison? I detected only the paralytic.”
“…?”
That was strange. Her symptoms had looked obvious earlier.
But if Adolf found nothing, perhaps the cause was something else.
Vikir started to turn toward Camus.
Whip!
Another rope lasso flew in.
This one was unlike the earlier snares.
Its path twisted with unsettling precision, almost like a living snake.
“…!”
Vikir frowned and knocked the loop away with the back of his sword.
Aiyen stood at the far end of the rope, staring with determination.
More precisely, she was staring at Camus.
“You should come quietly. We are running out of time.”
Her slurred Imperial made Vikir pause.
“Running out of time?”
What did she mean? Had she simply chosen the wrong words?
At that moment Morgue and Baskerville had Ballak badly outmatched.
So if anyone was running out of time, it seemed to be Ballak.
Explosions, bright magic, clashing steel, and screams echoed through the jungle.
Sssssssss…
The surrounding forest seemed to respond to the unnatural commotion.
Leaves began to rustle slowly.
Then,
“…It came!”
“It’s finally here!”
Ahun shouted in panic.
Every Ballak warrior who heard him froze for an instant.
“…?”
Vikir tilted his head.
Even his hunter’s senses, sharpened through the Age of Destruction, detected nothing obvious.
Only,
Bzzzz—
Beelzebub, hidden within the vessels of Vikir’s wrist, began warning him frantically.
“It’s coming!”
“Madam Eight-Legs!”
“She’s coming! She’s coming!”
The Ballak were hardened people who barely changed expression even under torture.
Seeing them this terrified was itself a warning.
…
…And then,
something emerged from the darkness.
A deeper blackness than the night itself spread its shadow across the jungle.
At that moment,
drawn by the light and noise of the battle,
an unknown horror was approaching.
