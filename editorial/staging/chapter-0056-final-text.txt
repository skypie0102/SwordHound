“….”
Vikir sat silently behind Aiyen on the wolf.
Riding in front, Aiyen kept glancing back at him.
“Did that hurt?”
“….”
“Are you angry?”
“….”
Aiyen twisted around to look at him directly.
Vikir answered in the same flat tone as always.
“Watch where you’re going.”
The wolf caught a root and lurched.
Aiyen snapped her attention forward and steadied it.
Even then, she kept stealing glances back at Vikir.
At this point it was difficult to tell who was master and who was slave.
When the tangled roots finally gave way to open ground, Aiyen slowed the wolf.
She looked back at Vikir again.
“I had no choice. Ahun is stubborn and vindictive. That was the only way to stop him from coming after you later.”
“….”
“…Why won’t you say anything?”
After several more prompts from his supposed master, Vikir finally answered.
“It hurts.”
It truly did. The inside of his mouth was split and crusted with blood, and even opening it hurt.
Aiyen flinched.
She slowly turned to study him.
“Does it hurt badly?”
Her gaze dropped to Vikir’s neck.
The collar had rubbed angry red marks into his pale skin.
“….”
Aiyen stared at the wound for a moment.
Then, following what seemed to be a Ballak habit for tending minor wounds, she briefly licked the raw mark.
Vikir recoiled at once, and Aiyen immediately drew back as if only then realizing how strange the gesture must have seemed to him.
Vikir frowned.
‘Do the Ballak normally tend wounds like that?’
‘Wolves and other canines do.’
Given how closely the Ballak lived with wolves, perhaps the habit was not unusual here.
Vikir, however, had no intention of adopting it himself.
“Licking an open wound can cause infection.”
If Aiyen tried it again, he intended to refuse firmly.
But she did not.
“….”
Aiyen merely looked away without answering.
The tips of her pointed ears had turned red.
◆◆◆
Like most Ballak warriors, Aiyen was straightforward by nature.
Once Vikir returned to his usual behavior—just quieter because of his injuries—her mood quickly recovered.
Feeling guilty about the earlier punishment, she offered him compensation.
“Ask me for one thing. If it is within my power, I will do it for you.”
Vikir answered without hesitation.
“Teach me archery.”
“…Archery.”
The reply seemed to catch Aiyen off guard.
She looked faintly disappointed that he had answered so quickly.
“Fine. Archery is fine.”
After a moment, a serious smile returned to her face.
“Since we are already hunting, I will teach you. You may even be better suited to a bow than you think.”
Aiyen shifted position so she could guide Vikir’s posture and arm placement from behind.
She corrected his stance carefully, keeping his injured body supported.
Vikir grunted as the movement pulled at his still-healing injuries.
“…Do I really have to learn like this?”
“The fastest way is to teach your body the motion. Is there a problem?”
With his body still badly damaged, Vikir had little energy to argue.
Aiyen handed him a spare bow.
The unfamiliar weight brought back old memories.
Young Baskerville hounds received basic training with many weapons, bows included.
And in the Age of Destruction, swordsmen who survived long enough learned to handle almost any weapon they could get their hands on.
Vikir already knew the fundamentals of archery.
Only the fundamentals, however.
‘Not enough to boast about.’
Before the regression he had been a competent shot, but not good enough to rely on a bow in serious combat.
At best, he could reliably take small game for food.
Among the Ballak, where archery was a core martial discipline, he might be able to improve much further.
That possibility made the hunt useful for more than food.
Aiyen continued correcting his archery as they traveled.
“Bakira will help us track today.”
Aiyen was clearly invested in the hunt.
Nearly three hundred Ballak needed at least two hundred kilograms of meat a day to eat properly.
Recent hunts had gone badly, and some tribe members had been surviving on mushrooms, berries, and roots.
“If you distinguish yourself today, life in the village will become much easier for you.”
Vikir nodded.
Just then, something changed.
“…!”
Bakira stopped abruptly.
The wolf had caught a scent.
Aiyen crouched and scooped up a handful of earth where Bakira had been sniffing.
Rotting wood dust, fallen leaves, moss, and sand were mixed together.
The soil was slightly damp and carried a sharp animal smell.
Aiyen’s expression sharpened.
“Oxbear.”
Vikir knew the monster.

A carnivorous bear and one of the strongest land predators of the Seventh Ridge, the oxbear stood near the top of the local ecosystem.
It had few natural enemies besides other oxbears.
Two great buffalo-like horns grew from its head.
Thick muscle, fat, and coarse fur protected its entire body.
Vikir had encountered the species many times during Baskerville training.
But a wild oxbear in its own territory would be far stronger and more tenacious than the caged specimens prepared for clan exercises.
“We are entering its territory.”
Aiyen turned into the wind and followed the scent.
Oxbears marked their range with pungent urine that warned prey and rival predators away.
Most creatures would have fled from the smell alone, but Aiyen was the youngest hunting leader of Ballak.
Vikir, hardened by decades of survival before the regression, remained equally calm.
Aiyen grinned at him.
“Not scared?”
“Not particularly.”
“Good. You have some nerve for an Imperial kid. Even veteran Ballak hunters treat oxbears seriously.”
Vikir’s composure clearly pleased her.
Soon the two of them were tracking directly into the wind.
Aiyen followed signs and scent with nearly animal precision.
At times, her nose seemed almost as useful as Bakira’s.
Vikir was an experienced tracker too, though most of his experience involved hunting or evading people.
‘Good. I can learn from this.’
He watched Aiyen’s monster-tracking methods closely.
A moment later, the trail paid off.
Before long, they found what they were looking for.
At the top of a rolling slope covered in tall brush, a huge oxbear pushed through the foliage.
Its two horns curved viciously, its fur was denser than any specimen Vikir remembered, and immense muscle bulged beneath it.
It was an old animal, close to eight meters tall—far larger than the five-meter norm.
“…That should be rated higher than A.”
Vikir’s expression hardened.
Fighting something that large head-on would be difficult in his present condition.
The sensible choice was to withdraw and search for easier prey.
Aiyen showed no intention of leaving.
“We are going after it.”
She crouched close beside Vikir behind the cover of the brush.
A dull ache ran through his still-healing body.
“Something that size will be difficult to bring down. It is abnormally strong even for an oxbear.”
“Idiot. Who said that one was our target?”
Vikir looked at her.
Aiyen grinned.
“Where there is a female, a male will not be far away. Watch.”
Before she finished speaking, the thicket rustled again.
A second oxbear approached the first.
The enormous oxbear they had first spotted was female.
The newcomer was a much smaller male.
It stood only a little over four meters tall—roughly half her size.
The female appeared to be in mating season.
As she shifted her massive body, the male crept closer and sniffed her fur.
He began grooming and nuzzling her cautiously.
“That one is the real target.”
Aiyen pulled Vikir lower behind the brush so neither bear would notice them.
Vikir watched the pair while Aiyen kept a steady hand on his arm to hold him in cover.
Rustle…
Aiyen shifted closer behind him so both of them would stay concealed.
She leaned near his ear so she could whisper without alerting the bears.
The cramped hiding place left almost no room for either hunter to move.
Aiyen’s pulse was quick with the excitement of the hunt.
Vikir had only one practical thought.
“You’re putting a lot of weight on me.”
