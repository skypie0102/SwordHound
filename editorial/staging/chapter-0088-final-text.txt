The blade-forests of Le Rouge Et Le Noir Mountain were merciless.
Every leaf could cut like a knife, and even bark, thorns, and roots could kill the careless.
Now two men were fighting for their lives inside that jungle.
Vikir and Ahmen.
Both carried swords and bows.
Ballak’s warriors formed a wide ring around the battlefield.
Dense foliage hid much of the terrain, but veteran hunters could still follow the fight from hundreds of meters away.
Ahmen ground his teeth.
“…You’ve only lived here for two years.”
Vikir had spent only two years adapting to the depths.
Ahmen, by contrast, had survived in this terrain for nearly seventy.
Even many younger hunters could not match his familiarity with the jungle.
“I’ll make you regret choosing the jungle instead of an open field.”
Ahmen moved quickly through the undergrowth.
He slipped behind tangled roots and vines, then nocked an arrow.
“Good. This will do.”
He crouched and found something beneath the roots.
A large centipede crawled there, red-bodied with black legs.
Ahmen drove an arrowhead through its head.
Venomous fluid coated the point.
He had also laid nearly invisible cutting vines across the ground.
A careless step could slice deeply enough to cripple a leg.
He disguised mud pits with dry leaves and sand and positioned sleeping hives nearby to create more traps.
“Just wait.”
Ahmen held the poisoned arrow ready and waited for Vikir to appear.
Then—
“…Who exactly are you waiting for?”
A cold voice touched his ear.
Ahmen froze.
A shudder ran through him.
He turned and found Vikir standing directly behind him without expression.
“H-how!?”
How had Vikir crossed the jungle without making a sound?
Ahmen never managed to ask.
Beelzebub flashed sideways.
Hit.
Vikir cut through the hidden vines.
Ahmen jumped backward to escape, landed in one of his own mud traps, and knocked loose several hives.
Bzzzzzzzz.
The disturbed insects swarmed the person nearest them.
Vikir calmly stepped away while Ahmen flailed in the mud.
Ahmen finally escaped by submerging himself and waiting until the swarm thinned.
By then his body was covered with stings.
He crawled out of the mud and looked up to see Vikir sitting on a branch, almost bored.
“Damn you!”
Ahmen raised his bow.
The centipede venom had washed off, but the arrowhead was still dangerous.
Boom.
Mud sprayed from the bowstring.
The arrow shot upward toward Vikir.
But—
Boom.
A red slash cut the arrow in half.
Before Ahmen could react, Vikir vanished from sight and appeared behind him.
Ahmen’s face was cut and blood blurred his vision.
The speed was frightening, but something else was even stranger.
“No sound!?”
Vikir was moving at tremendous speed without producing footsteps.
He crossed grass, mud, branches, stones, and fallen logs in complete silence.
Even tiny noises vanished beneath the ordinary hum of jungle insects.
“What trick is this!?”
Ahmen fired repeatedly, but his arrows struck only trees and animals.
Then Beelzebub began releasing black aura.
The dense, sticky energy marked Vikir as an advanced Graduator.
Ahmen had rarely seen aura that strong even among Ballak’s most experienced warriors.
‘He was this powerful?’
The strength did not fit Vikir’s age.
Ahmen retreated instinctively.
But Baskerville’s six fangs stayed on him.
The carnivorous swordsmanship closed in.
Six hidden strikes tore through Ahmen’s defenses.
Every graze carried another layer of burning pain.
The pain was far worse than an ordinary cut.
Cerberus’s invisible infernal fire scorched directly through Ahmen’s spirit.
“Aaaah!”
He screamed.
A sword wound should hurt, but this pain felt unnatural.
In nearly seventy years, Ahmen had been injured by arrows, spears, blades, and beasts, but nothing had ever burned like this.
Each cut felt as though fire were eating into the wound long after the blade passed.
The spectators could not see the spiritual flames and only saw Ahmen screaming from shallow-looking injuries.
“Ugh!”
At last Ahmen abandoned any attempt to look dignified.
Honor, pride, and tradition no longer mattered.
The Ballak warriors booed as the man who had demanded the Illiad fled from the younger fighter.
Woo-woo-woo!
The jeers echoed through the jungle.
Ahmen staggered backward and turned to fire another arrow.
But he had forgotten something.
Vikir had spent two years learning Ballak archery from Aiyen.
Ping!
An arrow rose in a clean arc.
Puck!
It struck Ahmen low in the body and dropped him.
“Urgh!”
His eyes flew wide.
Pain made his whole face twist.
He collapsed clutching the wound as leaves scattered around him.
Rustle.
Vikir walked closer without expression.
“You coveted an oxbear’s private parts two years ago. Consider this repayment.”
The remark referred to the old recovery-food incident after Vikir’s early days in Ballak.
Ahmen bit his lip hard enough to draw blood.
“You’re mocking me!”
“I wasn’t trying to. You’re not strong enough to count as entertainment.”
“Ugh…!”
Ahmen staggered back to his feet and dropped both sword and bow.
“…!”
Vikir felt the atmosphere change immediately.
The wind shifted.
Dark mana began gathering.
It crackled and converged around Ahmen’s hands.
‘There it is. A shaman’s hidden card.’
Vikir had expected something like this.
Ahmen traced symbols with his bloodied palms and began chanting.
A dark current spread outward from his hands.
Crackle, crackle.
Vikir jumped back when shapes reached toward his ankles.
Several figures rose into his path.
They were the bodies of orangutans Ahmen’s stray arrows had killed earlier.
Ahmen had reanimated the corpses through shamanic magic.
“…So that’s it. Ahmen came from Rokoko.”
Aiyen’s earlier story suddenly made sense.
Ahmen was an outsider to Ballak because he had originally belonged to the shamanic Rokoko people.
Rokoko’s corpse-working arts were treated as witchcraft by the jungle tribes and as black magic by the Empire.
Raising the newly dead was one of their known techniques.
In desperation, Ahmen used the fresh orangutan corpses as shields.
“Heh… The Illiad isn’t limited to bows and swords!”
Several Ballak warriors frowned at the use of sorcery in what they considered a warrior’s contest.
Ahmen had apparently spent years practicing magic privately while pretending to represent Ballak tradition.
“Go! Stop him! Buy me time!”
The reanimated orangutans rushed Vikir.
Each was large enough to slow an ordinary fighter.
Ahmen expected them to serve as effective meat shields.
But—
“Hm. Less impressive than I expected.”
Vikir stamped one foot.
“…?”
Ahmen opened his mouth.
Puh-lease!
Something struck him before he could speak.
The impact knocked him off his feet.
He hit the ground hard, the blow leaving him unable to move properly.
The nearby reanimated orangutans were crushed at the same time.
Vikir alone remained standing silently.
“????”
Ahmen stared upward, speechless.
A huge shadow covered him.
[Grrrr…]
The creature above him twitched its ears.
An old female oxbear raised one enormous forepaw over Ahmen.
