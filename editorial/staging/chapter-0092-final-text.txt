Every arrow Adonai fired was terrifying.
Yet Akwilla had caught and crushed one with her bare hand.
“Stay back.”
She stepped in front of Vikir and Aiyen.
Akwilla had already sent eight lesser Death Knights back to their graves; now she faced Adonai, the absolute summit of old Ballak.
Adonai represented the old era and Akwilla the new.
Two archers who stood at the peak of their respective generations faced one another.
Vikir swallowed.
A battle between two Master-class powers was rare even by his standards.
Even during the Age of Destruction, clashes at this level were not ordinary.
“…What happens now?”
“Why even ask?”
Aiyen answered immediately.
Her expression was completely certain.
“My mother never loses.”
The confidence in her voice was absolute.
Reality, however, began by suggesting otherwise.
Kararak—
Akwilla raised her great bow.
The heavy string drew back under tremendous tension.
BOOM!
A silver arrow of solid aura flew toward Adonai.
The shot pierced into the black pressure surrounding the Death Knight.
[…]
Adonai answered instantly.
Combat instinct remained engraved in his dead body.
BOOM!
Black aura erupted from him like steam.
The ground cracked, sulfurous vapor rose, and Adonai’s darkness spread like smoke from hellfire.
A black vortex climbed into the sky and gathered into heavy clouds.
Akwilla fired silver streaks into that darkness.
Silver and black trajectories devoured one another across the battlefield.
The exchange was so fast that even Aiyen and Vikir could barely follow it.
“Akwilla’s firing speed really does rival Adonai’s.”
“My mother’s archery is world-class. Accuracy is only part of it.”
The two living legends were giving every watching hunter a lesson simply by fighting.
Their contest was one of precision.
Prediction, interception, spinning shots that exploited the opponent’s air currents, and curved trajectories from above and the flanks appeared almost effortlessly.
BANG!
Akwilla ducked and a black arrow snapped a thick log behind her.
BOOM!
Adonai leapt away just as a silver arrow buried itself in the sandbar beneath him and blasted sand outward.
It became an all-out battle of attrition.
But as time passed, Akwilla was the one losing ground.
Spot!
A black arrow grazed her side, and Adonai’s shots steadily narrowed the space she had to breathe.
The problem was not skill.
Akwilla depended on physical arrows; Adonai did not.
She could create pure aura arrows in emergencies, but the mana cost was too high to sustain them.
Adonai, fueled by a Death Knight’s negative energy, could fire solid aura arrows again and again.
Worse—
“…The wind is against her too.”
Vikir frowned.
Adonai stood with the wind at his back while Akwilla fired into the headwind.
Jungle winds strengthened Adonai’s arrows and robbed power from Akwilla’s.
The disadvantage became visible as more cuts appeared across Akwilla’s body.
Every one of Adonai’s shots also carried Madam Eight-Legs’ poison.
Tsutsutsutsu…
The wounds darkened.
Akwilla’s face grew pale.
Ping-ping-ping.
Then her own arrows began veering in strange directions.
The wind seized them and carried them away before they ever reached Adonai.
It almost looked as though the jungle itself had chosen the dead ancestor.
Vikir searched for a way to reverse the situation.
If Akwilla fell, the remaining Death Knights could overwhelm the entire Ballak force.
“…Should we kill Ahmen first?”
That would require getting through Adonai’s unmoving defense.
Ahmen had also retained several other named Death Knights rather than collapsing the entire army into Adonai.
Ballak’s warriors were still fighting those ancestors; salt hindered them but did not instantly destroy the strongest ones.
“Someone needs to return to the village and bring everyone else! At this rate—”
Vikir raised his voice.
Aiyen covered his mouth.
“Shh. We’re almost there.”
“…?”
Vikir narrowed his eyes.
Akwilla was visibly tiring and her arrows seemed to be missing more often.
Adonai remained relentless.
What possible victory was Aiyen seeing?
But Aiyen understood archery at a level Vikir did not.
She watched Akwilla’s apparent failures with growing awe.
“I see it. I see what Mother sees.”
“…?”
Vikir still could not.
Then—
It happened.
BOOM!
An arrow suddenly struck the center of Adonai’s back.
A real, physical arrow quivered there.
It had arrived from behind, a direction no one had expected.
[…?]
Adonai paused, as though even his dead instincts were confused; only Ahmen should have been behind him.
But—
Pfft!
Another arrow struck the back of Adonai’s head.
Then another.
Beep-beep-beep!
A mass of arrows began arriving from the rear.
“Ugh!”
Ahmen flattened himself against the ground.
Several returning arrows tore into him as they passed.
“…!”
Vikir looked up with his mouth half open.
Only now did he recognize them.
They were the arrows Akwilla had appeared to miss moments earlier.
Pipipiping!
Another volley came from behind and converged on Adonai’s back.
Whizz-whizz!
The jungle wind itself was carrying them.
Vikir finally understood.
Akwilla’s arrows had never truly gone off course.
She had intentionally sent them upward into the great circulating winds, allowing them to loop around the jungle and return.
Like salmon climbing back toward the waters where they were born.
The arrows returned toward the center of the black vortex Adonai himself had created—the origin point of the wind.
Puck! Puck! Crack!
Adonai’s exposed back became covered in shafts.
After riding the circulating wind, the returning arrows struck with even more force than before.
They still could not pass cleanly through Adonai’s supernatural body, but the accumulated damage was real.
BOOM!
Another arrow struck directly into one already lodged in his thigh.
The second impact finally drove the first through, and Adonai staggered to one knee.
Akwilla looked exhausted.
“Great Ancestor, if you were alive and in your prime, you would never have made such a simple mistake. That saddens me.”
She drew one final arrow.
A blind shot approached from behind while Akwilla’s aimed shot came from the front.
The crippled Adonai could not evade both.
Pfft!
One arrow drove halfway through the back of his skull and another halfway through the forehead.
BOOM!
The two arrowheads met.
Thud.
Adonai dropped to his knees.
The legend of an older generation bowed before the myth of the new.
“….”
Akwilla stared at her kneeling ancestor.
The victory brought her little satisfaction.
Akwilla was in her prime, while Adonai had died of old age before being dragged back as an imperfect corpse.
He also lacked a living mind and judgment, making the contest fundamentally unequal.
“If Adonai had possessed the strength and mind of his prime…”
Akwilla shook her head at the thought.
Then—
“Kaaaaah!”
A muffled scream broke the silence.
Ahmen struggled beside Adonai’s fallen body.
His battle was lost now that the greatest Death Knight had fallen.
Only a few undead remained around him, while far more Ballak warriors closed in.
“Now die.”
Aiyen glared at the shaman.
But—
Ahmen still had not finished gambling.
He had spent nearly all his life and mana and could no longer use his lower body properly, but his will to survive remained.
“Don’t come closer!”
He dragged Adonai and the other ancestral corpses around himself.
Then he smeared blood from his hands and mouth across them threateningly.
“If you approach, I’ll stain your ancestors with a traitor’s blood!”
The threat stopped even Akwilla for a moment.
Ballak superstition held that a soul polluted by a traitor’s blood might be barred from the warriors’ paradise.
Ahmen used that belief as his final shield.
The tribe feared that their revered dead might be spiritually dishonored after already being violated through resurrection.
Ahmen’s real skill with witchcraft made the threat harder to dismiss.
“….”
Akwilla raised her bow, but Ahmen hid behind the ancestral bodies.
No one wanted to attack recklessly.
A careless shot might permanently desecrate someone they had revered since childhood.
Then—
“Everyone, step back.”
Vikir’s voice carried across the battlefield.
The warriors turned and saw him raising his bow.
But he was aiming nowhere near Ahmen.
“…What are you doing?”
Even Akwilla looked confused after the archery feat she had just performed.
Vikir was calm.
“Get to higher ground first.”
That was his only warning.
Then—
PING!
Vikir loosed the arrow in a high arc.
It struck the knot of vines binding together a heavy wall of stacked logs.
Pow!
The arrow severed the binding.
And then—
Grrr… snap… snap…
One vine after another broke and the logs began shifting together.
The barrier dam built for flood control collapsed.
CHHHHHH!
Behind it waited the swollen rainy-season river—water saturated with the white salt deposits of the upper valley.
