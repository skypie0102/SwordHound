Inner Library.
It was a vast library buried deep within the Baskerville stronghold, immense enough to rank among the greatest collections in the Empire.
“Come to think of it, this is the first time I’ve ever seen the inside for myself.”
Vikir passed through an entrance guarded by several knights and entered the restricted section.
Once he presented the pass bearing Hugo’s own signature, no one dared stop him.
A few of the knights even saluted him with faint smiles.
Before long, Vikir stood before the passage leading deeper into the Inner Library.
Carved directly into the black rock, the library stretched inward like the throat of some enormous beast.
Books packed the walls like rows of gigantic teeth, seeming to look down on him.
“I should hurry.”
Vikir began searching while keeping the remaining time in mind.
His thoughts were urgent, but his footsteps and gaze remained slow and deliberate.
The feel of old books, the smell of ink and dust.
None of it made him feel particularly comfortable.
The shelves were packed with manuals on weapon handling, calculations for killing an enemy by the shortest route, and efficient, rigorously logical theories of murder.
All of that knowledge was compressed into this one enormous library.
Generations of Baskerville swordsmen who had long since died seemed to continue a silent war of words through the theories they had left behind.
Was that why?
Entering the library felt less like stepping into a place of learning than walking through the corridor of a prison filled with cold-blooded murderers.
The sword manuals arranged like teeth seemed to stab at him with their gaze.
Vikir soon grew accustomed to the feeling.
Tap, tap, tap—
His footsteps sank into the darkness of the long corridor.
The deeper he went, the older the books became.
‘Cutting Incisor,’ ‘Crushing Molar,’ ‘Piercing Fang,’ ‘Double-Embedded Wisdom Tooth,’ ‘Lurking Wisdom Tooth’…
Familiar manuals, techniques he had desperately wanted to learn in his first life, and arts so advanced he had never dared dream of studying were lined up neatly like rows of teeth.
Heavy locks protected the valuable volumes from theft and unauthorized access.
Ordinarily, branch-family and illegitimate children found it nearly impossible to learn beyond the Fourth Fang, no matter how much mana or battlefield experience they possessed.
Direct descendants could study at least the Fifth Form, elite direct descendants the Sixth, and only the highest-ranking family leaders were entrusted with the Seventh.
Hugo Le Baskerville currently wielded seven fangs, enough to place him among the continent’s few Sword Masters.
His rivalry with the head of the Morgue family, a renowned Seventh-Circle mage, made the rough equivalence of their combat ranks easy to understand.
Vikir stopped walking.
The library was such a sea of books that an ordinary visitor could never hope to know where everything was.
Among those shelves, however, he spotted the book Hugo had authorized him to read.
It rested behind several layers of locks, fitting for a valuable restricted volume.
When Vikir used the key Hugo had given him, the protected book was finally revealed.

It was a noble swordsmanship manual Vikir could only have dreamed of reading in his previous life.
Vikir picked it up and carefully leafed through it.
The text explained methods of cutting, thrusting, guarding, crushing, severing, and grinding according to the fang-shaped trajectories traced by the blade.
Most importantly, the manual explained how to produce a total of six fangs.
Naturally, Hugo could not have expected an eight-year-old child to memorize the entire volume in a single day.
He had probably granted Vikir access out of a mixture of goodwill and the assumption that the boy could absorb only a fraction of it.
But Vikir was a veteran swordsman with forty years of life behind him—an elite Baskerville hound who had survived the Age of Destruction.
He had long since mastered the theory behind the First through Fourth Fangs.
He had even inferred much of the theory behind the Fifth Fang despite never being formally taught it.
For years he had remained stuck at the Fourth because one crucial principle was missing.
It was the formula he had thirsted for—the method of raising his swordsmanship beyond that wall.
“…So that was it. After the Fourth Fang, I had to redirect the flow of mana. That’s why I could never form the Fifth!”
At last, the blockage in Vikir’s understanding disappeared.
It took him only about three hours to understand and memorize the theory laid out in the book.
That speed was possible only because he had spent almost twenty years accumulating experience while trapped at the Fourth Fang.
Once he had committed the volume to memory, Vikir closed it.
Learning the path from the Fourth Fang to the Fifth and Sixth was valuable, but it was not the real reason he had come here.
Vikir returned to the shallowest part of the Inner Library.
It was an area that even the Guardian Knights on duty and the servants responsible for cleaning could freely enter.
“Young master? Why have you come back out?”
“Did you forget something? We can bring it to you.”
“If you’re hungry, shall we bring you a meal?”
The servants addressed him with unusual courtesy.
The mixture of respect and fear in their eyes suggested that word of his practical-evaluation performance had already spread.
But Vikir shook his head.
“I’m finished with what I came to do.”
The servants stared in surprise, and even the Guardian Knights on watch looked taken aback.
“Already?”
“Why not look through a little more?”
“You still have plenty of time.”
No one said it outright, but they clearly thought he was wasting an incredible opportunity.
It was understandable.
He had abandoned sword manuals worth risking one’s life to read after only a few hours.
Anyone who understood their value would naturally regret seeing him walk away from them.
Vikir himself would have felt the same way in his previous life.
But the current Vikir remained completely unconcerned.
“Before I leave, I plan to look through some other books as well. Don’t worry about me. Go back to your work.”
With that, Vikir headed toward the shallowest shelves, where miscellaneous books had been piled together.
Several knights who had been watching him whispered among themselves.
“If it were me, I’d stay in the deepest section and memorize every sword manual I could.”
“He’s still too young to understand their value. He’ll regret this for the rest of his life when he grows older.”
“We should tell the others not to worry. He’s spending his time on worthless miscellaneous books anyway.”
If they could have seen Vikir’s expression from the front, however, they would not have spoken so lightly.
Vikir was searching through the shelves with fierce concentration.
No one cared about these books, and they had been left in a dusty, disordered heap.
He searched slowly and methodically.
After several hours, he finally found what he had been looking for.
At the very edge of the shelves, in a place visible to everyone yet ignored by almost everyone,
an old book had been wedged between other volumes.
It was so dusty that no preservation magic appeared to have been placed on it at all.
“….”
Vikir stood motionless for a long moment, staring at the title.

It looked like a basic First-Form manual so elementary that even Baskerville children would not bother reading it.
An ancient book still technically classified as a swordsmanship manual.
But who in this era could possibly know the true value of this book?
Only Vikir, who had lived through the Age of Destruction, knew.
This shabby volume was one of Baskerville’s greatest treasures.
Vikir reached out and pulled at the book.
It seemed not to have left the shelf in many years and resisted at first.
Only after shifting several volumes that blocked it could he finally draw it free and expose it to the light.
“…Lurking Embedded Teeth.”
Vikir carefully brushed the dust from its cover.
Then he opened it.
Rustle—
He rapidly flipped through the pages.
Soon, the reason the book had been discarded among miscellaneous texts became obvious.
A page had been torn from the middle.
Other gaps followed, leaving the book uneven, as though several teeth had been knocked out.
Important sections showed obvious tear marks, breaking the explanations into disconnected fragments.
No one could make sense of such an incomplete manual, so it had naturally been dismissed as worthless.
But.
‘…I know.’
Vikir knew what had once been written on those missing pages.
The memories of the operations later called ‘Reclaim the Torn Pages’ and ‘Restore the Missing Teeth’ were still vivid in his mind.
In that previous life, the hound who crossed countless battle lines and gathered the pages that had been torn away was Vikir van Baskerville himself.
