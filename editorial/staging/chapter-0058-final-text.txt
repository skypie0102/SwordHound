Aiyen and Vikir followed the weakened male oxbear with care.
Its body was exhausted, but its nerves had become correspondingly sharp; the scattered urine along its path made that obvious.
“You hunt when your target is at its weakest.”
Vikir nodded while Aiyen coated an arrowhead with frog venom.
When was an enemy most vulnerable?
Usually while sleeping—especially after days without proper rest.
Aiyen examined the oxbear’s droppings and nodded.
“From the condition of this, he is in terrible shape.”
“He will probably sleep deeply tonight.”
“Of course. He has barely rested for three days and has spent almost all his strength. He’ll look for somewhere dark and remote that he normally avoids.”
Aiyen was right.
The oxbear seemed well aware of its condition and kept moving deeper into the jungle.
Dense growth surrounded them, with fallen trees hanging between living trunks and turning the forest into a maze.
Red and yellow leaves made orientation even harder.
A single rain could erase tracks within half a day.
Aiyen nevertheless never lost the trail.
Bent branches, flattened grass, the depth of footprints, even changes in the insects’ calls all gave her information.
To an experienced hunter, each was a signpost.
“After an oxbear passes through, you will not hear mice or insects nearby for a while.”
Bakira gave a low growl as if in agreement.
The wolf was also following a faint sweet scent too subtle for a human nose.
Earlier, Aiyen had packed fragrant berries into fish and left them for the oxbear to eat.
The scent now lingered on its breath and body wherever it went.
“….”
Vikir watched Aiyen’s tracking from beginning to end.
Some of her methods were familiar; others were new to him.
What he knew, he reviewed. What he did not know, he memorized.
Soon Aiyen climbed a tree and pointed toward the forest below the hill.
There it was: the male oxbear stumbling through the undergrowth.
They might have been able to attack immediately, but Aiyen remained cautious.
“We will have to postpone the hunt.”
“Why?”
Vikir looked toward her, and Aiyen frowned.
“He crossed into the swamp. Dangerous things live in there, so we do not enter carelessly.”
“Dangerous things?”
Vikir was about to ask what she meant.
Smack!
Aiyen’s hands flashed toward his face.
His cheeks reddened from the sudden slap, and while he stared at her, Aiyen held up one hand.
“This.”
Aiyen held her palm out between them.
In the center of her palm lay a dead mosquito.
Bakira growled and pawed uneasily near the insect.
Aiyen’s expression turned serious.
“There are three kinds of mosquitoes in that swamp. Ordinary blood-suckers are manageable. Bone-suckers and flesh-suckers are not.”
Vikir knew of them as well.
Among the mosquitoes of Le Rouge Et Le Noir Mountain, the bone-sucking variety was especially infamous.
A swarm could strip a moving creature down to a ruined sack of blood and organs with terrifying speed.
Vikir had seen the aftermath on comrades before the regression, so he understood Aiyen’s warning perfectly.
One thing, however, still puzzled him.
“Then why is your other hand still on my cheek?”
Aiyen had not removed her other hand.
She flinched, then answered as if the question were absurd.
“Do I have to?”
She gave his cheek another childish pinch.
Vikir frowned.
“…What are you doing?”
“I am the master. Is there a problem with me checking on my slave?”
Vikir stared at her in disbelief.
In his current condition, arguing over something this trivial was pointless.
“You’re surprisingly soft.”
“….”
For now, Vikir decided to endure the childish teasing.
◆◆◆
That night.
Aiyen chose a cliffside overlooking the oxbear’s hiding place.
“We attack at first light.”
Oxbears were nocturnal, so fighting one at night would give it the advantage.
The smarter choice was to wait for sunrise, when the exhausted animal would finally sleep.
Aiyen and Vikir built a simple shelter on firm rocky ground.
A few branches, a leather sheet, and broad leaves made a tent large enough for several people.
Their breath immediately fogged white in the air.
The Ballak called this place the Cold Valley, where snow could linger even into May.
“It gets so cold here that even the swamp mosquitoes stay away.”
Aiyen crawled inside the shelter and settled down.
She shifted the covering aside and looked toward Vikir outside.
“Come in.”
“….”
Vikir hesitated.
He studied the surroundings once more.
The ground was cold and the soil poor.
Few trees survived here; where old trees had died, only shrubs and weeds remained.
The low brush offered decent concealment, but surviving the night required shelter from the cold.
Rustle…
Vikir entered the tent.
Aiyen had scraped a shallow fire pit into the floor and packed it with dry leaves and kindling.
Crackle!
A small flame caught.
The fire warmed the cramped shelter.
The Cold Valley lived up to its name once darkness settled.
Anything turned away from the flames became painfully cold almost at once.
Vikir stared into the embers and let his mind wander.
He remembered companions from the Age of Destruction and the many faces he had left behind.
Some were people he could meet again in this life. Others were gone beyond any possibility of reunion.
Outside the shelter, dead trees rose through the fog like bones.
Vikir remained lost in thought as the fire burned low.
“Whew.”
The sound pulled him back to the present.
Aiyen was leaning into Bakira’s warm fur while taking a drink from a leather pouch.
The liquor smelled strong even from across the tent.
She also took out a piece of dried meat coated in white fat and began eating.
It resembled a slab of buttered bread, though it was far denser and richer.
After a moment, Aiyen looked toward Vikir.
“You should eat too. You need it.”
“…I do not have any jerky.”
Vikir answered, and Aiyen waved the objection away.
“A master is supposed to feed her slave. I brought your share.”
“…?”
Vikir tilted his head.
He had seen only one piece of jerky in the pouch.
Aiyen had already started eating that piece herself.
Then she seemed to remember his injured jaw.
“Wait.”
Aiyen broke off the softer inner portion and soaked it with a little hot broth and liquor to make it easier to chew.
She handed the softened food directly to Vikir.
“….”
Vikir accepted it without comment.
Aiyen watched until he had managed to swallow.
Gulp.
The warm food and drink went down easily enough.
“Better?”
Only then did Aiyen settle back.
She gestured at his swollen jaw.
“You cannot chew the hard outer strips yet. They are too tough.”
“…That much is obvious.”
Vikir frowned.
Even the softened meat made his jaw ache enough that he had to use a little mana to keep chewing.
Since Ahun’s beating, he had lived mostly on porridge and berries, so the dense food was welcome nourishment.
A moment later, the wolf shifted inside the cramped shelter.
Aiyen blinked in surprise as the sudden movement knocked her off balance.
Aiyen was pushed awkwardly toward Vikir.
There was scarcely room for both people, the wolf, and the small fire.
Firelight reddened Aiyen’s face as she tried to find a stable position.
She looked down at Vikir, amused by his inability to move quickly.
“You cannot even push me away right now.”
“Because you’re heavy.”
“I am not heavy.”
“You are heavy.”
“I am not.”
“I said you are.”
Aiyen fell silent and considered this very seriously.
“It is not that I am heavy. You are simply weak.”
She looked oddly pleased with that conclusion.
Vikir gave up arguing.
Whether because of language or personality, he could not follow her logic.
‘First priority: recover as quickly as possible.’
He needed his strength back if he wanted control over his own movements again.
Once fully recovered, leaving the jungle—or subduing anyone who tried to stop him—would be far easier.
The fastest path to recovery was sleep.
Vikir closed his eyes.
Seeing him shiver, Aiyen shifted the blankets and Bakira’s warm fur to block the cold air around him.
“Cold? Move your hands under the blanket. It is warmer near the fire.”
Vikir adjusted his hands beneath the coverings while Aiyen pulled the hide more securely over both of them.
Aiyen paused when his movements suddenly stopped.
“…Are you asleep?”
There was no answer.
A soft, steady breathing filled the silence.
Vikir had fallen asleep almost instantly.
