The practical evaluation was over.
Now came the distribution of rewards to the hounds.
Normally, hounds were rewarded with portions of meat or organs from their prey according to rank.
The same was true of the children of Baskerville.
They made shields, swords, necklaces, and other spoils from the remains of the demonic creatures they had hunted.
Through that process, the young hounds grew stronger in both body and mind.
The fierce competition that had continued through the examinations vanished once the rankings were settled.
Perhaps because they had endured extreme conditions together for so long, a strange sense of brotherhood began to spread through the class.
Of course, some siblings still hated one another, but most disputes had either been settled during the practical evaluation or ended when one side quietly died, so open infighting became surprisingly rare.
For a few days each year after the practical evaluation, an uncharacteristically relaxed atmosphere settled over Baskerville.
◆◆◆
Hugo Le Baskerville, head of the Baskerville family, was receiving a report from Butler Barrymore.
“…Who caught what?”
For a superhuman who had risen to Sword Master, doubting his own senses was almost unheard of.
But at that moment, Hugo was clearly doubting his ears.
Barrymore read the report again, his normally clear voice carrying unusual excitement.
“Vikir van Baskerville, age-eight class. During the practical evaluation, he ‘accidentally’ crossed the boundary and entered the Forbidden Zone. There, he successfully hunted Cerberus, a demonic creature of Danger Level A+. That is all.”
Even on the second reading, the report sounded absurd.
An eight-year-old child had entered the Forbidden Zone and returned alive.
That alone would have been an extraordinary achievement.
Even bringing back testimony or a rough map that helped clarify the terrain and ecosystem beyond the permitted boundary would have been immensely valuable.
Yet this unprecedented eight-year-old had entered that hellish territory, returned alive, and even brought back Cerberus—the guard dog of hell.
“…Cerberus is a high-ranking demonic creature that even the family’s Guardian Knights find troublesome to face.”
Hugo stroked his beard with a baffled expression.
But the detailed report that followed was even harder to believe.
Hugo picked up the report again and adjusted his reading glasses.
“He lured Cerberus into a trap and killed it with a spear? You expect me to believe that?”
“It had already been wounded by barbarians, and the spear was poisoned, Master.”
“Even wounded, it was still Cerberus. What poison could kill it? And where did he get such a thing?”
“…Nothing else was recorded in the hunting log. I tried to ask the young master, but he said he was tired and returned straight to the dormitory.”
At Barrymore’s answer, Hugo went blank for a moment, then gave a short laugh.
“The boy already knows how to hide things. Information is power, and power is value. One must raise and protect one’s own value.”
Hugo turned his attention back to the report.
Vikir had not immediately boasted about the poison that killed Cerberus, nor had he handed the corpse straight to the Guide Hounds. Instead, he buried and concealed it until the practical evaluation was over. Hugo found both decisions commendable.
Barrymore was impressed as well.
“His patience is remarkable. When I was eight, I would have done anything to earn praise from adults and respect from my siblings. If I had killed Cerberus at that age, I would have dragged it straight to the Guardian Knights just to be praised and noticed.”
“Ha. Then you would have ended up like old Santiago.”
Hugo was referring to a fisherman from an ancient legend.
The man caught an enormous monster fish at sea, but while towing it home he encountered a school of sharks. They stripped away all the flesh, leaving him to return with nothing but a gigantic skeleton.
“If Vikir had gone around dragging that enormous Cerberus corpse behind him, every ambitious sibling nearby would have targeted him. Losing the achievement would have been the least of it. He might have been killed.”
Barrymore felt a chill at the reminder of Baskerville’s bloody family customs.
“To be honest, I was surprised. I never expected a child from the van line, rather than the more prestigious le or la lines, to display talent like this.”
“You still don’t understand me, Barrymore. I am not like the previous family heads. Bloodline is not what matters when raising hounds. Good blood can produce a worthless dog, and poor blood can produce an excellent one. I do not care whether a son was born to an imperial woman or a street prostitute. Talent and courage are enough.”
Muttering in a low voice, Hugo looked through the window toward a distant spire.
His second son.
Barrymore understood at once that Hugo was thinking of the son currently secluded in training at the top of that tower.
“Now do you understand? Even excellent blood can produce a useless hound.”
“…My apologies.”
Hugo waved a hand.
“Enough. Fretting over a foolish son only makes me suffer. I need a change of mood.”
His gaze moved from the window back to the report.
Understanding his master’s intention, Barrymore gave a small nod.
“I’ll bring Young Master Vikir.”
◆◆◆
It was several hours later when Vikir stood before Hugo.
Hugo still had no taste for preliminaries.
He went straight to the point.
“How did you kill Cerberus?”
“I smeared chocolate on the spear.”
Vikir was the same.
A direct question received a direct answer.
There was no wasted flesh on a conversation between Hugo and Vikir.
A flicker of interest crossed Hugo’s eyes.
“Chocolate?”
“Chocolate is poisonous to canine demonic creatures.”
“So that is why you asked for chocolate the other day?”
“Yes.”
Vikir answered briefly.
Hugo thought for a moment before speaking again.
“If that is true, the information is extremely valuable. It will be useful when subjugating canine demonic creatures, and it could improve both our dispatch results and our bargaining position with the information guild.”
“I think so too.”
The corner of Hugo’s mouth lifted slightly at Vikir’s calm reply.
Then Hugo asked in a deliberately cold voice.
“I heard that when the butler questioned you, you refused to properly explain how you hunted Cerberus.”
“Correct. He is not my master.”
“Then who is your master?”
Vikir answered just as calmly.
“I belong to Baskerville. Wouldn’t the head of Baskerville be my master?”
At that, Hugo finally nodded with satisfaction.
“You have learned well. As a reward, the demonic creature you killed will belong to you in its entirety.”
Cerberus was an A+ demonic creature. Its corpse alone was worth a fortune.
Its teeth and claws could become weapons, its organs and flesh could be used as tonics, and its bones and hide could become armor.
Nothing from the corpse of a high-ranking demonic creature needed to be wasted.
Receiving the entire body was an enormous reward.
But Hugo gave Vikir another reward as well.
“You placed first in both the written and practical examinations, so the family’s expectations are high. If there is something you want, tell me.”
“Didn’t you already give me Cerberus’s corpse?”
“That was my decision. I want to hear what you want.”
Vikir’s eyes widened slightly.
Hugo’s words genuinely surprised him.
Hugo had never made a habit of asking his children what they wanted.
He gave orders. Who asked a hound for its opinion?
But unexpected variables were always welcome.
Thinking this might make things easier, Vikir stated his wish.
“I want to enter the Inner Library.”
“…!”
Hugo’s eyes narrowed.
The Inner Library was a vast collection buried deep inside the Baskerville stronghold, large enough to rival even the great library of the Morgue family.
Hugo rested a hand against his chin and thought for a moment, something rare for him.
“…The Inner Library. Access is normally restricted by bloodline. Did you know that when you asked?”
Vikir’s eyes widened.
He truly had not known.
‘I never had any reason to enter the library in my previous life.’
Etiquette, swordsmanship, and the other knowledge he needed had all been supplied through the family’s internal education.
He had been content with what he was given and had never shown any desire to learn more.
That was considered a hound’s virtue, and he had been trained accordingly.
‘…I didn’t know. If it’s impossible, I’ll find another way.’
Vikir quickly took a step back. It did not really matter; if necessary, he could always sneak in under cover of night.
But things turned out more easily than he expected.
“You may go.”
Hugo’s decision was immediate.
Before Vikir could even show surprise, Hugo continued.
“I cannot give you unlimited time. Will ten days be enough?”
“One day is enough.”
There was no reason to linger long enough to make Hugo suspicious.
Hugo looked down at Vikir with a faint smile.
“What book do you intend to read in a single day?”
“I learned about family history in my liberal-arts lessons. I simply want to know more about it as a member of Baskerville.”
It was merely a plausible answer chosen on the spot,
but Hugo seemed to like it quite a bit.
“Family history. Not bad. Learning the honorable history of your family is a good way to cultivate pride.”
The fact that Hugo spoke of family pride at all showed how much he liked the answer.
He even recommended a book himself.
“Deep in the Inner Library, on the middle shelf of the Sixth Controlled Section, there is a swordsmanship manual called the Baskerville Sixth Form. Read it.”
Vikir could hardly believe his ears.
Hugo himself was recommending a high-level swordsmanship manual.
For a child of the van line, the Fourth Form was normally the ceiling. Yet Hugo was offering him the Sixth.
The Baskerville Sixth Form.
It was a swordsmanship capable of producing six fangs, normally reserved for high-ranking members and elite direct descendants.
Hugo was allowing Vikir to study it, even if only briefly.
Considering that the current Hugo wielded seven fangs and the Hugo Vikir remembered from before regression had reached nine, this treatment was extraordinary.
Compared with the Fourth Form Vikir had learned in his first life, the Sixth Form belonged to an entirely different tier.
But.
It was not what Vikir had come for.
‘…It’s only the Sixth Form.’
Anyone else who heard that thought might have fainted.
But Vikir was not foolish enough to let such contempt show.
“Thank you. I will read it carefully and do my best to live up to your expectations.”
Vikir bowed to Hugo in gratitude.
On the surface, the gesture was perfectly humble and polite.
Of course, if Hugo had known what Vikir was truly plotting, he might have burned every secret manual in the Inner Library just to stop him.
“….”
Believing their business finished, Hugo indifferently turned his head away.
Having obtained permission to enter the Inner Library, Vikir maintained his polite manners to the end, then turned and left the room.
And.
He set out toward what might be the most valuable treasure in the entire Baskerville family.
