A massive high-rise hotel stood in the center of Underdog City.
An enormous club occupied its sixty-ninth and seventieth floors.
‘Burning Suspension.’
It was Underdog’s most exclusive social club, where the city’s wealthiest and most influential young people gathered.
The rich and powerful came here to burn through their youth.
Bottles of champagne worth as much as one hundred million gold were opened one after another while fast, upbeat music pounded through the floors.
Smoke from unfamiliar hookahs curled beneath colorful lights.
The atmosphere was so lavish that it could have passed for a ball at the Imperial Palace.
“Pile them up!”
“Bring everything in!”
“Let’s build the tower again today!”
Deep inside the club was a private room reserved for the highest-tier guests.
Seven young heirs lounged inside a VVIP room that cost ten million gold for six hours.
Chilled bottles of champagne worth more than one hundred million gold each arrived one after another.
At the center of the enormous table, champagne glasses were stacked into a pyramid.
The bottom layer held one hundred glasses in a ten-by-ten square.
Above them sat eighty-one glasses in a nine-by-nine square.
Above that came sixty-four glasses in an eight-by-eight square.
Then forty-nine, thirty-six, twenty-five, sixteen, nine, and four glasses were stacked in successively smaller layers.
One final glass was placed at the peak, completing the champagne tower.
The seven heirs laughed as they poured an entire bottle of the hundred-million-gold champagne, Donquixote Perignon 666, into the top glass.
Then—
Gurgle…
Once the top glass filled, champagne overflowed into the glasses beneath it.
Whenever a bottle ran low, the heirs tossed it aside without hesitation and ordered another.
Again and again.
Champagne cascaded from the upper layers and gradually filled the glasses below.
Eventually, all 385 glasses making up the tower were full.
One of the heirs laughed.
“Hey. Drink whatever spills onto the table.”
The waiters stationed by the entrance hurried over at once.
“Thank you, young masters.”
“Thanks to you, we get to taste liquor like this.”
“We’ll serve you with everything we have today.”
Smiling, the waiters licked the champagne dripping across the tabletop.
The heirs laughed and tossed gold coins at their faces.
“This is what you call the trickle-down effect.”
Champagne flowed from the peak into the lower glasses and across the table while the seven men laughed at the sight.
These seven were core members of City Hall’s Youth Self-Governance Committee.
The committee was formally only a civilian group of second- and third-generation heirs from wealthy local landholding and noble families, but its influence in the community could not be ignored.
Their wealth and connections were enough to secure minor public offices, and their status still placed them far above ordinary citizens.
Their families had lived in the region for generations and knew its people and institutions intimately.
For that reason, Baskerville delegated limited authority to them and allowed them to handle troublesome local matters.
In return for tribute, taxes, and local information, Baskerville tolerated a degree of autonomy.
Historically, however, the families gathered here were descendants of houses that had lost central imperial power struggles and been pushed out to the provinces.
Compared with Baskerville—a great house dispatched by the Emperor to expand the frontier—none of them were particularly remarkable.
Their heirs did not see themselves that way.
“It’s better to be the head of a snake than the tail of a dragon.”
“You can have more fun here, away from the Imperial Family’s eyes.”
“Exactly. I went to a club in the Imperial Capital recently, and this place is even more extravagant.”
“And Baskerville protects us. Could anywhere be safer?”
“Heh heh. At this point, aren’t the Baskervilles our hounds?”
The young heirs were astonishingly arrogant.
While Baskerville focused on expanding the frontier, rot spread through the territory behind its back.
So where did the money funding all this extravagance come from?
The ordinary income of the local families was actually modest.
Water taxes from reservoirs, road fees, tolls, livestock sales, and similar revenues barely covered servants, retainers, and estate expenses.
On paper, neither income nor expenditure looked extraordinary—but they had another source of money.
Illegal slave auctions.
People were kidnapped, imprisoned, and sold into slavery without lawful authorization.
As Baskerville’s aggressive frontier expansion displaced more barbarian communities, traffickers increasingly lured or abducted displaced people and sold them for profit.
They rode Baskerville’s conquests and picked up the human wreckage left behind.
The trade was untaxed and demand remained steady, so black money accumulated rapidly.
That illicit wealth paid for the heirs’ luxurious youth.
Because the money could not safely be deposited in legitimate banks, they simply burned through it in cash.
“The champagne tower is full! Bring them in. Bring the good ones.”
The heirs dismissed the waiters for a while.
Then they began talking among themselves.
“It’s nice now that that uptight Messina-Dena family is gone. We can enjoy ourselves without someone watching everything we do.”
“That’s how it should be. It’s our money.”
“Framing them and driving them out was the right call, wasn’t it?”
“Don’t let people like that join again.”
They smoked bubbling hookahs with lazy expressions.
One of them suddenly looked up.
“By the way, did you hear about the new Deputy Magistrate?”
“Yeah. We already requested an audience. His middle name is Van.”
“Van? Then he’s probably collateral blood or an illegitimate son.”
“He should feel honored that we called for him.”
“Heh heh. He’ll come running.”
Whenever a new Deputy Magistrate arrived, the committee performed what it called ‘taming the new official.’
It was nothing complicated—just an extravagantly dazzling party designed to make the newcomer want more.
It amounted to fastening a leash around the official’s neck: if he wanted continued access to their world, he would learn to obey them.
The heirs chuckled.
“His name is Vikir. Anyone heard of him?”
“No. Never.”
“He’s fifteen, and they say this is his first time living outside the family.”
“What? So he’s just a kid?”
“Exactly. I heard he threw a huge drinking party the moment he reached City Hall. Sounds pretty indulgent already.”
“Heh. Then he should fit right in with us.”
One of the heirs suddenly had an amusing idea.
“Why don’t we teach him a little lesson while we’re at it?”
He raised one finger and explained.
“Invite him to a huge party. Bring the best liquor and plenty of women and let him enjoy himself.”
“And then?”
“When it’s over, stick him with the bill.”
The heirs burst out laughing.
“Good. That sounds fun.”
“Imagine his face when he sees the total.”
“He needs to learn how much his seniors spend to entertain him. If he takes it for granted, he’ll learn the hard way.”
“A fifteen-year-old official won’t have that kind of money, and he won’t dare ask his family without being reprimanded.”
“Then afterward we can say it was only a joke and pay it ourselves.”
The seven heirs happily planned how to humiliate the new Deputy Magistrate.
Just then—
“Young masters! The women are here!”
A waiter burst through the door with a broad grin.
A number of women soon entered the club room, visibly amazed by its luxury.
Some were familiar faces, and the heirs waved at them.
“Hey, you came back?”
“After last time?”
“Send her away. We’re tired of her.”
“Why? I thought she was pretty.”
“You—come over here and sit with me this time.”
The women entered one after another.
…Hm?
Something strange was mixed in at the very end of the line.
A very young-looking boy, perhaps barely into his teens.
He followed the women into the room so naturally that none of the waiters thought to stop him.
The seven heirs looked over the women while making crude jokes and eventually noticed the boy.
“Who’s that? Did someone call a male attendant? He is rather good-looking.”
“What? Don’t you know him?”
“No.”
The heirs, waiters, and women all exchanged puzzled looks.
How had this boy simply walked in?
One waiter frowned.
“He walked in so boldly that nobody questioned him. You little brat—how dare you come in here…!”
He never managed to grab the boy’s hair.
Crack—
Something thick and hard broke.
Only then did the waiter realize his wrist had been twisted in an impossible direction.
“Aaagh!”
The boy had broken a grown man’s forearm with raw physical strength.
“Who the hell are you?!”
Three or four waiters rushed him at once; less than a second later, every one of them was on the floor.
The seven heirs’ expressions hardened.
“Who are you, kid? Did you come here looking for trouble? Do you know who we are?”
“I know.”
The boy’s voice held no emotion.
“You’re the fools from the local committee.”
There was no respect, fear, submission, or even visible contempt in his tone—only indifference.
The heirs stared for a moment.
Then they burst into laughter.
“That’s right. We’re the fools from the committee.”
“So he knows who we are.”
“Then we only need to learn who he is.”
“Kid, who are you?”
They found the situation amusing.
But only for a moment.
The boy’s next word erased every smile.
“Vikir.”
The new Deputy Magistrate.
Vikir Van Baskerville had arrived in person.
The heirs quietly lowered their feet from the table.
Then they stood and asked hesitantly.
“D-Deputy Magistrate? Why are you here…?”
“You asked to see me.”
The seven heirs exchanged blank looks.
Then—
“Hahahaha! You’re straightforward, Deputy Magistrate. We didn’t expect you so quickly!”
The atmosphere had already soured.
The heirs had also just been insulted, and their pride was bruised.
“Should we start educating the newcomer right away?”
“Sure. Let’s make him split the liquor bill.”
“We can entertain him, sit him down comfortably, then make him pay when the party ends…”
But their little plan never had a chance to begin.
THUD!
Vikir placed his hand on the table.
Then—
Crackle…
A dark crimson Baskerville aura slowly rose around him.
Mana traveled from Vikir’s body through his palm and into the table.
A violent, precisely controlled resonance spread through the wood.
Gurgle, gurgle…
The champagne in the glasses suddenly began to boil.
Then—
The enormous champagne tower at the center of the room changed.
CRACK!
The single glass at the very top exploded.
Glass shards and champagne spray glittered as they scattered downward.
CRACK!
Clink! Clink!
Clink! Clink! Clink! Clink!
Clink! Clink! Clink! Clink! Clink! Clink!
The four glasses on the next layer burst.
Then the nine below them.
Then sixteen.
Then twenty-five.
Then thirty-six.
Then forty-nine.
Then sixty-four.
Then eighty-one.
Finally, all one hundred glasses on the bottom layer exploded in succession.
The champagne tower collapsed.
It did not fail from the base; destruction propagated backward from the top down.
Crash—crash—crash—
Countless glass fragments and champagne droplets rained across the VVIP room.
Drenched beneath the shower, the seven heirs could manage only one sentence.
“…We’ll pay for the drinks.”
