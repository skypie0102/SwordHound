It had all begun by accident.
In his first life, while the Hound unit Vikir belonged to was clearing a dungeon near the frontier and the Black Mountains, they discovered a strange artifact.
It was a fragment from a swordsmanship scripture.
A single torn page.
Vikir immediately sensed that there was something unusual about its contents and memorized them before doing anything else.
When he later submitted the page, Hugo instantly realized that it contained something extraordinary.
Hugo searched the Inner Library for the book to which that page belonged.
What he eventually found was an ancient swordsmanship scripture now known as Lurking Embedded Teeth.
It had not been hidden among the restricted high-level arts, but neglected on a shallow shelf among miscellaneous books no one cared to examine.
The sudden discovery of the scripture’s true nature threw the entire Baskerville family into an uproar.
“This swordsmanship is the true essence of Baskerville.”
Hugo praised it without reservation.
At first glance the book had seemed insignificant, but once a missing page was restored, its entire system took on a different meaning.
It was a swordsmanship that appeared to defend against an enemy’s attack, concealed its aggression, then suddenly sprang out and bit into a fatal opening for the purpose of inflicting devastating pain.
The ancient Baskerville ancestor who wrote it had recorded a path capable of producing ten fangs.
The Baskerville Tenth Form.
Hugo had already reached Sword Master with seven fangs, so there was no way such a scripture could fail to interest him.
…But even after Vikir recovered the missing page from the first half, the scripture was still incomplete.
More pages were missing from the latter portion.
There were seven torn pages in total.
Vikir had found one. The other six were still missing.
Hugo spent the next several years hunting down the torn pages of Lurking Embedded Teeth.
The Baskervilles pursued them relentlessly.
After investing enormous amounts of time and wealth, Hugo determined that the missing pages had been scattered among territories and possessions connected to the Seven Great Families that supported the Empire.
Naturally, he began taking them back one by one.
The campaign came to be called Operation Reclaim the Torn Pages, or Operation Restore the Missing Teeth.
Vikir slowly shook his head as the memories returned.
He could almost smell the blood again.
“…It was a long operation.”
Hugo unleashed his hounds to recover the pages.
Whether the other families understood the pages’ true value or not, their resistance was fierce.
That was only natural.
No one welcomed another family’s hounds bursting into their own courtyard.
In practice, Baskerville came close to declaring war on every other Great Family.
Countless Baskerville hounds died in the process.
The brothers and companions Vikir had grown up beside disappeared at an unbelievable rate.
Faces he had seen in the dining hall one day would be gone the next.
The empty spaces left by fallen teeth were filled by new ones, and those new teeth soon fell out as well.
Through all that grinding, Vikir was the permanent tooth that refused to fall.
He became a savage black hound, fighting and fighting without end.
Eventually, Vikir recovered all seven missing pages and delivered them to his master.
Several fingers and toes, both ears, burns and cuts covering his body, and the brothers he had lost along the way.
The reward for all of it was simple.
“Well done.”
One sentence of praise from Hugo. Why had Vikir once believed that was enough?
“…What a stupid life.”
Vikir ground his teeth.
In the first timeline, Hugo had used everything Vikir and the other hounds brought back to strengthen himself and his two sons.
Hugo’s cultivation rose from seven fangs to nine, and the prestige of Iron-Blooded Sword Baskerville soared with him.
All of it had been built on the blood of the hounds and the bodies of the dead.
“That will not happen in this life.”
Vikir closed his eyes and recalled the missing pages.
Everything that had been written on them remained in his memory.
Even so, the Vikir of his first life had never been able to advance beyond the Fourth Fang because he did not know the original scripture.
Seven isolated pages were still only fragments.
Without the original framework, the knowledge was useless.
…But not anymore.
The original copy of Lurking Embedded Teeth was now in Vikir’s hands.
Seven pages had been torn away, but every missing passage was still perfectly preserved in his memory.
Years of carrying those pages through sieges and battles—sometimes with no way to protect the paper itself—had forced him to carry their contents in his mind instead.
Vikir began reading.
‘The beginning of the Tenth Form shares a principle with the First. Once the sword’s opening trajectory reveals aggression, every technique that follows inherits an innate limitation…’
Indeed.
To anyone who lacked the missing context, the book would have looked like meaningless nonsense.
Ten forms? What could such claims possibly mean?
But once Vikir mentally restored the torn pages and filled the gaps, the apparent bluster became interlocking pieces of a coherent masterpiece.
After making sure that no one was watching him, Vikir began moving his body in accordance with the text.
His four fangs had already been honed to their limit in his first life. What came after them was where the real lesson began.
The scripture explained how to reveal the Fifth Fang, create the Sixth, understand the danger of the Seventh, reach toward the Eighth, determine whether the Ninth truly existed, and finally approach the Tenth.
His mana was still insufficient and his eight-year-old body immature, but someday he would be able to reproduce everything described there.
‘Fifteen. Before then, I’ll recover all the power I had before regression.’
And that would still not be the end.
Afterward he would push beyond his old limits, advancing through the Fifth, Sixth, and Seventh Fangs that had once been beyond his reach.
He would never again endure the humiliation of possessing power his hound-taught swordsmanship could not match, leaving him suppressed, beaten down, and dismissed by those above him.
“First, I’ll memorize everything in this book.”
Once the memorization was complete, the physical scripture would disappear from the world forever.
Then Hugo would never find it, and he would never gain the knowledge needed to reach nine fangs in this timeline.
For an eight-year-old, it was a surprisingly satisfying form of revenge.
Vikir gave a dry laugh.
He still had at least twelve hours remaining inside the Inner Library.
For a child trained by Baskerville, memorizing one thin volume in that much time was hardly impossible.
Half a day later.
Unaware of what would happen afterward, Vikir continued reading the book.
Exactly ten hours and fifty minutes later.
Fwoosh—!
A small fire broke out in the shallowest part of the Inner Library, where the miscellaneous books and cleaning staff were concentrated.
No one was injured. No one died.
The only meaningful damage was a single old volume burned to ashes.
It was such a minor incident that even the servants could easily shrug it off.
