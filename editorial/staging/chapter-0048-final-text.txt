Vikir thought,
‘She looks familiar.’
He was certain he had seen the woman before.
But he had no time to dwell on it.
Pfft!
Her long leg snapped out and struck Vikir in the abdomen.
A numbing shock ran up his spine under the unbelievable weight of the blow.
There was no way she could produce that kind of force without aura.
Vikir flipped several times through the air before landing on his feet.
‘A master.’
The Ballak woman before him was no ordinary warrior.
Charcoal covered her face, making her features hard to read, but she appeared to be in her late teens.
Fighting her while continuing to conceal his real ability would be difficult.
Vikir lowered his stance and prepared for a serious exchange.
Then the woman spoke.
“You said we would meet again, didn’t you?”
Her Imperial was awkward.
At last Vikir remembered where he had seen her.
She was the girl once confined like an animal at the illegal slave auction in Underdog City.
The traffickers had intended to sell her to depraved buyers.
Vikir had turned the auction house upside down, opened her cage, and allowed her to escape.
He had even left her a potion.
“So this is how you repay a favor.”
The Ballak girl shook her head at Vikir’s frown.
“I was slave. Revenge for capture. Morgue. Nothing to do with you.”
Apparently, House Morgue was connected in some way to how she had fallen into the slavers’ hands.
That was plausible enough; Morgue and the frontier tribes had been clashing over the ruby development for years.
This raid itself might be retaliation for her capture and treatment.
Vikir narrowed his eyes.
‘Then she must hold a fairly high position.’
The Ballak girl lifted her chin toward a wolf in the distance.
Camus, caught by a lasso, had been secured across the beast’s back.
“For what happened to us, we repay. This woman is next leader. We take her.”
The confrontation between Ballak and Morgue had reached its objective. The girl swung her bow and signaled the surrounding wolves.
It was the order to retreat.
Ballak’s warriors immediately began falling back together, as though Camus’s capture had completed the mission.
The girl hesitated briefly, then looked at Vikir.
“If you want your woman back, follow.”
“…You can speak Imperial?”
Vikir kept talking, trying to gather information and buy a little time.
But that was all she intended to say.
The Ballak girl wheeled her wolf around and vanished into the jungle.
Vikir moved to pursue her.
Whip, whip, whip!
A dense rain of arrows made pursuit impossible.
‘Should I reveal more of my power?’
If he used everything he was hiding, he could probably break through the arrow storm.
But,
“Camus!”
Adolf arrived with reinforcements and charged onto the battlefield.
Vikir stepped back from the arrows.
With so many witnesses present, revealing his true strength was no longer acceptable.
“Give me back my niece!”
Enraged, Adolf cast a massive spell that overturned the earth.
But Ballak’s warriors were fast and experienced.
The vanguard slipped past Adolf’s magic and disappeared beyond the water while the rearguard scattered in several directions to confuse pursuit.
Most of the raiders escaped into the jungle, leaving only a few casualties behind.
“This… this is absurd!”
Adolf stood there stunned.
The reinforcing Morgue mages were equally speechless.
Barely three minutes had passed between the emergency signal and Morgue’s response, yet the Ballak raid had been so disciplined and fast that almost everything was already over.
The raiders had planned carefully, while Camus’s lack of real battlefield experience had left her vulnerable.
Losing slaves or crops would have been one thing; losing the family’s future head was a disaster.
“How are we supposed to fix this!”
Adolf stamped the ground and stared toward the darkening horizon.
Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain was dangerous even by day and far worse after sunset.
Entering the jungle at night bordered on suicide, and even Morgue’s mages hesitated to pursue.
One mistake could wipe out the entire force.
Then,
“We can still catch them.”
Vikir stepped forward.
He stared into the dark forest.
“I went in there when I was young.”
That was only partly true; at eight he had entered briefly once.
But before regression Vikir had spent countless missions searching, scouting, hunting, and killing there, and knew the terrain extremely well.
The Morgue mages stared at him in disbelief.
One of them raised an eyebrow.
“Entering Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain after dark is suicide…”
He never finished.
“Anyone who does not want to come may stay. There will be no penalty later.”
Adolf Morgue stood before Vikir with bloodshot eyes.
He had already recognized that Vikir’s abilities were extraordinary.
Adolf bent until their eyes were level.
Grip!
He seized Vikir’s hand in both of his and spoke desperately.
“Please. Help me save my niece.”
Vikir nodded.
The raiders had been aiming their lassos at him before Camus took the attack instead.
A debt had to be paid, so Vikir turned without another word and entered the jungle tangled with roots.
‘This may also be my chance to loosen Hugo’s control over me.’
A crisis could become an opportunity, and Vikir intended to see whether he could use this one.
Dusk deepened.
Night settled over the forest.
◆◆◆
The jungle of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain was packed with demonic beasts, poisonous plants, and natural traps.
Bone-sucking mosquitoes, venomous thorn vines, soundless spiders, and narrow ravines hidden beneath leaves were everywhere.
An ordinary person would not survive long, and even highly trained fighters could not relax here.
At night the jungle became so dark that even the tribes were cautious about crossing it.
Creatures that slept through the day awakened.
No wonder even Morgue’s bravest warriors hesitated to enter after sunset.
…But,
one figure moved through all those dangers at astonishing speed.
Vikir. Vikir van Baskerville.
He burned certain grasses to drive away poisonous insects, crossed ravines hidden beneath leaves and dirt, and rubbed animal waste onto his clothing to conceal his scent.
Every movement showed long practice.
The Morgue and Baskerville warriors following him could only stare.
“…Are all Baskerville boys like this?”
“Absolutely not. He is a special case.”
Staffordshire shrugged at Adolf’s baffled question.
Vikir, actually leading the group, remained completely calm.
‘These were basic tracking skills during the Age of Destruction.’
Warriors of that era had needed far more than swordsmanship.
Foraging, scouting, tracking, concealment, ambush, assassination, medicine, treatment, cooking—survival demanded all of it.
That was simply the age they had lived through.
Slash!
Vikir split a rotting log blocking the path and stepped through.
Graduator aura vibrated and rotated at the sword tip, cutting the obstruction apart instantly.
Everyone was impressed by how he found and removed every danger himself.
Especially the Baskerville triplets following close behind.
“Cool.”
“Cool.”
“Impressive.”
Lowbro’s speech was still slightly awkward after his severed finger had been reattached, but all three meant the same thing.
Then,
Stop.
Vikir halted at the front of the line.
He studied bare footprints in damp mud, checked the wind, lowered his body, and circled behind a bush.
He was keeping their scent out of the wind.
The Morgue and Baskerville warriors followed him behind the cover.
Then they saw a faint light ahead.
Ballak hunters were gathered around a small campfire.
Rustle—crack—snap.
At night, even tiny human sounds could carry through the forest like thunder.
The Ballak had deliberately scattered dry leaves and twigs around the temporary camp.
Anyone approaching carelessly would announce themselves.
Vikir considered how to cross without making noise.
Then,
“…Silence.”
Adolf uttered a short spell.
A translucent layer of magic immediately covered everyone’s feet.
To Vikir’s surprise, their footsteps made no sound at all.
It was support magic—Adolf’s specialty.
Vikir had once worked as an assassin and knew exactly how absurdly useful silent movement was when approaching a target.
Soon the pursuing group was close enough to observe the camp.

The Ballak hunters rested, unaware that they had become the hunted.
By the faint campfire, Vikir identified the targets one by one.
The silver-and-black-haired woman who had blocked his sword and kicked him earlier was there.
She was giving orders like the leader of the raiding party.
The man who had fired the tranquilizer thorn and thrown the lasso followed her instructions with obvious complaints.
Vikir continued scanning until he found the final target at the center of the group.
Camus Morgue.
She was kneeling with her clothing removed.
Her wrists and ankles were secured to stakes behind her, and a collar circled her neck.
At least there were no visible signs that she had been beaten or otherwise abused.
Camus was furiously cursing the Ballak and did not appear frightened.
Vikir let out a small sigh.
‘Every time I meet her, some disaster seems to ruin her clothes.’
Then,
Tap.
Someone touched Vikir’s shoulder.
He turned and found Adolf Morgue standing there.
Adolf’s face and voice hardened.
“I will never forget this favor, nephew.”
The form of address sounded strange, but this was not the time to question it.
Adolf was the second-ranking power of House Morgue’s magical faction.
The famed magic star was about to reveal what he could truly do.
