The rainy season finally reached Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.
Dark clouds buried the sky, and this year’s rains looked worse than anyone remembered.
Even the oldest villagers said they had never seen rainfall this heavy.
Rivers burst their banks, and ground that had been dry days earlier became violent swamp and torrent.
Almost everything vanished beneath the floodwater.
Everything except Ballak’s village.
“Pull it tighter!”
“Raise the support posts!”
“Secure it! The whole thing will float away!”
Ballak’s once-low settlement now stood high above the flood.
The tribe had not moved to another region.
Instead, they had rebuilt the village upward with planks, logs, rope, and living trees.
More than fifteen meters above the ground, elevated houses now sheltered the tribe from the rising water.
Vikir had led the massive civil-engineering effort that lifted the settlement into the canopy.
“…At least the village will not be washed away.”
He watched the river rise only a few meters below the lowest platforms.
Rope-and-plank bridges linked the tallest trees, and every barracks had been anchored against the wind.
Drainage channels had also been cut through the lower ground around the trunks.
Stone and packed earth formed banks that redirected the strongest currents.
Together, those measures kept Ballak relatively safe from the flood.
Several men were still reinforcing a vine bridge between trees despite the wind and rain.
They waved when Vikir crossed a nearby plank walkway.
“Vikir! Our house is still standing because of you!”
“Good thing we listened!”
Vikir did not answer their thanks.
His gaze narrowed on a massive shadow moving beneath the muddy water below.
“Danger.”
He gave the two men a short warning.
Both immediately looked down.
Only a few meters beneath them, a huge shape moved through the brown floodwater.
Then—
Splash!
The surface exploded.
A monstrous fishlike head burst from the water, its mouth more than five meters across.
It lunged toward the Ballak men above.
Aquatic monsters had begun doing this often since the floods rose.
Floodwater let energized creatures attack prey among the trees.
This one did not get far.
“Get lost!”
Arrows slammed down from the canopy.
Aiyen had been guarding the workers.
Thud!
Several shafts punched into the creature’s skull and forced it back beneath the surface.
Vikir hurried across the rope bridge and climbed higher into the rain-lashed branches.
“Catfish!”
By the time he looked down, the current had already carried the shadow away.
Aiyen grinned at his frown.
“That was not a catfish.”
“What? You saw that head.”
“…You did not see the rest of the body.”
Before Vikir could respond—
The water broke again on the opposite side.
An enormous body rose behind the head he had mistaken for a fish.
The flat skull and huge mouth had made the creature look like a catfish.
But the impossibly long body following it belonged to something else entirely.
It was a gigantic snake.

The species’ body was said to resemble one enormous intestinal tract.
Its mouth and appetite were large enough to swallow an elephant, and old stories claimed truly ancient specimens could consume whole settlements.
Despite their size, Monsieur Hushu were notoriously silent on land and in water.
The specimen before Ballak was ancient, scaled, and large enough to threaten the elevated settlement itself.
Aiyen bit her lip.
“Tch. The flood brought us a dangerous one.”
Even among naturally fast and powerful giant snakes, this specimen was formidable.
Its size and behavior suggested long experience.
[Shhhhh!]
The snake opened its enormous mouth, exposing rows of teeth across the entire cavity.
Aiyen fired immediately, but hard wet scales and blinding rain ruined clean shot angles.
Then—
“Snakes are better handled up close.”
A black shadow appeared behind Monsieur Hushu.
Vikir moved across its back without the monster detecting him and bared Baskerville’s hidden fangs.
Beelzebub emerged through the artery at his wrist.
Dense, viscous Graduator aura coated the blade.
The Baskerville hunting style came alive.
Six concealed fangs unfolded.
They carried a single purpose: break the target with overwhelming pain and damage.
Blood-red aura spun violently around Vikir’s sword.
The snake’s hard scales shattered, and the softer flesh beneath burst open.
[Kyaaaaaah!]
Monsieur Hushu screamed.
Vikir kept moving, seized the creature’s tongue for leverage, and drove Beelzebub into one eye.
With its neck and eye badly damaged, the snake recoiled while spraying hot blood and plunged back toward the water.
The current carried it away almost as much as its own retreat did.
“….”
Vikir considered pursuing it.
In weather this violent, however, the risk was not worth the reward.
“What a waste.”
Aiyen was already beside him, looping a safety rope around Vikir’s waist.
Tethered together, they climbed back toward the higher branches.
By then, most of the village had gathered on the wooden platforms to watch.
“Amazing! Vikir was incredible!”
“That snake has ruled this area for decades!”
“You drove it off!”
The villagers cheered.
Aiyen immediately objected.
“Did none of you see my arrows? Without me, you would have—”
“Boooo!”
The children jeered at her.
“Ugh. This is why I hate children.”
Aiyen shook mud and water from herself.
Then she glanced at Vikir, who was equally soaked.
“You are not going to catch the Red Death just from getting drenched, are you?”
“No one can promise that. Get to a fire and dry yourself.”
Vikir walked away as though the monster attack had been routine.
Aiyen watched him go and repeated the word she had been using for two years.
“Pass.”
◆◆◆
Vikir entered his barracks.
Outside, wind and rain hammered the village; inside, the shelter was warm and bright.
Heavy stones and strong ropes held the hide walls in place despite the storm.
Vikir’s Red Death measures had made him a hero not only to Ballak but to neighboring tribes.
Yet the hero now faced a problem entirely outside his old experience.
It was a role he had never handled before or after regression.
“Uncle!”
The moment Vikir entered, Pomeranian ran over and clung to him.
Until then she had been sitting alone, staring at the portrait inside her family pendant.
She avoided nearly everyone except Vikir.
Since arriving, she had followed him constantly and resisted being separated from him for ordinary daily routines.
“…I’m hungry.”
Vikir still had little instinct for handling a small child.
So he defaulted to the problem he could solve.
He built a small cooking fire in the corner.
The rainy season had brought snails everywhere, making food easier to gather.
Vikir steamed snail meat with butter, herbs, and dairy.
He fried another portion with sugar and egg-based cream.
The rest he minced with strong spices and vegetables into a simple savory dish.
“Delicious, Uncle.”
“Your uncle can cook.”
Aiyen had naturally joined Pomeranian beside the meal.
Pomeranian looked from Vikir to Aiyen.
“Mommy. Daddy. Same.”
She giggled while comparing them, and Aiyen rubbed a finger beneath her nose.
“Children really are wonderful.”
“…Didn’t you say you hated children?”
“When did I say that?”
Vikir pressed a hand to his forehead; the barracks had already felt crowded with Pomeranian, and Aiyen made it feel smaller still.
“Do not be so stiff. At least you can leave the barracks by yourself now.”
“….”
Vikir could only sigh at Aiyen’s teasing.
Just then—
Whirr.
The entrance covering snapped aside and let in wind and rain.
A heavy stone normally held it secure, so the opening had clearly been deliberate.
A figure stepped through the darkness outside.
It was Ahun.
“…?”
Vikir and Aiyen frowned at the same time.
Ahun usually arrived carrying some challenge or complaint.
But this time his expression was completely different.
His face was pale, twisted with fear, and close to tears.
Then Ahun cried out desperately.
“Please help me, Vikir. My sister…!”
