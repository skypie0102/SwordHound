“Have a safe trip! Leave the administrative work to me!”
With Chihuahua saluting behind him, Vikir left Underdog City Hall.
Vikir rode a black horse toward the edge of the territory, while another black horse followed close behind.
“Good to see you again, Master.”
The rider was Guardian Knight Staffordshire Baskerville, a member of the Pit Bull Knights.
He had once overseen Vikir’s training and had also stood beside him during the raid on the illegal slave auction.
“How is Uncle Boston Terrier?”
“The Count is still very much alive. Ever since the slave-auction incident, he has been telling me to recruit Master Vikir into the Pit Bull Knights. He has given me the same mission.”
“What mission?”
“To make certain the Wolfhound Knights never steal you from us.”
Staffordshire chuckled after saying it.
Vikir nodded.
Servants, supplies, and the rest of the party followed them toward Red Awl Mountain on the outskirts of the territory.
As its name suggested, the reddish mountain rose steeply from the earth like an awl.
A broad, flat basin spread around its foot, making the pointed mountain look even sharper.
The great tributary flowing down from Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain deposited rich sediment here, making the land extremely fertile.
Sugar cane, cotton, tobacco, and other crops grew across the basin, forming some of Baskerville’s principal agricultural products.
Beyond the vast farms, reddish veins appeared in exposed layers of rock.
Where earthquakes had cracked the strata, red glimmers shone from within—the ruby veins.
Morgue workers were mining rubies there.
Simple forts of timber and stone rose above the mining district.
Because Baskerville had leased the area to Morgue, the border now required careful observation.
Vikir’s sharp gaze swept over the farmland behind him and the mines ahead.
He saw nothing openly illegal.
Slaves tended and harvested crops or worked pickaxes as usual.
Morgue also appeared to be observing the treaty, perhaps because Baskerville’s delegation was present.
Staffordshire lowered his voice.
“Officially, this mission is an inspection of our territory and a goodwill visit, but….”
“I know. The real purpose is to establish an outpost for exterminating the barbarians beyond the mountains.”
Otherwise there would have been no reason for Baskerville knights to follow the delegation so closely.
The Baskerville force had entered the leased area disguised as an ordinary territorial inspection party.
Morgue, of course, knew the truth.
For some time, Morgue’s own armed forces had been gathering here disguised as miners.
Now Morgue and Baskerville intended to join forces against the barbarians.
Unaware that large forces of swordsmen and mages from both houses had assembled, the barbarians would raid as usual for crops and captives—and that raid would trigger the counterattack.
Staffordshire explained.
“I hear Morgue’s Light Faction has one senior representative and twelve mid-ranking members here, while the Dark Faction has one senior representative and nineteen mid-ranking members. There are also around a hundred other mages outside those ranks.”
“Morgue is committing seriously to this.”
By comparison, Baskerville seemed to have sent only Vikir, a civil official, and a few dozen ordinary knights.
That appearance did not matter. Baskerville’s true elite force was waiting in ambush beyond the mountains, exactly as agreed with Morgue.
Vikir himself really was here to inspect the territory and exchange goodwill.
‘If Hugo is right, this will also be a useful chance to study the barbarians’ movements and ecology.’
Before long, the Baskerville party entered the mining district.
Unfinished mines lay everywhere, surrounded by wooden palisades, makeshift forts, and tall watchtowers.
Then,
“Hmm?”
Vikir slowed his horse after noticing something.
The smell of burning reached his nose.
It was the smell of meat cooked past its limit and charred black.
As though sensing the same thing, the horses grew frightened and hesitated.
Something stood upright from the ground ahead.
Long and pointed, the structures rose vertically toward the sky.
Something had been impaled midway up each one.
Vikir kicked his hesitant horse forward.
The strange sculptures became clear.
They were enormous iron spikes.
Only a mage could create such things by gathering the trace iron scattered through the soil.
“A mage.”
Such a sight was hardly surprising within territory occupied by Morgue, a family famous for magic.
What had been pierced and pinned upon the spikes was another matter.
Bones and blackened flesh.
Every corpse impaled there had been burned.
Demonic creatures and humans hung together, and some bodies had burned away so completely that only empty spikes remained.
Whish—
Whenever the wind blew, the skewered remains crumbled into black powder.
Thud.
A charcoal-black lump that might once have been barbarian or monster fell from a spike and scattered into ash.
“That is quite a warning.”
Staffordshire looked up at the burned bodies.
It could only be a warning from Morgue to the monsters and barbarians nearby.
And Vikir already knew someone who favored warnings like this.
‘…You’ve grown quite a bit.’
Old memories stirred in him.
“Who goes there?!”
“Stop!”
“Identify yourselves!”
Sharp shouts came from the watchtower ahead.
Vikir looked up and saw three girls descending from its top.
They walked through thin air as though descending an invisible staircase.
Fiery red hair and formal dresses looked strangely out of place in the murderous fortress.
Vikir already knew them from his memories before regression.
‘The Morgue triplets: Highsis, Midsis, and Lowsis.’
They were sixteen years old this year.
Together they specialized in water, earth, and plant magic, and the synergy among the three was formidable.
Born on the same day and at the same hour, they were called the Three Flowers of Morgue.
The rest of the world had another name for them.
Samhwa.
The Three Plagues.
Each sister possessed an outrageous personality; together, they were said to turn any gathering upside down.
Their pride bordered on arrogance, making them notorious even among Baskervilles.
They were infamous for ruining the atmosphere of supposedly friendly annual competitions.
Their strong magical talent only made their abrasive personalities harder to tolerate.
Now the Morgue triplets guarded the fortress controlling entry into the leased territory.
Vikir rode forward.
“We are emissaries of House Baskerville.”
Highsis, standing at the front, sneered.
“So?”
“We are here to inspect the territory and exchange goodwill. Open the fortress gates.”
“Not now. We already sent word to the people in charge, so wait.”
They were being told to wait with no promise of when that wait would end.
Vikir asked,
“How long?”
“Who knows? Maybe orders will come tomorrow. Ho ho ho.”
It was no longer procedure. It was provocation.
Vikir’s eyes narrowed.
“Why are you stopping Baskervilles from entering Baskerville land? You are remarkably arrogant for tenants.”
“What? Tenants? Don’t you know the Tenant Protection Act? Your own administration made that law. Do you not know your family’s laws?”
“I already amended it. Troublesome tenants can now be evicted.”
“….”
While Highsis was speechless, Vikir urged his horse another step forward.
“I am the Deputy Magistrate of Underdog City. This visit was arranged in advance. I will say this one last time.”
“….”
“Open the gate.”
The three Morgue sisters exchanged glances.
Then Highsis, the eldest, smiled.
“We heard there was a young hotshot in Underdog.”
“Oh my, whatever shall we do?”
“We have plenty of experience dealing with cocky boys!”
The three sisters gathered mana in their palms.
Then—
Boom!
Water, plants, and earth magic erupted in front of the Baskerville delegation.
Vikir frowned and pulled his horse back.
The sisters’ laughter carried through the rising cloud of dust.
“Just wait, you ignorant swordsmen!”
“Even Baskerville cannot barge into Morgue-held land!”
“Let’s see how impressive Baskerville’s new supernova really is!”
Staffordshire moved beside Vikir.
“I don’t think words will solve this.”
Vikir had reached the same conclusion.
Just then,
“You ignorant girls! Do you even know who you’re mouthing off to?!”
“Ridiculous!”
“Ridiculous!”
Three angry shouts erupted from the Baskerville delegation.
Three familiar figures emerged from the dust.
Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro.
The Baskerville triplets, who had been riding toward the rear of the party, stepped forward.
Standing shoulder to shoulder again, they glared at the descending Morgue sisters.
They snorted.
“Ignorant?”
“You think you rank above us?”
“Don’t get ahead of yourselves.”
But the three brothers immediately denied that they meant themselves.
“Not us.”
“Not us.”
“Not us.”
“Then who?”
“Who?”
“Who?”
At the sisters’ question, the three brothers turned their heads in perfect unison.
“He is the face of House Baskerville!”
The brothers looked in that direction with a mixture of awe and fear.
They were looking at Vikir.
