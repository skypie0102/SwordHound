A rare prodigy had appeared in Baskerville.
A young warrior who had reached the complete Low Sword Expert level at only eight years old.
“Is there anything stronger than an orc?”
Vikir’s question sent a stir through the training ground.
The members of House Baskerville were generally cold and indifferent to others, but there was one exception: battle always caught their attention.
Even the cold-blooded Hugo was no exception.
“…Hmm.”
Hugo fell into thought.
Watching him, Vikir considered his own plan.
‘It’s best to reveal my strength in moderation.’
He had already thought this through.
Showing extraordinary results at such a young age could actually make Hugo less suspicious of him.
Displaying enough promise would also give Vikir a legitimate excuse to move beyond Hugo’s direct supervision.
He might be assigned to a distant province or sent to study in the Imperial Capital.
‘…Or perhaps I could enter the Academy.’
He did not particularly welcome that third option, so he set it aside and cleared his thoughts.
For now, he needed to focus on what was in front of him.
Meanwhile, Hugo finished thinking.
“Vikir has a point. You’re at an age when you need real combat experience.”
It was hardly an ordinary thing to say to an eight-year-old, but within Baskerville it made perfect sense.
Hugo continued.
“It seems an ordinary orc is beneath my son’s level. Do we have anything stronger among the family’s captives?”
He spoke as casually as if he were searching a specimen box for a better insect.
But every creature kept by Baskerville was a vicious monster.
None of them were suitable opponents for an eight-year-old.
Knowing that, even Butler Barrymore hesitated to answer.
Unable to tolerate the silence, Hugo spoke first.
“Come to think of it, didn’t we keep one specimen captured during the campaign against the barbarians?”
“…Yes. There is one left.”
Butler Barrymore answered reluctantly.
Hugo gave a faint smile.
“It must have weakened after going so long without proper feeding.”
“Perhaps, but starvation may only have made it fiercer. It hardly seems suitable for a boy who has only just turned eight…”
“If anything goes wrong, the Guardian Knights will intervene. That is not something the butler needs to worry about.”
Hugo waved away Barrymore’s concern.
His deliberation had been brief, and his decision even quicker.
Soon, the Guardian Knights began hauling Vikir’s next opponent up from the depths of the cellar.
Thump, thump, thump, thump—
The ground trembled faintly.
A gigantic creature emerged, bound in heavy shackles and restraints.
It had a short neck, two arms and two legs, grotesquely twisted teeth, a weight of more than four hundred kilograms, and a height of roughly four meters.

Its regenerative power was so extreme that it scarcely recognized ordinary mortal wounds; unless its neck was completely severed, it would keep recovering.
A huge, green-skinned humanoid monster stood before them.
Sticky saliva dripped past ragged lips and twisted gums around its protruding fangs.
[Grrrrr—roooar!]
The troll rattled the chains and heavy lead weights fastened around its neck, wrists, and ankles.
Its gaunt condition made it obvious that it had been starved for a long time.
“….”
Vikir narrowed his eyes at the troll.
The moment it was brought into the center of the training ground, the hunt began.
Click—
Vikir surged forward with his short sword ready.
Hugo and several veteran Baskervilles watched with interest as the boy showed not the slightest trace of fear.
Then—
Boom!
The troll swung one massive arm.
Vikir ducked beneath the fist and slipped into the blind spot beside its flank.
It looked like the perfect chance for a killing blow.
But—
Whoosh!
Vikir did not stab the troll in the side.
He simply passed through its blind spot and reset his stance.
Hugo’s mouth curved faintly.
“Good judgment.”
Trolls possessed extraordinary regeneration and could readily heal wounds to the flank or abdomen.
Unless an attack struck somewhere truly decisive, dealing such damage would only waste energy. Vikir therefore ignored the apparent opening.
Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud!
The troll continued attacking.
It smashed its fists into the ground and stamped its feet in a relentless barrage.
An ordinary eight-year-old Baskerville would have been overwhelmed by those attacks and begun making mistakes. Vikir was different.
‘Too slow. There isn’t even any tension.’
He was a veteran who had spent more than forty years on battlefields. This level of pressure could not disturb his composure; suppressing a yawn was harder.
Vikir evaded every attack with almost insulting ease.
In fact, the troll was the one exhausting itself each time it rampaged.
The veteran Baskervilles watched in amazement.
“He’s deliberately draining the troll’s strength. Remarkable.”
“Is that boy really only eight? I can’t believe it.”
“Heh. To think I’d live long enough to see a child like that born into our family.”
Vikir’s judgment never wavered; he kept choosing the most efficient course.
The troll grew more agitated as it fought against both Vikir and its heavy restraints.
Its reserves of strength were draining rapidly.
Its attacks grew less accurate, its movements slower, and its patterns increasingly simple.
Vikir calmly watched the troll destroy its own stamina, waiting for the single moment that would decide the fight.
Then—
Rooooar!
The troll hurled its entire body forward in a desperate charge.
Vikir recognized the opening and immediately moved.
An eight-year-old body could evade a troll, but ordinarily it could not kill one.
Not even an eight-year-old genius should have been able to do so.
That was common knowledge, and Vikir understood it better than anyone.
If he revealed his true power, killing the troll would be easy enough—but doing so would create far more troublesome problems.
So Vikir had waited for a moment when he could kill it while revealing as little as possible.
Thud-thud-thud-thud!
The troll charged, and Vikir turned its own momentum against it.
He thrust from the opposite direction, using the weight and speed of the troll’s body to help his blade cut through tough hide and muscle toward the nerve bundles in its neck.
Then—
Whoosh!
A faint gaseous aura gathered at the tip of Vikir’s blade.
It was the same level of aura that had astonished everyone during the orc fight.
Shrrrk—
The aura took on the distinct outline of a fang and flew straight toward the troll’s throat.
Bang!
One sharp fang sank deep into the troll’s neck.
In the next instant, Vikir twisted his body through the opening and tore a gaping hole through the side of its throat.
“Oooooh!”
The spectators erupted in astonishment.
Vikir’s decades of battlefield experience looked, to everyone else, like impossible genius.
…But—
[Grrrrrrrr!]
The troll was not dead.
It jammed a fist against the hole in its neck as the torn flesh began knitting itself back together.
A disappointed sigh escaped the onlookers.
“Ah… one fang still isn’t enough to kill a troll.”
“With gaseous aura, I suppose that much was inevitable.”
“Still, for an eight-year-old to get this far is already an extraordinary achievement.”
Baskerville First Form. One fang was not enough to finish a troll.
Vikir had known that from the beginning.
Beelzebub’s Bleeding ability could make the troll die from continued blood loss eventually, but winning that way would prove nothing about the level Vikir intended to reveal.
“Enough. Any further would be dangerous. We’ll stop here.”
Hugo raised a hand to end the fight.
“The restraints.”
Vikir spoke.
“Release the restraints.”
Every Baskerville present stiffened.
Release a troll’s restraints now, after all this?
“….”
“….”
“….”
Every gaze moved between Vikir and Hugo.
“….”
Even Hugo was momentarily speechless.
At last, he slowly opened his mouth.
“My son, you have already proven your worth. Knowing when to be satisfied and step back is also part of a swordsman’s discipline.”
His stern, almost paternal admonition startled the gathered Baskervilles; Hugo rarely showed such concern.
But their surprise lasted only until Vikir answered.
“I’m almost finished. If I fail to kill it with the next strike, I’ll stop.”
It was a declaration that he needed only one more attack.
Vikir’s words shocked everyone present, Hugo included.
How could he possibly kill a troll whose regenerative tenacity had just been demonstrated so clearly?
“….”
Hugo said nothing. His silence was permission to continue.
[ROOOOAR!]
The troll went berserk.
Several Guardian Knights stepped forward, removed its cuffs and chains, then withdrew from its path.
Completely free at last, the troll hauled itself upright.
Its eyes were so bloodshot that the pupils were almost impossible to see.
[RAAAAAAGH!]
With a roar that seemed dragged up from its entrails, the troll charged Vikir at full force.
Vikir merely watched it approach with cold eyes.
‘How much of my power should I show?’
He had already decided the answer earlier.
Snap!
The tip of Vikir’s short sword shone a little brighter than before.
Several veterans immediately noticed that the density of his aura had changed.
But there was no time to dwell on it. Vikir was already moving.
Shrrk—crack!
The sword flashed toward the charging troll, its fang-shaped aura now unmistakable.
Freed from its restraints, the troll was moving faster than before, which meant the opposing reaction force would be even greater.
Then—
BOOM!
The troll’s faster, heavier charge drove its own body onto Vikir’s returning blade with even greater force.
And then—
This time, two fangs were clearly embedded in the troll’s neck.
The first fang ripped open its throat.
A second fang—smaller and fainter, but unmistakable—bit viciously into the side of its neck.
Baskerville Second Form.
Intermediate Sword Expert.
…! …! …! …! …!
Hugo and every other Baskerville stood frozen, mouths hanging open.
Thud!
The decapitated troll’s enormous body collapsed to its knees.
