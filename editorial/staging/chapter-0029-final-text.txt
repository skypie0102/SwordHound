Dortes Meyer Street was the main thoroughfare of metropolitan Underdog.
A large public notice had been hung in the middle of its crowded central square.
It was unusual for the city administration to post such a prominent notice there, but the contents themselves were old news.
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Illegal Human Trafficking. Penalty: Death>
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Illegal Gambling. Penalty: Amputation of the wrist>
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Illegal Usury. Penalty: Removal of an eye>
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Illegal Brothels. Penalty: Facial branding>
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Illegal Lobbying. Penalty: Exile beyond the territory>
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
…
…
They were all laws that had existed for years.
Law in territory ruled by Ironblood Baskerville was famously severe.
Almost no citizen believed those laws would actually be enforced fairly.
“Hah. The law always sides with whoever has power.”
“The people at the top make fortunes and still lecture us about law.”
“In this world, the fool is the one who actually obeys it.”
“The law is far away. A fist is close.”
Underdog’s crime-weary citizens mocked laws that, in practice, might as well not have existed.
They had watched Baskerville officials collude with local magnates, wealthy families, and privileged heirs for years.
Those with money, connections, and force bent interpretation and enforcement toward their own interests.
Those with none of those things had almost no way to resist injustice.
They could cry, drink, curse their luck, or vent their anger on someone even poorer and weaker.
After living under that pattern long enough, distrust of the law had become natural.
“Still, the old Deputy Magistrates were finally arrested for bribery. Maybe something will change.”
“You really think so? The new Deputy Magistrate is fifteen.”
“Fifteen? That’s my eldest daughter’s age. What can a kid like that possibly do?”
“Exactly. Give it a little time and the local power families will swallow him through lobbying too.”
Public opinion toward the new Deputy Magistrate was not favorable.
Some pointed to his age, others to unenforced laws, entrenched criminal organizations, or the temptations waiting for an inexperienced official.
Then—
“Everyone! Get to the central square! Something big is happening!”
The already crowded market grew even more chaotic.
People rushed toward the square to see what was happening.
Thousands gathered.
Yet in the middle of the crowd was an open space as cleanly empty as a crater.
No one dared step inside the circular gap.
At its center stood a cold-faced boy holding a wooden stake.
It was Vikir Van Baskerville, the newly appointed fifteen-year-old Deputy Magistrate.
◆◆◆
Vikir held the stake in one hand.
It was about seventy centimeters long and weighed roughly three kilograms.
Citizens crowded around to see the new official, but none came very close.
A mountain-like pressure radiated from the boy’s small frame.
Behind that untouchable presence stood several visibly nervous city officials.
They were members of the Magistrate’s office, including Chihuahua Baskerville.
“What in the world is he planning?”
“He’s been standing there since dawn.”
“Look how many people came…”
Citizens and officials alike had no idea what Vikir intended.
Then—
“Listen.”
Once enough of an audience had gathered, Vikir finally spoke.
“I am the newly appointed Deputy Magistrate.”
The murmuring around the square grew louder.
“He’s that young?” “He looks even younger in person.” “He’s cute.” “What can a child like that do?”
Vikir ignored the unserious comments.
“You have all seen the notice posted on the north side of the square.”
For a moment, the crowd looked north.
A second set of legal provisions had indeed been written across the huge banner.
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Tax Evasion. Penalty: Removal of the kneecap>
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Smuggling. Penalty: Death by drowning>
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Illegal Drugs. Penalty: Amputation of the tongue>
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Embezzling Military Supplies. Penalty: Extermination of three generations>
<Baskerville Autonomous Territory Constitution, Article 00, Section 0 — Prohibition of Adultery. Penalty: Six hundred lashes>
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
…
…
They were familiar laws precisely because everyone knew they were rarely enforced against the powerful.
For people without money, influence, or strength, however, those laws were anything but abstract.
Countless commoners had been punished under them; the number of nobles and wealthy magnates punished was effectively zero.
The crowd looked away from the banner and back toward Vikir.
Vikir continued.
“These are solemn laws established long ago to preserve the order of Baskerville’s autonomous territory.”
A few quiet laughs escaped the crowd.
The laughter openly mocked statutes that functioned only when convenient to the wealthy and powerful.
Vikir made no attempt to identify or punish the people laughing.
“….”
He simply swept the crowd with an indifferent gaze.
His expression sat somewhere between tolerance and complete disinterest.
Then his dry voice continued.
“The laws of Baskerville remain in force. A change of Deputy Magistrate does not change them, and they will be enforced.”
When Vikir ignored the mockery, the crowd grew bolder.
“Sure.” “Of course.” “We’ve heard that before.”
Unable to bear it, Chief Secretary Chihuahua shouted from behind him.
“Watch your mouths! He is an appointed official of House Baskerville! Do you need a blade at your throats before you remember where you are?”
Chihuahua was a local administrator with a strong reputation among ordinary citizens.
Even many of the people jeering Vikir quieted when Chihuahua intervened.
“If Mr. Chihuahua says stop, stop.”
“Honestly, the chief secretary has more presence than the Deputy Magistrate.”
“I wish Chihuahua had been appointed instead.”
“Look at the kid. He can’t even answer a boo without Chihuahua protecting him.”
“The law is only going to get worse.”
Under normal circumstances few citizens would dare say such things, but Vikir’s age, young face, and apparent indifference made them reckless.
“….”
Vikir still did not react.
Even while being compared unfavorably to his own subordinate, he stood without expression.
Chihuahua and the other officials flushed with embarrassment, unsure how to repair the situation.
Even the jeering citizens gradually lost energy when Vikir refused to respond.
“Maybe he really is just a child and doesn’t know how to handle insults.”
“How is someone like that supposed to enforce the law?”
“The criminals will run wild for a while longer.”
Resignation, mockery, anger, pity, and contempt all converged on Vikir.
Then—
Vikir lifted the wooden stake high.
And—
THUNK!
He drove it straight into the ground.
About ten centimeters of the stake sank into the earth.
It was solid but still only a small wooden stake.
Even a child should have been able to pull it free.
…?
While the crowd stared in confusion—
Vikir took several steps back and spoke.
“Under the legislative authority vested in the Deputy Magistrate, I hereby promulgate a new special law.”
Vikir Special Law, Article 1, Section 1.
“Anyone who pulls out this stake will receive one hundred million gold in cash.”
That was the entire law.
