The club known as Burning Suspension had closed its doors.
Not merely closed—the place had been burned to the ground.
Vikir had set fire to the entire hotel building that housed Burning Suspension.
…Roar!
Burning Suspension became an enormous bonfire.
The vast den where the heirs of local noble families had gathered to squander wealth and indulge themselves burned away before the eyes of the entire city.
Though it was night, the flames lit Underdog City as brightly as day.
A few young wastrels lingered before the club, reluctant to leave.
“What? Don’t want to go, you little pigs?”
Under the cold gazes of the Pit Bull Knights lined up before the burning building, they tucked their tails and fled.
Vikir had not stopped at burning the club.
The seven heirs of the seven local families—the VVIPs who had spent the most money and lived most extravagantly there—were displayed before the flames.
Only their heads remained.
The severed heads still bore clear marks of torture.
Beneath them hung long lists of the crimes they had committed while alive.
At dawn the next day, Vikir personally addressed the city.
He stepped into the square without using a spokesman.
“Before the law, status means nothing. Everyone is subject to the law.”
His tone was flat, but the effect was enormous.
Public opinion of Vikir ranged from favorable to overwhelmingly favorable.
Very few people spoke against him.
Whenever three or more citizens of Underdog gathered, they praised Vikir as a remarkable administrator.
Naturally, the local families that had lost their pampered heirs did not remain silent.
House Montblanc, House Pierre, House Louis Vuitton, House Chanel, House Ferragamo, House Hermès, and House Prada each submitted a formal protest to the Deputy Magistrate’s office.
But Vikir did not bend.
“They were involved in numerous crimes, including the illegal slave trade—one of the Imperial Family’s greatest taboos. There can be no punishment but death.”
The Emperor treated illegal slave trafficking as one of the Empire’s most serious crimes.
That was because most people sold through illegal slave markets were barbarians taken from outside the Empire.
Of course, this was not because the Emperor cared about the rights of foreign barbarians.
The real concern was private raiding: wars launched for the purpose of capturing people.
Local nobles needed private troops to conduct such raids, and those forces could just as easily become the foundation of treason.
A slave hunt organized beyond the Imperial Court’s sight could grow into rebellion or a coup.
Indeed, several large rebellions in the past had begun with troops originally raised for slave hunting.
Thus the Emperor’s formal position was simple.
“War may inevitably create slaves, but war may not be waged for the purpose of creating slaves.”
From that point on, illegal slave trafficking was treated almost as severely as treason, a first-class felony of the Empire.
Vikir went directly to the point.
“I have secured the promissory notes, ledgers, and cash records of the seven executed criminals. House Baskerville is formally investigating the matter, and the findings will be reported to the Imperial Court.”
In short: if they challenged the investigation, they could be treated as traitors.
After that declaration, the seven families abruptly stopped protesting.
This was no longer a moment to complain to Vikir out of anger or grief over their dead sons.
Their entire families were now facing ruin.
In the end, the seven arrogant family heads came to the Deputy Magistrate’s office in person, knelt, and begged for mercy—an unprecedented sight.
They had to bow before the man who killed their sons and plead for their houses to survive.
It was the price of having raised their children badly.
They pressed their foreheads to the marble floor, but Vikir remained unmoved.
“I have already completed a full examination of Burning Suspension’s black ledgers. Everyone implicated will be summoned, and their punishment will be determined according to their crimes.”
Writing out every consequence would have filled countless pages.
But the central rulings were simple.
Article 1. All assets belonging to House Montblanc, House Pierre, House Louis Vuitton, House Chanel, House Ferragamo, House Hermès, and House Prada are to be confiscated and transferred to the treasury.
Only assets obtained illegally would normally be seized, but legal and illegal property had become practically impossible to distinguish, so the seizure would encompass everything.
Article 2. The aforementioned houses are to be destroyed for treason.
The reference point for liability is the seven heirs already executed in connection with the case.
Article 3. Members of the seven houses implicated in the affair will receive double sentences under the special provisions of Noblesse Oblige.
Articles 1 and 2 do not overlap.
It could be summarized even more briefly in three lines.
-You.
-Are.
-Fucked.
Since the new Deputy Magistrate’s arrival, blood had barely had time to dry on the execution ground.
It was the foul blood of vermin that had been feeding on the city.
“The flower beds will be richer for it.”
Vikir murmured as he looked at the flowers beside the execution grounds.
The white lilies Chihuahua had carefully tended were now stained red.
…In any case.
The local conservative bloc that had obstructed government decisions at every turn now kept its head down in silence, and once-arrogant regional power holders trembled at the shadow of a government official.
The seven local families that might once have played a power game with Vikir had been uprooted and effectively destroyed.
Rumors also spread to other cities that Vikir held a portion of House Baskerville’s military authority.
Who would not hold their breath before a commander backed by Baskerville military power?
Nothing in Underdog City could stand in Vikir’s way any longer.
Instead, a new group emerged to support him.
“The crimes of the Seven Native Houses had already gone far beyond the line.”
“If you intend to condemn the new Deputy Magistrate as cruel, condemn me first.”
“Rotten flesh does not recover through pleading and appeasement. It must be cut away. This was deserved.”
Honest officials once penalized for their integrity, along with nobles dismissed or driven out for being too upright, began declaring their support for Vikir one after another.
They possessed little wealth or political power, but ordinary citizens respected them as intellectuals, teachers, and mentors.
As more such respected figures followed, support for Vikir’s reforms grew rapidly.
Using the crackdown as a turning point, Vikir reexamined old precedents and released those who had been wrongfully convicted or imprisoned for conscience.
Investigating past cases required so much manpower that City Hall hired large numbers of new officials and contractors, sharply reducing unemployment in Underdog.
The added payroll was funded by revenue seized and taxed from the illegal underground economy.
As a result, Underdog’s citizens began holding mass demonstrations in front of City Hall every day.

Chief Administrator Chihuahua had been smiling for days.
The distrust and contempt with which he had first greeted Vikir were completely gone.
“Working, Deputy Magistrate? I brought you coffee.”
“You’re the chief administrator. Focus on your own work. You don’t need to make coffee for me.”
“This is personal thanks. You saved me when that clown nearly stabbed me the other day.”
“For saving your life, one cup of coffee seems rather cheap.”
“Cheap? I put in two spoonfuls of sugar, two spoonfuls of cream, and only a little water.”
The two had grown close enough to trade jokes like this.
Vikir was learning handwriting from Chihuahua.
More precisely, he was learning how to imitate different hands.
Using those copied styles, Vikir drafted new laws and revised old ones.
He pushed measures including joint ruby-mine development with Morgue, agricultural price stabilization, rewards for contributions to the territory, amnesty for slaves who earned merit, balanced development of neglected districts, guaranteed rewards for obeying the law and institutionalized penalties for breaking it, higher taxes on outside merchants and the wealthy, lower taxes on poor farmers, campaigns against superstition, land nationalization, and more.
Vikir selectively borrowed policies that he remembered working well in the future, then filtered and adjusted them to fit the present society.
Naturally, the legislators were astonished by his knowledge of law and bowed to him with even greater respect.
Underdog City was developing rapidly.
“…Are you really only fifteen?”
Chihuahua asked in awe, and Vikir answered calmly.
Soon afterward, Vikir began one of the most important tasks of the day.
“Chief Administrator.”
“Yes.”
Chihuahua hurried over and stood beside him.
Vikir gave his instruction in his usual indifferent tone.
“Is the prisoner still in the dungeon?”
