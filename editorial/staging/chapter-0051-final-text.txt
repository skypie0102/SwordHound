…
Something peered out from the darkness.
A darkness deeper and heavier than the night around it spread across the swamp.
Deeper, thicker, heavier, darker—‘it’ was there.
The creature had clearly been drawn by the light and uproar created by Baskerville and Morgue.
“Madam Eight-Legs is here.”
The Ballak warriors visibly panicked.
Seeing people famous for their fearlessness react like this made even the Morgue and Baskerville forces understand the danger.
Vikir in particular already had some idea what was watching them from the dark.
Before regression, he had crossed the jungles and swamps of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain countless times.
He remembered something an old native had once told him during those wandering years.
‘The Madam with Eight Legs.’
A fragment of local folklore, almost a ghost story.
It spoke of an unspeakable horror hiding at the bottom of the swamp, deep inside caverns formed by rotting roots.
“…Shh. Don’t make a sound.”
Vikir covered Camus’s mouth with one hand and whispered into her ear.
“It cannot see well, but its hearing and sense of touch are extremely sharp.”
Light and sound were especially dangerous around it.
Creak… scrape…
An unnatural noise came from beyond the tangled roots of the swamp.
It resembled the uncomfortable groan of an old person, or the last gasp of someone drowning in mud.
Rustle, rustle, rustle.
Then came the sound of dense, bristling hair brushing through everything in its path.
Judging by the sound alone, the approaching creature was enormous.
Even Adolf Morgue could not identify the species lurking in the darkness.
“Is this an undocumented demonic beast? How could something this large have gone undiscovered?”
Adolf’s question was reasonable.
But Vikir knew just how vast and unexplored the depths of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain really were.
He silently recalled information from the far more complete monster records of his previous life.
‘Madam Eight-Legs. An annihilation-class demonic beast. Within another decade, it will grow into something even more terrifying.’
Danger Level: S.
A single creature at that level could bring ruin to a nation.
Facing it head-on offered almost no chance of victory.
And here, inside its own swamp territory at night, it was even more dangerous.
Drip… drip… drip…
Something viscous fell from above onto leaves, branches, and people’s heads.
Drops of slime landed from many places at once, creating the illusion of a light rain.
…
Silence.
Perfect silence.
There had not been so much as an insect chirping in the swamp for some time.
And now everyone understood why.
Veteran Baskerville swordsmen, experienced Morgue mages, and fierce Ballak warriors alike had become as helpless as insects.
Everyone froze and held their breath.
They could only pray that the enormous nightmare above would pass them by.
Then,
“Aaaaaah!”
A tearing scream split the air.
Vikir immediately turned his head.
Camus stood nearby, eyes wide.
“…That wasn’t me, all right?”
She crossed her hands in an X, as if promising not to cause another problem.
Vikir let out a small breath of relief.
The one screaming was Ahun of Ballak.
“My head! Madam’s poison!”
Ahun flailed while grabbing at his hair.
He quickly cut the contaminated section away with a knife.
Sssssssss…
The severed hair melted on the ground.
But escaping one drop did not mean escaping what followed.
‘It’ reacted to Ahun’s scream.
A dense mass of darkness descended toward the Ballak warriors.
Aiyen seized Ahun by the back of the neck and ran.
“You idiot! If you weren’t the shaman’s eldest son, I’d leave you here!”
Ahun shrank under Aiyen’s growl and did not argue.
The Ballak warriors immediately scattered through the swamp in every direction.
“We run too!”
At Adolf’s command, the Morgue and Baskerville pursuers began retreating.
But there was one important difference between their retreat and Ballak’s.
Each Ballak warrior fled alone, and almost none chose the same direction.
Morgue and Baskerville, by contrast, withdrew together as one group.
That small difference helped ‘it’ choose its prey.
Crash-crash-crash—
Blade-hard leaves snapped apart as though they were nothing.
A gigantic mass of fur forced its way through the foliage and thorns toward them.
It was coming at terrifying speed.
Thud! Crack! Thump!
Heavy impacts sounded from several directions.
Swordsmen and mages were being smashed aside by attacks they could not even identify.
“What in the world is that thing!?”
Adolf shouted while running with Camus in his arms.
Staffordshire also ran while carrying the injured Baskerville triplets.
Blood marked the triplets’ mouths, but their eyes remained wide as they stared at the thing chasing them.
Then,
“That’s enough.”
Vikir stopped.
At this rate, everyone would die.
Whirr—
Vikir turned around.
Camus saw him stop and screamed.
“V-Vikir! What are you doing!?”
She sounded genuinely terrified.
Without turning back, Vikir gave her a short wave.
“I’ll buy you time.”
‘It’ was not an opponent he could face while continuing to hide his strength.
And this crisis had become an opportunity—a perfect chance to escape Hugo’s surveillance legally.
Vikir instinctively knew that the moment had arrived to execute a plan he had considered for years.
Death—and disappearance.
Once a hound died in the field, all surveillance and control over it ended.
The theory of Baskerville’s Tenth Form was already engraved in Vikir’s memory, while his mana and aura were gradually recovering toward the level of his previous life.
What he needed most was time somewhere no one could watch him.
This swamp was ideal for disappearing, training, and growing stronger before eventually returning when the time was right.
“No! Vikir! Come back!”
Camus cried out.
Vikir kept his back turned to her desperate voice.
Once he could rival Hugo—or at least conceal and control his power perfectly—he could return to the family and perhaps even be welcomed as a hero.
After all, everyone would believe he had sacrificed himself to save House Morgue’s heir and the expedition.
Vikir steadied his stance.
‘If I do not use enough power, I really will die.’
He had no intention of recklessly fighting an S-rank monster to the death.
The important question was how convincingly he could stage his disappearance while surviving.
Vikir stood before ‘it’ like a man preparing to sacrifice himself.
He did not bother saying things like, ‘We’ll all die at this rate,’ ‘I’ll hold it back, so run,’ or ‘Tell my family I fought bravely.’
His actions already conveyed enough.
“…Nephew.”
Adolf bit his lip, face grim.
He bit hard enough to draw blood, but he had to get his poisoned niece back to safety as quickly as possible.
“Master…”
Staffordshire, burdened with the injured triplets, looked back at Vikir with trembling eyes.
Finally, Camus.
“….”
Tears covered her face as she stretched both hands toward Vikir, voice already hoarse from screaming.
Adolf’s strong arm prevented her from going back.
“Go.”
Vikir turned away from everyone and drew his sword.
Then,
“Do not let my nephew-in-law’s sacrifice be in vain!”
Adolf shouted through his grief.
The surviving Morgue and Baskerville warriors finally fled from ‘it’.
“….”
Vikir watched until they had put enough distance between themselves and the battlefield.
Then,
Crash-crash-crash—
the rapidly approaching creature broke through the darkness and lunged toward him.
At the same moment,
Vikir released everything he had been hiding.
Mana strengthened by the River Styx.
Aura refined by his mastery of Baskerville’s advanced swordsmanship.
And Beelzebub, the rare demonic sword fused into his wrist, extended its long fang.
Boom-boom-boom!
Aura as red as blood and black as the abyss erupted violently from Beelzebub’s tip.
It formed five enormous fangs and closed on ‘it’.
SKREEEEE!
The monster screamed in shock and pain.
Boom! Boom! Boom!
Light burst outward as though a miniature sun had fallen into the swamp.
Vikir’s full aura forced a false dawn into the darkness.
Adolf and Staffordshire were focused on escaping and did not see it, but Camus and the triplets, carried facing backward, witnessed the explosion clearly.
Flash!
They saw Vikir unleash the absurd power of an Intermediate Sword Graduator.
