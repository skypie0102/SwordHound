Tink-tink-tink-tink-tink!
Over the clang of the alarm bell, a watchman shouted.
“Outsiders have appeared!”
Something had happened in the village.
Vikir immediately rose to his feet.
“…Is it war?”
Beside him, Aiyen turned with a puzzled expression.
“Are you serious?”
“…?”
When Vikir looked back at her blankly, Aiyen reached out and roughly ruffled his hair.
She smiled.
“There isn’t a band of lunatics anywhere around here stupid enough to challenge Ballak first.”
She was right. Unless the attacker was a high-ranking demon, few would willingly pick a fight with Ballak.
Beyond the frontier, almost no human force could face them directly except perhaps Baskerville or Morgue.
Even the cannibalistic Rokoko tribe was no match for Ballak.
For a tribe of only three hundred people, Ballak possessed military strength wildly out of proportion to its size.
On this part of the continent, the only presence they truly feared was Madam Eight-Legs, the nightmare ruling the surrounding jungle.
“…Then who are the outsiders?”
Vikir asked, and Aiyen rose and pulled aside the entrance curtain.
“It’ll be faster if you see them yourself.”
At her words, Vikir looked beyond the barracks.
“…!”
The sight outside was not what he had expected.
Among the newcomers were pale-skinned people with blond, black, and even blue hair.
They wore clean Imperial clothing and spoke a familiar tongue. They were unmistakably people of the Empire.
“…House Bourgeois.”
Vikir’s expression tightened slightly.
House Bourgeois was one of the Seven Great Families of the Empire.
Baskerville stood for the sword and Morgue for magic; together with five other houses, they formed the great pillars sworn to the Emperor.
Bourgeois had amassed enormous wealth through trade and finance, standing among the Seven Great Families alongside houses such as Quovadis and Leviathan.
Then—
“It’s been a while since they last came.”
Aiyen’s blunt comment broke Vikir’s train of thought.
She was already frowning.
Bourgeois merchants were infamous for going anywhere profit could be made.
They had most likely crossed the frontier with goods to trade to the jungle tribes.
Aiyen clearly did not welcome them.
“They sell things we don’t have and make people feel they’ll regret it if they don’t buy. I don’t like merchants who work that way.”
Vikir agreed with her, at least in part.
Before his regression, Hugo had once summarized his opinion of Bourgeois in a single sentence.
“Fools think money is power because they’ve never seen what real power looks like.”
His voice had been filled with open contempt.
Hugo hated watching Bourgeois grow fat in the wealthy heart of the Empire while Baskerville bled along the frontier.
The fact that their merchants slipped into Baskerville territory to trade with frontier tribes only made that resentment worse.
Other great families disliked Bourgeois for similar reasons, yet none could completely avoid doing business with them.
Diplomacy, commerce, real estate, banking, and much of the Empire’s monetary system passed through Bourgeois influence.
“But how did they get all the way here?”
Vikir studied the merchants with suspicion.
To reach Ballak from Bourgeois territory, they should have had to pass through land controlled by Baskerville or Morgue.
Moving enough supplies for a caravan this deep into the frontier would have been nearly impossible without local assistance.
Only one explanation came to Vikir’s mind.
“…Smuggling.”
They had crossed Baskerville territory without authorization and pushed all the way to the frontier.
Most likely, they had entered through Underdog, the Imperial city closest to these lands.
“….”
Vikir remained still as the Bourgeois caravan reached the village center and began unloading its cargo.
“As usual, we’ll hold a five-day market! We came to Ballak before any of the other tribes!”
The spokesman for the caravan was a middle-aged merchant with a long mustache.
His name was Lord Smuggler.
He wore a gentle smile, but the avarice in his eyes made Vikir distrust him immediately.
The merchants opened their packs and began distributing small goods among the villagers.
Cheap glass beads, brooches, whistles, perfumes, and cosmetics glittered in the sunlight, and even hardened Ballak warriors looked at them with interest.
“Here! Free samples! Take one and try it!”
The merchants scattered trinkets among the crowd.
Older villagers in particular reacted with delight.
“These glass beads are said to bring pleasant dreams. Take one.”
“Perfume for the ladies!”
“And cosmetics too!”
The beads glittered and had been treated with a pleasant scent.
The perfume and cosmetics were similarly fragrant.
In a jungle where such manufactured scents were rare, the novelty alone was enough to attract the villagers.
Once the free gifts had drawn a crowd, the merchants began the real business.
Most of what they sold was grain and vegetables that were difficult to cultivate in the jungle.
Because Ballak did not use Imperial currency, the merchants demanded payment in goods.
Animal hides, bones, teeth, antlers, rare monster parts, mushrooms, spices, medicinal herbs, and similar forest products all became barter.
Sometimes they demanded gemstones or raw gold.
Watching the exchanges, Vikir reached an immediate conclusion.
“What a rip-off.”
Ballak villagers were trading diamonds for sacks of corn.
To them, exchanging an apparently useless stone for edible grain seemed perfectly sensible. Vikir knew the diamond’s actual value, which made the trade absurd.
Then—
“Wait!”
Aiyen stepped between the two sides.
Her gaze moved from the diamonds to the corn.
She planted herself between the Ballak girl offering the stone and the merchant holding the grain.
“That trade is unfair. One diamond for one sack of corn is ridiculous.”
Vikir silently agreed. At last, Aiyen had noticed the problem.
Surely she would stop the merchants from exploiting her people.
But—
“…You should get at least two sacks of corn for it.”
Aiyen turned toward Vikir and flashed a triumphant victory sign.
Vikir let out a quiet sigh.
She was a formidable warrior, but she knew almost nothing about prices in the wider Empire.
Other Ballak villagers were no better. They bargained fiercely, yet had no idea how badly the underlying exchange rates favored the merchants.
“This radish isn’t even sweet. A pearl shell is worth at least two radishes.”
“Look at this cabbage! It’s wilted and full of insect bites. I’m not giving you a whole pair of antlers for that. One antler.”
“Do you know how long I spent drying these otter pelts? Add at least two more potatoes.”
Everyone in Ballak considered themselves an expert haggler.
Yet valuable shells, antlers, hides, pearls, diamonds, wolf teeth, and bear bones were being exchanged for sweet potatoes, corn, radishes, barley, cabbage, and other common produce.
“We really can’t win against Ballak’s warriors.”
“You drive a hard bargain. I shouldn’t raise my price any further, but just this once I’ll meet your demand.”
“We merchants have to make a living too.”
They complained loudly while accepting the Ballak goods as fast as they could.
Before long, the potatoes, sweet potatoes, corn, cabbage, radishes, carrots, barley, rice, and cheap trinkets were nearly gone.
In their place, the caravan carts filled with antlers, medicinal mushrooms, turtle shell, ivory, bones, hides, peony, reishi, matsutake, gemstones, gold, and even eggs or young specimens of rare beasts.
The merchants kept their faces composed, but inwardly they were ecstatic.
The resale value of what they had obtained was tens of thousands of times greater than what they had paid.
Once the main trade was finished, their politeness disappeared.
With more valuables than their carts could comfortably carry, they began deciding which villagers were worth dealing with.
Then—
A young girl stepped forward.
She looked no older than twelve, with dark hair and dark eyes.
In both hands she carried several plump caterpillars carefully roasted on twigs.
Among Ballak, they were a prized delicacy.
The girl offered the skewers to the Bourgeois merchants.
“Excuse me. Could I trade these for a glass bead?”
The merchants answered her coldly.
“We still have beads. What is that supposed to be?”
“Sunbeetle caterpillars.”
Several younger Ballak children looked at the skewers with obvious envy.
Sunbeetle larvae were rare and rich, with a flavor the tribe compared to milk and egg yolk.
The Bourgeois merchants looked at the food with contempt.
“…You don’t expect us to eat that, do you?”
“That may be fine for people from the jungle, but not for us.”
“No wonder you people are so filthy.”
“Bring something worth trading next time.”
They mocked and harassed the girl in Imperial, confident that she could not understand them.
Smack!
One merchant abruptly knocked the skewers aside.
The roasted caterpillars fell into the dirt.
The girl could not understand their words, but their sneers and contempt needed no translation.
Just then—
Crunch.
A boy bent down, picked up one of the fallen skewers, and ate the caterpillars without hesitation.
It was Vikir.
He stepped in front of the merchants while finishing the food.
His face and clothes were still stained with charcoal from the village fires.
“….”
The girl stared up at him in surprise.
Vikir gave her a reassuring pat on the head, then faced the merchants.
When he spoke in fluent Imperial, every merchant in front of him froze.
“This trade is invalid.”
