“The way to destroy an underground economy is simple. Find the core hidden among the scattered points.”
Vikir repeated what he had told Chihuahua.
Underdog City possessed a vast underground economy, controlled by a handful of major players.
Thanks to his memories from before the regression, Vikir already knew where its heart lay.
But even he could not dismantle an organization of that scale alone.
So Vikir had prepared in advance.
“…Could I borrow something?”
It was part of the conversation he had with Hugo before leaving the Baskerville estate.
That was when Vikir had made his request.
“Could I borrow one of the knight orders?”
Hugo considered the request for a long moment before granting it.
He gave Vikir the right to command one of House Baskerville’s knight orders for half a day.
“I trust you will do nothing foolish. My son.”
Even limited access to the family’s military power carried enormous significance.
It meant Hugo recognized Vikir as his son and intended to treat him accordingly.
Thus the Pit Bull Knights, one of the pillars of House Baskerville’s military strength, came under Vikir’s command.
For the next six hours, all one hundred Sword Graduators of the iron-blooded order—knights notorious for leaving no survivors—would obey him.
And Vikir was now using them to sweep away Underdog City’s corruption in a single stroke.
“Hugo is especially sensitive about military power. I was fortunate to borrow the Pit Bull Knights this smoothly.”
Hugo was deeply wary of local rulers keeping private forces, so lending out one of his own knight orders was no small concession.
But his trust in Vikir was stronger than expected, which made everything easier.
Vikir glanced at the Pit Bull Knights lined up behind him.
Even a fighting dog should not bare its teeth without reason.
A third-rate fighting dog trusts only its own strength and looks down on its enemies.
A second-rate fighting dog reacts too quickly to every movement an enemy makes.
A first-rate fighting dog has patience and composure, but killing intent still shows in its eyes.
At the very peak, a fighting dog remains as calm as an inanimate object no matter how fiercely an enemy rages or threatens it.
The virtue of the wooden rooster.
As though carved from wood.
By that standard, the Pit Bull Knights were an extraordinarily disciplined pack.
They stood behind Vikir like wooden posts, waiting for his command.
Vikir set them in motion.
“Bite them all to death.”
A hundred pit bulls finally bared their fangs.
Vikir shouted after the charging hounds.
“Do not kill anyone who stays down. But if they move, kill them without mercy.”
It was a warning.
Vikir spoke loudly enough for every enemy to hear.
Those who had lost the will to fight immediately threw down their weapons, flattened themselves on the floor, and raised their hands and feet.
Most, however, could not bring themselves to do so.
“Aaaagh!”
“Help me!”
A noblewoman who screamed and struggled was decapitated without hesitation.
Her head rolled beneath the fur coat now soaked red with blood.
A fleeing middle-aged man was cut open on the spot.
Anyone who fled or kept resisting was cut down in turn.
…But that did not mean those who stayed flat on the floor escaped unharmed.
“L-let’s just lie still. Once things calm down, we can slip away.”
Several people lay face-down with their hands and feet raised.
It was the posture of people waiting to be cuffed.
But.
“We don’t carry handcuffs.”
A Pit Bull Knight smiled as he stepped toward them.
Just as they were about to ask what would be used to restrain them instead—
Slash! Slash!
The Pit Bull Knight’s blade passed over their limbs.
Wrists and ankles fell away.
“Aaaagh!”
“What are you doing?!”
The ones who tried to crawl away collapsed like insects.
The Pit Bull Knights paid them no further attention and launched themselves at the next moving targets.
Meanwhile,
a dark-haired middle-aged man stood beside Vikir with a thick cigar in his mouth.
He had an imposing inverted-triangle build, a square jaw, sunken cheeks, sunglasses over his eyes, and scars across his face.
It was Count Boston Terrier Le Baskerville, commander of the Pit Bull Knights.
“Nephew. Satisfied?”
“Yes, Uncle.”
“Hmph. If you need anything else, say the word.”
Boston Terrier had taken an immediate liking to his nephew, largely because he approved of how cleanly and decisively Vikir handled things.
And since Vikir was one of Hugo’s favored sons, Boston Terrier saw no disadvantage in getting along with him.
Vikir likewise had little reason to dislike Boston Terrier, a man uninterested in authority, honor, or political maneuvering and interested almost solely in battle.
Just then, Chihuahua spoke from nearby in a trembling voice.
“Deputy Magistrate… is it really all right to cut off their wrists and ankles like that? Some of them are nobles.”
“What does it matter? They are going to be executed anyway. Handcuffs would be a waste. Just make sure they cannot escape.”
Boston Terrier grinned even wider at Vikir’s answer.
“Hmph! Nephew, you should join my Pit Bull Knights someday. This uncle will train you properly.”
Vikir answered only with a faint smile.
Barely a minute after the Pit Bull Knights stormed in, the situation was already over.
Completely over.
Underdog City’s major criminals were either dead or captured.
A few had happened to be absent, but their identities and locations could be traced through seized ledgers and contact records.
“This… this is unprecedented! It’s perfect!”
Chihuahua trembled with excitement as he watched the criminals being hauled away.
The war on crime had been declared suddenly and had ended with astonishing speed.
There would still be cleanup work, but that would be simple by comparison.
Even the local families whose sons had died would find it difficult to object openly now.
Underdog City’s future suddenly looked clear, transparent, and bright.
“Congratulations! Surely no one will dare oppose Lord Vikir now…?”
Chihuahua turned around excitedly.
But Vikir, the man who should have heard the congratulations, was already gone.
◆◆◆
“…It should be around here.”
Vikir had already returned to the rear of the auction house.
The slaughter was still continuing beyond the stage.
While everyone’s attention remained on the fighting, Vikir slipped into the warehouse where the auction goods were stored.
Gold and silver treasures were piled high inside.
They had all been paid in cash by nobles attending the auction.
“…That should make for healthy tax revenue.”
All of it would be transferred to the city treasury, strengthening House Baskerville’s finances in the process.
The thought of Hugo being pleased complicated Vikir’s mood, but it was irrelevant for now.
Vikir searched the warehouse.
Piles of account books and receipts filled the shelves.
He decided to preserve them all; they would become valuable evidence for hunting down the remaining criminal network.
But Vikir had come here for something else.
He grabbed a white cloth covering something in one corner of the warehouse and pulled it away.
There it was.
“…?”
What appeared before him was not what he had been searching for at all.
An iron cage, with the barbarian girl imprisoned inside.
The same girl who had been thrashing so violently earlier was now curled up in one corner.
“…Ugh.”
She let out a pained groan.
At a closer look, malnutrition was only part of the problem; her entire body bore fresh marks of abuse, as though she had been whipped outside the cage.
“She must have been punished for biting off that trader’s finger.”
Vikir clicked his tongue.
He took a small vial of potion reserved for the knights from his belt.
Then he opened the cage and sprinkled the medicine over the girl’s wounds.
Ssssss…
With a sharp crackling sound, wounds closed and new flesh formed.
The groaning girl opened her eyes, saw Vikir inside the cage, and retreated fearfully into the corner.
Vikir studied her in silence.
Black hair streaked with silver. Pointed ears. A face smeared with charcoal-black ash.
Familiar bite and claw scars marked her arms and thighs.
“Cerberus?”
An old memory surfaced.
During his practical examination at eight years old, Vikir had crossed the safety boundary into Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain and encountered the A+ Danger Level demonic creature Cerberus.
Cerberus’s flanks had been covered with arrow-shaped wounds.
“Hmm. Could her tribe be the barbarians who hunted Cerberus back then…?”
It was a reasonable possibility.
The barbarian girl, somewhat recovered now, crouched in the corner and glared at him.
Vikir spoke a single word.
“Fang.” In the barbarian tongue, it meant ‘go.’
The girl’s eyes widened.
Vikir merely shrugged.
He knew a handful of basic words in the language.
Before the regression, he had fought the barbarians of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain countless times.
“П л л.” The girl only blinked at Vikir’s command to flee.
Vikir set the remaining potion on the ground and stepped out of the cage; only then did she cautiously follow.
“….”
She stared at Vikir for several seconds, then tore through the tent and ran.
She took the vial of potion Vikir had left behind.
“Ans andnn ‘mm’ mw.” The girl said something at the last moment, but spoke too quickly for Vikir to understand.
“Now, let’s find what I actually came for.”
Vikir turned and resumed his search.
Gold and silver treasures, account books and receipts, promissory notes, antiques, works of art….
But Vikir wanted something else.
“Here it is.”
He pulled aside the red cloth covering the deepest corner of the auction warehouse.
A massive horned demonic beast lay beneath it.
