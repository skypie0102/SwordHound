The black forest was unnaturally quiet.
The silent forest was unnaturally dark.
Stars scattered across the sky like coarse salt on black cloth, yet not a single ray reached through the dense canopy to the jungle floor.
Not even insects could be heard.
Crackle, crackle.
Only the faint fire gnawing at the wood broke the silence.
“….”
Camus Morgue sat bound and gagged, glaring at the Ballak hunters.
Even while being dragged here, her sharp memory had picked up several names and roles among her captors.
Beyond the weak warmth and glow of the campfire, she watched their silhouettes.
The apparent leader was a girl of about seventeen.
Her name was Aiyen.
Her hair mixed silver and black, her ears were sharply pointed, and charcoal darkened her face.
One thing was obvious: she was strong.
She had been powerful enough to hold off Vikir, a Sword Graduator.
Beside her, a young man grumbled while feeding wood to the fire.
The one who had fired the tranquilizing thorn was named Ahun, and his body was still aching from Vikir’s earlier counterattack.
Several other young men and women were present, most around seventeen or eighteen.
Rather than building a large fire, they banked the embers under ash and sat around the low heat.
“I didn’t catch anyone worth taking as a husband.”
“I liked the boy we met earlier.”
“Maybe we’ll catch him another time.”
Three young women chatted quietly.
Camus could understand some Ballak even though she could not speak it well, so she grasped the meaning of their conversation.
Around this season, Ballak hunting parties conducted what they called husband hunts, wife hunts, son-in-law hunts, and daughter-in-law hunts.
Their targets were other tribes and, at times, people along the Imperial frontier.
The purpose was simple: bring outside blood into the tribe and reduce the dangers of close inbreeding.
Naturally, those seeking a spouse preferred strong, healthy outsiders.
A captive judged suitable might be incorporated into the tribe as a partner; one judged unsuitable could instead be enslaved or killed.
Women were especially valued because the tribe considered them crucial to future generations.
Perhaps that was why Ahun had been staring at Camus for some time with an ugly smile.
“The men of the tribe will be pleased. We captured an excellent woman.”
Understanding him, Camus ground her teeth.
“You think I’ll submit to your tribe? I’d rather die.”
Camus tried to injure herself, but Ahun gagged her before she could.
Chuckling, Ahun placed an arrowhead beneath her chin.
“This arrogant captive needs discipline.”
He started toward Camus.
Snap!
Someone stepped between them.
Aiyen—the young warrior commanding the Ballak hunting party.
She pressed the end of her bow against Ahun’s chest.
“Back off.”
Aiyen’s command was short.
Ahun frowned.
“What is this, Captain? I caught her.”
“Enough.”
“What do you mean? I fired the tranquilizer and threw the lasso!”
“And who kept you alive long enough to do it?”
Aiyen’s cold question silenced Ahun for a moment.
She continued.
“If I had not stopped that ‘boy with the sword,’ you would already be dead.”
She meant Vikir.
Ahun visibly trembled at the reminder.
He shouted in frustration.
“So this woman is yours?”
“Of course.”
“But Captain, you’re a woman!”
“So?”
Ahun had no answer.
Cold killing intent rolled from Aiyen’s body.
A silver, liquid aura gathered around the bow in her hands.
Camus’s eyes widened.
It was unmistakably the aura of a Sword Graduator.
Though still at the lower end of that realm, Aiyen had clearly crossed the threshold.
A seventeen-year-old Graduator—another prodigy existed besides Vikir and Camus herself.
Aiyen did not seem to care whether Camus was impressed.
Ahun, meanwhile, immediately yielded before her power.
“Challenge me whenever you like. I’ll beat you into the ground.”
“…I’m not looking for trouble.”
“No?”
“…No. Fine. I give up. She’s yours.”
Ahun turned away, angry and resentful.
Only then did Aiyen look back at Camus.
Camus did not mistake the intervention for kindness.
It was not.
“Good eyes. Strong body. Good blood.”
Aiyen examined Camus with the cold appraisal of someone measuring a valuable captive.
“I was once captured by people from your Empire and sold as a slave, and Morgue was involved.”
“….”
“So now you will learn the other side of that experience.”
Aiyen closed her eyes briefly as if remembering someone.
“Only you will not be as fortunate as I was.”
She opened them again and stared hard into Camus’s eyes.
“I will take my time breaking your pride until humiliation becomes ordinary to you.”
Aiyen never finished.
Boom!
A violent explosion erupted from the bushes.
“Die, you beasts.”
The voice sounded like a death sentence.
At the same instant, lances of ice tore through the Ballak hunters.
Three or four warriors in the rear fell before they could react.
Behind the attack stood Adolf Morgue, red mana streaming around him.
Morgue and Baskerville’s pursuit force poured onto the battlefield.
“Now I’ll repay the debt.”
Staffordshire and the Baskerville triplets cut into several Ballak hunters.
Morgue’s mages attacked with equal desperation.
Offensive spells filled the darkness with light and thunder.
…?
For some reason, the Ballak hunters did not respond effectively.
The difference in strength was enormous, but if they focused on escape rather than fighting, they should still have been able to reduce their losses.
So why were they hesitating?
Both the hunters and their wolves looked badly unsettled.
Strangely, they seemed more frightened by the light and noise of the surprise attack than by the Morgue and Baskerville warriors themselves.
“Damn it! These lunatics are making this much noise in the jungle at night!”
Ahun sprang back in alarm.
His anxious gaze was fixed not on the swords and spells around him, but on the darkness beyond the camp.
Aiyen showed the same concern.
“….”
She moved immediately.
Instead of countering Morgue’s magic or Baskerville’s swords, Aiyen kicked sand over the campfire and extinguished it.
Once the last glow vanished, the Ballak party began to move.
They mounted their wolves and fired arrows while retreating deeper into the jungle.
“They’re running! Don’t let a single one escape!”
Staffordshire urged Baskerville’s hounds after them.
Through the darkness, another shadow approached Camus.
Vikir cut through the ropes and collar securing her to the stake and freed her in an instant.
Even under the paralytic poison, Camus recognized him.
“…You! You came!”
“Shh.”
Vikir lifted Camus and started to withdraw.
No—he tried to.
A silver shadow appeared in front of him.
“I knew you would come, ‘boy with the sword.’”
Aiyen stood squarely in Vikir’s path.
Her eyes traveled over him as she spoke.
“A body strong enough to cross this distance so quickly.”
“….”
“A mind strong enough to navigate the jungle in darkness.”
“….”
“And the courage to risk yourself to save the woman you came for.”
“…?”
Vikir frowned.
Why was this Ballak woman evaluating him in the middle of a battle?
He started to move past her.
Aiyen pointed toward Camus in his arms.
“I won’t ask about the past.”
The corners of Aiyen’s mouth lifted slightly.
“Pass.”
