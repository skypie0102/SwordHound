It was a moonless night, the sky smothered by dark clouds.
Lord Smuggler and the remnants of the merchant caravan pushed through the jungle with clenched teeth.
“Damn these mosquitoes. I hate this jungle.”
They trudged on in exhausted irritation.
Long-stingered insects clung to them as they slapped their way back toward the hidden base camp.
A rough-looking band of armed men was waiting there.
The shaggy mercenary at the front smirked when he saw Lord Smuggler.
“Judging by your faces, the trade did not go well.”
“….”
Lord Smuggler said nothing and adjusted his pack nervously.
The nearby mercenaries laughed.
“See? Ballak are not people you bargain with.”
“Why bother trading with them at all?”
“Kill them, take the goods, and be done with it.”
The hired force consisted largely of ex-convicts, escaped prisoners, deserters, and other men wanted by the Empire.
Lord Smuggler warned them not to underestimate the tribe.
“Ballak are dangerous in a fight. You are new to this frontier, so you do not understand what they can do.”
“Then why did you bring us? We were hired precisely in case the trade went bad.”
“You were hired as protection against monsters and raiders. I would still rather avoid an open war with Ballak.”
Lord Smuggler pressed his lips together.
Ordinarily he would never have considered a direct attack, but the fortune in goods still sitting inside Ballak’s village made caution difficult.
The mercenaries and newer merchants began arguing for action.
“We paid heavily at the guild auction for the right to join this caravan. We cannot walk away empty-handed.”
“Our protection fee is a share of the gross profit. Do you think we crossed monster territory for nothing?”
“Hit them at night, burn the village, seize the cargo, and leave.”
The veteran traders knew enough about Ballak to be afraid.
Greed, however, made even them reluctant to abandon the valuables they had lost.
In the end, most of the group favored an attack, while the few dissenters fell silent.
Lord Smuggler rested a hand on the sword at his waist.
“Very well. Once it is fully dark, we move quietly, reclaim the goods, and deal first with the one who ruined today’s trade.”
The mood around him changed immediately.
Everyone knew who he meant.
“That little bastard? Fine. Leave him to us.”
“I want first chance at the brat who humiliated us.”
“Something about him was strange. The coloring on his skin did not look natural.”
“Maybe he is Imperial. That would explain the language.”
The men talked over one another.
Spears and swords were sharpened, and preparations were made to burn the settlement if necessary.
Then—
In the darkness, several mercenaries began what they called a ritual.
It was nothing more than sharing a match for their cigarettes.
Scratch.
The first cigarette caught.
One mercenary drew on it and kept the match burning.
A second leaned in and lit his own cigarette from the same flame.
He was about to shake the match out.
A third mercenary stopped him.
“Do not waste it. There is still one more cigarette.”
“You really are new. You do not know the old battlefield rule?”
The first two mercenaries sneered.
“Never let three men light from the same match in war.”
“What kind of rule is that?”
“One match, two men. No more.”
The third mercenary snorted.
“I do not believe in superstitious nonsense.”
He hurriedly bent his cigarette to the flame before the match went out.
The flame died the instant the third cigarette caught.
Thwack!
A dull impact sounded from the darkness.
The third mercenary’s cigarette vanished with him.
The first two stood frozen as hot blood sprayed across their faces.
Their companion had been killed by an arrow before any of them saw the shooter.
They had no time to react.
Thwack! Thwack!
Two more arrows sliced through the dark.
Each shot used the glow of a cigarette and the sound of breathing as a target.
“Hk!?”
Lord Smuggler dropped his own cigarette immediately.
Then—
Thwack!
An arrow struck the glowing cigarette on the ground.
The force of the shots tore through the surrounding brush as more arrows began raining out of the black water and trees.
“Drop the cigarettes!”
An arrow struck the mercenary captain as he shouted the order.
He collapsed before he could finish speaking.
Anyone who cried out became another point of sound for the hidden archers to target.
The force of more than a hundred mercenaries was cut down with terrifying speed.
It happened in seconds.
Meanwhile—
Beyond the arrow storm, Ballak’s hunters watched the lights and listened to the panic.
“First light gives the position. The second gives distance. By the third, fire.”
Hunt Captain Aiyen gave the order.
Ping!
After loosing another arrow, Aiyen turned with a broad smile.
“So they do exist—idiots crazy enough to attack Ballak first.”
Ballak was a warrior people by nature.
They had no reason to avoid a battle their enemy had chosen to begin.
Aiyen continued sniping merchants and mercenaries with obvious satisfaction.
Ballak archers were trained to identify the faintest traces of light and sound.
A cigarette ember was more than enough for them to place an arrow.
Sound worked the same way.
Whether a shout had just left the mouth or was still forming in the throat, their arrows followed its source.
Aiyen was happily drawing again when Vikir caught her arm.
“Enough.”
Her eyes widened.
“…What?”
“Do not kill all of them. Leave survivors.”
“Why?”
Aiyen frowned.
“Do not tell me you are going to start talking about mercy and forgiveness like some soft Imperial noble.”
“Nothing like that.”
Vikir raised one hand and cut her off.
He watched the few remaining lights retreat through the jungle.
“A group this large probably has a second force or a rear camp.”
He wanted survivors precisely so they would flee along their established route.
Following them would reveal any reserves, supply camps, and escape paths behind the first assault force.
Aiyen paused at the cold practicality of the idea.
Then the corner of her mouth lifted.
“…Good. That passes.”
Vikir still had no idea what standard she used for these mysterious passing marks.
◆◆◆
Vikir’s suspicion proved correct.
With an arrow lodged in his shoulder, Lord Smuggler staggered through a narrow canyon of rock.
Beyond it waited the larger rear camp and the mercenaries who had not joined the first attack.
Roughly another hundred armed men emerged to protect the survivors.
“We are stronger at close range!”
“Their arrows cannot get through our shields!”
“Aura users, forward!”
“Mages, raise barriers against the arrows!”
Several mercenary mages immediately erected defensive shields.
But—
PING!
Something different arced overhead.
Several arrows sailed high above the barriers with ropes tied to their nocks.
Large wooden barrels hung from the other ends of those ropes.
“…Oil?”
The mercenaries stared upward.
Multiple arrows worked together to drag each barrel over the camp.
Crash! Crash!
The barrels shattered against the ground and magical shields, spraying oil across everything nearby.
Then fire arrows followed.
Crackle!
Flames swept around the mercenary camp in an instant.
Even those who escaped the fire would lose food, water, medicine, weapons, and shelter—supplies they needed to survive the jungle.
They were trapped.
Some would die in the flames; the survivors would still face the frontier without supplies.
Lord Smuggler shook with rage.
“They tied ropes to arrows and used them to throw barrels? Since when did Ballak fight like this?”
He had seen Ballak archers before, but never tactics this elaborate.
Had he understood how adaptable they were, he would never have started the attack.
Then—
While Lord Smuggler stumbled through the firelight, someone came into view.
Vikir.
He stood calmly beyond the edge of the flames.
Lord Smuggler ground his teeth.
“You planned this too, did you?”
“…You finally noticed.”
Vikir glanced over the burning camp.
Bodies, fire, shattered equipment, and explosions filled the clearing.
His answer carried the obvious implication that Smuggler’s insight had come far too late.
Lord Smuggler’s face twisted.
“I will kill you!”
At that moment—
Vikir picked something up from the ground.
A bow and arrow.
Ping!
The shot struck Lord Smuggler low in the abdomen.
“Urgh!”
It was painful and dangerous, but not immediately fatal.
“…I was not actually aiming there.”
Vikir cleared his throat.
Aiyen had begun teaching him archery, but he still considered his accuracy inadequate.
Creak.
Apology or not, he drew another arrow.
Lord Smuggler raised one bloodied hand.
“Wait! You cannot kill me. You will regret it—I am serious!”
“Why?”
Smuggler pulled a bloodstained document from inside his clothing.
“This is an official prospecting permit from Underdog! It is genuine, stamped by the deputy magistrate. Baskerville authority stands behind me!”
For the moment, the claim was true.
Smuggler was listed as an officially licensed explorer.
Vikir paused.
“Bring it here.”
He beckoned.
Grimacing through the wound, Smuggler approached and held the permit out.
His other hand crept toward the dagger hidden at his waist.
Then—
Rub, rub.
Vikir wiped the jungle stain from his face.
Lord Smuggler’s eyes widened.
“Y-you are…!?”
Recognition shocked him badly enough that the hidden dagger slipped from his hand.
Vikir looked down at the fallen weapon with a dry smile.
Then he spoke.
“I revoke it.”
He took the permit and drew a decisive X across the authorization tied to his office.
In Smuggler’s hands, the paper had been protection; under Vikir’s own authority, that protection vanished.
Vikir threw the invalidated permit into the fire.
At the same moment—
Thwack!
An arrow struck Lord Smuggler squarely in the forehead.
Then—
Thwack! Thwack! Thwack! Thwack!
Several more arrows struck almost the same point.
Lord Smuggler dropped without another word.
“Annoying man.”
Aiyen stepped up beside Vikir with a snort.
Just then—
“Captain! Time to move!”
Ahun shouted from beyond the flames.
Aiyen grabbed Vikir before his injured body could slow their retreat.
She hauled him onto Bakira’s back and the wolf raced away.
Behind them, surviving mercenaries and merchants began shouting.
“Ballak are retreating! There cannot be many of them—chase!”
“The fire is almost out! If they needed fire, they must be afraid of fighting us directly!”
“We survived! Recover the supplies and counterattack!”
Aiyen gave an incredulous laugh.
“Idiots. They think the fire was meant to kill them.”
“…They will understand soon.”
Vikir’s answer was cold.
Then—
Sssssssss…
The swamp water stirred.
Leaves across a wide area bent in the same direction.
Something enormous was moving through the darkness.
Tsutsutsutsutsutsu…
A deeper blackness spread over the water.
The mercenary camp was still full of explosions, bright flames, and high-pitched shouting.
One creature in this jungle was especially sensitive to exactly that kind of disturbance.
Madam Eight-Legs.
The legendary swamp terror had taken notice.
“Put out the fire! Once it is dark we can turn this around—!?”
“Counterattack! We can still—!?”
“What is that? Something huge just moved over there—!?”
“Help! Help me—!?”
The screams disappeared one after another.
Aiyen and Vikir clung to Bakira as the wolf ran at full speed.
…
They did not slow until every sound behind them had vanished.
