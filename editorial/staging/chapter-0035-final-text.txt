Night.
The outskirts of Underdog City.
For some reason, luxury carriages had gathered in a deserted clearing behind the city where almost no one normally went.
Black tents covered the enormous open ground.
Masked nobles and wealthy patrons slipped into the tents.
Tonight was the slave auction—an auction never officially reported to House Baskerville.
Every captive sold here was therefore undeclared merchandise.
Many were people who could never lawfully have been traded as slaves in the first place.
There were barbarians displaced from their homes, nobles taken from distant regions, and ordinary people kidnapped without warning.
They had been chained, threatened, or drugged until the will to escape or report their captors was broken.
Or they were in the process of being broken.
“Welcome, welcome! Welcome to tonight’s Freak Show. Come in, come in!”
A host dressed like a Pierrot clown greeted the arriving guests.
A middle-aged man approached the clown.
The man had a long goatee and a slightly shabby appearance, and he kept glancing around with obvious unease.
The clown inspected his credentials.
“Hmm, hmm. Mr. Chihuahua Montblanc. Ah, a member of House Montblanc. And this magnificent signature is certainly the Montblanc mark no one else can imitate… hm?”
The clown stopped the man calling himself Chihuahua Montblanc just as he was about to pass.
Chihuahua visibly stiffened.
The clown narrowed his eyes.
“Your certificate says you’re here not to buy slaves, but to sell one?”
“Ah. Yes. I came to sell.”
“What are you selling?”
“I’ve got him tied up in the back for now. There’s only one, and he’s young, so he doesn’t take much space.”
The clown smiled and bowed again.
“I see. House Montblanc usually buys slaves in bulk, so when you came as a seller I wondered whether you were dissatisfied with your previous purchases.”
Chihuahua wiped cold sweat from his forehead and shook his head.
“Not at all. I have always been satisfied with the quality of the slaves sold here.”
“Of course. We sell only carefully selected captives—people who have lost families and homes, or who were secretly abducted and thoroughly conditioned until they obey and no longer think of running or resisting. We guarantee the merchandise.”
The clown bowed politely once more.
“Then allow me to welcome you to the Freak Show.”
◆◆◆
A strange tension hung over the auction hall.
Masked men and women sat in a circle around the central stage beneath clouds of hookah and tobacco smoke.
Many of the people who moved the most money through Underdog’s underground economy were gathered here.
A woman in a butterfly mask stared hungrily at a barbarian captive on stage, while a man in a bat mask studied a former mercenary being sold beside him.
The auction house made almost no meaningful distinction between the things presented on its stage.
Ancient jars, famous artwork, fine swords, rare animals, exotic beasts, and human beings branded as slaves all passed beneath the same lights.
To the wealthy patrons gathered here, the distinction scarcely mattered.
They were accustomed to treating objects, animals, and people as merchandise alike.
Masked dignitaries covered their mouths with fans as they chatted.
“I hear it’s fashionable in the Imperial Capital to collect slaves with unusual appearances.”
“I don’t care about trends like that. If a slave is beautiful, that’s enough for me.”
“I prefer useful ones—good workers or capable fighters.”
“I’m fascinated by tragic histories. Fallen nobles and kidnapped heirs have much more interesting stories.”
“Whatever your taste, let’s hope tonight has plenty worth buying.”
Most of the audience’s attention remained focused on the human captives.
The clown-costumed auctioneer stepped onto the stage and raised his voice.
“Ladies and gentlemen! This ancient sword and shield have sold for thirty million gold! Any higher bids? Sold to the gentleman in the snake mask! Next, look at this magnificent jeweled crown, perhaps once worn by the ruler of some kingdom now lost to history…”
Then—
The crowd’s attention suddenly shifted away from the human captives.
The next lot was not human, but it still seized every eye in the room.
A black buffalo covered in dense, lean muscle.
Huge horns curved from its massive body beneath three glaring crimson eyes.

It possessed nineteen hearts and was said to continue charging until every one of them stopped.
It was an enormous demonic beast of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.
No one fled when it appeared on the stage for one simple reason.
This Murciélago was already dead.
The auctioneer shouted.
“Ladies and gentlemen! This lot was secretly obtained from a Baskerville hunt—an invaluable A-rank demonic beast! Hide, meat, bones, organs—nothing needs to be wasted!”
Nineteen distinct wounds marked the Infernal Buffalo’s body.
Apparently it had died only after all nineteen hearts were destroyed.
The price of the carcass immediately surged upward.
“One hundred million gold!”
“One hundred twenty million!”
“One hundred forty million!”
“One hundred ninety million!”
“Two hundred million!”
“Two hundred fifty million!”
The auction house erupted with excitement even though the lot was not human.
Buoyed by the successful sale, the auctioneer moved on to the main business.
“Now, ladies and gentlemen, it is time for the human and humanoid lots!”
Captives of every kind began to appear on stage.
A barbarian man stared ahead as though he had given up on life; a kidnapped noblewoman cried to be sent home; an ordinary boy trembled without understanding where he was; a fallen noblewoman screamed that death would be better.
“One hundred million!”
“Two hundred million!”
“Two hundred fifty million!”
“Add another seventy million!”
“That woman is mine! Nobody touch her!”
“Talk when you have more money.”
The more the captives cried, begged, and despaired, the more feverish the surrounding bidders became.
Then—
The auctioneer, pleased by the rapid sales, suddenly looked confused.
“…What? This one too? You’re putting this one up? Really? Fine. We’ll try.”
He stammered while arguing with someone backstage.
After a moment, he composed himself.
“Ladies and gentlemen! The next lot is different from the rest—unique, full of spirit, and still with plenty of room to be trained!”
In other words, even the traffickers had not managed to control her.
After doing his best to dress up the problem, the auctioneer had the captive dragged toward center stage.
Unlike the previous captives, this one arrived inside a sturdy iron cage.
“GRAAAH!”
A furious roar burst from inside.
A barbarian girl with an ash-blackened face thrashed violently against the bars.
She looked about seventeen.
Her black hair was streaked with silver, her ears tapered into pointed triangles, and beneath the ash smeared over her face her natural skin seemed light brown.
A thorned collar circled her neck, and the animal hides she wore were torn nearly to rags.
She was lean and powerfully conditioned, her body marked with scars, and her ferocious snarling discouraged even curious bidders.
She shouted something in a language none of the patrons understood.
The smell of animals, dirt, and weeks without proper washing spread across the stage, making several nobles turn away.
“…Who is going to buy this?”
Even the auctioneer muttered to himself.
Still, he had merchandise to move, so he forced himself to advertise.
“Ladies and gentlemen! Traders found this one deep in the jungle! A fierce captive like this offers a real challenge—put her to hard labor, guard duty, or gladiatorial combat and prove your training skill! Opening bid: five million!”
…
No one raised a hand.
Usually someone would call the minimum just to preserve the auction’s momentum, but this time not even that happened.
“Fine! Three million! Does anyone have three million?”
…
“Two million! Two million and she’s yours! I’m telling you, we barely cover costs at that price!”
…
“All right, one million! One million with my eyes closed! At that price you could buy her for labor alone!”
…
Still no one bid.
The auctioneer groaned and waved his hand.
“Forget it. She isn’t selling. I told you to bring guaranteed merchandise. Take the cage backstage and screen the lots better next time.”
The traffickers came onto the stage and began dragging the barbarian girl’s cage away.
She managed to bite off one trader’s finger through the bars, creating another uproar.
The auction hall dissolved into chaos.
The organizers had to regain control somehow.
So what could they do?
The auctioneer decided it was time to reveal the premium lot.
“Ladies and gentlemen! Attention! Keep your eyes on the stage, because what I consider tonight’s finest item is coming out now!”
Then a young man stepped onto the stage.
Heavy chains hung from shackles around his wrists.
He looked about fifteen years old.
Despite the restraints, his stride remained effortless.
His calm, composed bearing drew an involuntary murmur from the audience.
When he reached center stage, torchlight from above fell across his face.
The boy’s features became fully visible.
The entire audience fell silent at once.
“…Wow.”
The expressions of many nobles in the hall changed as they stared at the final lot.
