“Aaaaah!”
Camus hurled fire, ice, lightning, and chunks of earth across the training ground.
Vikir avoided every attack by a narrow margin while steadily drifting toward the boundary of the neighboring arena.
“You bastard! Stop running and fight me properly!”
Camus shouted as she chased him, recovering some confidence now that Vikir was retreating instead of forcing close combat.
The three bumps on her forehead, however, made her look more aggrieved than intimidating.
While dodging, Vikir lifted a hand and tapped his own forehead three times.
That faintly mocking gesture was enough to make Camus lose what remained of her composure.
“I’m not letting you off, even if you beg!”
As Camus made one last furious rush, Vikir stopped retreating.
That was the moment his calculation paid off.
BOOM!
In the neighboring arena, a fifteen-year-old Baskerville and a fifteen-year-old Morgue clashed head-on.
Sword aura and magic burst together, scattering fragments in every direction.
The Morgue teenager unleashed a powerful fire spell that sprayed flame across the nearby grounds.
Vikir and Camus were both caught in the edge of the blast.
BOOM!
An explosion erupted, followed by cries of alarm.
“Camus!”
“Lady Camus!”
“No! It’s—!”
The Morgue spectators watching from the sidelines jumped in shock.
Even so, the accident itself was relatively minor.
The spillover flames from the neighboring arena were not strong enough to pose serious danger.
However—
“Aah!”
Camus looked down and realized that much of the outer fabric of her training clothes had burned away.
She hurriedly pulled off the scorched material before it could keep burning, leaving her in her underclothes.
Smoke, flame, and dust still concealed her from the crowd, but a clearing gust of wind would expose the situation within moments.
Because the magical damage appeared minor, the supervising adults had not yet rushed onto the field.
Only worried voices carried through the smoke.
‘N-no, not like this…!’
For Camus, the possibility of being seen in such a state in front of both families was mortifying.
‘What do I do? What do I do…?’
She was terrified that the humiliation would damage the dignity she had guarded so fiercely.
An earlier childhood punishment in which her mother had driven her outside after she talked back only made the memory of public embarrassment sharper.
‘Someone… anyone, please help.’
Camus crouched low and tried to cover herself as best she could.
She could only imagine the embarrassment waiting once the smoke cleared.
Then—
Flutter!
Fabric suddenly settled around Camus’s body.
It was heavy enough to cover her completely, but soft against her skin.
When she looked up, she found a dark Baskerville training cloak wrapped around her.
“Put that on.”
Vikir stood nearby after giving her his outer garment.
Camus stared at him and stammered.
“…What about you?”
Vikir merely shrugged.
“Children can get away with being half-dressed without losing their honor.”
Adults might attach shame or impropriety to exposed clothing, but Vikir did not apply those standards to small children.
Children sometimes ran around barely dressed; to him it was not a matter worth attaching adult meaning to.
Camus herself might disagree, but that was Vikir’s practical view.
After making sure she was covered, Vikir stood back up.
Then—
Camus pointed at his face.
“Y-you’re bleeding!”
Vikir raised a hand and wiped his forehead.
A fragment scattered by the neighboring clash had apparently grazed him through the smoke.
The injury was minor, but in an eight-year-old friendly match, visible blood was more than enough to justify ending the bout.
Just then—
Whoooosh!
A strong wind swept across the arena and carried away the smoke and dust.
It revealed a dazed Camus wrapped in Baskerville training clothes and Vikir standing nearby with blood on his forehead after surrendering his outer garment.
No one outside the smoke knew exactly what had happened, but everyone could tell that the atmosphere between the two children was no longer combative.
“….”
Camus’s expression in particular made it obvious that the fight was over.
She stared blankly, all of her earlier fury gone.
Meanwhile—
the Baskervilles looked over the young hound who stood completely unbothered by his own state.
“Did he lose to the Morgue girl or win? I can’t even tell.”
“The boy’s body is remarkably sturdy. The River Styx must have favored him.”
“Look at the training on him. He certainly hasn’t been neglecting his regimen.”
The memorable match between the two eight-year-olds ended abruptly when Camus lost the will to continue.
◆◆◆
After the friendly competition ended—
Hugo called Vikir aside near the edge of the training ground.
As they walked toward the main building, Hugo asked a question.
“How was fighting Morgue’s young lady?”
“Interesting. It reminded me why this event exists.”
“Combat against a mage is very different from combat between swordsmen.”
“I’ll continue learning.”
Hugo and Vikir spoke as they walked.
Anyone else in Baskerville who witnessed the exchange would probably have been surprised.
Hugo was not known for casually conversing with his children.
Vikir himself found the moment unfamiliar.
‘Was he always capable of acting like this?’
Vikir’s memories of Hugo from his own childhood were not especially clear.
He remembered a man who was cold, strict, and cruel.
There was a family rumor that Hugo had become especially twisted after losing his first wife and eldest daughter.
‘…That has nothing to do with me now.’
While Vikir set the thought aside, Hugo asked his next question bluntly.
“What do you think should be done with the ruby mine?”
It was the real issue Hugo had been waiting to discuss, continuing the argument from before the competition.
Camus’s position had essentially been that Baskerville could not exploit the ruby vein efficiently, so Morgue should receive the mining rights in exchange for compensation.
Baskerville therefore needed a practical response rather than a purely emotional refusal.
Hugo looked at Vikir with faint expectation, and Vikir answered.
“I think we should give Morgue the mining rights.”
Hugo’s eyes widened slightly.
“Give away the ruby-mining rights?”
“Yes.”
“Why?”
Hugo asked again, and Vikir answered without hesitation.
“The ruby zone on Red Fang Mountain lies downstream from the harsher frontier. Farther up the mountain system are powerful demonic creatures and barbarian territories. I saw enough of that when I crossed into the Forbidden Zone during the practical exam.”
Vikir remembered the old arrow wounds in Cerberus’s side.
Hugo’s expression began to brighten almost imperceptibly.
“Hmm. And?”
“Baskerville intends to keep expanding its frontier through those wild lands. If Morgue commits people to the ruby zone, we can use their position to reduce the cost of securing Baskerville’s new territory.”
A cold light appeared in Hugo’s eyes.
“Ho. That is the answer.”
He understood exactly what Vikir meant.
If Morgue received the mining rights, it would station a substantial workforce and military presence around the downstream ruby zone.
Baskerville could then push farther into the wilderness while driving displaced monsters and hostile barbarians toward Morgue’s occupied mining area.
When Baskerville seized territory from hostile inhabitants, it preferred to eliminate every remaining seed of retaliation.
Cutting the Root.
The family doctrine demanded killing the enemies on the claimed land and hunting down those who escaped before they could regroup.
Only then did Baskerville regard the territory as truly secured.
Otherwise, survivors could eventually gather strength and return for revenge.
But tracking every fleeing enemy across the wilderness was costly and exhausting.
Hugo’s solution was to make Morgue’s ruby operation the direction into which those fleeing enemies would naturally be driven.
“Ha! Morgue wanted red rubies. They can have rubies made redder with blood. Raspane’s people may come to regret entering our frontier.”
Hugo was clearly pleased with Vikir’s answer.
Morgue would receive ruby access; Baskerville would gain room to expand. On paper, the dispute could end amicably.
“Well done. That answer fits my own plan perfectly.”
Hugo placed a hand on Vikir’s head.
Vikir thought to himself.
‘Of course it does. The plan was yours in my first life.’
Before his regression, Vikir himself had been one of the hounds used to drive monsters and barbarians toward Morgue territory.
For now, however, he merely lowered his eyes and answered like an obedient son.
“Even so, I think we should carefully monitor every Morgue force entering our territory.”
“No need to worry. I have already been increasing the number of hounds assigned to that region.”
Hugo’s answer surprised Vikir—not because of the plan, but because Hugo had chosen to tell him about it.
Vikir was startled by how noticeably Hugo’s gaze had softened toward him.
Just as they were about to enter the building—
“My lord.”
Someone called to Hugo from behind.
Adolf Morgue was standing there.
Beside him was Camus, whose eyes were still red from the earlier match.
She clutched the edge of her uncle’s cloak and kept her head lowered, looking unusually subdued.
Hugo narrowed his eyes.
“Acting representative Adolf. Is there still unfinished business?”
“There is one important matter left to discuss.”
“Then come inside and finish it.”
Hugo opened the door and entered the room.
Adolf followed him in and took a seat.
“….”
Camus remained silent.
Before entering, she glanced once at Vikir, then lowered her eyes again and walked past.
She was still wrapped securely in the Baskerville garment Vikir had lent her.
Adolf addressed Hugo.
“Morgue has considered several amicable solutions to the ruby-mine dispute. Territorial rent was only one possibility.”
“Then what else are you proposing as the price of access?”
“What about a marriage alliance?”
Hugo’s brow moved at the unexpected suggestion.
It was not quite a frown.
He leaned forward.
“Marriage?”
“Yes. A marriage between a child of Morgue and a child of Baskerville.”
Simply accepting money for opening family territory could look dishonorable; a marriage alliance would make the arrangement appear like cooperation between houses.
Considering the long rivalry between Morgue and Baskerville, however, the proposal was still awkward enough to sound almost absurd.
Hugo gave a short, disbelieving laugh at an option he had not seriously considered.
“Between whom?”
“Morgue’s eldest daughter and Baskerville’s eldest son.”
Adolf looked toward Camus.
Camus’s head snapped up in surprise.
She clearly had not been warned about this proposal.
“My niece is an exceptional child. Beauty, intelligence, talent—she lacks nothing. I think she would suit Baskerville’s little sun quite well.”
Hugo cared little for the sales pitch.
“My eldest son is twenty this year. That is quite an age difference.”
“Eight and twenty. Twelve years is hardly impossible to overcome.”
“You are forty this year, Adolf. Would you be eager to marry someone twelve years younger who is still a child now?”
“….”
Adolf abruptly found nothing to say.
Hugo waved a hand.
“Besides, my eldest son is already engaged.”
“What? Since when?”
“The arrangement was made before birth. I would not expect you to know.”
Hugo’s answer left Adolf looking as though a headache had begun.
Just then—
Camus suddenly raised her head.
“I won’t marry anyone weaker than my mother!”
Her declaration was completely firm.
Both Adolf and Hugo turned toward her.
Only then did the adults seem to remember that they had not asked the child involved what she wanted.
Adolf smiled gently and patted Camus’s head.
“My dear, if you refuse every man weaker than your mother, you may spend most of your life unmarried—or you’ll need to find someone much older.”
“I care about potential too. And I don’t want someone much older. I prefer someone my age or younger. There were plenty of boys around my age at the competition!”
Adolf could only shrug.
Hugo looked at Camus with mild irritation.
“Child, this is not a buffet.”
“I know. I’m not trying to pick anyone at random.”
Camus lifted her head and looked into the distance.
Vikir was standing there.
Hugo noticed where her attention had gone and slowly rubbed his chin.
“No! Not him!”
Adolf sprang up and covered Camus’s eyes.
“He is Van, not Le! Even if a dragon rises from a ditch, it still came from a ditch!”
Adolf whispered the objection into Camus’s ear, but Hugo’s superhuman senses made secrecy impossible.
“…True or not, that is surprisingly irritating to hear.”
Hugo muttered under his breath.
Then he turned toward Vikir.
“If marriage is being discussed, the wishes of the people involved matter as well. What do you think, my son?”
Vikir answered without hesitation.
“If Father orders it, I will obey.”
It was the answer of an utterly loyal hound.
Hugo laughed with open satisfaction, while Adolf’s expression twisted.
To Vikir, even marriage sounded like another command to be carried out when ordered.
From Adolf’s perspective, that was an outrageous way to discuss Morgue’s precious direct descendant.
Hugo then deliberately prodded Adolf further.
“Baskerville does not judge worth solely by direct or collateral birth. A Le can be useless, and a Van can be exceptional.”
“…Is that really what you say to a representative of a friendly house?”
Adolf looked down at Vikir with a smile.
The smile itself was cold and hard.
“I didn’t get a proper look at you during the match. Would you mind if I examine you now?”
Vikir finally raised his gaze to Adolf.
A Sixth Circle Master—one of the highest-ranking combatants of the Morgue Family.
He was also a senior representative trusted with difficult diplomatic and political affairs.
‘And he is notoriously overprotective of his niece.’
Vikir recalled the man’s reputation from his first life.
Adolf leaned down and whispered near Vikir’s ear.
“Child, this is not really something you need to hear, but becoming Camus’s husband would require a certain standing. The marriage idea is only family talk, and personally I don’t consider you remotely suitable yet.”
A faint pressure accompanied his words.
That political smile sat oddly on the face of a man who had just objected to Hugo pressuring Camus.
But—
“….”
Vikir did not move at all beneath Adolf’s pressure.
Instead, Adolf’s own brow furrowed.
“Why aren’t you answering? Are you listening to me?”
Vikir finally opened his mouth.
“You said it wasn’t for me to hear, so I stopped listening.”
