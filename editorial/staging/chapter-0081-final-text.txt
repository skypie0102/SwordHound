The priests of Quovadis had prayed and worked without rest, and every phase of the final trial had succeeded.
They had distilled an essence of divine power strong enough to cleanse a wide area with a single drop.
Three drops of the extremely concentrated holy water were prepared as a reserve.
Plany de la Verge—Saintess’s Tears.
A single drop was sufficient to purge the plague from the slums.
Dolores recited a prayer of blessing and thanksgiving over the three completed drops.
[Behold, all of you. Here is a mother and her only son. Know the grief of a mother who watches her son suffer; let this sorrow console every holy sacrifice in this world…]
The ancient lament of the first saintess was a prayer for all who endured sacred sacrifice.
The three drops of holy water began to shine.
Archbishop Mozgus bowed and placed the small glass vial on a ceremonial tray.
Dozens of bishops followed behind him as the holy water was carried away.
It was destined for a reinforced underground vault beneath Quovadis’s official residence in St. Mecca.
Priests gathered throughout the main building to watch the procession.
“….”
Dolores felt the tension in the room immediately.
Quovadis itself had been divided between the Old Covenant and the New Covenant, and representatives of both factions were now gathered in one place.
The Red Death was serious enough to force even bitter rivals into the same hall.
A grave-looking middle-aged man turned his attention toward Dolores.
“Come closer, my daughter.”
Dolores hesitated for only a moment before lowering her eyes respectfully.
“…Yes, Father.”
She crossed the hall and stood before him.
Dolores studied his face.
Humbert L. Quovadis.
He was a Cardinal and the de facto highest authority of the Old Covenant beneath only the Pope.
Humbert reached out and lightly stroked Dolores’s head.
“You have rendered great service this time.”
“You flatter me.”
Dolores remained extremely careful around her father.
The stiffness between them was unusual for an ordinary father and daughter, but not especially strange within one of the Empire’s great houses.
Humbert accepted her formal demeanor without comment and nodded.
“Very well. Report what has happened.”
“I prepared a written report. Shall I give it to you now?”
“Say it aloud. Writing is too stiff.”
Dolores nodded.
“We will use the first batch of Saintess’s Tears to cleanse the waterways beneath St. Mecca.”
“Have you identified the source?”
“Yes. An unregistered well above the slums.”
Dolores had always possessed an investigator’s mind.
During her first year at the Academy, she had even joined its detective club.
She focused on testimony from slum residents, especially the children who had seen a frightening figure near one particular well.
When several children insisted that a ghost had cursed the well, Dolores investigated the site herself.
The water was heavily contaminated, and a discarded glass container suggested that someone had deliberately introduced the plague.
There was more.
“We also recovered suspicious evidence near the well.”
Dolores paused there.
At Humbert’s questioning look, she unwrapped the objects she had brought with her.
His eyes widened.
“What is this…?”
A dagger.
A large serpent was engraved into it—the unmistakable emblem of House Leviathan.
A second object rested beside it.
It was a gold button marked with the scythe-and-hammer emblem associated with House Bourgeois.
“Where did you find these?”
“They were lying in separate places near the well.”
Humbert’s eyes moved rapidly.
He was already assembling a chain of cause and effect in his mind.
“You said the plague itself appeared artificially designed.”
Creating something this virulent would require both exceptional knowledge of poisons and enormous research funding.
BANG!
Humbert stamped one foot.
The marble floor cracked, and nearby stone columns trembled.
“I declare a crusade! How dare those vipers and money-grubbers profane us like this!”
Quovadis was powerful enough that even the Emperor treated its will carefully; Humbert took the apparent provocation as an intolerable insult.
Tension among the Seven Great Families had always existed beneath the surface.
“….”
Dolores could only stare.
Then Humbert said something that made her stiffen.
“You called him the Hound of the Night?”
His handsome face hardened.
“He is suspicious as well. Detain him and place him before the Inquisition. We will make him explain everything he knows.”
Dolores visibly panicked.
Her usual composure disappeared.
For a moment she looked simply like a sixteen-year-old girl terrified by the order.
“He isn’t guilty!”
“…?”
Her sudden protest made Humbert’s expression sharpen.
“…Him?”
He stared down at his daughter in disbelief.
“Daughter, what are you saying?”
Dolores had always obeyed him without open complaint.
Except where the New Covenant was concerned, she had almost never opposed her father’s wishes.
But now—
“….”
Dolores met his gaze with trembling fists.
She was clearly determined to defend the Night Hound.
“A man who worked that hard for the poor cannot simply be evil!”
Dolores had heard testimony from many patients.
They spoke of a masked man who stayed awake through the night treating them while repeatedly apologizing.
They spoke of a man who used a little holy power of his own and gave away everything he carried to those in need.
‘Why did he keep apologizing to the patients?’
Perhaps, Dolores thought, he was sorry he could not cure them sooner or more completely.
She understood that feeling because she, too, often felt sorry before people who were suffering even when others told her she had nothing to apologize for.
The feeling never disappeared simply because gratitude would have been more logical.
The Night Hound had seemed to understand that instinctively.
She remembered a conversation with him from not long before.
‘I was fortunate enough to be born with divine power, yet I am weak in body, mind, and faith. Someone stronger should have received this gift.’
Dolores had often wondered why the gods had chosen her when so many people possessed deeper faith but no divine power.
The Night Hound had turned toward her while she struggled with that question.
‘The fact that you suffer over it may be precisely why you were chosen. Perhaps the love of the gods is brightest in those who descend into the lowest and harshest places.’
Dolores had breathed out softly.
The answer had sounded like the words of a veteran saint who had already survived every rise and fall of religious life.
It struck her like advice from her future self.
Almost like hearing a wise saint from an older age—or an older sister she could trust.
That was how the Night Hound sometimes felt to her.
‘When I am older, will I be able to say something like that?’
‘…Perhaps you will.’
His answer had carried a strange confidence in her future.
Dolores had treasured that confidence.
That memory was part of why she now defended the Hound of the Night so fiercely before Cardinal Humbert.
But—
“….”
The more forcefully Dolores defended him, the colder Humbert’s expression became.
Humbert gestured toward several bishops.
“Prepare an immediate arrest. We will question him ourselves.”
Dolores’s objections changed nothing.
Humbert strode toward the room assigned to the Night Hound, his mood more dangerous than before.
Dolores hurried after him.
If necessary, she was prepared to physically stand between them.
But—
“!?”
Everyone stopped outside the room.
Whirrr.
The room was empty, and curtains billowed through a broken window like pale ghosts.
The Hound of the Night was already gone.
Then an urgent shout rose from downstairs.
“Trouble! One drop of Saintess’s Tears is missing!”
For an instant—
“…Ah.”
Dolores stood speechless.
She finally understood, at least vaguely, why he had come to St. Mecca.
And she felt an equally vague certainty that, now that he had achieved his purpose, she might never see him again.
While Humbert and the paladins organized a pursuit, Dolores stared through the broken window into the darkness.
The empty room, cold wind, and fluttering curtains made his departure feel suddenly real.
Thump.
Her heart sank once again.
This beat felt different from the one before.
