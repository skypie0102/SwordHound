“That’s it.”
Vikir closed the book.
He had memorized every word in it.
He had committed the text to memory so thoroughly that he believed he could recite it with his eyes closed—or even backward.
The results appeared almost immediately.
Swish—!
Vikir raised his hand like a blade and traced the sword trajectories he had learned from the scripture.
The First Fang appeared cleanly.
Then came the Second, followed by the Third.
Up to that point, the movements were well within what his eight-year-old body could reproduce.
Snap—!
Then the Fourth Fang emerged, the Baskerville Fourth Form he had relied on throughout his first life.
But.
Pop—!
Vikir’s hand continued past the Fourth Form and carved one more sharp trajectory through the air.
The Fifth Fang. It was smaller and blunter than the others, but unmistakably there.
The Baskerville Fifth Form.
Unsteady though it was, Vikir had recreated it with his own hands.
“…Unbelievable.”
Vikir repeated the movement several times.
Every attempt succeeded.
His child-sized hands and short arms prevented the trajectory from being perfect, but the shape was undeniably real.
The Fifth Fang had begun to take form.
“…Is there a sword around here?”
If he had a real blade, he would have tested the aura immediately.
But there was hardly going to be a sword lying around inside the library.
Nor could he casually draw Beelzebub from the artery in his wrist merely for an experiment.
Still.
He was so happy that tears almost rose to his eyes.
A hot feeling surged up from his chest and caught in his throat.
Vikir forced himself to calm the overwhelming emotion.
“As long as my mana can support it, I can definitely recover—and surpass—the level I had in my previous life.”
In swordsmanship alone, he had already obtained something stronger and more refined than what he once possessed.
His path no longer stopped at the Fifth Fang. It now extended all the way toward the Tenth.
Vikir closed his eyes and examined the mana inside his body.
The mana pool enlarged by the River Styx continued to accumulate power little by little.
He could not measure his exact cultivation with certainty, but this breakthrough had clearly pushed him across another threshold.
“…Graduator.”
Vikir had crossed from Sword Expert into the realm of Sword Graduator.
A Graduator could produce aura dense enough to take on a liquid, materialized quality rather than the gaseous aura of a Sword Expert.
That denser aura could envelop the entire blade and be shaped with far greater freedom.
When the liquid-like aura blade of a Graduator met the gaseous aura of a Sword Expert, the difference was as decisive as water meeting vapor.
Sword Graduators possessed physical and martial ability that surpassed ordinary human limits.
Their numbers were a measure of a family’s prestige and, by extension, a meaningful part of the Empire’s military strength.
In his first life, Vikir had not reached this realm until around the age of thirty.
Now, after regression, he was only eight.
The difference in growth was enormous.
It was a realm many swordsmen could only dream of reaching in an entire lifetime.
By the rough sword-and-magic equivalence, it corresponded to approximately a Fourth-Circle mage.
In Baskerville’s long history, there could not have been many—if any—who reached such a level at his age.
“…And the character of my swordsmanship has changed completely.”
The swordsmanship Vikir had used in his first life was the Stabbing Fang style commonly taught to Baskerville hounds.
Stabbing Fang. Four fangs.
With those four fang-like thrusts, Vikir had pierced countless defenses and driven through enemy hearts like an awl.
But Stabbing Fang was a dangerous style.
It could make a hound useful in battle very quickly, but its limits of growth were equally clear.
In exchange for reliable killing power, the user was forced to accept tremendous personal risk.
It was exactly the kind of swordsmanship Baskerville would teach to hounds expected to be used as expendable pieces.
The swordsmanship encoded in Lurking Embedded Teeth was different.
It concealed its power and aggression, moving slowly and quietly until the instant it chose to bite.
Its sword lines could appear calm, graceful, even dignified.
Beneath that appearance, however, lay a malicious intent to inflict the greatest possible pain on the enemy.
Once the first bite landed, it flowed into an unrelenting chain of brutal attacks that could make the victim wish for death.
It was a frenzied offensive art that still protected its user—a vicious union of attack, defense, concealment, and pain.
“Perfect for hiding my strength.”
Vikir muttered quietly.
At a glance, the movements produced by the scripture still resembled familiar Baskerville fang techniques.
That meant concealing his true swordsmanship should not be especially difficult.
After all, the entire art was built around lurking unseen until the moment of attack.
Vikir glanced around and confirmed that no one was watching.
His heart still pounded with the exhilaration of reaching a higher realm.
Buzz—
The sound of a fly’s wings brushed his ears.
Vikir looked down and saw the black awl-tip of Beelzebub protruding from his wrist.
“Get back in there.”
Vikir scolded the weapon, refusing to let it expose itself whenever it pleased.
At the same time, he calmly calculated how much Beelzebub changed his current combat strength.
Vikir van Baskerville.
Eight years old.
He could reliably produce four fangs, and his aura had materialized enough to place him at the beginning of the Sword Graduator realm.
Add decades of combat experience, an unstable but real Fifth Fang, and Beelzebub’s ability to absorb demonic-creature skills, and his actual fighting power rose far above what his apparent age suggested.
‘If I truly commit to it, I should be able to fight an Intermediate Sword Graduator.’
‘An assassination would be effectively certain. In a head-on fight, perhaps fifty-fifty.’
“…The real question is how much of my strength I should reveal.”
Who would believe that an eight-year-old had reached the realm of Sword Graduator?
Showing everything would be foolish.
In this world, it was wise to keep roughly thirty percent of one’s power hidden.
“….”
Vikir paused and considered how an eight-year-old child ought to behave.
It did not take long to find the answer.
He needed to raise Hugo’s expectations just enough without making the enemies around him overly wary.
There was no reason to conceal so much strength that he remained trapped and suffocated under direct supervision.
On the contrary, displaying the right amount of talent could create an opportunity to slip out from under Hugo’s eyes.
“A provincial posting, for example. Or admission to the Academy.”
As soon as Vikir left the library, he would have to report the results of his study to Hugo.
He had already calculated exactly what level of ability he would pretend to demonstrate when tested.
And now.
Vikir took a reading magnifier from his pocket and left it by the window at the proper angle.
Sssss—
Under the focused light, the cover of Lurking Embedded Teeth gradually blackened.
Fwoosh—!
Flame rose from the book.
Vikir silently watched the sword scripture burn.
With that, the physical book containing Baskerville’s path toward the Tenth Form disappeared from the world.
It survived only inside Vikir’s memory.
From a distance came the alarmed voices of servants.
“What is that smell?!”
“Sniff, sniff—over by the miscellaneous shelves!”
“There isn’t even preservation magic in that section! Hurry!”
The servants rushed over carrying buckets of water and found Vikir wearing an apologetic expression.
“I’m sorry. I left my magnifying glass carelessly. If this is something that really must be reported, I’ll explain it properly to the butler.”
The servants immediately waved their hands as if to say it was nothing.
“Young master, there’s no need for you to apologize.”
“It was our fault for not watching the area properly.”
“At least it happened among the miscellaneous books. That’s fortunate, really.”
The servants seemed especially attentive to Vikir’s phrase, ‘if it really must be reported.’
Vikir looked down at the ashes scattered across the floor.
“It was only a worthless miscellaneous book. If everyone simply treats it as though nothing happened, there should be no problem. Besides, you all know the butler is overwhelmed with the ruby-mine dispute involving the Morgue family. Who has time for something this trivial?”
The servants nodded vigorously.
“Ah! Yes, exactly.”
“We servants only want our masters to remain comfortable and untroubled.”
“If the young master is willing to overlook it, we would be very grateful.”
They were understandably relieved to escape an annoying report.
“I see. You already have enough work cleaning the library, so I’m sorry for causing more trouble. I remember the contents of the burned book, so I’ll make sure an appropriate transcription is prepared to replace it.”
Having said what he needed to say, Vikir turned away.
“What a thoughtful young master. We should tell the others how considerate he was.”
“Hey! If you tell the story, you’re supposed to mention that a book burned too!”
“Just praise him. Say he was kind and impressive!”
As his realm advanced, Vikir’s hearing had become sharper as well.
He could hear the servants whispering behind him, but he paid them little attention.
He needed to rehearse how he would respond when Hugo summoned him after he left the library.
