The seven young heirs gathered in the VVIP room were clever, ruthless, and accustomed to getting their way.
They represented the houses of Montblanc, Pierre, Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Ferragamo, Hermès, and Prada.
The seven provincial families had lived for generations without needing to read anyone’s mood except Baskerville’s.
Even Baskerville officials had often accepted their money, public influence, and friendship.
Life had almost never gone against them, so they assumed this incident would end the same way.
They admitted they had played a slightly cruel prank this time.
…But—
This time was different.
Crash—crash—crash—!
A rain of glass shards and champagne spray poured through the VVIP room.
Broken glass and bubbling liquor glittered under the chandeliers.
The seven heirs stood drenched beneath the sharp, hot shower with blank expressions.
‘Did he destroy the champagne tower through mana resonance?’
‘Just by touching the table?’
‘And from the top down instead of the bottom up?’
Every one of them had received at least basic martial instruction, so they understood how absurd Vikir’s feat was.
Injecting mana into an object, forcing resonance, and making it rupture at a chosen point required far more than ordinary control.
More importantly, the upper glasses had shattered before the lower ones.
Breaking a glass at the top while leaving the glasses touching the table intact required extraordinary finesse.
The fact that the force traveled vertically rather than simply spreading horizontally made the feat harder still.
It meant manipulating threads of mana finer than hair as though they were extensions of one’s own limbs.
This was mana control in the realm of genuine masters.
All seven reached the same conclusion.
‘Sword Graduator!’
Graduators were monsters who could split boulders with sword aura and bring down flying creatures through force alone.
To heirs who had barely reached the Sword Expert realm, such a person was almost untouchable.
“….”
Vikir’s face remained expressionless.
The dark crimson aura moving around his shoulders felt like the presence of a grim reaper.
If death possessed a visible form, it might have looked like this.
Soaked in champagne, the seven heirs began trembling.
‘He’s insane—and they said he was only fifteen!’
‘You’re Intermediate Sword Expert! Do something!’
‘Shut up! You wouldn’t last a second!’
As frantic glances passed among them, one heir suddenly muttered.
“…Wait. Vikir. I’ve heard that name before!”
Everyone looked toward him.
He stammered as the memory returned.
“Vikir Van Baskerville—the supernova of House Baskerville! Seven minutes under the River Styx as an infant, strangled two venomous snakes in the Cradle, hunted Cerberus at eight, and defeated Morgue’s young magical prodigy in a duel…”
The others stared as though he were reciting an absurd urban legend.
But what if those stories were true?
Their eyes returned to Vikir.
A liquid-like aura was the symbol of the Sword Graduator realm.
If the stories were false, then the overwhelming deathly pressure surrounding this fifteen-year-old became even harder to explain.
How could anyone reach Sword Graduator at fifteen?
Even in Baskerville, a house notorious for geniuses, such a thing was unprecedented.
They doubted the Imperial Academy—or even the Imperial Capital—could produce an easy comparison.
In the end, the heirs could only force awkward smiles.
“…We’ll pay for the drinks.”
The flattering smiles they usually forced onto other people were now stretched across their own faces.
Vikir answered without changing expression.
“Of course. You should pay for the alcohol you consumed.”
The seven immediately shut their mouths.
‘Right. He hasn’t actually drunk anything with us yet.’
They had accidentally exposed the plan to saddle him with the bill.
Because they had not actually carried out the trap yet, the heirs thought they might still invent an excuse and laugh it off.
…If the person facing them had not been Vikir.
He was fifteen in body, but his soul had already been worn down by a lifetime of hardship.
Vikir saw through what the seven had intended almost immediately.
“So you planned to entertain me and then dump the cost of your drinking on me?”
The seven heirs flinched and shook their heads desperately.
“N-no! Of course not!”
“Who would do something that shameless?”
“How could we possibly treat someone from Baskerville that way…?”
The seven understood that their opponent was not merely powerful.
His judgment and experience were just as dangerous.
In the end, they lowered their tails.
They had been arrogant enough to call Baskerville their hound, but before Vikir they no longer dared repeat the thought.
A Baskerville hound, it seemed, could not be so easily leashed.
Years of pride as heirs of powerful local families still made them try to salvage some dignity.
“Actually, Deputy Magistrate, we only wanted to test you.”
The seven composed themselves and adopted polite expressions.
Vikir said nothing.
Taking his silence as permission to continue, they relaxed slightly and began explaining themselves.
“Honestly, Underdog City is deeply corrupt.”
“If you intend to purify a place like this, you must be able to resist temptations of this level.”
“You have proven yourself worthy to lead us. We will follow you.”
“Your noble spirit will be an example to us for the rest of our lives.”
“We will repay the lesson you gave us today in a way that will not disappoint you.”
They bowed solemnly.
A few even winked, trying to restore a casual atmosphere.
Then—
“….”
One corner of Vikir’s mouth slowly rose.
A smile.
That tiny change transformed the entire room.
A moment earlier, the atmosphere had merely felt like a graveyard.
That alone had terrified the seven heirs.
But now?
The pressure became almost maddening.
A suffocating weight pressed them down as though they were being buried alive.
Their breathing became shallow; every forced smile vanished.
Vikir walked slowly toward the seven heirs, who had shrunk back like frogs before a snake.
“A master may test his hound whenever he wishes.”
“….”
“But the reverse is never permitted. A hound does not test its master.”
The words echoed an old lesson Hugo had once taught.
Vikir paced before the frozen heirs.
“You have money and power, so you think there is nothing to fear?”
No one answered.
Vikir continued.
“You have simply never seen anything truly frightening.”
Their arrogance, calm, and practiced condescension existed only because reality had never punished them for it.
The price of that performance was about to become heavy.
Vikir pulled a thin wooden stake from his clothes.
It was the same kind of stake that had earned Judy ten billion gold days earlier.
THWACK!
A dull impact rang out.
The stake flexed and snapped forward, smashing the face of the nearest heir into a bloody mess.
The first blow came so suddenly no one could react.
Teeth, saliva, blood, and tears scattered.
In the span of three blinks, Vikir struck the man seven times across the face, head, neck, and shoulders, then moved on to the others.
Moments later, all seven were sprawled across the VVIP-room floor.
Thwack! Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud!
Vikir continued beating them for a long time.
“Do you know why you’re being beaten?”
“N-no! I don’t! Why are you suddenly doing this?!”
“If you don’t know, then you still need to be beaten.”
Vikir swung the stake with the same calm tone.
Looking at his expressionless face, the heirs began to believe they might genuinely die.
“I-I know! I think I know why you’re hitting us!”
The seven begged through split lips and broken teeth.
Blood ran freely from battered faces and mouths.
Vikir remained unimpressed.
“If you already know, then accept the punishment.”
The stake did not stop moving.
