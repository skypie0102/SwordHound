Vikir thought.
‘?’
What exactly was happening?
For the first time since his regression, something had happened that he had never expected.
The infamous Baskerville triplets who would later be called Hugo’s Trident had begun acting friendly toward Vikir.
Before the regression, those three had driven Vikir across the line between life and death several times, and ultimately helped cause his capture and execution.
…But now?
Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro stood around Vikir in a circle.
In the past they had surrounded him to harass him; now they were surrounding him to protect him.
“He is the face of our line. We can’t let just anyone pick a fight with him. Know your place.”
“Protect him.”
“Protect him.”
Lowbro even turned and winked at Vikir.
Vikir was even more baffled.
‘Did breaking his finger really make him this obedient?’
Then again, this was not the first time the triplets had acted strangely toward him.
‘They have certainly become much more civilized since that public troll hunt.’
Perhaps it had started even earlier—after Vikir killed Cerberus during the first practical examination at eight.
Something had already felt wrong when they approached him while he was eating haggis and pretended to be friendly.
Vikir had always ignored siblings of his own generation.
They were too weak and immature to deserve his attention.
But that indifference seemed to have caused some mutation in the triplets’ minds.
After all, House Baskerville respected strength above almost everything else.
More importantly, the three appeared to be laboring under a peculiar misunderstanding.
“Leave this to us, Vikir. We’ll repay you for recommending us for the Academy.”
“Leave it to us, Vikir. We’ll repay you.”
“Leave it to us, Vikir. We’ll make it up to you.”
The triplets spoke with fierce determination.
Vikir remembered his recent conversation with Hugo.
He had mentioned the three casually when Hugo asked which brothers he was close to, and apparently the recommendation had moved them deeply.
Vikir decided not to correct the misunderstanding.
“….”
Children who believed they were doing something magnificent could be oddly amusing.
(Even if these particular children had terrible personalities, that was at least one endearing trait.)
Vikir considered them for a moment.
‘They might be useful.’
These three would unquestionably grow into very useful killing machines.
Their judgment was poor and independent thinking was not their strength, but they were exceptionally good at carrying out orders.
They had handled countless covert and dirty assignments in the old timeline, proving both their skill and their loyalty.
Hounds in the truest sense—ferociously obedient to their master.
Vikir knew that better than most, having once been bitten by those same teeth.
At last, Vikir yielded.
“I’ll give you a chance, brothers.”
The triplets’ faces immediately brightened.
They stood before Vikir looking happier than when Hugo praised them during the monthly evaluations.
‘If they’re this determined, there’s no reason to stop them.’
Vikir decided to remain in the background for the moment.
Then,
the three sisters of Morgue and the three brothers of Baskerville faced one another.
The two sets of triplets had met repeatedly at annual events and were notorious for getting along badly.
The Morgue sisters attacked first.
“You idiots only know how to bunch together in threes.”
Lowsis was the first to place her hands against the ground.
A specialist in powerful water magic, she formed countless droplets and fired them forward.
Pff! Pff! Pff!
The high-speed water carried tremendous force.
Ordinary falling water might wear through stone given enough time, but Lowsis’s mana-filled droplets could punch through rock in an instant.
The ground was riddled with holes.
Lowsis’s barrage also saturated the soil with water.
Midsis moved next.
A specialist in earth magic, she swept her hands and gathered the soaked soil together.
Rumble, rumble.
At Midsis’s command, the wet, malleable earth rose into a wall around the three sisters.
Sharp stones protruded from its surface like blades, making the barrier both defensive and offensive.
Finally Highsis, the eldest, stepped forward.
A specialist in plant magic, she flicked her hands and awakened seeds beneath the ground.
Thick stalks and vines surged upward, fed by Lowsis’s water and rooted in Midsis’s soil.
Earth, water, and plant life reinforced one another, strengthening Morgue’s fortification while pressing in on their opponents.
It was easy to understand why they were called the Three Flowers of Morgue.
But Baskerville’s three brothers were not helpless.
“Heh heh. What are you weeds going to do without any meat?”
“What are you going to do?”
“What are you going to do?”
Highbro charged first.
A pale gaseous aura formed a single sharp fang and slammed into the earthen wall.
Then—
Crack! Boom!
The wall split, exposing the inside.
It was only first-circle magic, so the barrier’s defensive power had obvious limits.
Middlebro and Lowbro followed with their blades.
Their rapidly rotating aura attacks vaporized incoming water droplets and shredded the growing vines.
Clang! Crack! Boom!
Sword and magic collided violently.
Magic drew in mana to reproduce the power of the elements, while swordsmanship burned mana and life force within the body into explosive force.
Which was stronger, magic or the sword?
It was an old argument between neighboring Morgue and Baskerville.
Today, the three sisters and three brothers stood at the front of that debate.
All six fought as representatives of their family banners.
“….”
Vikir was bored to death.
What tension could there be in a battle between three talented young mages limited to first-circle magic and three junior Sword Experts who had only recently mastered Baskerville’s First Form?
Besides, Vikir felt no meaningful attachment or brotherhood toward Baskerville.
‘I wish they would all just die.’
It was an exceedingly cynical attitude.
The other Baskerville knights did not share it.
Even without saying so, they were clearly enjoying the contest.
Staffordshire looked toward Vikir’s sullen face.
“Aren’t you enjoying this?”
“What is there to enjoy?”
“Isn’t this a contest of talent and potential among young people who may someday carry their families?”
“By that definition, I’m young too.”
“Master, you are already… well beyond the level anyone in that age group should be compared with.”
“This is a waste of time. We need to get through quickly. I have matters to discuss with the fortress commander—”
Vikir never finished the sentence.
BOOM!
A tremendous explosion tore through the area.
“Aaaagh!”
“Ugh!”
All three Morgue sisters and all three Baskerville brothers froze in shock.
Flames roared up, swallowing everything nearby.
Sharp iron spikes erupted from the ground, turning the field into a forest of thorns.
Crash! Crack!
The earth wall studded with stone blades collapsed at once.
The fierce heat boiled away the water and burned the plants while the shockwave knocked aside the Baskerville brothers’ swords and sent them flying.
A boundary of fire and iron—red and black—split Morgue from Baskerville in an instant.
The spell was powerful enough that even Baskerville knights who had been watching the children’s fight for amusement half-drew their swords.
Crackle, crackle, roar!
In a heartbeat, the surrounding cotton fields became an inferno.
The Baskerville triplets barely escaped beyond the wall of flame.
Even so, their hair had been scorched black.
“What the hell! You almost burned us alive!”
“What?!”
“What?!”
The brothers looked up, ready to protest Morgue’s excessive response.
The Morgue sisters on the opposite side had fared even worse.
They had been scorched by flames and scraped and torn by iron spikes.
Highsis, Midsis, and Lowsis crawled on the ground with naked fear in their eyes.
“H-hah… I almost died just now….”
“If I’d dodged any later, I really would have died….”
“Sisters… I’m scared….”
Seeing their condition, the Baskerville triplets immediately swallowed their complaints.
Whoever had intervened was merciless even toward members of Morgue.
Every head turned toward the source of the fire magic.
A calm voice came from beyond the wall of flame.
“…What is going on here?”
Cold, but somehow still youthful.
The caster who commanded both iron spikes and searing flame stepped into view.
She was the girl who would one day be known as the Ironblood Empress.
Within Morgue, her fire-and-iron magic would earn her the names Queen of Fire and Skewers and Queen of Red and Black.
‘…But right now, she is still young.’
Vikir raised his eyes.
He remembered an eight-year-old girl.
Now she had grown into a fifteen-year-old.
Camus Morgue.
After seven years apart, she looked down at Vikir.
