Practical tests were common in Baskerville, sometimes held several times in a single day.
Most bouts pitted siblings against one another so they could learn each other’s strengths and weaknesses, and a bell usually signaled the start.
Ding—!
The young hounds of Baskerville, tense in the unfamiliar environment, snapped back to attention the instant they heard that familiar sound.
Then, just as they had been taught, their fighting instincts took over.
One of the nine-year-olds sprinted across the wasteland.
The year before, he had gained a considerable advantage by seizing favorable terrain before anyone else.
But although he was clever enough to remember last year’s advantage, he was not clever enough to realize how much attention his rush would attract.
Several quick-witted eight-year-olds immediately chased after him.
They had no idea what he was after, but they intended to take it from him once he found it.
Still sharper children sometimes pretended to know exactly where they were going in order to draw their rivals after them.
Then they would lure those bothersome flies into dangerous pits or swamps and dispose of them all at once.
Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro were precisely that sort of cunning.
“Take that side first.”
“Don’t order me around.”
“We know what we’re doing. You worry about yourself.”
Those three nine-year-olds had already shown considerable talent. The previous year, when they were only eight, they had used similar tricks to eliminate several older children.
Within hours of the bell, defeated children began appearing one after another.
Some were knocked unconscious, some tumbled down steep slopes, some sank into swamps, and others were stabbed or cut.
The details varied, but the underlying cause was always the same.
They had been brought down by their own siblings.
Clang—!
The clash of metal rang throughout the forest.
As soon as the test began, Baskerville children drew the blunted short swords they had been issued and turned them on one another.
The blades were dulled so they would not easily inflict fatal wounds.
Killing another participant was not encouraged in this examination.
It was not strictly forbidden, but it was strongly discouraged.
Killing someone carried a heavy point penalty, so the children avoided directly taking another participant’s life whenever possible.
Of course, that was another matter if someone was strong enough to absorb the penalty—or clever enough to eliminate an opponent without the Guide Hounds noticing.
Even with Guardian Knights serving as Guide Hounds and keeping watch around the clock, unexplained deaths and accidents were common during the practical test. Some children undoubtedly used that fact to dispose of rivals or people they hated.
…But.
Vikir was nowhere to be found in this vicious competition.
From the beginning, he had shown no interest whatsoever in scoring points.
The moment the bell rang, he slipped away from the crowd and headed for a barren patch near the boundary adjoining the Forbidden Zone.
‘…Was it here?’
Vikir searched his memories from both his examinee days and his later years as an instructor.
Following this route led to a stretch of wasteland lying just inside the secured boundary.
An old dead tree stood there. Its roots had rotted deep underground, leaving the surrounding soil unusually soft.
Before long, Vikir found the spot he remembered.
The soil there was noticeably redder than anywhere around it.
He snapped off a branch and began digging.
While the other children fought and competed for points in the distance, Vikir silently kept digging.
Hidden from sight, the Guide Hounds assigned to the test continued observing him.
Yet even after several days, Vikir was still doing nothing but digging the same pit.
‘Looks like he plans to hide here and waste the month.’
‘A little disappointing, isn’t it?’
‘No point watching him any longer.’
Vikir had shown extraordinary talent since childhood, so the Guardian Knights had expected a great deal from him. What they saw now left them deeply disappointed.
Hiding in a pit and waiting out the test was typical behavior for cowards and low-ranked children without confidence in their abilities.
Eventually, the increasingly bored Guardian Knights began turning their attention away from Vikir one by one.
…But.
The Guardian Knights had been so focused on the struggles among the children that they failed to notice something else.
They never considered how deep an eight-year-old had managed to dig—or what he was actually doing inside the pit.
◆◆◆
“This place still feels like a cradle.”
Vikir.
Vikir sat at the bottom of the pit, completely at ease.
Coming back here felt unexpectedly comfortable and warm.
Guardian Knights watched the area from outside, and none of the other competitors dared venture this far out of the way.
When he was thirsty, he collected dew from the broad leaves hanging over the pit wall. When he was hungry, he caught passing snakes or earthworms and roasted them.
It tasted far better than the haggis and preserved food supplied at Fang Castle.
Even the bed he had made for himself was more comfortable.
The children’s beds at Fang Castle were made from demonic-creature hides and thorny materials, leaving them hard and rough.
Here, however, the pit was lined with dry grass, wood shavings, and ash from burned trees, making it warm and cozy.
Vikir closed his eyes for a moment as he roasted a rat he had caught the night before.

Even information about the lowest-ranked demonic creatures remained perfectly clear in Vikir’s memory.
That was thanks to the monster encyclopedia he had desperately memorized before his regression.
Of course, before Vikir regressed, he had hardly been the only person to memorize entire monster encyclopedias.
In the Age of Destruction, everyone had been a hunter.
‘…But now I’m the only hunter left.’
Perhaps his current position was closer to that of a hound raised by a hunter.
Yet that only made the hunter’s fate seem more tragic.
“What could be more ironic than a hunter being killed by his own hound?”
The thought suddenly reminded him of a conversation he had once had with Hugo, shortly before his execution.
It had been not long before Vikir was sent to the guillotine.
‘When you train a hound, you have to accept that it may die. Usually two out of three do. Do you think that’s cruel?’
‘No.’
‘Of course not. The master who trains a hound must also be prepared to die from its bite. A master must keep himself trained as well—you never know when a vicious hound’s fangs will turn on you.’
Hugo himself had understood that truth.
Perhaps that was why Hugo trusted no one in the world—not even himself.
“…”
Vikir shook his head and cleared the thoughts circling through his mind.
Then he took a single bean from the small pouch at his waist.
A Bloody Bean—a dark-red cacao bean with an extraordinarily high cacao concentration.
Vikir tossed one into the campfire.
The gamey smell and other unpleasant odors faded from the fatty rat meat, replaced by a faint cocoa aroma.
‘Cacao beans are excellent for removing gamey odors—especially highly concentrated Bloody Beans.’
It was a cooking trick he had learned from a veteran soldier in his platoon after being drafted into the war against the demons.
“…Camping always brings back the past.”
Vikir looked around.
Every gaze that had been watching him was gone.
The Guide Hounds had apparently decided his behavior was uninteresting and moved elsewhere.
…Which meant the time had finally come.
Vikir reached toward the pile of firewood.
He drew a short knife from his waist and began carving the wood.
Wooden stakes.
Vikir planted the sharpened stakes upright across the bottom of the pit, their points aimed toward the sky.
Dozens upon dozens crowded the pit floor.
Together they resembled the lower jaw of a savage beast, its fangs bared.
Even Vikir had not yet decided exactly what those fangs would bite.
Beyond the boundary, inside the Forbidden Zone of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, lived demonic creatures of a size and strength beyond what people of this era could imagine.
…But most of them were familiar enough to Vikir, who had survived the Age of Destruction.
“I’ve used up all the wood.”
Vikir realized that every piece he had cut earlier was gone.
There was nothing left to carve into more stakes or throw onto the fire.
He climbed out of the pit to gather more.
Now that even the Guardian Knights’ eyes had disappeared, he could cross the boundary into the Forbidden Zone to cut timber.
Before long, Vikir quietly slipped across the border and entered the forbidden territory.
◆◆◆
The blackened forest was unnaturally dense.
Trunks and roots twisted around one another until the whole forest became a vast, tangled maze.
Dark caves opened between roots scorched by old forest fires, looking almost like passages leading down into hell.
Any ordinary young Baskerville hound would have instinctively avoided such a gloomy, ominous place. Vikir, however, deliberately searched deeper among the caves.
‘Let’s see… this should be their habitat.’
Vikir walked through the forest for a long time.
He had crossed these woods countless times as an instructor and scout in his previous life, so he knew them almost as well as his own home.
Soon.
Vikir found what he was looking for.
Sizzle…
It was the sound of embers burning beneath ash.
Following the smell of something scorched, he quickly reached the source.
A pile of dung.
Unextinguished flames still licked at the black heap of excrement.
As far as Vikir knew, only one demonic creature produced dung hot enough to keep burning like that.
He raised his head and followed the root-lined caves deeper into the forest until he found the creature responsible.
A massive body, sharp teeth and claws, and eyes that glowed like lamps.

The sulfurous fire pouring from its eyes and mouth came from the depths of a burning oil hell, and would not go out until the life serving as its fuel had been consumed completely.
Even the monster encyclopedia contained little information about such a dangerous creature.
Vikir, however, had fought many monsters of this kind during the Age of Destruction.
“Finally found you.”
Finding these creatures was the reason he had come this far into the mountain.
Vikir immediately turned and ran.
The instant the Hellhound spotted him, it charged like a rabid dog.
Woof! Woof! Woof!
The Hellhound’s roar thundered directly behind him.
At the last instant, Vikir threw himself sideways into a nearby cave passage.
Whoosh—!
Unable to stop its momentum, the Hellhound staggered several steps past him.
“Just as I thought. An idiot that only knows how to charge straight ahead.”
Most creatures that had crawled up from hell were poor at anything except direct, linear movement.
Even a high-ranking demonic creature like a Hellhound looked clumsy when forced into an abrupt change of direction.
Grrrr…
The Hellhound wheeled around and resumed the chase.
Vikir prepared the next pattern.
Splash—!
The drinking water he had collected from two days’ worth of dew scattered across the ground, forming a long narrow line.
…Flinch!
The Hellhound slammed to a halt.
Strangely, Hellhounds would not cross water.
Not even a film of water that was shallow and thin.
Because the beast knew little beyond charging in straight lines, the spilled water forced it to detour around the wet ground.
Every hunter who had survived the Age of Destruction knew this habit, but poor research meant it was still virtually unknown in the present era.
‘Once the Demon Realm Gate opens and Hellhounds spread across the continent, people will discover it soon enough.’
Vikir turned to face the Hellhound.
The beast had lost some speed while circling the water, but it remained terrifyingly dangerous.
Even knights of at least intermediate Expert rank struggled to fight a Hellhound one-on-one.
Before his regression, Vikir himself had only become capable of reliably killing one alone at around eighteen.
But this time was different.
Vikir had not come all this way without preparing.
“Canine demonic creatures always run with their mouths open.”
As a seasoned hunter who had survived the Age of Destruction, he had brought a secret weapon for precisely this situation.
The most effective weapon against canine demonic creatures.
Click, click—!
Beans knocked softly against one another in his palm.
Bloody Beans.
It was chocolate.
