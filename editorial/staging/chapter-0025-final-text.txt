“You said it wasn’t for me to hear, so I stopped listening after that.”
At those words, Hugo quickly covered the corner of his mouth with one hand.
Everything above the hand remained expressionless, but the gesture obviously concealed a smile.
Camus stared at Vikir with wide eyes as well.
“Heh… heh heh heh….”
Only Adolf’s forehead visibly throbbed.
He straightened and looked from Vikir back to Hugo.
“My lord, will you allow me to speak with this child for a little while?”
For a senior representative of Morgue to show this much personal interest in an eight-year-old was extremely unusual.
Hugo had little difficulty recognizing that Adolf was overreacting.
“What are you planning to do with him? Is the Magic Star of the Empire going to bully an eight-year-old boy?”
Hugo had not forgotten Adolf’s earlier accusation and returned it word for word.
But Adolf refused to back down.
“Heh. This boy might one day become my niece’s husband. I’m asking as her uncle, not as Morgue’s representative.”
“Uncle! It’s not like that!”
“Hush, Camus. Let your uncle handle this. Some things need to be checked personally.”
Adolf answered his niece with unusual firmness.
Then he gave Hugo a meaningful look, silently asking him to allow the request.
“….”
Hugo waved one hand, already tired of dealing with Morgue for the day.
Do as you please.
◆◆◆
Vikir accepted Adolf’s request without hesitation.
Adolf Morgue had been an absolute powerhouse even in Vikir’s first life, someone extremely difficult to confront directly. At forty, he was already trusted to represent the family at major official events.
The opportunity to experience even a fraction of Adolf’s power was valuable.
Vikir suspected Hugo had allowed the test for exactly the same reason.
After the annual event, the surrounding spectators were cleared away.
Vikir and Adolf stood facing each other on a wide training field.
Hugo Le Baskerville and Camus Morgue remained as observers.
They watched from a distance while the two stood in the center.
Adolf spoke first.
“Child of Baskerville, I intend to test your qualities. Show me what you can do.”
Vikir nodded while thinking something very different.
‘If I actually showed you everything, you’d probably faint.’
How would he have fared against Adolf at his own peak before regression?
Perhaps fifty percent through ambush or assassination, but only around ten percent in a frontal fight.
Current Vikir had not yet recovered all of that former strength, and he had no reason to expose his present maximum either.
He only needed to satisfy Adolf’s expectations in moderation.
‘Let’s see how strong a Sixth Circle Master really is at this point in time.’
Masters could often gauge one another from even a brief exchange.
Vikir intended to use the opportunity to measure one of Morgue’s top combatants.
Then Adolf spoke.
“Child, I’ll give you a handicap.”
He raised one hand and swept it toward the ground.
Rumble!
Earth rose and spun together into the shape of a jar.
Whoosh!
Flame from Adolf’s hand baked the clay hard.
Gurgle…
The finished jar then began filling itself with water.
It looked simple, but Adolf had casually combined earth, fire, and water magic in succession.
Snap!
Adolf snapped his fingers, and the water-filled jar floated upward and settled on top of his head.
Balancing the jar effortlessly, he looked down at Vikir.
“I’ll fight you with this full jar on my head. If you make even one drop of water spill from it, you win.”
In other words, Vikir was free to attack however he liked.
Vikir gripped his short sword and took his stance.
Adolf folded his arms and watched him.
Then—
Tap!
Vikir moved first.
When she saw him charge directly, Camus cried out in alarm.
“W-wait! Don’t charge my uncle head-on!”
Adolf looked back at Camus with deep disappointment.
“Camus, I would have preferred you not tell him that.”
At the same moment, a transparent barrier formed in front of Adolf.
Bang!
Vikir’s charge struck the shield and rebounded violently.
“Shield magic is one of the best answers to swordsmen. Even an aura blade has difficulty forcing its way through.”
Adolf was not exaggerating.
He was particularly skilled at shield magic and could form barriers of different sizes, thicknesses, and shapes according to the situation.
The barrier he had just used was only large enough to cover the relevant part of Vikir’s body, minimizing wasted mana.
Vikir, however, immediately used the rebound instead of resisting it.
Tap!
He let the backward force create distance, turned, and moved sharply to the side.
At the same time—
Kick! Kick!
Vikir sent several loose stones flying in the opposite direction.
They shot straight toward the water jar on Adolf’s head.
“Aiming directly for the jar is too obvious.”
Without even turning toward the stones, Adolf formed another shield with one hand.
At the same time, he produced a second, smaller barrier to intercept Vikir’s next approach.
Vikir rebounded again.
Adolf opened his mouth.
“From now on, I’ll attack too.”
Before he had even finished speaking, spears of flame appeared.
The fire spears plunged through the air, each carrying enough force to gouge deep holes in the packed ground.
Camus shouted when she saw them.
“Uncle! That’s not fair! You never used magic like that when you sparred with me!”
“…Camus, whose side are you on?”
Adolf looked genuinely wounded by his niece’s complaint.
Meanwhile, Vikir kept moving through the rain of fire.
‘Hmm. So this is about where he stands right now.’
The Adolf Vikir remembered from his first life was not fundamentally different in style.
This level of danger was already familiar to him.
‘I know how to handle him.’
Decades of battlefield experience now sat behind the harmless face of an eight-year-old child.
Used together, those two advantages could make an opponent underestimate him and create unexpected openings.
‘Or, if necessary, kill him.’
The thought was grim, but there was no reason to take matters that far.
Hugo was watching, and Vikir needed to handle the test appropriately.
“Uwaaaah!”
Vikir moved as though desperately fleeing the fire.
Then he drew his short sword and rushed Adolf again.
But—
“Impossible.”
Adolf’s shield stopped the blade cold.
Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang!
Vikir swung again, and the barrier stopped every strike.
Adolf sighed.
“You can repeat the same attack a hundred times. That sword still won’t pierce my shield.”
Vikir did not stop.
Bang.
The sword came down.
Bang!
Again, it struck the barrier.
Vikir repeated the same motion while Adolf calmly blocked it with the same shield.
Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang! Bang!
Every strike landed on exactly the same point.
Vikir reproduced the trajectory with machine-like precision, never missing the chosen spot.
Eventually even Adolf noticed the pattern.
“…Ho.”
Vikir’s blade was striking one exact location over and over.
That section of the magical shield was accumulating localized fatigue.
Because only one point was being stressed repeatedly, its efficiency had begun to decline.
“Hahaha! So you intend to wear through my shield by striking one point. Bold. I have to praise the concentration and sword control required to hit the exact same place every time.”
Then Adolf’s gaze cooled.
“But you’re still striking a boulder with an egg. Which will fail first—my shield, or your sword?”
He was correct about the immediate comparison.
Bang! Bang!
Vikir struck the same point again.
CRACK!
At last, the short sword shattered.
Vikir gathered himself after the break and stepped back.
Hugo silently stroked his chin, while Camus looked disappointed.
Adolf faced Vikir across the distance.
“I admire your persistence and precision, but you must learn to judge the opponent in front of you. You’re talented, but still far from qualified to become my niece’s husb—”
Adolf was about to finish the lecture.
…But—
a cool wetness suddenly touched the top of his head, stopping him mid-sentence.
“…What?”
Adolf finally sensed that something had changed.
Drip. Drip.
Water dampened his hair and began running down his face.
“…?”
He looked upward in confusion at the jar balanced on his head.
“…Ah.”
A shard from Vikir’s shattered sword had ricocheted upward and embedded itself in the side of the jar, opening a small leak.
Water ran from Adolf’s crown down his face and neck while he stood frozen.
Step. Step. Step.
Vikir calmly walked up to him.
“The jar really was full to the brim.”
Naturally, he was talking about the water.
