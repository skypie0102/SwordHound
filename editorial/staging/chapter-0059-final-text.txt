Dawn had not yet come.
Vikir forced himself upright.
Every unhealed joint screamed in protest, but he had no choice.
If he stayed where he was, he would die.
BOOM!
The small tent tore apart like paper.
An oxbear’s claws had ripped through it from the darkness.
Aiyen gave a short, humorless laugh.
“So you knew we were following you, you sly beast.”
The male oxbear was only half the female’s size, but it was still an enormous carnivore more than four meters tall and weighing well over a ton.
The seasoned animal had realized it was being tracked.
Instead of simply fleeing, it had waited until the hunters settled down and launched its own ambush.
Aiyen snatched up her bow and arrows and retreated from the ruined shelter.
Bakira crouched beside her, snarling.
Aiyen studied the oxbear carefully.
“Now I understand why that old female chose you.”
The enormous female had seemed unusually selective about her mate, and Aiyen had wondered why she chose this relatively small male.
If size alone had been the point, there had been plenty of other candidates.
Now the answer was obvious.
His value was cunning rather than raw strength.
GRUNT!
The oxbear lunged with both massive horns and its front paws.
Aiyen vaulted onto Bakira and nocked an arrow.
Ping!
Two arrows streaked toward the oxbear’s face.
The animal lowered its head and raised its horns to shield its eyes.
The arrows curved in flight and struck both shoulders instead.
Ballak archery was considered the finest on the continent, and Aiyen was among her tribe’s best young practitioners.
Krrrk—krrrk—krrrk.
Aiyen set several arrows against the same bow.
Ballak bows were built with multiple nocking points.
Arrows could be staggered for rapid successive shots or released almost simultaneously.
Thrum-thrum-thrum!
Aiyen fired again and again with barely a pause.
The second, third, fourth, and fifth arrows followed the first within the span of a heartbeat.
Her rate of fire was monstrous.
Drawing so many arrows demanded tremendous arm strength.
Aiyen reinforced the draw with the aura of a Low Sword Graduator-equivalent warrior.
GRRRR!
The storm of arrows slowed the oxbear’s charge.
Even its thick muscles and dense fur could not fully stop Aiyen’s shots.
More troublesome still, her arrows did not always follow simple straight paths.
She fired upward, sideways, and even backward when the angle demanded it.
High shots fell in arcs onto the skull, while side shots curved into the flanks.
Other arrows ricocheted from trees or rocks and changed direction again.
Every shaft also carried aura.
The oxbear had no way to predict how troublesome that would become.
Thunk! Thunk!
The oxbear ducked behind dense brush.
The thorns were thick enough that it seemed reasonable to use them as cover.
But Aiyen was not shooting ordinary arrows.
“A skilled archer can pierce steel when she wishes—and spare a leaf when she does not.”
Aiyen remembered a lesson from her mother, Chieftain Akwilla.
She released another arrow toward the high brush concealing the oxbear.
Whoosh!
The arrow did not simply smash through the brambles.
Instead, the aura carried by its head passed through the vegetation without wasting its force there.
A heartbeat later, the stored force reached its true target.
Boom!
Blood burst from the oxbear’s neck behind the thicket.
GRAH!
Penetration.
The intangible force projected from the arrowhead crossed the intervening brush and struck the body hidden behind it.
Vikir’s eyes sharpened.
‘So this is Ballak archery.’
It was a technique capable of killing an enemy through cover.
And the Ballak practiced it as ordinary high-level hunting craft.
Vikir himself had learned to transmit mana through objects without destroying the intermediate surface, but he had never applied the principle to an arrow in flight.
‘If I can learn that, my aura control will become far more precise.’
He looked at Aiyen again.
She was seventeen and already comparable to a low-tier Graduator.
Even among Baskerville’s prodigies, Vikir had rarely seen talent mature that early.
In raw talent, Aiyen could stand beside—or perhaps surpass—even Camus Morgue.
Yet the oxbear was still dangerous enough to make her tense.
It was a relatively small male, immature compared with the old female and badly weakened after days of exhaustion.
Even so, an A-rank oxbear remained an A-rank monster.
Aiyen clenched her teeth as her supply of arrows dwindled.
Bakira, who had fought beside her throughout the pursuit, was tiring as well.
The valley was bitterly cold, the terrain offered little complexity, and the moonless darkness made sight difficult.
With those advantages, the oxbear prepared to finish the fight.
A typical oxbear attacked first with a horn charge, then a sweeping blow from its forepaws.
The bite that followed those two attacks was usually the killing move.
ROOOAR!
The oxbear charged with all its remaining strength, spreading its forelegs wide enough to catch Aiyen and Bakira together.
Its dagger-like teeth were ready to close on Aiyen’s head.
“We’re nearly out of arrows. Do we retreat, or risk everything on one final shot?”
Aiyen hesitated over the decision.
Then something happened that Aiyen, Bakira, and the oxbear had not anticipated.
“Move.”
Vikir stepped into the battle.
The badly injured slave was now an unexpected third combatant.
He had climbed onto a high branch above the oxbear.
Vikir kicked off and dropped directly toward the charging monster.
“Vikir! What are you doing?”
Aiyen stared in horror; from her perspective, the leap looked suicidal.
But Vikir had already chosen his angle.
As he fell, he gripped several discarded arrows in one hand.
The oxbear barely reacted to his descent.
Vikir had jumped from its left side.
The old female’s earlier blow had swollen the male’s left eye nearly shut.
Vikir had deliberately entered that blind spot.
Then he struck.
Thuk!
Several arrows drove into the damaged left eye together.
Sizzle—
The smell of scorched flesh filled the cold air.
Aiyen’s residual mana still clung to the arrowheads, and Vikir heated the metal as he drove them in.
The hot points burst the swollen eye and tore into the nerves behind it.
GRAAAAAH!
The oxbear’s scream changed.
Hatred gave way to fear.
Vikir tore his improvised weapons free and dropped toward the ground.
Thud!
Even the landing sent agony through his half-healed body.
The oxbear towered over him.
Aiyen’s face went white.
“No!”
She fired immediately, but her arrow only buried itself in the dense muscles of the beast’s back.
Bakira lunged and bit into a hind leg, but the oxbear barely reacted.
RAAAH!
Blind with pain and terror, the oxbear opened its jaws.
It abandoned horns and claws and went straight for the bite—its most lethal close-range attack.
Vikir remained perfectly calm.
“….”
He was not trying to die.
He was calculating whether his recovering body could withstand the technique he was about to use.
“Come out.”
At Vikir’s low command, his faithful hound bared its teeth.
Psshk!
A black awl emerged from the artery of his wrist.
The magic sword Beelzebub had been released.
Bzzzz…
Like the wings of a fly, the weapon vibrated hungrily before the mass of flesh bearing down on it.
One of the powers stored in Beelzebub’s three slots surged toward the awl’s tip.

Cerberus was the three-headed hound that symbolized Hell itself.
ROAR!
Oil-fed flame erupted around Beelzebub’s aura.
Water could not easily smother the infernal burning once it had taken hold.
The technique persisted with terrifying intensity as long as Vikir continued feeding it.
Flaming fangs met the oxbear’s open jaws head-on.
GRAH!?
The oxbear’s charge stopped.
Pain and fear finally cleared enough of its rage for instinct to recognize what stood before it.
What was this?
The prey in front of it was not merely a wounded human.
The presence released through Beelzebub carried the pressure of a far higher predator—the remnant power of a demon far beyond an oxbear.
A weapon that had waited quietly until now had finally shown its teeth.
Psshk!
The sound was brief and ugly.
The awl extending from Vikir’s wrist shot into the oxbear’s mouth.
It pierced tongue, gums, palate, and finally the brain behind them.
At the same time, Cerberus’s Burn erupted from the point.
Few pains were more overwhelming than being burned from within.
Heat boiled through the oxbear’s skull.
GRAAAH!
Its final scream shook the valley.
Vikir’s attack had lasted only an instant, but it was decisive.
The oxbear’s extraordinary toughness and regeneration kept it alive for a few moments longer, nothing more.
Even so, the fight was not quite finished.
In its death throes, the oxbear let its enormous body fall toward Vikir.
Pain locked Vikir’s battered limbs.
The dying beast intended to crush him under its own weight.
But it never reached him.
Whump!
Aiyen threw herself forward and dragged Vikir clear.
Bakira caught both of them against her broad, furred back and bounded away.
“Are you all right!?”
Aiyen’s eyes raced over Vikir from head to toe.
Vikir managed a difficult nod.
At the same moment, the monster finally fell.
THUD!
The oxbear’s head struck the ground.
Only days earlier it had been secure in a warm den; now its hunt, flight, and counterattack had all ended here.
Such reversals were ordinary in a jungle where predator and prey constantly exchanged roles.
The hunt was over.
Aiyen pulled Vikir closer to make sure he remained conscious.
She checked his wounds with frantic hands, no longer pretending calm.
“Stay awake. Tell me where you’re hurt. You are not dying on me after all that.”
Vikir could only let out a tired sigh at the successful hunt.
