Aiyen was a Ballak warrior and the chieftain’s daughter.
She spoke to Vikir.
“Down.”
Was she trying to establish their hierarchy?
No—the command did not seem intended that way.
Because,
Whip—
Vikir felt something cutting through the air toward his back.
At the same instant,
Tug!
Aiyen yanked the rope around Vikir’s neck.
As Vikir ducked, a thin arrow skimmed past him and struck the ground.
Aiyen shouted,
“You kids! Go practice somewhere else!”
Bushes rustled and a group of young children ran away.
Every one of them was carrying a small bow.
“….”
Vikir lifted his head and looked at Aiyen again.
Water still ran from her as she stood beside the pool.
She was the same girl he had rescued from the slave auction.
Aiyen spoke.
“We meet again, right?”
Her Imperial was clumsy, so Vikir answered in Ballak; his grasp of her language was better than hers of his.
“…We meet again.”
Aiyen’s eyes widened when she heard Ballak from Vikir’s mouth.
“You speak our language?”
“A little.”
She stepped closer with open curiosity.
She wore only the simple deerskin clothing customary around the spring and showed no embarrassment about it.
Aiyen stopped directly in front of Vikir.
“We were grateful then. We repay kindness twice and vengeance tenfold.”
“….”
“You saved me once, so I have saved you twice.”
“….”
“I never expected you to challenge ‘her.’ I had already fled, but I came back to check.”
Without the lasso Aiyen had thrown, Madam Eight-Legs would almost certainly have killed Vikir.
…Then what was the second rescue?
Vikir’s questioning look made Aiyen’s eyes narrow like a fox’s.
“Didn’t you see it on the way here?”
She pointed past the bushes toward the acrid smoke rising from the village.
Vikir remembered the prisoners he had passed.
He might otherwise have shared their fate.
Apparently Aiyen had prevented that.
Which raised the more fundamental question.
“Why did you save me?”
If it had only been repayment, there would have been no reason to lasso him in the first place.
And Aiyen had not been the only Ballak woman aiming a rope at Vikir that day.
Several of the women in the raiding party had tried to capture him.
Vikir asked directly.
“Was that part of the husband hunt?”
“Oh. You already heard about it.”
Ballak was not unique; many tribes in the jungle took spouses from outside their own people.
The custom brought new blood into small tribes and reduced close inbreeding.
As a warrior tribe, Ballak valued physical strength heavily when choosing outsiders.
Aiyen slowly circled Vikir.
She looked him over with the detached eye of an appraiser.
Meanwhile, Vikir’s thoughts were elsewhere.
What if Ballak informed Baskerville that he was alive and tried to negotiate with him as a hostage?
That would ruin everything.
His plan to disappear from Hugo’s surveillance and rebuild his strength would collapse.
Preventing that would require escaping or destroying the tribe—both impossible in his current condition.
But Vikir had worried needlessly.
Aiyen apparently had no intention of exposing him.
She looked directly at him.
“Don’t misunderstand. I am not like the others. I do not need a husband.”
“….”
“I captured you to use as a slave.”
Vikir had questions about how desperately she had pursued him, but kept them to himself.
When he remained silent, Aiyen flicked the rope in her hand.
“Your body is too broken to be useful as a warrior or spouse right now. Get used to being a slave instead.”
Ballak’s spouse-hunt custom normally judged male captives primarily by whether they could strengthen the tribe.
Those judged unsuitable might be rejected or killed, but Aiyen was explicitly claiming Vikir for another purpose.
Aiyen tossed Vikir the clothes she had left by the water.
When he caught them, the collar rope pulled tight.
Aiyen started back toward the settlement.
“You will live in front of my house and serve me.”
Every step made Vikir’s ruined body scream, but he said nothing.
At least Styx and the power taken from Murciélago were helping him recover quickly.
Aiyen gave Vikir his first order.
“First, we establish proper forms of address.”
Walking ahead, she stopped at the edge of the settlement and looked back.
She narrowed her eyes down at him.
“Call me Master.”
◆◆◆
Vikir entered the Ballak village again.
The tribe lived freely within the dense jungle.
The concept of private property seemed weak.
Anyone without a place to sleep could stay in a neighbor’s tent without causing offense.
Someone hungry could ask food from a household with plenty, and the food would be shared.
The community seemed to accept that possessions came and went, so what existed now should be shared.
People gathered in the larger tents of friends, talked and played, then fell asleep together.
Firewood, meat, and alcohol circulated freely, with little concern for status.
Even outsiders who had once been captured could move freely after being recognized as members of the tribe.
Overall, village life felt lively and welcoming.
But the remains of condemned captives at the thorn trees along the boundary sent the opposite message.
Ballak was merciless to hostile outsiders and remarkably free toward those accepted inside.
Even so, there were sacred areas that ordinary members could not enter casually.
Vikir’s destination was one of them.
A huge tent stood at the center of the village.
It looked large enough for two dozen people, yet even children playing nearby did not approach it.
Passing warriors bowed toward its entrance.
People roasting meat kept smoke from drifting toward it, while those butchering prey made sure the smell of blood carried elsewhere.
‘…The chieftain’s home?’
Vikir guessed from the size and treatment of the tent.
Aiyen gripped the rope at his neck and walked straight inside.
“Mother.”
Vikir saw the person she had come to meet.
A woman sat cross-legged on a chair carved with a great eagle, leaning back at ease.
Her expression was severe and scars marked her body.
She wore garments and a cloak made with eagle feathers, while an enormous longbow rested behind her.
One especially prominent scar crossed the area around one eye.
This was **Akwilla**, current chieftain of Ballak.
At Aiyen’s pull, Vikir silently knelt before her.
The pressure Akwilla gave off immediately confirmed another fact: she was the woman the Empire called the **Night Fox**.
She was the archer who had scarred Swordmaster Hugo across the bridge of the nose.
She had also wounded Adolf badly enough to remove him from action for a time.
Her existence was one of the main reasons Baskerville and Morgue could not simply overrun Ballak’s frontier.
She had killed sixteen Sword Graduators, ten Fourth-Circle mages, and six Fifth-Circle mages during the famous ten-day clash.
And she had escaped without a wound.
She was also responsible for the arrow-shaped scars Vikir had once seen on Cerberus.
‘What if I faced her at my peak before regression?’
Could his former self have defeated this woman?
‘Zero chance in a direct fight. Zero by assassination. Maybe… twenty percent if I ran.’
And that twenty percent referred only to surviving the escape.
Vikir’s thoughts raced as he measured the woman before him.
Akwilla belonged in the same predator class as Hugo and Adolf—far beyond what Vikir could handle in his current state.
Akwilla stared down at him.
“Daughter. Is this the Imperial boy you’ve been so interested in?”
“I never said it like that… but yes. This is him.”
“Hmm. Younger than you. And I recognize that face.”
Akwilla narrowed her eyes at Vikir.
Her gaze felt like a great predator calmly examining lesser prey.
Vikir kept his head lowered and avoided meeting it more than necessary.
Fortunately, Akwilla did not seem to recognize Hugo in Vikir’s face.
Hugo’s features were broad and severe, while Vikir had inherited more from his mother.
Even so, Akwilla immediately understood how badly injured Vikir was.
“I see. Why bother bringing home a man this badly broken?”
“Treat him and he’ll be useful. He’s the one who attacked Madam Eight-Legs.”
“Hmph. Impressive, but charging something beyond your level only gets you crippled.”
Akwilla studied him once more.
“So this is not about the spouse hunt after all?”
“Mother! No. I’m keeping him as a slave.”
“What? After everything you kept saying before…?”
“That was before. I need a slave to help with hunting.”
Aiyen hurriedly cut off her mother’s teasing.
Akwilla gave her daughter a puzzled look, then shrugged.
“…Fine. He is your captive. Use him as you see fit.”
Aiyen immediately bowed and tugged Vikir’s rope.
Once they were outside the tent, Aiyen’s expression became noticeably more serious.
The embarrassment she had shown in front of her mother vanished.
“Now I’m giving you a job.”
Aiyen looked down at Vikir, who was roughly a head shorter than she was, and spoke firmly.
“It will be difficult.”
…Apparently, his life as Aiyen’s slave was beginning with a demanding assignment.
