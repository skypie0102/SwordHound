Goo-wook… goo-kook…
The orangutan corpses began moving again.
Though they had been dead only a short time, decay had already turned much of their flesh soft and exposed bone beneath.
All living things come from nature and eventually return to it.
Being born from nature and returning to nature is part of the contract that lets life belong to the ecosystem.
However—
Some beings refuse to return their bodies after life has ended.
The undead.
They came from nature but refuse to go back.
They are like tenants refusing to leave after a lease has expired.
A ghost forcibly occupies a body that should already have been surrendered to the earth.
Nature therefore acts to reclaim what is owed.
All things must eventually return to the soil.
To reclaim a body from an occupying spirit, decay accelerates, which is why undead flesh rots and skeletons are laid bare so quickly.
That was why most undead appeared as corpses or bones.
But—
On rare occasions, spirits that possessed extraordinary power in life could resist that process.
Their strength allowed them to retain bodies much closer to the state they had possessed while alive.
Crack! Crack! Crack!
Those were the kinds of undead now crawling out of the Tomb of the Brave.
“…Impossible!”
Aiyen stared with her mouth open.
Skeletons and desiccated figures emerged from stone and earth across the valley.
Black-panther burial shrouds hung from their bodies, and empty darkness filled their eye sockets.
Long ago, these had been Ballak’s greatest warriors and chieftains.
Bows, spears, swords, and axes came out of the graves with them as the dead advanced together.

A death knight was a dead warrior who had once reached exceptional heights, sustained after death by dark mana drawn through soul and body.
Most lost their sanity and became creatures of hatred and rage.
A few legendary death knights were said to retain intelligence, though human history recorded only a tiny number of such cases.
Ordinary death knights were often heavily armored to compensate for their damaged bodies.
Ballak’s resurrected ancestors wore only old burial garments, yet moved toward the living with the speed of the wind.
They were answering Ahmen’s hatred.
Then Akwilla shouted.
“Everyone, prepare for battle!”
Aiyen drew and fired immediately.
Her arrow aimed for Ahmen on the ground.
But one of the approaching death knights loosed an arrow first.
Puff! Puff! Puff!
The answering shots carried tremendous force and formed a shield around Ahmen.
“Heh… die! All of you, die!”
Behind the dead warriors, Ahmen’s body aged rapidly.
Controlling so many death knights was burning through his life force.
Soon the faceless dead formed a wall of swords and spears between Ahmen and Ballak.
The present generation faced its own past.
Warriors of the old age and new age confronted one another.
“Then it’s time to show our ancestors how frightening the younger generation has become!”
Aiyen fired first.
Ping!
Silver aura filled her arrow.
To her shock, the death knight in front caught it with one hand.
Crisp!
The arrow stopped, but the hand that caught it cracked and collapsed into dust.
The death knight stared at its ruined hand.
Perhaps in life that warrior could have caught the shot without injury.
Its dead body was weaker than the one remembered by its instincts.
Then—
Boom!
A second death knight swung a sword.
A cutting wave tore through everything in its path.
The speed caught Aiyen off guard.
Boom!
Vikir intercepted the strike.
Then—
He swept one hand toward the two advancing undead.
A cloud of white powder struck their faces.
Chiiit!
Smoke rose with the sound of something scorching.
The death knights recoiled.
“Careful. Every one of them was a named warrior in life.”
Aiyen answered with a grin.
Even in the crisis, she shifted closer so Vikir could cover her blind side.
“You’re still the only man I trust at my back.”
“….”
“In battle, I mean. Don’t misunderstand.”
Aiyen enjoyed teasing him, but the moment was too dangerous for games.
The death knights were already attacking again, faster and harder.
Her expression became serious.
“Vikir, what did you just use?”
She meant the powder that had driven the undead back.
Vikir answered simply.
“Salt. Undead hate salt.”
Salt was a traditional purifier even in the supernatural logic of this world.
Vikir scooped some from the pale mineral banks near the river and scattered it toward another death knight.
The white crystals blackened as they burned against the undead body.
The death knight recoiled, creating an opening.
Puff!
Vikir seized its back and drove Beelzebub through its temple.
The Ballak warriors who had struggled against their ancestors immediately understood.
“Salt! Bring salt!”
“Do what Vikir did!”
“It works!”
Even then, many Ballak warriors hesitated to injure the ancestors they revered.
They used salt mainly to keep distance and avoid close combat whenever possible.
As Ahmen’s ritual strained toward its limit, the death knights began falling one by one.
Akwilla sneered.
“Our ancestors were never meant to become puppets for a necromancer.”
The insult was aimed directly at the dying shaman.
Then—
“Kuh… yes. Quality instead of quantity.”
Ahmen laughed grimly.
He smeared blood across his face in complex ritual lines and drew still more power from his remaining life.
The forbidden rite threatened not only his body but whatever remained of his soul after death.
Ahmen invoked it anyway.
Thud. Thud. Thud.
One after another, the surrounding death knights collapsed.
Ahmen kept his eyes closed and continued chanting.
“I found him! I found him! Alone in the deepest darkness, without even a proper grave—he was really there!”
Black mana spiraled violently around him.
Ballak’s warriors struggled just to remain standing.
“…It ends now.”
Akwilla, the Night Fox and chieftain, raised her bow.
Her arrow flew straight for Ahmen’s forehead.
Pow!
A single silver streak cut through the storm of dark mana.
It seemed inevitable that the shot would destroy Ahmen’s skull.
But—
Kwagik.
The arrow stopped in midair.
Someone had caught Akwilla’s supposedly unstoppable shot with a bare hand.
“…!?”
Every Ballak warrior looked up.
A tall, lean figure stood before Ahmen.
The man wore a ragged burial shroud.
His face was skeletal and leathery, lips gone and teeth exposed.
The ancient fabric fluttering around him belonged to an age long past.
Heavy bones, exceptional height, and enough strength to catch Akwilla’s arrow bare-handed marked him as something beyond the other ancestors.
No one immediately recognized him.
No one except Akwilla.
“…!”
The chieftain stared at the undead warrior with trembling eyes.
Since childhood, Akwilla had heard every legend of Ballak’s great chiefs and heroes.
She had always believed she could stand beside most of them.
Her talent, potential, and once-in-a-century ability justified that confidence.
Akwilla herself was already regarded as one of the greatest chieftains Ballak had produced.
There was only one ancestor she never compared herself to.
One hero had declared war on the Empire alone, forced enemies to surrender, united the tribes of the depths, and led Ballak to an unmatched peak.
Even Akwilla could only admire that figure without reservation.
He was Ballak’s eternal legend and spiritual icon.
Adonai—the greatest archer since their recorded history began.
