So, did you learn anything?
Hugo Le Baskerville.
He summoned Vikir to his office the moment the boy emerged from the Inner Library.
Hugo had cleared enough of his schedule to wait for Vikir’s return; he was plainly looking forward to hearing the result.
Aside from his first and second sons, Vikir had never seen Hugo show this much interest in one of his children.
But that was no reason to become flustered or carried away. In his first life, Vikir had lived long enough to be older than the Hugo standing before him now.
“A little.”
“Oh?”
A light flashed in Hugo’s eyes.
One day in the library should not have been enough for an eight-year-old to gain anything meaningful.
Most Baskervilles of the same age were only beginning to sense mana and had barely, if ever, experienced real combat.
Under those circumstances, what could a child possibly understand from a high-level swordsmanship manual?
Yet Hugo looked at the son whose very birth he barely remembered with an anticipation even he could not explain.
“What did you feel? What did you understand in the library?”
“It was… warm, and pointed, and sharp, and soft, and prickly… something like that.”
“….”
Hugo rubbed his forehead for a moment.
Children’s explanations were often difficult to decipher.
But perhaps it was a master swordsman’s intuition. Something in Vikir’s clumsy list of sensations suddenly made sense to him.
“…Impossible.”
Even as he muttered the word, Hugo slowly shook his head.
Aura.
The sign that a swordsman had truly stepped into the world beyond ordinary swordsmanship.
A realm most swordsmen could never reach in an entire lifetime.
Even the prodigies of Iron-Blooded Sword Baskerville generally had to train relentlessly until around age fifteen before reaching it.
It was the realm of a **Low Sword Expert**.
Hugo frowned again.
Vikir was only eight years old. What, exactly, was Hugo expecting?
And yet.
Hugo could not take his eyes off the boy standing before him.
What if…?
The possibility made Hugo impatient enough to speak.
“…Can you show your father what you mean?”
Members of Baskerville instinctively disliked revealing the full extent of their strength to one another.
That instinct persisted even between parents and children, and between siblings.
For that reason, Hugo chose his words cautiously even with an eight-year-old son.
But Vikir’s easy answer scattered those concerns immediately.
“Of course.”
The Baskerville training ground.
A small child stood alone at the center of the broad arena, surrounded by onlookers.
Vikir stood with his eyes closed, holding a short sword suited to his size.
The moment word spread that Hugo intended to test Vikir’s level, people flocked to the training ground to watch.
“Is that the boy who stayed beneath the River Styx for seven minutes?”
“I heard he strangled two venomous snakes in the Cradle.”
“And during the practical evaluation, he even killed Cerberus.”
Surprisingly, Hugo made no attempt to drive the spectators away.
He simply stared at Vikir in the middle of the arena.
Butler Barrymore, standing beside Hugo, asked a question.
“My lord, how do you intend to test Young Master Vikir’s ability?”
“…I’ll have him face a suitable demonic creature.”
Hugo’s answer was cold to the core.
He intended to put a monster in front of an eight-year-old child.
Before long, Vikir’s opponent arrived.
The creature brought in by the Guardian Knights was a huge, green-skinned humanoid.

Because of their roughly human appearance and intelligence, orcs were classified as pseudo-humanoids.
Their powerful bodies, low intelligence, and high fertility led them to live in tribes, making them no less dangerous than many barbarian groups.
Even unarmed, an orc possessed combat strength comparable to four or five ordinary adult men.
Their bodies were packed with lean muscle, giving them exceptional strength, resistance, and regenerative ability.
This one had been captured alive by Baskerville. Once a member of a warlike tribe, it now seemed ready to vent its hatred on the entire world.
The people around the arena began to murmur.
“Even for Baskerville, an orc seems a bit much for an eight-year-old…”
“You’d normally need to be at least fifteen to fight one.”
“If something goes wrong, the Guardian Knights will intervene.”
Even among the Iron-Blooded Sword Baskervilles, most of the spectators found the matchup unreasonable.
“….”
But Hugo said nothing, and the test proceeded.
Then.
Clang—!
The orc’s restraints were released and it was driven into the center of the arena.
Prodded forward by the Guardian Knights’ spears and swords, it advanced while drooling and baring its teeth and gums.
Vikir stood waiting with his sword in hand.
[Graaah!]
The orc lunged at him with both arms spread wide.
Seeing only an eight-year-old child, it assumed the kill would be easy and let down its guard.
Vikir dropped low and slipped beneath its grasp.
It was a practiced movement that exploited the difference in size and height.
“Oh?”
Interest flashed through Hugo’s eyes, mirrored by the other Baskervilles watching.
But before their surprise could settle, Vikir moved again.
Swish—!
He pivoted with the short sword.
His body was still that of an eight-year-old, but the River Styx and his training had made him far heavier and stronger than he looked.
Putting his full weight behind the blade, Vikir severed one of the orc’s wrists.
[Grrr!?]
For a heartbeat, the orc did not understand what had happened.
The child in front of it had vanished from its line of sight, and one wrist was suddenly gone.
Before it could process that, another brutal fact arrived.
After taking the wrist, Vikir slipped beneath the orc and sliced through one ankle as well.
Thud!
By the time the orc understood that its wrist was gone, its ankle had already been severed and its body was crashing to the ground.
Silence fell over the arena.
Every Baskerville present stared wide-eyed.
“…He’s eight?”
“What is with those movements? He looks like a veteran who’s spent years on a battlefield.”
“He did all of that without even using aura. What was I doing at that age…?”
The crowd around the training ground continued to grow.
Even Guardian Knights and servants gathered to watch.
But.
“….”
Hugo alone remained silent.
Vikir understood exactly why.
‘I still haven’t shown him what I supposedly learned in the library.’
Cutting the orc’s wrist and ankle had been nothing more than physical technique.
He had not yet shown the result Hugo actually wanted to see.
Now.
Vikir drew a deep breath and lightly flicked his short sword.
The weapon weighed roughly four kilograms—heavy for a child—but in Vikir’s hand it moved like a twig.
That alone was impressive, but what came next stunned the crowd.
Fwoom—!
A faint glow appeared at the tip of the blade.
The instant the Baskervilles recognized it, a collective cry erupted.
“Aura Blade!”
Gaseous aura—the hallmark of a Sword Expert.
It flickered from the tip of Vikir’s sword like ghostly fire.
The reddish aura burned as though blood itself were feeding the flame.
It was one of Baskerville’s defining symbols: killing intent given concentrated form.
The orc recoiled instinctively and backed away.
It tried to use its natural Rapid Regeneration to close the wounds at its wrist and ankle.

Unfortunately for the orc, Beelzebub’s power inside Vikir made that recovery impossible.
The Hellhound’s Bleeding skill overwhelmed the orc’s Rapid Regeneration.
Now the orc was forced to experience the full lethality of a Hellhound’s bite.
Even shallow wounds that would ordinarily stop bleeding continued to open and bleed for far longer than normal.
With a severed wrist and ankle, the effect was devastating.
Gulp… gulp… gulp…
The blood coating the short sword slowly disappeared.
Beelzebub, hidden within the weapon and Vikir’s body, drank the orc’s blood greedily while the helpless creature continued to spill more.
And in that moment.
Fwoom—!
Vikir swung.
Everyone gathered around the arena saw it.
One ghastly fang formed in the air.
The Baskerville First Form.
A single **Stabbing Fang** appeared and drove deep into the orc’s neck.
The orc’s breathing stopped almost at once.
The wound also carried the searing agony of Cerberus’s Burn.
Vikir deliberately displayed the sort of swordsmanship Baskerville normally associated with a prodigious fifteen-year-old.
The crowd immediately erupted.
“That’s insane. At eight, I couldn’t even feel aura.”
“I could barely gather a ball of mana in my palm.”
“He’s already a complete Low Sword Expert? How is that possible?”
The previous youngest known Sword Expert had been around fourteen.
Even in Baskerville, a family overflowing with sword prodigies, an eight-year-old reaching that realm was unprecedented.
Barrymore spoke with a mixture of shock and delight.
“…Congratulations, my lord. A once-in-an-age genius has appeared. This is a blessing for the family.”
“…Hmm.”
Hugo nodded without changing his expression.
But Barrymore saw the corner of Hugo’s mouth twitch upward beneath his mustache.
Realizing that praise for the boy was not unwelcome, Barrymore added a little more.
“An orc can overpower four or five grown men, yet an eight-year-old killed one that easily. I doubt another family in the Empire has a child like him—”
“Hmph. Enough, Butler.”
Hugo cut Barrymore off with visible discomfort.
Then he spoke with a dissatisfied expression.
“That orc seemed somewhat strange.”
“My lord?”
“Orcs are known for their regeneration, yet that one bled excessively. Sudden blood loss inevitably dulls movement. Was there poison on Vikir’s sword?”
“That should be impossible. The Guardian Knights inspected the weapon before handing it over. It was an ordinary short sword.”
“Hmph. Then the orc must have been unusually weak.”
Hugo was not a man who accepted praise gracefully.
Barrymore gave a faint smile and shrugged.
Whatever Hugo said, Baskerville had plainly produced an extraordinary prodigy.
At only eight years old, Vikir had publicly demonstrated the level of a complete Low Sword Expert.
Everyone present, Hugo included, was still absorbing the achievement when—
“Wait.”
Vikir drew every eye back to himself.
Hugo had begun to turn away, but stopped and looked back.
Their gazes met.
Vikir spoke calmly.
“Do you have anything stronger than an orc?”
He asked for the next opponent without hesitation.
