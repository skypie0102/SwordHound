Ten people live ten different lives; a hundred people live a hundred different lives.
Every person's life is unique and cannot truly be reduced to a single formula.
But—
in Ironblood Baskerville, a person's life could be charted and organized almost like one.
A typical comparison looked like this:
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
Low Sword Expert = First-Circle Mage
Intermediate Sword Expert = Second-Circle Mage
Advanced Sword Expert = Third-Circle Mage
Low Sword Graduator = Fourth-Circle Mage
Intermediate Sword Graduator = Fifth-Circle Mage
Advanced Sword Graduator = Sixth-Circle Mage
Sword Master = Seventh-Circle Mage
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
It was a rough equivalence between the realms of swordsmanship and magic.
Even the ages at which Baskerville's young hounds advanced tended to follow a brutal pattern unlike ordinary children.
Every stage of their upbringing was harsh training in which life and death were never far apart.
As the survivors endured and absorbed those trials, their growth usually followed another familiar progression.
Put into a second formula:
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
Low Sword Expert ≈ 15 years old
Intermediate Sword Expert ≈ 18 years old
Advanced Sword Expert ≈ 20 years old
Low Sword Graduator ≈ 30 years old
Intermediate Sword Graduator ≈ 35 years old
Advanced Sword Graduator ≈ 40 years old
Sword Master = no fixed age
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
Most young Baskerville hounds advanced along something close to that path.
Children of the family usually first sensed mana at seven or eight, when concentrating it in their hands and feeling its warmth was already an accomplishment.
Only around fifteen could most of them properly channel mana into a sword and emit aura.
That was the realm of the Low Sword Expert.
The sword forms supporting that mana mattered just as much. Most Baskervilles learned the numbered Baskerville Forms.
The First Form drew one fang, the Second drew two, the Third drew three, and the sequence continued upward one fang at a time.
Since most fifteen-year-old Baskervilles who reached Sword Expert had also mastered the First Form, another formula emerged:
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
Low Sword Expert = Baskerville First Form = one fang
Intermediate Sword Expert = Baskerville Second Form = two fangs
Advanced Sword Expert = Baskerville Third Form = three fangs
Low Sword Graduator = Baskerville Fourth Form = four fangs
Intermediate Sword Graduator = Baskerville Fifth Form = five fangs
Advanced Sword Graduator = Baskerville Sixth Form = six fangs
Sword Master = Baskerville Seventh Form = seven fangs
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
But those permitted to learn the Fifth Form and beyond belonged to the elite among the elite.
They were, as a rule, the recognized direct heirs of the house.
Everyone in Baskerville knew that the current patriarch, Hugo Le Baskerville, could manifest seven fangs.
His eldest son, Osiris, who was away on assignment, could manifest five; Hugo's second son, training in seclusion, could manifest four.
By contrast, children carrying the middle name Van—collateral or illegitimate descendants outside the recognized direct line—were normally forbidden from learning the Fifth Form or anything beyond it.
No matter how much mana such a hound accumulated, the taught sword curriculum imposed a hard ceiling on the fangs they could reliably display.
Yet almost no one complained about that rule.
No one. Not one.
Baskerville swordsmanship was so formidable that mastery of even four forms was enough to shake the wider world.
…But—
there were exceptions.
Even in Baskerville, irregulars occasionally appeared who refused to fit the family's formulas.
The unfortunate irregulars were hounds who failed to keep pace with the standardized progression.
There was nothing remarkable about them. Baskerville simply disposed of failures.
Some died in training and became lessons for their siblings; some died or vanished on missions and became numbers in casualty reports; some were assassinated for family interests; a rare few succumbed to illness.
Baskerville suffered many casualties, but the number dwindled as each surviving generation grew older.
Age brought experience, and experience brought survival.
However—
a very small number of irregulars broke the formula in the opposite direction.
Even within a family famous for geniuses, a handful were acknowledged as geniuses among geniuses.
Osiris Le Baskerville, Hugo's eldest son and heir, was one such person.
He was the small sun expected to lead Baskerville in the future, a prodigy praised by virtually everyone.
Until recently, Osiris had stood alone as the greatest young genius in the family.
But now another rumor had begun to spread.
A pearly cloud over an infant star. A tiny sun rising beneath the greater sun.
People whispered that another genius had appeared beneath Osiris.
Vikir Van Baskerville. Eight years old.
According to Hugo Le Baskerville himself, Vikir had already reached the realm of an Intermediate Sword Expert.
Two fangs in swordsmanship; roughly two circles by a mage's comparison.
At eight, he had achieved what most Baskervilles did not reach until eighteen.
Even relatives who normally cared little about one another found themselves discussing Vikir whenever several gathered together.
“…There's really a child like that?”
“Whose line is he from? Van? Then even his exact parentage is unclear.”
“Worth cultivating, perhaps. If not, then perhaps it would be better to deal with him early…”
Some were curious, some indifferent, and some immediately calculated how his existence might affect their own interests.
Either way, Vikir had already bared enough of his fangs to draw the family's attention.
◆◆◆
‘Idiots.’
Vikir himself cared nothing for the looks or rumors gathering around him.
He already knew exactly where Baskerville's internal power struggles, alliances, and dirty tricks ultimately led.
How could he not? In his first life he had spent decades as a hound shifted from one faction and battlefield to another.
Plop. Plop.
Vikir watched another serving of haggis drop onto his plate.
‘I only intended to show two fangs in the first place. That's enough.’
Now that he had revealed a controlled portion of his ability, he could begin making use of more of the family's resources.
As he had already concluded, moderate recognition was useful if he wanted room to grow beyond Hugo's immediate supervision.
Vikir sat alone in a far corner of the dining hall and ate his haggis.
As the salty, gamey meat went down, he reviewed several matters in his head.
First came his actual strength.
‘Publicly, I'm an Intermediate Sword Expert who can use the Baskerville Second Form.’
In reality, Vikir's reliable combat level was already Low Sword Graduator, with four fangs he could use consistently.
The insight gained from Lurking Embedded Teeth had also put him on the threshold of Intermediate Sword Graduator, though his Fifth Fang was not yet fully stable.
At this pace, breaking through the Fifth-Form barrier would only be a matter of time.
‘I'll reach Advanced Sword Graduator before I'm seventeen.’
By then, he intended to command the Sixth Form as well—far beyond the four-form ceiling that had trapped him in his first life.
And there was more.
Wriggle—
The spoon in his hand suddenly twitched.
Beelzebub, hidden along the artery of his right wrist, had moved.
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
<Gluttonous Fly ‘Beelzebub’> / Awl
Slot 1: Burn — Cerberus (A+)
Slot 2: Bleeding — Hellhound (B+)
Slot 3: Super Regeneration — Troll (C+)
[[SOURCE-NBSP]]
Beelzebub had discarded the weaker Norvegicus regeneration and filled the third slot with the troll's superior ability.
Making an enemy bleed while rapidly regenerating one's own wounds was a vicious combination.
With Beelzebub added to his hidden strength, Vikir's practical combat ability was even more dangerous than his public rank suggested.
He considered all this while continuing to eat.
He was already on his sixth serving, and ever since taking Beelzebub his appetite seemed to grow rather than diminish.
Then—
“…Hey.”
A hesitant voice came from behind him. Vikir turned.
“?”
Three unexpected figures were standing there.
Highbro Le Baskerville, Middlebro Le Baskerville, and Lowbro Le Baskerville.
The three brothers who would later be known as Baskerville's Trident stood awkwardly behind him.
Vikir's brow furrowed.
“What do you three want?”
He saw no reason to soften the question.
When Vikir narrowed his eyes, the triplets instinctively flinched.
Was there anything easier to handle than a dog that was already afraid?
Vikir tightened his grip on the spoon he had been eating with, and all three immediately waved their hands.
“N-no! It's not like that!”
“We just wanted to…”
“When you killed Cerberus and the troll… that was really amazing!”
“…?”
Vikir frowned, genuinely unsure what he was looking at.
‘A two-faced tactic? Some kind of trick?’
But the glances passing among the brothers showed none of the calculation required for sophisticated psychological warfare.
The longer Vikir stared at them, the more awkwardly frightened they became.
Tap—
He had just shifted the spoon into a grip suitable for flicking it at three foreheads when another voice interrupted.
“Young Master.”
The voice came from his side.
Butler Barrymore stood there with his usual impeccable posture.
He had arrived without fanfare and addressed Vikir in his customary respectful tone.
“The patriarch is asking for you.”
◆◆◆
Hugo Le Baskerville was seated on a couch beside the window, visibly irritated.
“Is the butler not here yet?”
At Hugo's question, a maid quickly bowed.
“I looked down from the window. Butler Barrymore has just entered the first-floor lobby with Young Master Vikir.”
“They should be here any moment.”
Hugo nodded, then shifted his gaze to the neighboring couch.
A middle-aged man with a carefully groomed mustache sat there.
Adolf Morgue.
He was a senior representative of the Morgue Family, the great house of mages long regarded as Baskerville's rival.
Adolf was the brother of Raspane Morgue, head of the family, and frequently represented Morgue at outside functions.
Adolf lifted the teacup before him and took a sip.
Then he looked directly at Hugo with a thin smile.
“I hope this year's annual event proceeds without incident.”
The annual event was the friendly exercise held between Baskerville and Morgue.
Following an old imperial maxim that swords and magic complemented one another, the two houses trained their younger generation together once each year.
The participants were children roughly eight to fifteen years old, but the event also served as a restrained display of force before the Empire.
This year the atmosphere was colder than usual because Hugo and Raspane were already at odds over a newly disputed ruby mine near the border of their territories.
It was against that background that Adolf had personally come to Baskerville.
The official purpose was the annual exchange, though neither side believed that was the only reason for his visit.
Hugo answered as though none of it mattered.
“It's merely a joint exercise. It has always been that way.”
“Heh. Didn't one Baskerville child and one Morgue child both suffer serious injuries last year?”
“They survived and received treatment in time. How can you call that a loss?”
A vein pulsed at Adolf's forehead at Hugo's utter lack of concern.
After a strained smile and a few coughs, Adolf changed the subject.
“Ahem. Then let us discuss this year's exercise. Ah, before that—”
Just as he moved toward the real business, another thought seemed to occur to him.
“I hear a supernova has appeared in Baskerville. I admit I'm very interested to see this child.”
“…He is being brought here now.”
Hugo answered with exaggerated indifference.
Adolf's sharp eyes still caught the minute upward twitch at the corner of Hugo's mouth.
‘That lizard actually reacts when someone praises his child. How unusual.’
Adolf briefly considered reporting that curiosity to Raspane before continuing.
“For Baskerville to produce a once-in-a-century genius is surely a blessing for the Empire.”
“I would not go that far.”
Hugo offered the minimum amount of formal modesty.
Adolf's next words, however, were deliberately provocative.
“Then it is doubly fortunate. Morgue has also produced a once-in-a-century genius.”
Hugo's eyebrows shifted.
Adolf smiled and gestured toward a side door.
“Come in, Camus. Greet the head of House Baskerville.”
The door opened as though moved by magic.
A small girl entered with attendants following behind her.
Her hair burned red and her eyes shone like rubies.
She had a neat little nose, soft cheeks, and an unusually composed face for a child.
Camus Morgue.
The girl had only recently turned eight, yet she crossed the room and stood beside Adolf without showing the slightest nervousness.
Hugo's brow tightened almost imperceptibly.
She was Morgue's own once-in-a-century supernova.
Even in a prestigious family overflowing with magical talent, Camus was already spoken of as a genius.
Camus Morgue.
She held one edge of her uncle's cloak while looking around with bright, fearless eyes; even Hugo's severe expression softened a fraction.
Her cuteness aside, Adolf's point was plainly a challenge.
“We have an eight-year-old genius as well. Shall we see how Baskerville's compares?”
Morgue had brought a child-sized rivalry directly into Hugo's office.
‘Hmph. I'm not going to dignify such an obvious provocation.’
Hugo began to turn away.
Then Camus's next words forced his attention straight back.
“Are you the thief who stole our ruby mine?”
