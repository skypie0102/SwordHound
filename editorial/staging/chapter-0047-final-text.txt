Ballak—brown-skinned warriors from beyond Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.
Armed with bows and blades, they were attacking Morgue’s fortress.
Ping—
Another arrow flew, and another soldier tumbled from the rampart.
Within moments, Ballak warriors overwhelmed the skirmishers and surged over the walls.
Boom, boom!
They set fire to the cotton fields below and began dragging away slaves.
Woof! Woof!
The barking of huge wolves sounded everywhere.
Ballak warriors rode the beasts in groups of two or three, thick rope lassos coiled in their hands.
They cast the loops with practiced precision, capturing men, women, and children alive.
It looked less like battle than a hunt.
The sudden attack threw the entire fortress into chaos.
Flames spread, soldiers fell, and captives were dragged away alive.
Into that confusion stepped Vikir.
Several Ballak women brightened when they noticed him.

“?? ?? ???”
“???? ????? ???? ???????”
Three lassos spun through the air and came for Vikir’s neck at once.
A moment later,
Snap—
three thick ropes tightened around his throat.
The mounted women kicked their wolves into motion.
But,
Crack!
the wolves were forced to stop.
Vikir had not moved a single step.
Creak—
The ropes tightened around his neck, but he remained planted in place.
Then Vikir caught the lines in both hands and pulled.
Wham!
The wolves and all three riders were yanked off balance and thrown to the ground.
The expressions of the Ballak men nearby hardened.
They shouted and raised their bows toward Vikir.
Whip, whip, whip!
Arrows of astonishing speed converged on him.
But Vikir’s hands were faster.
Whoosh!
He drew his longsword and filled it with aura.
Liquid aura—the mark of a Sword Graduator—gathered into a single point at the tip.
Clang-clang-clang!
Vikir traced a figure eight with the blade and cut every incoming arrow apart.
The Ballak warriors recoiled at the sight of his aura.
They understood that this degree of control belonged only to warriors who had crossed an exceptional threshold.
Vikir narrowed his eyes at the Ballak force.
Brown skin. Hair in shades of silver, gray, and black.
Ash-darkened faces, thorned collars, huge wolves, and bows as their primary weapons.
‘Exactly as I remember them.’
Vikir had fought Ballak warriors countless times in his previous life.
They were aggressive, but more importantly, each warrior was individually formidable.
A fortified plain favored the defenders; meeting the Ballak deep inside a dark jungle would have been far more dangerous.
‘Before we left, Hugo repeatedly warned me not to engage seriously until we joined Baskerville’s main force.’
The land belonged to Baskerville but was currently leased to Morgue, so Vikir had no reason to risk his life over it.
“….”
“….”
Vikir released just enough pressure to check the warriors, and they hesitated instead of charging him blindly.
They had already seen the unnerving swordsmanship he had displayed moments earlier.
Then,
Boom!
A violent explosion sent flames surging upward.
Part of the earthen wall collapsed, and a girl emerged through the smoke.
Camus Morgue—the girl who would one day become the Ironblood Empress.
She glared at the Ballak warriors with bloodshot eyes.
“Die!”
Camus crossed her hands.
Quadruple Casting—four offensive spells manifested at once and tore across the battlefield.
Fire and wind, steel and stone combined into firestorms and showers of rock.
The Ballak riders pulled back on their wolves and shouted signals to one another.
They appeared to be preparing to retreat.
Even while driving them back, Camus glanced toward Vikir.
Her eyes fixed on the drops of liquid aura gathered at the tip of his sword.
“You’re a Sword Graduator? That’s incredible.”
Camus sounded genuinely impressed.
A Sword Graduator stood far beyond the reach of ordinary swordsmen.
Most people could train their entire lives without ever entering that realm.
Even in House Baskerville, renowned for producing sword prodigies, reaching it before thirty was exceptional.
“I knew it. You’re the only man I can acknowledge.”
Camus grinned and moved to Vikir’s side.
Apparently assuming that maintaining his aura had exhausted him, she took a protective stance.
“Stay back. It’s dangerous.”
She stepped in front of him, raising walls of steel and stone while hurling fire, ice, and other spells across the field.
She had reached the Third Circle and could still Quadruple Cast.
At fifteen, her talent fully justified her position as a future head-of-house candidate.
“I’ll avenge Rosie!”
Camus poured her mana into the retreating barbarians.
But,
the battlefield punished even geniuses who lost focus.
Pfft!
Camus flinched at a sharp sting in the back of her neck.
‘An arrow?’
If it had been a true Ballak arrow, she would have been dead before she could finish the thought.
She reached up and pulled the object free.
It was tiny and thin enough to slip between the floating defensive barriers.
A cactus thorn.
Ting—
Camus’s head began to swim.
The thorn had been coated in a paralytic poison.
“????! ??? ???? ?????!”
A Ballak warrior pointed at Camus and shouted with delight.
He was apparently the one who had fired the dart.
Now,
Whirr—
he threw the lasso in his hand toward Camus.
They clearly intended to take her alive.
But,
Snap—
the lasso stopped halfway.
Vikir had reached out and caught it in midair.
Creak…
A contest of strength began between Vikir and the Ballak warrior.
The warrior pulled with everything he had, but his strength could not match Vikir’s superhuman body.
Wham!
Vikir jerked the line and ripped the man from his wolf, sending him crashing to the ground.
‘He probably broke half the bones in his body.’
Vikir shook his head while the warrior writhed on the ground.
Then he turned back toward Camus.
“We should retreat.”
“What? What about the farmland and the captives?”
“We should avoid a full engagement until we link up with Baskerville’s main force.”
Camus looked conflicted.
Rationally, withdrawing now was the correct decision.
But,
she had only just learned what had happened to her beloved adopted sister, and grief was overpowering her judgment.
Rosie had been taken alive by a barbarian tribe and must have suffered terribly before her death.
The thought flashed through Camus’s mind, and her fists tightened.
Just then,
PING!
A piercing sound came from somewhere in the distance.
Vikir instinctively snapped his head aside.
An arrow tore past the back of his neck and struck the fortress wall.
Boom!
The impact was strong enough to punch deep into the rampart.
“…!”
Vikir looked toward the shooter.
A lone female warrior sat on the back of a huge wolf.
Her hair mixed black and silver, her ears were sharply pointed, and ash covered her face.
As if she had already expected Vikir to dodge, she immediately cast a lasso after the arrow.
The snake-like loop curved toward his throat at a precise angle.
“….”
Vikir remained calm and reached for his sword.
Blood-red Baskerville aura drew four sharp fangs through the air.
The lasso split apart in midflight.
Then,
“Danger!”
A hand struck Vikir’s back.
He turned and saw Camus’s stiffening body pushing against him.
Behind her, the Ballak man Vikir had thrown down earlier was hauling on another lasso through clenched teeth.
Vikir had assumed the impact had crippled him, but the man was tougher than expected.
The rope was cinched around Camus’s waist, and the warrior began dragging the paralyzed girl away.
‘…So it still happens.’
Vikir let out a quiet sigh.
A momentary lapse in judgment could carry a heavy price.
Morgue’s future head would learn that lesson personally.
‘In the original history, she was kidnapped by the barbarian tribes once as well.’
The same event had occurred before Vikir’s regression.
As a teenager, Camus had been taken as a prisoner and hostage.
She had eventually returned after slaughtering her way free.
That experience had contributed to the terrifying reputation she carried later in life.
‘But I still have a job to do.’
This was not strictly Baskerville’s problem, but Baskerville and Morgue were strategic allies.
Vikir released his aura at once.
Clang!
A weapon suddenly intercepted his blade.
“…A bow?”
Vikir narrowed his eyes.
The silver-and-black-haired Ballak woman had swung the full body of her bow into his sword and stopped it.
Then,
Vikir met her eyes.
A strange sense of familiarity struck him.
‘She looks familiar.’
As he searched his memory,
the woman spoke first.
“…I said we would meet again, didn’t I?”
