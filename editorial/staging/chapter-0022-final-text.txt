“Does Morgue not teach you not to covet what belongs to others?”
Vikir’s words as he entered the patriarch’s office changed the mood once again.
Adolf’s faintly amused expression hardened.
Camus glared at Vikir with open irritation.
Hugo, meanwhile, wore almost the same subtle expression Adolf had been wearing moments earlier.
“….”
“….”
It was a confrontation between two geniuses with no shortage of pride.
The once-in-a-century supernovas of the two houses faced one another in the center of the room.
Vikir studied Camus Morgue.
Red hair, ruby-bright eyes, a sharp little nose, round cheeks, a few freckles, and the unmistakable softness of childhood.
‘Is this really the Camus Morgue I knew?’
Her childhood face overlapped with the adult face in his memory.
Strip away the baby fat, add years of hardship and age, and the features would become unmistakably familiar.
The Ironblood Empress of House Morgue.
In Vikir’s first life, she had been celebrated as an unmatched genius even within Morgue, a family that had produced countless great mages.
Her magical talent was accompanied by striking beauty, and as an adult she knew exactly how to use both charm and intelligence in politics.
She eventually became a figure capable of drawing even royalty into her orbit and wielding personal influence as political power.
She did not hesitate to enter marriages of convenience when they benefited Morgue, and the family’s prestige rose with each alliance.
In her prime, she fought monsters and barbarians along Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, piercing and burning enemies and leaving a frontier marked by blood and ash.
In later years, she came to be known by the grand title of the Heavenly Empress.
“Hey! What did you just say?”
…But all of that belonged to a distant future. Right now, Camus was only eight years old.
Camus demanded an answer in an angry voice.
“Why is the ruby mine yours? It belongs to us!”
Adolf’s expression brightened while Hugo’s darkened.
Vikir dismissed Camus’s protest with a single sentence.
“You came all this way just to throw a tantrum? You must be tired.”
This time Hugo’s mouth curved while Adolf’s expression stiffened.
Meanwhile—
Camus gritted her teeth when she realized childish indignation was getting her nowhere.
She changed her manner and returned to the cool, logical attitude she was proud of.
Ssssss—
A faint chill seemed to gather around the girl.
Her innocent expression vanished as she spoke in a cold, precise voice.
“Since you dislike irrational arguments, I’ll explain logically why the ruby mine belongs to Morgue.”
Camus produced a map and began explaining the terrain around the mine.
“The only practical surface entrance to the ruby vein lies in Morgue territory. Baskerville cannot mine it independently, so it is more profitable to grant Morgue the underground mining rights in exchange for compensation for use of the land.”
She was the cherished direct daughter of a prestigious mage family and had rarely, if ever, been defeated in an argument.
Her reasoning was sharp enough to make many adults hesitate, much less children her own age.
But Camus’s confidence was immediately challenged.
“Rubies are mainly useful as magical materials. Baskerville has little need to mine them—but opening Baskerville territory merely for rent would be the real foolishness.”
Camus stared at Vikir.
For the first time, she had met someone her own age who simply refused to accept her logic.
Weren’t boys her age usually dull and easy to dismiss? The unfamiliar resistance confused her.
Meanwhile—
the exchange between Camus and Vikir sounded nothing like an ordinary argument between eight-year-olds.
Both children understood the competing interests of Morgue and Baskerville well enough that even Adolf and Hugo listened with interest.
Camus narrowed her eyes.
“How strange. I’ve never met anyone like you. There’s no one my age in Morgue who can actually argue with me.”
“Then go outside more.”
“…Excuse me? I’ve inspected the estate plenty of times already. What makes you think you know anything about my life?”
“I don’t care about your life. We’re talking about the ruby mine. If you want to change the subject, do it by yourself.”
Vikir’s complete indifference only made Camus’s eyes burn brighter.
‘He’s the first boy I’ve ever met who treats me this indifferently!’ It was a clichéd sentiment, but no less real for being common.
And in Camus’s case, it was understandable.
As a direct descendant of Morgue, she had grown up surrounded by attention and indulgence.
She had almost never received such blunt dismissal from anyone her own age.
Worse, this was an argument she believed she absolutely could not lose.
Her unbroken pride flared hotter.
Camus pressed the issue.
“The mine entrance is in Morgue territory. The mining rights therefore belong to Morgue!”
“The vein extends beneath Baskerville territory. The mining rights therefore belong to Baskerville.”
“Baskerville doesn’t even care about rubies!”
“Correct. Baskerville cares about its territory, which is exactly why it doesn’t want you digging through it. Is that difficult to understand?”
Morgue wanted the ruby; Baskerville wanted control of the land. Their interests simply did not align.
The adults behind them held essentially the same opposing positions, which was why the dispute had remained unresolved.
Then—
Camus’s ruby eyes flashed.
“Fine. I’ll show you exactly who the ruby vein belongs to!”
Camus snatched up a brush and ink.
She began drawing on the floor.
She drew one large circle around Vikir and another around herself.
The two circles touched at their edges.
Then Camus had a large sheet of paper raised between the two circles as a dividing wall.
The paper was so large that two servants had to hold it upright from either side.
From the other side of the paper wall, Camus spoke.
“This side is your land, and this side is mine.”
“….”
Vikir gave no reply.
Hugo and Adolf watched with interest as the two eight-year-olds continued their territorial argument.
Then—
Rip!
The paper wall tore open.
Camus pushed one arm through the opening.
Her small pale arm crossed the paper boundary and entered Vikir’s circle.
Camus asked Vikir a question.
“Now, whose arm is this?”
Adolf’s face brightened at the analogy.
Hugo’s expression crumpled.
Camus’s point was obvious.
Even after crossing the boundary, the arm remained hers; by her logic, a ruby vein crossing beneath a border remained Morgue’s as well.
It was the same old argument used when fruit from one person’s tree grew across a neighbor’s fence.
But—
“…Hmm. Who does this arm belong to?”
Vikir was not inclined to play along politely.
Grab!
One strong hand—far too powerful for an ordinary eight-year-old—closed around Camus’s forearm.
Before Camus could cry out, Vikir answered first.
“It’s mine now.”
An immense pressure radiated from Vikir in an instant.
It was not mana and not the aura of a sword rank; it came from the weight of his existence and experience.
It was the killing presence of a hunter who had survived the Age of Destruction.
For a moment, even Adolf and Hugo reacted to it.
Eight-year-old Camus had even less chance of ignoring it.
“…What?”
Camus tried to pull her arm free, but Vikir’s grip did not budge.
Rip—
The paper tore wider as Vikir pulled her toward him.
The distance between them shrank until their faces were almost touching.
“…!”
Camus’s eyes wavered for a moment.
She had never been this close to another child outside her own clan, especially a boy her own age.
Beyond the torn paper, Vikir’s face looked both ordinary and strangely unfamiliar to her.
Dark hair, dark eyes, pale skin.
His scent was unlike the people she had grown up around, and his breathing was so quiet she could barely hear it.
The unfamiliar closeness startled her badly enough to knock the argument out of her mind for a moment.
For an eight-year-old who had spent most of her life within Morgue, the experience was entirely new.
‘…He’s kind of cute.’
The thought crossed Camus’s mind before she could stop it.
For a moment, she forgot the seriousness of the dispute.
That moment of confusion made her blush and try to pull back.
“W-why am I yours? Don’t be ridiculous!”
She lowered her gaze, flustered enough that it was hard to tell whether her protest had any force behind it.
Adolf and Hugo watched with complicated expressions.
“Hmph.”
“Ahem.”
The mixture of serious diplomacy and the awkward emotions of two eight-year-olds created an atmosphere none of the adults quite knew how to handle.
Just then—
Vikir broke the awkward silence.
“Who said you were mine?”
His grip tightened at the same time.
Camus was pulled firmly back toward him.
Before she could shout in surprise, Vikir’s other hand moved.
“Your arm is mine.”
As he spoke, Vikir drew something from his waist with lightning speed.
It looked like a sharpened dagger.
The sight was shocking enough that Camus, Adolf, and even the normally expressionless Hugo all reacted at once.
