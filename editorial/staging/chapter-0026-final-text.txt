Many people were still born into Baskerville, and many still died.
The River Styx flowed on, one generation pushing the next forward.
Seven years had passed since the day two eight-year-old prodigies faced each other.
◆◆◆
“This instructor can be an angel or a devil depending on what you do!”
A young hound with a short beard shouted in a rough voice.
His name was Staffordshire Baskerville. Thirty-five years old, he was a veteran knight at the Intermediate Sword Graduator level.
He had not been born into the direct or collateral bloodline and had once been an outsider without even a Van middle name, but exceptional talent and upright service had earned him the Baskerville surname.
His Stabbing Fang swordsmanship was said to have reached a perfected Baskerville Fourth Form.
Four fangs, drawn cleanly and reliably.
That combination of strength and experience was more than enough to qualify him to guide the younger Baskervilles.
“Now! This is the final exercise. Train as though it were real combat—you all know that by now!”
Staffordshire looked over the fifteen- and sixteen-year-old Baskervilles before him.
They were exhausted, but the cold resolve in their eyes had grown sharper with every year.
Every trainee here had reached at least Low Sword Expert and could manifest a proper First Fang.
After checking their formation, Staffordshire turned his attention forward.
A gigantic bear stood on its hind legs in the middle of the training ground.
The captive animal weighed roughly eight hundred kilograms and stood around three meters tall in its current sub-adult state, but there was something even more striking about it.
Two sharply curved horns rose from its head.

Among land predators, the oxbear was a near-perfect killing machine and one of the dominant creatures of the Seventh Ridge ecosystem.
It had almost no natural enemies except other monsters of comparable rank.
Two enormous buffalo-like horns curved from its head.
Thick muscle, heavy fat, coarse fur, and dense hide covered its entire body.
[Grrrrr…]
The oxbear in the training ground was not fully grown.
It had also spent a long time in captivity and was poorly nourished.
That only made it more irritable and aggressive.
Staffordshire swung his sword to keep the creature’s attention while speaking to the trainees behind him.
“Do exactly what you practiced on monster corpses. You dissected an oxbear before—you know how tough the hide is, how dense the muscles are, and where the organs sit.”
The normally mild-looking Staffordshire became a different man in front of the monster.
Killing intent sharpened his eyes, and the aura at his sword tip carried oppressive weight.
His presence was strong enough to dominate the oxbear, but he deliberately did not attack.
This was not his hunt.
Then—
[GRAAAH!]
The oxbear charged.
It drove forward like a buffalo, horns leading.
The young Baskervilles responded according to their instincts.
Some scattered left and right while others tried to meet it head-on with their swords.
BANG!
Those who evaded stayed standing; those who challenged the charge directly were thrown aside.
Staffordshire continued instructing them.
“When something stronger than you charges head-on, dodging is natural. But don’t flee too far. Slip to the side and take a small bite as it passes.”
The Guide Hound’s instruction continued without pause.
The young hounds absorbed every word.
“Don’t get greedy for a killing blow at the start. You’ll invite a counterattack. Attack the target’s center of balance first—here, the right hind leg—so its movement and reactions deteriorate.”
Following their instructor’s directions, the young hounds repeatedly attacked the bear’s legs whenever it rose onto its hindquarters.
By concentrating damage on one hind leg, they widened the wound until the limb was almost useless.
That effectively sealed the monster’s most dangerous horn charge.
Only then did the young Baskervilles bare their teeth in earnest.
They had become hounds capable of continuing the hunt without Staffordshire directing every movement.
Baskerville First Form—more than thirty individual fangs tore into the oxbear from all directions.
The most aggressive among them were the triplets: Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro.
Slash! Thud! Slash!
They hunted the bear like the dominant members of the pack.
They shoved aside siblings who rushed ahead recklessly and those too slow or frightened to keep formation.
Then—
THUD!
The oxbear collapsed.
“Well done.”
Staffordshire raised his sword and walked toward the carcass.
Slash!
He split open the abdomen, exposing steaming flesh and organs.
After the fourth training period, lunchtime followed naturally.
Staffordshire distributed the oxbear’s meat and organs to the young hounds on the spot.
Red chunks of meat still steamed in the air.
The young Baskervilles tore into the raw flesh without the hesitation ordinary people would have shown.
Staffordshire spoke while they ate.
“Listen while you eat. As of this hour, three and a half years of basic training and three and a half years of practical training are complete. Congratulations on graduating.”
Seven years had turned the children who entered training into young warriors.
Every trainee present could now channel mana into a sword and draw at least one proper fang.
But Staffordshire made it clear that this was only the beginning.
“Practice against captured monsters is little more than a tutorial. From now on, you have to build real experience yourselves.”
Advice from an old hound covered in scars was worth remembering.
“The wild creatures of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain are nothing like monsters left in cages for years. They are healthy, cunning, calm, and experienced. Sometimes even I encounter old individuals I would rather not fight carelessly.”
The oxbear they had just killed was a perfect example of the difference captivity made.
A fully grown wild oxbear combining its horns, forepaws, jaws, and experience could threaten even accomplished knights.
Controlled variables, a safe environment, and fixed rules belonged to training.
The unexplored wilderness ahead would erase those guarantees.
That was where the young hounds had to go next.
They already understood that much.
Staffordshire gestured toward the countless scars marking his face.
“You’ll face the hell ahead on your own. Survive through the blood you inherited or through the experience you earn yourselves. I have nothing more to teach you. That is all!”
With a short Baskerville salute, he relinquished his role as their Guide Hound.
The young Baskervilles briefly stopped tearing at the meat and organs in their hands.
They steeled themselves for roughly twenty-one months of work in the unexplored frontier.
Once Staffordshire was out of sight, the graduates gathered into small groups and began talking.
“Which knight orders are you applying to?”
“First choice Doberman. Second Pit Bull. Third Rottweiler.”
“None of those overlap with mine. I’m thinking Wolfhound, then Shepherd, then Bull Mastiff.”
The Seven Knight Orders of Baskerville.
Each order served a different purpose—watching, searching, guarding, assassination, suppression, or open warfare.
Among them, the Wolfhound Knights stood at the pinnacle and symbolized the military power of the house.
“I want the Wolfhounds.”
“You don’t get in just because you want to. They choose you.”
“I’ll be realistic and apply to the Dobermans. Outside the Wolfhounds, that’s one of the more attainable paths.”
“If I join the Dobermans, I’ll spend my life watching walls. I’d rather join the Pit Bulls and stay on the battlefield.”
The children continued murmuring about their futures.
Naturally, their gazes began converging on one place.
“…But where is he going?”
That was the question shared by almost everyone present.
Even Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro—who had been the most active in the group hunt—put down their meat and looked over.
Vikir Van Baskerville.
A boy of uncertain maternal line, widely rumored to be one of Hugo’s illegitimate sons.
He had not participated in the group oxbear hunt at all and instead sat alone some distance away.
At Vikir’s feet lay another dead oxbear.
It was far larger than the captive sub-adult more than thirty trainees had just killed together.
The other young Baskervilles stared at him with open awe.
“Is he really fifteen like us?”
“How does one person kill an oxbear alone?”
“I heard he’s already an Advanced Sword Expert.”
“They say he can show three fangs too.”
“Three Baskerville Forms at fifteen? That’s practically Guide-Hound level!”
Admiration for strength existed everywhere, but nowhere was it more direct than in Baskerville.
Vikir paid little attention to their whispers.
‘Advanced Sword Expert.’
At that stage, the aura at a sword tip had become so dense that it hovered near the boundary between gas and liquid.
Even direct-line Baskervilles commonly reached that realm only somewhere around their early twenties; Vikir was publicly believed to have reached it at fifteen.
…But—
Vikir’s actual hidden realm had already advanced beyond that public reputation.
His aura had reached **Intermediate Sword Graduator**, two major steps above Advanced Sword Expert and comparable in raw realm to Staffordshire.
With the River Styx’s blessing and the demonic artifact Beelzebub added on top, his real combat ability was more dangerous still.
If the family understood the full extent of it, the consequences would be enormous.
‘And now I can use five Baskerville Forms. In swordsmanship alone, I’ve already surpassed my first life.’
Before regression, Vikir had never been taught beyond the Fourth Form.
Because his current body and mana still had to catch up with the new sword theory, perfecting Intermediate Sword Graduator would take somewhat longer than he had once hoped.
Vikir closed his eyes and recalled the oxbear he had just killed.
The wild monster had been a difficult target, but by using his hidden strength he had brought it down alone.
He quietly measured his current body against his first-life peak and recalculated how much remained to be recovered.
The chatter of young hounds only beginning to bare their fangs held little interest for him.
Then—
one topic finally caught Vikir’s attention.
A nearby graduate mentioned it casually.
“…So someone from our generation is going to the Academy?”
The Imperial Academy, Colosseo.
It was an elite compulsory school where the Seven Great Families sent selected heirs and talented youths for advanced education.
Because admission capacity was limited, each family could send only a small number of students.
Naturally, only the most promising young people were chosen, and that made the subject relevant to Vikir.
He had no interest in prestige and little need for ordinary academic instruction.
‘But it would give me a chance to get away from Hugo and strengthen myself independently.’
There were other reasons as well: debts from his first life, opportunities to seize future resources, and artifacts he knew would eventually appear.
As Vikir considered the possibilities—
“Young Master.”
A voice called from behind him.
It was an older voice, still strong, carried by a warm and gentle manner.
Butler John Barrymore came to stand beside Vikir.
Barrymore was one of the very few people Vikir had regretted hurting or disappointing in his first life, and over the years Vikir had come to trust him.
The butler leaned closer and lowered his voice.
“My lord wishes to see you.”
Hugo Le Baskerville.
It remained unusual for the patriarch to summon one of his children personally.
At Vikir’s questioning look, Barrymore gave a faint smile.
“I believe he has decided your position.”
