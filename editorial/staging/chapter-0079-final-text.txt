Some time later—
Dolores had been thinking constantly as reports of new patients increased.
“Divine healing treats the people already sick, but it does not stop new infections.”
Healing individual victims did nothing to remove the source.
As long as one or more wells remained contaminated, new cases would continue appearing.
The slum’s wells drew from connected underground water, so the outbreak could not truly end until the water source itself was purified.
“There are forty-two registered wells, plus however many unofficial wells the residents dug themselves…”
Dolores worried over the scale of the problem.
The practical answer was to focus divine power into the deepest connected water source.
Quovadis possessed a method for creating a concentrated purification essence.
Priests could compress holy power into a small solid form and release it into the water so that purification spread through the connected groundwater.
The Quovadis clergy immediately began testing that approach.
Multiple trials were organized for a concentrated water-purification essence.
If the trials succeeded, the slum outbreak could be ended at its source.
While other priests concentrated their power into the purification work, Dolores continued treating the most urgent cases.
“This really is a terrible disease.”
Even people who had been healed could apparently be infected again.
Its hidden period could be short while the suffering before death could stretch much longer.
To Dolores, the symptoms seemed almost deliberately assembled from the worst features of many diseases.
It felt malicious by design.
The Red Death seemed built to prolong pain rather than merely kill quickly.
“…I cannot sit here. There is still another person to heal.”
More patients were arriving even now.
Dolores rose again only minutes after sitting down.
Then—
Sway.
Her footing faltered.
Even Dolores, born with immense holy power, was reaching the limits of overwork.
“I can still continue.”
She forced her exhausted body out of the temporary quarters as soon as a little strength returned.
Every minute mattered to people still suffering.
Then—
“The saintess is coming!”
“What a holy sight!”
“Saintess, we are with you!”
“You have nothing to fear while we are here!”
Several men followed Dolores toward the slums.
They were nobles or sons of powerful noble houses.
Many were the same petitioners who had been denied entry to her residence earlier.
“…Why are you here?”
Dolores asked them.
Her blond hair, clear blue eyes, and calm voice immediately drew their attention.
Even hearing her speak was enough to excite them.
Then the competition for her approval began.
“I came to offer my strength to the saintess!”
“How could a noble lady enter such a filthy district alone?”
“We brought carts of donations and supplies. Tell the poor to gather!”
“I would follow the saintess anywhere, no matter how miserable the place.”
Each young man tried to outdo the others.
Dolores answered their admiration with a cold look.
When she kept walking without dismissing them outright, they interpreted silence as permission to follow.
“I have never actually been somewhere like this. It is an interesting experience.”
“I thought everyone in the slums would look half-starved, but they look surprisingly ordinary.”
“Some of those children even have decent clothes and shoes. Are these people really that poor?”
“I hope our donations are not so generous that they lose the desire to work for themselves.”
Servants hauled carts of relief goods behind them.
Eventually Dolores reached the temporary ward holding infected patients.
She pushed through the curtain without hesitation.
The young nobles stopped at the entrance.
“Is it safe? There are plague victims in there.”
“If I get infected… my family line…”
“The saintess can heal us if anything happens.”
“The brave are rewarded. I am going in.”
Several immediately backed away.
Others took a breath and forced themselves inside.
The ones who entered began gagging almost at once.
The ward reeked of sweat, blood, vomit, waste, unwashed bodies, and fever-heated air.
Red Death seemed to hang over the room like a visible specter.
The heat, stench, cries, and moaning made the ward feel like a living tomb.
“Ugh…”
These sheltered heirs had never stood inside suffering this raw.
Splash.
Waste from a nearby patient struck the floor.
Droplets splashed onto expensive shoes and clothing, and the young men panicked.
“Disgusting! Filthy!”
“Do you know how much these shoes cost?”
“Priest! Treat me first! I may be infected!”
“Open the way! I am leaving!”
Before they had even adjusted to the ward’s air, nearly all of them fled.
Dolores watched them go with quiet disappointment.
“…As expected.”
Her beauty had been famous for years.
Inside Quovadis, outside the family, and at the Academy, men constantly noticed her.
Wherever she went, admiration followed.
Few dared openly court the saintess, but she could always feel expectation behind their gazes.
The same sort of men often followed whenever she volunteered during vacations or free days.
Many recoiled from sick people or openly displayed contempt.
Some even tried to remove unpleasant patients when Dolores was not looking.
That history made her distrust people whose charity appeared only when they wanted her attention.
If she became ugly, filthy, or unpleasant to look at, she suspected many of those admirers would vanish.
Bringing romantic ambition or vanity into a relief ward felt profane to her.
In a place where people were fighting for life, attention should remain on the patients.
Selfless goodness, sacrifice, service, kindness, and compassion defined the ideal Dolores tried to follow.
To her, those qualities were the closest human reflection of divine grace.
Dolores had long committed herself to living as a seeker and servant rather than a social ornament.
With renewed resolve, she went deeper into the ward.
The farther she walked, the worse the heat and smell became.
Cries of pain mixed with the oppressive presence of the Red Death.
Drawing on her remaining holy power and stamina, Dolores approached the isolation area holding the worst cases.
Even veteran priests found this section difficult to endure.
Then—
“…!”
Dolores stopped in surprise.
The innermost ward, where she expected only exhaustion and despair, was unexpectedly full of purposeful movement.
The patients were still terribly ill, but the atmosphere was different.
“Bring more fluid support!”
“Holy power is not the only thing that can keep someone alive. Get the instruments.”
“…Incredible.”
Priests nearby watched with open admiration.
Their energy was completely different from the exhausted workers in the outer wards.
The harshest place in the camp somehow contained the strongest sense of life.
One man was working alone at the center of the filth and danger.
Covered in grime, he checked patients without hesitation and gave practical first aid even when pain made them curse or strike out.
The Hound of the Night.
He moved rapidly from patient to patient while directing the priests around him.
