St. Mecca held about 160,000 people and served as one of House Quovadis’s central cities, famous for clean water and surrounding forests.
People moved solemnly along streets paved in white marble.
Whenever the great clock tower rang, pedestrians stopped and prayed toward the Quovadis holy tablet visible above the official residence.
Even dropped gold was left untouched, and public order was maintained through strict religious custom.
“…I would suffocate living here.”
Vikir looked down from the roof of a tall building.
Men and women alike wore white coverings that concealed almost everything except their eyes.
Quovadis culture strictly suppressed displays of desire and greed.
Fashion, exposed skin, and bright color were almost absent.
The contrast with Ballak’s physically free jungle culture could not have been greater.
After two years among Ballak, Vikir found St. Mecca unnaturally rigid.
He waited until night before heading toward the slums far from the city center.
Broken windows, cracked walls, stagnant low ground, and steep stairs replaced the polished avenues.
Slums looked much the same everywhere, even under the authority of Quovadis.
Here, however, many residents had been pushed outward for religious offenses or minor misconduct.
They had committed nothing serious enough for prison but were still barred from the sacred center.
Checkpoints and paladins filtered them into the outskirts.
Vikir climbed a steep stairway into the highest, darkest part of the district.
It was the most isolated section of the slums.
Then twelve bells rang through the darkness: midnight.
Few people remained outside, and those who did immediately bowed toward the Quovadis residence.
Only the Hound of the Night remained standing.
Vikir moved through the darkness to a well and produced a sealed vial.
Inside was a sample carrying the fictional Red Death taken from Aheul’s symptoms.
He emptied it into the well.
The act was intended to force Quovadis healers to confront the same disease threatening Ballak.
“They will react soon enough.”
From Vikir’s prior knowledge, the Red Death could remain hidden for days before symptoms appeared.
Different exposure routes changed how quickly signs developed.
‘This is Quovadis territory. They should be able to contain it before anyone dies.’
Vikir paused, judging when to move to the next part of the plan.
Then—
“…!”
A group of children approached the well.
They carried buckets and insect-catching tools.
The oldest child peered toward the shadowed figure.
“Is someone there?”
The children stopped near the well, unable to make out more than Vikir’s silhouette.
It was a moonless night, and his face was hidden behind a mask.
Recognition was unlikely, but Vikir refused to take chances.
He let killing intent leak through the disguise.
A red glow shone through the mask lenses.
[I have cursed this well.]
The children collapsed in terror.
Vikir gave them a harsher warning.
[Anyone who drinks from this well will die.]
The warning was unnecessary for the deception itself.
He simply did not want children caught in the scheme.
“It’s a ghost! A demon!”
“The well is cursed!”
“I’m never drinking from it!”
The children fled.
After making sure no one remained nearby, Vikir buried an object beside the well.
It was the serpent-marked evidence recovered from the earlier contaminators.
The emblem belonged to House Leviathan.
Vikir believed Leviathan had deliberately triggered the original Red Death outbreak in the frontier.
“Let’s see how you answer each other.”
Quovadis and Leviathan had been hostile rivals for generations.
If the same disease appeared in Quovadis territory alongside Leviathan evidence, that rivalry would immediately sharpen.
Quovadis should suppress the local outbreak quickly, but the political aftermath would be another matter.
“I need to move.”
People were already approaching the district to draw water.
Vikir wanted Quovadis attention before innocent residents suffered serious harm.
He also needed a cure for Aheul as quickly as possible.
◆◆◆
The Hound of the Night moved to the Quovadis official residence in central St. Mecca.
Several carriages were parked outside despite the late hour.
Illness did not wait for daytime.
Even at midnight, people sought the saint’s healing.
Most visitors were nobles or wealthy merchants arriving in expensive carriages, their fine clothing contrasting with sick faces.
“Saint! We heard you were resting here. Please receive us!”
“Please examine my illness just once!”
“Let me see the saint!”
Word had spread that Dolores, a direct Quovadis descendant, was staying in St. Mecca.
Dolores was the pride of Quovadis and a second-year Imperial Academy student serving as vice president of the student council.
Her school vacation had drawn wealthy petitioners from across the region.
Nearly all of them were highborn or rich.
Amid the repeated knocking, a voice finally came from behind the door.
“…Who are you, and why have you come at this hour?”
The voice was gentle and feminine but carried unmistakable authority.
It sounded exactly like what one expected from a saint.
The waiting nobles and merchants brightened at hearing Dolores herself.
Then they rushed to answer.
One recited an endless chain of noble relations and knightly connections.
Another listed guild offices, civic titles, and prestigious patrons.
A third offered estates, villas, and carriages in exchange for treatment.
But—
No amount of status or wealth opened the gate.
Dolores kept the door closed.
“Your wealth and lineage are not reasons for me to open this door.”
One by one, the petitioners left exhausted after loudly advertising their importance.
As the night deepened, the line vanished until Vikir alone remained.
The residence fell completely quiet.
Vikir stepped forward and knocked politely.
Dolores answered again.
“Who are you, and what brings you here at this hour?”
Vikir considered his answer.
Everyone who had announced wealth, family, and status had failed.
“That is not what a saint wants to hear.”
Vikir already knew the kind of person Dolores was.
Before regression, he had seen her on the worst battlefields.
She had treated wounded soldiers while covered in blood herself.
White holy power had poured from robes stained red by other people’s wounds.
Even from a distance, the sight had been solemn and extraordinary.
So Vikir answered simply.
“…Only a lost lamb.”
Silence followed from behind the door.
Vikir waited.
Then—
Click.
The Quovadis gate that had remained closed to everyone else opened.
