Several days had passed since Vikir returned to Ballak.
The Red Death itself had been suppressed, but the damage it left behind was far from healed.
Many survivors were weak from dehydration and digestive illness, so the recovering patients needed large amounts of nourishing food.
Ballak’s healthy hunters therefore spent every day gathering meat and medicine for their families and friends.
Vikir and Aiyen were among those hunters.
“…Shh. Found it.”
Aiyen spotted the prey first.
Vikir’s own senses had sharpened enough that he located it almost immediately.
Two years earlier, Aiyen would point at distant prey while Vikir struggled to see what she meant.
Now his tracking and observation were nearly on her level.
The animal they were stalking was one they had encountered before.
Woooooo…
High on a branch, an enormous snake was swallowing a jaguar whole.
It had the broad mouth of a catfish, a flattened snout, and rows of sharp teeth.

The species was infamous for its grotesquely long digestive body.
Its mouth could swallow enormous prey in a single bite, and legends claimed ancient individuals could devour entire settlements.
Despite their size, Mushuhushu moved almost silently over land and through water.
At first glance, the creature barely resembled an ordinary snake.
This particular old individual was far larger than most of its kind.
Exceptional Mushuhushu could reach around forty meters and weigh several tons.
Ordinary adults usually stopped growing well before that size.
Aiyen studied the monster carefully.
“His name is Ka’ah. Around here, only Gustav, the old swamp lizard, can really contest him.”
Her caution was justified.
Ka’ah’s size was dangerous enough, but his near-silent movement made him an exceptional ambush predator.
Camouflage, silence, strength, and sheer mass had kept him dominant in this part of the jungle for years.
Today, however, he had met opponents who remembered him.
Vikir bared his teeth.
“You’re old. Time to repay the debt from the rainy season.”
Ka’ah had attacked Ballak during the great flood.
On that night of torrential rain, the swollen river had carried him directly into the village.
Vikir had fought the snake then, but the flood prevented him from finishing it.
He had only managed to drive Ka’ah away before the current separated them.
“Now the water’s down and he’s back in his territory. He’s clearly recovered too—look at that belly.”
Aiyen pointed toward the middle of the snake.
The jaguar was not the only recent meal inside him; several huge bulges showed that other animals had already been swallowed.
The prey formed a chain of swollen shapes along Ka’ah’s body.
Aiyen raised her bow.
“If we kill him, we get the snake meat and whatever he swallowed. The half-digested prey will be tender enough for the recovering patients.”
“Agreed.”
Vikir nocked an arrow.
Aiyen nudged him and drew her own bowstring.
“People forget how much muscle a serious bow takes.”
Her weapon made the point for her.
A real war bow demanded more upper-body strength than many blunt weapons.
Aiyen’s specialized bow was even stranger, using multiple strings and allowing several arrows to be loosed together.
Drawing it required extraordinary strength.
For most fighters, it would have been harder than swinging a sword or mace.
Then—
Puff-puff-puff.
Aura-laden arrows tore toward Ka’ah’s throat.
[SHHHHHHH!]
By the time the snake reacted, the arrows were already embedded around its neck.
Pit.
Vikir drew Beelzebub from his wrist.
Ka’ah lunged toward Aiyen, but Vikir had expected exactly that response.
The instant the snake closed the distance, Vikir slipped under its jaw and cut upward.
Snap.
Scales broke and blood sprayed.
At Vikir’s current Graduator level, his aura had grown dense and viscous enough to look almost like liquid blood.
He stayed close, dodging Ka’ah’s violent twists while repeatedly driving the blade into vulnerable flesh.
Ka’ah finally changed tactics.
[WOOSH! WOOSH!]
The snake began retching.
Vikir understood immediately.
“…It’s trying to vomit up its prey to regain mobility.”
Large serpents commonly emptied their stomachs when a recent meal made escape impossible.
After feeding, such creatures could remain sluggish for long periods; in sudden danger, expelling that weight was the fastest way to move freely again.
Ka’ah had spent the flood displaced from his territory and likely unable to hunt normally.
He had also burned enormous energy surviving the flood and fighting Vikir.
Now that he was back, he had been eating heavily to recover.
That full stomach made his movement slower and more awkward.
[Gurgle… gurgle…]
Ka’ah tried to force the swallowed carcasses back up.
Losing them would shed an enormous amount of weight.
But—
“No chance.”
Aiyen interrupted the attempt.
She fired again and again toward the snake’s throat.
Her true target was the jaguar Ka’ah had swallowed moments earlier.
Her arrows entered through damaged scales and pinned the jaguar carcass inside Ka’ah’s esophagus.
The fixed carcass became a plug.
Everything else Ka’ah tried to vomit piled up behind it.
The snake’s neck swelled grotesquely as the trapped mass backed up inside.
Its movement became even more unnatural, and the swollen body restricted how freely it could twist.
That was perfect for Vikir, who had been targeting the back of the neck and underside of the jaw from the beginning.
Kwasak!
Beelzebub severed the nerve cord linking Ka’ah’s spine and brain.
Fluid and blood burst from the wound.
Six Baskerville fangs dug into the opening without mercy.
Pow! Pow! Thud!
Vikir kept striking the same damaged area.
He tore through flesh, shattered bone, and severed nerves until the old predator could no longer control its body.
Meanwhile, Aiyen kept firing into the rear half of the snake to keep it off balance.
Boom!
Another cluster of arrows struck deep into Ka’ah’s vulnerable underside.
“How’s that?”
“….”
Vikir could only sigh at how relaxed Aiyen sounded in the middle of the fight.
Then—
THUMP!
The enormous Mushuhushu crashed to the ground.
For the first time in a while, Ballak had brought down truly massive prey.
◆◆◆
Ballak’s hunters gathered for a communal meal and recovery effort.
Everyone had been working to restore strength to those weakened by the plague.
Fish fattened by the rainy season, river crustaceans, mushrooms, roots, berries, and medicinal plants all went into the food.
Until now, the sub-adult oxbear Ahun had caught had been the largest recent contribution.
Then Vikir, Aiyen, and Bakira returned with Ka’ah.
Ahun slapped Vikir on the back and laughed.
“No matter what I do, I still can’t beat you! Hahaha!”
It took a huge number of Ballak warriors and wolves to move the snake.
The old ruler of that region had been reduced to an enormous pile of usable meat.
The lean cuts were roasted, smoked, steamed, and fried.
Bones went into broth, medicine, and tools.
Blood and organs were cooked into rich soups.
The fatty snake meat restored strength quickly to the recovering patients.
Even the animals inside Ka’ah’s stomach provided soft meat suitable for people who still had trouble eating.
The village showered Vikir with gratitude.
But Vikir’s attention was elsewhere.
“I’m glad we caught the Mushuhushu. It paid off.”
The real jackpot was inside Beelzebub.
One of the weapon’s three ability slots now held Ka’ah’s soul.

Ka’ah’s ability had been absorbed.
The new passive reproduced the snake’s ability to move without making sound.
“…Silent Heel.”
Even an ordinary step could now be muted almost completely.
For Vikir, that ability had obvious value in survival, infiltration, assassination, ambush, and escape.
Combined with his archery and Ballak movement techniques, it would be exceptionally useful later.
“It resembles the sound-suppression magic Adolf used back then.”
Adolf’s silencing magic had been extremely effective during the old clash over Camus.
Vikir watched the corresponding Beelzebub bead glow with the Mushuhushu’s captured power.
Then—
A shout drew the entire village’s attention.
“The shaman has finished his ritual!”
The sentry’s announcement changed the atmosphere immediately.
Ahmen had entered the temple at the beginning of the Red Death crisis claiming he would heal the tribe through sacrifice, and now, after all this time, he had finally emerged.
