What a shocking sight.
Hic—!
Even Hugo Le Baskerville, patriarch of the Ironblood Sword Hounds, hiccupped in surprise.
Vikir’s action had been that sudden.
“Aaaaah!”
Camus squeezed her eyes shut and screamed. As far as she knew, her arm was about to be cut off.
Faced with a crisis unlike anything she had experienced before, she cried out like the frightened child she still was.
Even Adolf, her uncle, had never seen the normally fearless Camus cry like this.
“S-stop!”
Adolf instinctively released his mana.
The pressure of a Sixth Circle Master surged toward Vikir.
But—
Swish.
As though he had expected exactly that response, Vikir immediately released Camus’s arm and sprang backward.
“U-Uncle! He almost cut off my arm…!”
Camus ran to Adolf and buried her face in his cloak.
Adolf stared at Vikir in disbelief before turning sharply toward Hugo.
“My lord! What is the meaning of this?”
Hugo ignored Adolf’s protest and merely tilted his head as he looked down at Vikir.
“Son.”
At the word, Vikir answered politely.
“Yes, Father.”
“That prank went a little too far.”
“I apologize. It is a fairly common prank among Baskerville brothers.”
Vikir then took the apparent dagger by its tip and bent it.
Bend—
The blade folded easily. It was nothing more than a crude rubber toy.
Adolf stared at it and shouted in disbelief.
“W-what? A toy knife?”
“Have you never seen boys play with toy swords? Baskerville children handle things like that from the time they can walk.”
Only then did Adolf look carefully at what Vikir had been holding.
It was an obviously fake rubber knife, the sort of harmless toy even ordinary children might own.
Adolf’s mistake had been allowing Vikir’s sudden killing intent and movement to keep him from examining the weapon itself.
Seeing the famously careful and composed Adolf stumble so badly gave Hugo a small measure of satisfaction.
Hugo promptly forgot that he himself had been startled a moment earlier.
“I’ve already thought of an answer concerning the ruby mine. Morgue may even like it.”
“….”
“So let us finish this friendly exercise first.”
Hugo made no effort to hide that he wanted the current conversation over with.
Adolf, still dumbfounded, tried to complain under his breath.
“…That brat. I’m not letting this go. Just wait.”
Unfortunately, he had little room to protest while Camus was sniffling into his cloak.
Thus an important meeting between two great houses was absurdly interrupted by a quarrel between eight-year-olds.
◆◆◆
‘Magic and the sword may oppose one another in ordinary times, but in a crisis they complement each other and save the nation together.’
In accordance with that old imperial ideal, Morgue and Baskerville held a friendly joint competition each year.
Children from roughly eight to fifteen gathered to test their abilities against one another.
Traditionally, the fifteen-year-olds drew the most attention with serious sword-and-magic bouts, while the eight-year-olds were limited mostly to theory and mana sensitivity.
…But—
this year, everyone’s attention had shifted elsewhere.
The eight-year-old division normally attracted little interest.
Yet the two children standing there had become the main event.
Vikir Van Baskerville of Ironblood Baskerville.
Camus Morgue of the great mage house Morgue.
At Morgue’s suggestion, the eight-year-old match had been moved beside the field where the fifteen-year-olds were conducting their real combat trials.
This time, the two eight-year-olds would fight directly as well.
The contrast between Vikir’s indifference and Camus’s simmering anger could not have been sharper.
“Prepare yourself.”
“….”
“Once the match begins, I’m not going easy on you.”
“….”
“Say something!”
“….”
“Ugh!”
Vikir merely yawned.
The more indifferent he seemed, the harder Camus stamped her foot.
At last—
the bell rang to signal the start of the match.
The contest began.
“Yaaaaaah!”
A loud cheer rang across the training grounds.
Camus shouted and surged forward.
She drew on her mana immediately and attacked Vikir with everything she had prepared.
It was an impressive opening, but tactically poor in the long run because it exposed all of her cards at once.
Even so, Camus’s talent was extraordinary.
Quadruple Casting.
She maintained four spells simultaneously when many mages struggled to cast even two in succession.
They were only First-Circle spells—Fireball, Ice Spear, Lightning Ring, and Mud Wall—but even many fifteen-year-old Morgue children would have struggled to control all four together.
Maintaining them simultaneously required talent far beyond the ordinary.
Camus truly was a once-in-a-century prodigy even within Morgue.
Boom! Boom! Crack!
Child-sized fireballs and ice spears flew while lightning struck the ground around Vikir.
Even a direct hit would have hurt only modestly by Vikir’s standards, and he avoided them with minimal movement.
“It looks impressive, but you’d be better off concentrating on one spell for now.”
“What business is that of yours, you magic-ignorant idiot!”
Camus chased him while carefully controlling her three offensive spells.
Her determination to land at least one clean hit was obvious.
But—
Whoosh!
Vikir slipped through the barrage and moved toward Camus instead of away.
Then—
Bang!
He kicked apart the Mud Wall protecting her.
“Huh?”
Camus sucked in a breath.
At this distance she could no longer safely call down her offensive magic.
As the base of the wall collapsed, Vikir’s face appeared directly in front of her.
Camus felt her heart hammer.
‘I’m finished!’
She squeezed her eyes shut as Vikir’s hand flashed toward her face.
Then a sharp sting struck her forehead.
Snap!
Camus stumbled backward with tears in her eyes and looked up.
“…?”
Her forehead hurt, but nothing else had happened.
Vikir had merely flicked her forehead hard enough to leave a small bump.
“Y-you! Are you making fun of me?”
“….”
“D-don’t come any closer!”
Camus canceled one of the three offensive spells she had been maintaining.
Thump!
She formed a second Mud Wall, creating two layers of defense around herself.
“Ha! Two walls now! Let’s see you get through this!”
The obvious problem was that with two walls in front of her, Camus could no longer see outside.
But—
Bang!
Vikir punched through both layers as easily as wet paper.
His hand appeared directly in front of Camus’s face.
“Whose hand is this?”
The hand had crossed through Camus’s wall and entered her protected space.
While Camus stared speechlessly, Vikir’s fingers moved.
“The answer is: the stronger person’s.”
Snap!
Another tear sprang to Camus’s eye.
He had flicked her again.
The second strike landed on exactly the same spot, building another small bump over the first.
To make matters worse, Vikir’s hand withdrew the instant after the blow.
He was not trying to hurt her seriously; he was teasing her with the minimum force necessary.
“H-how dare you toy with me—aaaaaah!”
Camus was eight years old, a genius girl who had spent her whole life treasured like a precious jewel.
Unable to contain her frustration any longer, she screamed.
She canceled all of her offensive spells and instead erected four Mud Walls around herself.
“Now what? Four walls! Not even you can get through all of these! Ha!”
Camus did not seem to care that she had made herself completely blind.
If anything, she was relieved that Vikir could no longer see her rubbing her aching forehead and sulking.
“….”
After a moment of silence, she began to wonder whether Vikir really had failed to penetrate the four walls.
Camus smiled in triumph.
“Ho-ho-ho! What happened? Can’t get through anymore? Come on, idiot!”
But there was still no answer.
…
Several moments passed.
Trapped inside her own mud enclosure, Camus rubbed her forehead and finally thought the situation through.
‘Wait. How am I supposed to get out?’
Her vision was completely blocked; she had no idea what Vikir was doing outside.
She could not even easily change tactics while boxed in by her own four layers of mud.
‘Maybe I can open a tiny hole and look out.’
With some effort, Camus made a small viewing hole in one wall.
She cautiously put her face near the opening.
Snap!
Vikir’s hand appeared like a ghost and delivered a third flick to the exact same spot on her forehead.
“Kyaaaaaah!”
Three bumps.
With three small lumps stacked on the same place, Camus writhed in anger and pain.
She raised blazing eyes and immediately dispelled all four Mud Walls.
As the walls collapsed, she spotted Vikir standing calmly at a distance.
“I’m going to kill you! Aaaaaah!”
Camus was now too furious and frustrated to care about dignity.
She abandoned defense completely and brought four offensive spells up at once.
That degree of simultaneous offensive control would have been difficult even for a fifteen-year-old Morgue mage.
Facing the enraged Camus, Vikir considered his options.
“…What am I supposed to do with this?”
If he genuinely wanted to kill her, he could break her neck almost instantly. That obviously was not the problem.
Dealing with children was troublesome precisely because Vikir had no instinct for where ordinary play should begin or end.
He had spent almost his entire first life on battlefields, not entertaining children.
That experience gave him little help in calming a furious eight-year-old Morgue prodigy.
Eventually Vikir reached a decision.
‘Morgue’s problems can be solved by Morgue.’
It was always cleaner to solve a problem without unnecessarily dirtying his own hands.
Boom!
A glance sideways showed the neighboring arena in the middle of an intense battle.
An unnamed fifteen-year-old Baskerville and a fifteen-year-old Morgue mage were clashing fiercely with sword and magic.
They were so focused on one another that neither noticed anyone moving near the edge of their field.
The Morgue teenager was preparing a powerful fire spell, and explosive bursts were already shaking the arena.
Whoosh—
Vikir retreated until he stood close to the boundary of the neighboring match.
The furious Camus followed him without hesitation.
‘If this goes too far, the referees will intervene anyway.’
Vikir adjusted his position slightly, and Camus followed relentlessly.
Then—
BOOM!
An explosion erupted, followed by alarmed cries.
“Camus!”
“Lady Camus!”
“No! That’s—!”
Vikir saw the situation he had been waiting for begin to unfold.
