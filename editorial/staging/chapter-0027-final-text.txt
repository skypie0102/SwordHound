The air in Hugo’s office was still cold and faintly metallic after all these years.
Dust from old books mingled with stale smoke, cold coffee, and the faint scent of blood.
Like a veteran hound, Vikir read the room through smell before anything else.
Then the middle-aged man seated behind the desk turned away from the window and faced him.
Hugo Le Baskerville.
The iron-blooded patriarch of House Baskerville.
Seven years had brought his present face noticeably closer to the Hugo Vikir remembered from before his regression.
Vikir’s attention settled on a comparatively recent scar crossing the bridge of Hugo’s nose.
The scar had existed in Vikir’s first life as well, but he had never known how Hugo received it.
“Greetings, Father.”
“Mm.”
Hugo acknowledged him with a short nod.
Vikir remained composed and let the conversation proceed naturally.
When he asked about the scar across Hugo’s nose, Hugo dismissed it as nothing worth mentioning.
“An arrow grazed me while I was subduing barbarians in the Black Mountains.”
Vikir was genuinely surprised.
Someone had actually managed to wound Hugo Le Baskerville with an arrow.
A Swordmaster of Hugo’s caliber would never be struck by an ordinary stray shot; the archer must have deliberately targeted him.
Hugo gave a quiet laugh.
“There is a woman among them who knows how to use a bow.”
“Did you capture her?”
“No. But I left a matching mark on that fox-like woman’s face.”
A swordsman at Hugo’s level could project sword aura far enough to threaten enemies well beyond normal blade range.
Hugo touched the scar over his nose as though the old wound still faintly ached.
After a short pause, he changed the subject.
“In any case, congratulations on completing your training.”
“Thank you.”
“From here onward, it becomes harder.”
Vikir nodded.
Staffordshire had said essentially the same thing at the end of the training course: the real work began after graduation.
Three and a half years of basic training had built theory, physical conditioning, discipline, and combat judgment.
Another three and a half years of practical training had added live combat against monsters and people, along with wilderness-survival skills.
But the deep frontier of the Black Mountains would invalidate the safety assumptions behind almost everything learned in training.
Out there, survival came down to inherited strength, accumulated experience, and the willingness to risk death.
‘Normally, the next twenty-one months would be spent opening the Black Mountain frontier.’
Vikir recalled the path he had taken in his first life.
Under the normal assignment, he would now have become an apprentice of the Pit Bull Knights and been sent into Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain for twenty-one months of lethal frontier work.
He would have been thrown directly into the fighting like a disposable hound—used until he broke and replaced without hesitation.
…But—
Hugo proposed something Vikir had not expected.
“You will serve as a House Representative.”
That was genuinely surprising.
Hugo intended to put Vikir on the family’s civil-administration track.
Baskerville was a warrior house, so entering administration did not mean abandoning combat.
Talented young Baskervilles—especially those marked as future elites—often passed through administrative office before taking higher military command.
Hugo’s eldest and second sons had followed the same broad path.
“A man must be strong. Power is no different from a sword.”
Vikir nodded at Hugo’s words.
While expendable hounds accumulated experience by dying on battlefields, favored elites learned politics and administration safely within the family’s power structure.
When those elites eventually drew their own swords, they could command veterans who had already survived years of real combat.
They entered the battlefield later, but with authority, knowledge, resources, and experienced subordinates already gathered beneath them.
‘And only the elite are taught beyond the Baskerville Fifth Form.’
That was another reason ordinary expendable hounds were denied the higher forms.
Restricting advanced swordsmanship let late-deploying elites close the combat gap with front-line hounds far more easily.
It was the same logic by which ruling classes could preserve power by restricting ordinary people’s access to knowledge.
“I will follow your command.”
Vikir bowed respectfully.
He had originally hoped to move farther from Hugo, yet circumstances kept drawing him closer.
Still, Vikir had revealed only a fraction of his real strength, and proximity to the center of power could be useful in its own way.
Hugo briefly explained what serving as a House Representative meant.
“Representatives assist with administration, legislation, and judicial affairs throughout Baskerville territory, attract useful business, and handle the family’s local affairs.”
A successful Representative could later rise to the upper administrative ranks and deal with matters beyond the family’s internal territory, though Vikir had little interest in that distinction for now.
Creak—
Hugo opened a drawer and removed several official papers.
Butler Barrymore immediately stepped forward with the family seal and the necessary ink.
“Your appointment is Deputy Magistrate of Underdog City.”
Vikir knew Underdog City well.
It was the busiest city in Baskerville’s enormous territory—large enough that calling it merely a town would have been absurd.
The Magistrate held the city’s core legislative and judicial authority, while Deputy Magistrates supported that office and handled delegated administration.
Vikir examined the appointment document.
“So I will be making laws and enforcing them.”
“Yes. At present, both the Magistrate’s office and the Deputy Magistrate posts are vacant.”
There was a reason an entire layer of Underdog’s government had been left empty at once.
Underdog’s previous Magistrate had been Hugo’s second son, with several members of his faction and family serving as deputies.
But Hugo’s second son had since withdrawn for prolonged training, while the deputies left behind had been dismissed or suspended en masse for taking bribes.
Hugo intended to place the authority and responsibilities of those vacant offices into Vikir’s hands.
“The city is practically without a master. Can you handle it?”
Vikir nodded.
Deputy Magistrates were normally officials in their thirties; only exceptional prodigies reached such a post in their early twenties.
Those unusually young officials were usually being groomed for positions that would one day represent the family itself.
‘The Academy is one route into that elite track.’
After four years at the Academy, such heirs could return and move almost immediately into high local office.
It was an elite course designed for the elite.
Hugo’s decision to give the post to fifteen-year-old Vikir was extraordinary even by those standards.
“You are giving me a great deal of authority.”
“And a great deal of responsibility.”
Hugo answered with a dry smile.
“Your achievements are at least ten years ahead of the children this family calls geniuses. I will treat you accordingly.”
That treatment included obligations as well as privileges.
The more authority a person received, the heavier the duty placed on them.
Viewed from the other direction, a heavier duty also justified granting greater authority.
“I will do my best to meet your expectations.”
Hugo nodded without another word.
Then—
THUMP!
Hugo’s seal came down on the appointment document.
With that stamp, Vikir became Deputy Magistrate—and effectively acting Magistrate—of Underdog, a metropolis of roughly half a million people.
‘This is useful.’
Vikir immediately began calculating.
‘If I use the Deputy Magistrate’s authority properly, I can strengthen myself far more safely than by being thrown straight into frontier combat.’
His concealed fangs and claws could be sharpened without unnecessary exposure.
Then Vikir spoke again.
“Father.”
“…?”
Hugo looked up.
A trace of warmth had returned to the patriarch’s eyes after the appointment was settled.
Vikir caught that rare opening immediately.
“There is one matter connected to the ruby-mine negotiations with Morgue.”
Seven years earlier, eight-year-old Vikir’s handling of the Morgue delegation had pleased Hugo enormously.
At the time, Hugo had promised him a reward, but Vikir had never specified what he wanted.
Now Vikir chose to collect that old reward.
“There is something I would like to borrow.”
Hugo hesitated very slightly.
“Hmm. Yes. I did promise you a reward and told you to ask when you wanted something.”
“If it is difficult, you do not have to grant it.”
Hugo’s thick eyebrows twitched.
“It is hardly difficult.”
He immediately pulled out another document and stamped it with the patriarch’s seal.
“I trust you will not do anything foolish with it, son.”
No matter how often Hugo called him ‘son,’ Vikir still could not become accustomed to hearing it.
In truth, he was even more surprised by how readily Hugo had granted the request.
‘Before my regression, I would never have dared ask him for a favor like this.’
Things might proceed more smoothly than Vikir had expected.
◆◆◆
After leaving the patriarch’s office, Vikir walked down the long corridor.
Butler Barrymore followed behind with a gentle smile.
“Young Master.”
“Mm?”
“The patriarch is very fond of you.”
Vikir gave no answer.
Barrymore continued anyway.
“He is not throwing you straight into the field. He is giving you the authority of a de facto city ruler. That means he intends to cultivate you for something far greater.”
If Hugo had wanted nothing more than a disposable hound, he would have sent Vikir directly into lethal combat and let the battlefield season or destroy him.
While expendable hounds learned through repeated brushes with death, future masters were allowed to gather knowledge, political judgment, and authority from safer positions.
From that protected foothold, the elites could later rise to heights ordinary hounds were never allowed to approach.
In the end, the lower ranks could rarely surpass those above them, no matter how hard they fought—because the system itself preserved the difference.
Vikir knew that bitter truth better than almost anyone.
“That assumes I perform well as Deputy Magistrate.”
“You will, Young Master. If you produce even respectable results there, I am certain your name will be on the next Academy admission list.”
Barrymore had already noticed Vikir’s interest in the Academy.
He simply could not guess the real reasons behind it.
Meanwhile—
Vikir’s mind filled with policies, reforms, and notorious administrative methods he had seen or heard about in his first life.
He tucked the Deputy Magistrate appointment bearing Hugo Le Baskerville’s seal securely into his clothes.
‘I need to produce results quickly.’
There were countless ways to earn recognition and rise rapidly.
Vikir chose the fastest and safest one.
