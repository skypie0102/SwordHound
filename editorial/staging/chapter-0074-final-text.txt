In the southwest of the Empire stood St. Mecca, a polished city at the center of House Quovadis territory.
White-stone boulevards, slow carriages, and relaxed foot traffic gave the city an almost leisurely air.
White-armored paladins guarded the entrances.
Then—
Ding, ding, ding.
A three-horse carriage rolled up to the checkpoint.
The paladins stopped it.
“Brief inspection.”
The merchants inside presented their passes and identification.
The paladins examined the papers carefully.
“Joseph Josta, oil merchant, 108 Dortmund Street, first floor. What are you carrying?”
“Oil, exactly as the import permit says.”
The merchant stroked his mustache and gestured back.
The wagon was packed with large metal barrels.
The paladins looked inside.
“Mind if we check?”
“Go ahead.”
The merchant nodded, and several armed paladins climbed aboard.
The wagon dipped under the weight of their armor.
They inspected the barrels one by one.
Each was heavy and tightly sealed.
The paladins struck the metal sides.
Boom.
Every barrel sounded completely full.
“Do you have to inspect that one too?”
“Of course. There could be more than oil inside.”
The paladins accepted the answer without suspicion.
“Pass.”
The wagon entered St. Mecca.
A short time later it turned off the main roads into a deserted back alley.
The merchant checked the surroundings before prying open one barrel.
Cre-eak!
He worked a long crowbar beneath the lid until the seal broke.
The bent iron cover opened onto black oil.
The merchant called nervously.
“…You can come out now.”
Something inside moved.
Splash!
The surface broke and a figure rose from the oil.
The man wore a broad black hat, a plague-doctor-style beaked mask, and a heavy trench coat.
Covered in oil, he climbed from the barrel and stood in the wagon.
The merchant shuddered at the sight.
The masked man reached into his coat and handed him something.
It was an oiled oxbear fang.
“Payment. Thank you.”
That was the agreed price for hiding him inside the cargo through the city gate.
The merchant accepted the valuable tooth and pocketed it.
The masked man stepped away, leaving a trail of oil in the alley.
The merchant finally worked up the courage to ask.
“…Who are you?”
The man tapped the beak of his mask.
“The Hound of the Night.”
Then he disappeared into the darkness.
The merchant stared at the still-bubbling oil and muttered.
“…How does anyone stay submerged in oil for more than two hours?”
◆◆◆
Deeper in the alley, the Hound of the Night removed his mask.
“Puh!”
Vikir shook oil from himself and breathed deeply.
He had not held his breath under liquid for anything close to this long since surviving seven minutes in the River Styx as an infant.
Today he had infiltrated St. Mecca, heartland of House Quovadis.
“…The Hound of the Night.”
The alias borrowed from Akwilla’s famous title, Night Fox, even if the wording still felt awkward.
Even so, Vikir preferred it to being known again as the Hound of Baskerville.
“….”
For a moment he thought back over the journey.
Exactly four days had passed since he left Ballak.
Two days were spent escaping the depths, one surveying Baskerville territory, and one infiltrating Quovadis land.
The enormous Black Mountain frontier had been easier to cross because of the floods.
Flood channels, floating logs, and debris let Vikir move many times faster than normal.
Dense vines and branches created natural routes over places where ordinary roads had vanished.
Hungry monsters still attacked along the way, but a fully restored Vikir had little trouble with them.
Giant fish, old reptiles, bears, wild dogs, and venomous bats had become routine after two years adapting to the depths.
“…There was one dangerous exception.”
‘Those bone-sucking mosquitoes were awful.’
Swarms pursued him even through heavy rain, forcing him to spend nearly half a day driving them off.
Pomeranian, still physically weak, caught a cold during the trip.
The first place Vikir visited after reaching Imperial civilization was Underdog City.
Chihuahua, staring at the full moon from his apartment, fainted three times when Vikir suddenly entered through the window.
Vikir chose him because Chihuahua remained one of the few Baskerville-affiliated people he genuinely trusted.
“Deputy… are you a ghost? How did you even get in here?”
“I used to work here every day. Of course I know the layout.”
Once Chihuahua recovered, Vikir explained what had happened.
“You really are unbelievable. Only you could disappear for two years and come back after living with frontier tribes.”
“I will return publicly when the time is right. Until then, keep today secret.”
“And who is this child who looks exactly like you? Did you have a daughter while you were gone?”
“She is blood family, but not in the way you are imagining. Stop talking nonsense.”
Vikir entrusted Pomeranian to Chihuahua.
“She is important. Do not interrogate her until I return. Feed her, keep her safe, and give her a basic education.”
Chihuahua had never forgotten Vikir and still became emotional when remembering him.
He immediately summoned a city-hall physician to examine Pomeranian and provide medicine and food.
“I will protect her with my life until you return.”
Chihuahua spoke with complete seriousness.
He was still physically unimpressive after two years, but Vikir trusted his word.
To cover the cost of raising the child, Vikir handed over valuable oxbear hide and horns.
“Why are you giving me all this?”
“Sell it as needed.”
“This much could be worth more than ten years of my salary.”
“Make sure Pomeranian lacks nothing.”
“You are very protective of your daughter. I have three daughters myself, so I understand.”
Chihuahua gave him a knowing, fatherly smile.
The assumption was wrong, but Vikir did not bother correcting it again.
Then—
“Ah! Deputy, there is something else.”
Chihuahua hurried to a cabinet and pulled out a thick stack of old papers.
Vikir’s attention sharpened.
They were newspaper clippings from the previous two years covering Baskerville, Morgue, and major regional events.
“I kept these in case you ever came back.”
Chihuahua scratched the back of his neck.
Vikir was genuinely grateful that the man had prepared for his return.
In exchange, Vikir agreed to help with Underdog’s renewed criminal problems caused by the long vacancy in its top office.
“If the situation becomes difficult, find Cindywendy of Messinadnaro. She owes me.”
Vikir lowered his voice.
Chihuahua’s eyes widened.
Messinadnaro Sen Cindywendy was the criminal financier Vikir had released through a plea deal two years earlier.
“How do I find her?”
“Remember Judy, the girl I gave ten billion gold?”
“Of course.”
“Cindywendy will probably seek her out. Making ten billion is difficult, but protecting it without allies is even harder.”
Chihuahua nodded.
Fresh out of prison and without capital of her own, Cindywendy would naturally look for an investor she could control or advise.
She had once served as an investment adviser even to figures such as Baron Gambino.
“But if I find her, why would she help the government?”
“Offer her a profitable business.”
“What kind?”
Vikir answered directly.
“A monopoly on legal trade with the frontier tribes.”
The tribes along the frontier had largely stopped Imperial trade after the Bourgeois incident two years earlier.
If Vikir, now trusted by Ballak, reopened that route, the merchant controlling it could become immensely wealthy.
Cindywendy’s experience would also help Underdog counter the criminal resurgence that followed Vikir’s disappearance.
Chihuahua nodded slowly.
Within hours of Vikir’s return, problems that had festered for two years already had possible solutions.
His respect for Vikir deepened again.
Whoosh!
Vikir vaulted back out through the window.
The Hound of the Night slipped away from Underdog.
“Deputy Magistrate! Where are you going?”
Leaving Chihuahua’s cry behind, Vikir continued toward the place needed to fulfill his promise to Aiyen.
He needed somewhere Aheul’s Red Death could be treated quickly.
‘One drop of their holy water should be enough.’
His destination was House Quovadis, one of the Empire’s Seven Great Families and the foremost house of holy power.
