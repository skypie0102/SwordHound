“…Who are you, really?”
Dolores asked with open uncertainty.
A stranger had appeared without warning and predicted disaster for Quovadis.
His clothing was bizarre, yet the soul she sensed beneath it felt unusually clear despite its scars.
“Who are you, and how do you know all of this?”
Dolores’s voice trembled slightly.
Quovadis often received well-connected visitors who understood politics and worldly affairs.
Very few could anticipate events with anything like this precision.
Fewer still arrived before an epidemic had even been officially reported.
Dolores and Mozgus could no longer ignore the mystery of the visitor’s identity.
“Yes. Answer the saintess. Who are you?”
Mozgus asked too, gripping the delicate teapot hard enough that it looked in danger.
The demand sounded like a defense of Dolores’s authority, but simple curiosity was plainly part of it.
Both priests stared at Vikir.
He gave them only a short answer.
“Hound of the Night.”
Their expressions became complicated.
“…Hound of the Night. That is an ominous name.”
“And inventing your own title so casually is remarkably irreverent.”
Dolores and Mozgus both sounded unimpressed by the alias.
Vikir offered nothing further.
Mozgus finally forced the conversation back toward the emergency.
“Whatever your identity, what you described is possible. We need to investigate immediately, report upward, and prepare a house-level response.”
Urgency spread through the residence.
Dolores formally ordered Mozgus to organize a relief force for the suspected epidemic.
She also sent an official report to her father, Cardinal Humbert, whose monastery was nearby.
From there, the matter would soon reach Nabokov I, head of House Quovadis and Pope of the Rune Church.
That made the warning a major institutional issue.
Mozgus hurried away to carry out the orders.
Dolores remained behind, still studying Vikir.
Vikir met her gaze without discomfort.
He deliberately did not tell her the full story of Baskerville, Morgue, and the first frontier outbreak.
Doing so would reveal that the Red Death had originally appeared in the waters of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.
Baskerville and Morgue had also benefited politically from the destruction that followed there.
The frontier tribes served as major predators in the mountain ecosystem; when the Red Death devastated them, lower demons multiplied and monster waves struck Imperial settlements.
Vikir remembered that history clearly.
Before regression, more than forty percent of the frontier tribes had died as the epidemic spread.
The resulting demon surge increased the political and military importance of Baskerville and Morgue.
Hugo converted that leverage into greater power for the house, and many more hounds were consumed in the process.
Vikir had been one of them.
Whatever else was true—
Keeping Ballak alive now also served Vikir’s revenge against the people who had engineered or profited from the disaster.
Each intervention connected to another.
Vikir closed his eyes and thought through the consequences still ahead.
Meanwhile, Dolores continued watching him.
‘…A lost puppy. Exhausted and alone.’
She could not explain why the thought fit a man calling himself a hound.
The stranger had defeated Mozgus and stood against an entire paladin force, yet somehow still seemed wounded and isolated.
His presence carried blood, violence, and the grief of someone whose soul had been broken repeatedly.
Sorrow, hatred, loneliness—and the impression of a man choosing an ascetic road through all of it alone.
To Dolores, he resembled a wandering seeker or prophet.
She remembered the sentence he had quoted earlier.
“Theology is ultimately the study of understanding human beings.”
The old scriptural wording was obscure enough that even trained theologians rarely used it. Dolores therefore assumed Vikir must have deep religious education.
Perhaps, she thought, he was a Rune believer who practiced doctrine in places of extreme suffering rather than inside comfortable temples.
‘How else could he know and remember such words?’
That was Dolores’s conclusion.
She was sixteen, gifted with unusual spiritual sensitivity but still capable of youthful misinterpretation.
Her mistaken picture of Vikir continued growing more elaborate.
Vikir, meanwhile, was simply waiting for access to the holy power that could cure Aheul.
“….”
“….”
They looked at one another while thinking completely different things.
The untouched tea cooled quietly between them.
◆◆◆
Later—
Vikir hurried toward the St. Mecca slums.
Dolores, Mozgus, and dozens of paladins and priests followed.
“Is the plague really here?”
Dolores asked while climbing the steep stairs.
Even some of the paladins still seemed doubtful.
Vikir never slowed, moving ahead like a scout.
From a high rooftop, he scanned the district chimney by chimney.
The weather was not especially cold, yet smoke rose heavily from several homes.
Vikir focused on those houses first.
Several already contained people developing Red Death symptoms.
The earliest signs were chills and fever.
Then came widespread pain, vomiting, and diarrhea.
Finally, red spots appeared across the skin and frightened the victims further.
The chills explained the excessive fires burning inside some homes.
But a house without chimney smoke could still contain patients.
Poor families often lacked enough firewood and simply wrapped themselves in dirty blankets against the cold.
Dolores began to weep at the sight.
“Do not be afraid. Rune’s love will not abandon you.”
She touched sick people directly while praying and blessing them.
Vikir stayed behind her and assessed the outbreak.
“Good. No children.”
His warning at the well had worked; none of the current patients were children.
Because Quovadis had been alerted quickly, most infections were still early and relatively mild.
The cases appeared limited to people directly exposed to the well water and people infected through close contact with those first patients.
The outbreak needed to be suppressed now, before anyone died.
Dolores gathered the sick in the central square and prayed.
[Light of my life, flame of my life—look upon the young and poor gathered here, and carry away their suffering.]
Her prayer rang clearly through the square.
Then—
A single tear slid from Dolores’s eye.
The sight was fragile and solemn.
Her appearance was beautiful, but Vikir’s attention remained on the holy power gathering around her.
When the tear struck the ground, a miracle answered.
Flash!
White holy light spread outward and erased the red marks from the patients.
One tear and one prayer were enough to cleanse several people of the Red Death.
“Saintess! Thank you!”
“You saved us!”
“I’m healed—it’s a miracle!”
People who had been shaking and vomiting moments earlier stood with their strength restored.
Dolores smiled through her tears as she watched them recover.
Behind her, Vikir stared at the holy effect.
“That is it. That is what I need.”
He had found the healing power he needed to take back to Aheul.
