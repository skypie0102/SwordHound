The doors of the Quovadis residence opened.
As Vikir expected, no person stood directly behind them.
Only a stone statue waited inside, speaking with a saintly voice.
[What business brings you here?]
Dolores’s voice came from the statue.
Apparently she had no intention of meeting Vikir in person yet.
“…Understandable.”
His current appearance was deeply suspicious.
A wide black hat, a plague-doctor beak mask, and a dark cloak concealed him from head to toe.
No sensible saint would immediately approach someone dressed that way.
Vikir stepped toward the statue.
“I came to report an outbreak in the slums.”
He presented a container of water carrying traces of the fictional Red Death.
A capable Quovadis cleric should be able to detect the unnatural contamination.
Then—
[…]
The statue fell silent.
Vikir understood that Dolores had stopped transmitting her voice through it.
And then—
Click.
The door behind Vikir slammed shut.
It was not the only one.
Clang! Clang! Clang! Clang!
Doors and corridors around the lobby sealed one after another.
In moments, the reception hall became an enclosed combat chamber.
Clatter-clatter-clatter!
White-armored paladins appeared along the second-floor terrace.
“….”
Vikir scanned the room.
The ordinary lobby had clearly been designed to convert into a defensive battlefield.
With every exit sealed and paladins positioned overhead, an intruder had nowhere obvious to retreat.
Even the decorative statues offered almost no real cover.
“….”
Vikir remained still.
The response was close to what he had expected.
Then—
“Who are you?”
A rough voice sounded among the statues on the first floor.
A large man stepped into the open.
He stood more than two meters tall, with a scarred shaved head and white armor wrapped in Quovadis cloth.
He had no eyebrows, an enormous nose, and a broad mouth.
His eyes curved gently as though smiling, but the pressure around him was severe.
Arms as thick as logs held an enormous holy book.
Behind the gas mask, Vikir narrowed his eyes.
‘…Mozgus. It has been a long time.’
Mozgus Quovadis had once fought beside Vikir before the regression.
Vikir had trusted the man at his back against demons.
Mozgus’s body was an iron wall, and his holy power could flood an entire battlefield with light.
He was incapable of compromising with what he considered injustice and could be more merciless than demons when enforcing his idea of justice.
Vikir remembered the man dying spectacularly during the Demon War after facing an overwhelming demon force.
‘Come to think of it, I learned several interrogation techniques from him.’
The memory reminded Vikir of the methods he had later used against Underdog’s criminal elites.
But those memories belonged only to Vikir; in this timeline, Mozgus had never met him.
Mozgus raised the massive scripture and addressed the masked stranger.
“You claim to report a plague, yet you dress like a criminal. Remove the mask and show proper respect before asking to see the saint.”
“I have a reason to keep the mask on. My duty ends with delivering the warning, so I will leave.”
Vikir set down the contaminated-water sample and raised his hands slightly.
Then he began backing away.
“Blasphemer!”
Mozgus charged.
He swung the massive book down like a weapon and followed with a crushing step.
Vikir moved first.
He slipped through the opening created by Mozgus lifting the book.
Boom!
The book smashed a deep crack into the floor.
Vikir watched the white aura leaking from the edges of its pages.
‘That strange weapon again. He is somewhere between Intermediate and Advanced Sword Graduator-equivalent.’
Mozgus was still only partway toward the strength Vikir remembered from the previous timeline.
But inside Quovadis territory, where holy power could continually restore him, the matchup was less simple than raw rank suggested.
Mozgus growled when Vikir took a fighting stance.
“I can feel something foul in that water. If it is truly plague-tainted, perhaps you are the one spreading it.”
“If I were the culprit, why would I report it?”
“Who knows? It could be a trick meant to draw out the saint.”
Vikir shrugged; the suspicion was reasonable.
“I am reporting suspicious people spreading disease in the slums.”
“Your clothing is more suspicious than your story. You can explain yourself in custody.”
Mozgus swung the book again.
Fwap!
The scripture opened and its white pages snapped violently.
Blades of white aura shot from their sharpened edges toward Vikir.
“Have you ever been cut by stiff paper? It hurts.”
Mozgus used the open book itself as a strange cutting weapon, turning hundreds of page edges into aura blades.
Shhk-shhk-shhk!
Sheets of parchment flashed in every direction.
Vikir moved like a black ghost and avoided the entire barrage.
The footwork came from Ballak hunting and assassination methods.
At the same time, Beelzebub emerged from his wrist.
Shk!
Dense liquid aura gathered at the blade tip.
“Graduator!”
Mozgus and the surrounding paladins reacted in shock.
Vikir’s aura met Mozgus’s directly and refused to give ground.
Then Vikir activated the Oxbear ability stored within Beelzebub.
His effective body weight surged toward roughly six hundred kilograms.
Mozgus tried to overpower the smaller man physically and was stunned when Vikir did not move.
“It is like pushing a boulder! What are you?”
Even Mozgus’s enormous frame could not match an Oxbear’s raw physical stability.
Vikir raised Beelzebub.
The hidden Baskerville killing style unfolded.
Nothing about the external posture obviously resembled orthodox Baskerville swordsmanship. But its internal principles were unmistakably those of the hound.
Six concealed fangs tore through Mozgus’s aura.
Because the form was not publicly recognizable, Vikir judged that revealing this much would not expose his identity.
A long blade extended from his wrist and divided into six killing trajectories.
The attacks cut toward Mozgus’s head, neck, shoulders, and back.
“…Ghk!?”
Mozgus tried to close the book and use it as a shield, but he was late.
“If you wanted a shield, you should have raised it sooner.”
Vikir cut across the back of Mozgus’s hand as he gripped the scripture.
Then—
Roar!
Cerberus Burn erupted from Beelzebub.
The oil-fed infernal flame spread across Mozgus’s wounded hand.
“Aaaagh!”
Mozgus rolled across the floor, clutching the burning injury.
Ordinary water and sand could not easily smother Cerberus Burn.
Fwap-fwap-fwap!
Loose pages from the holy book caught fire as well.
Vikir looked down at Mozgus and briefly lowered his weapon; killing him had never been the purpose of coming here.
But—
“You blasphemer! What sorcery is this?”
Mozgus’s fighting spirit had not broken.
He released a surge of holy power and seized water flowing from one of the statues. Under his divine power, it became holy water.
Hissss!
The holy water extinguished the Cerberus flame on his hand.
Even so, the exchange had already been decided in Vikir’s favor.
Mozgus felt a heavy pressure in his throat.
Looking up, he found Vikir standing with one foot planted on the enormous holy book.
“Standing on scripture is blasphemy!”
“You were throwing and swinging it as a weapon.”
“….”
Mozgus had no immediate answer.
Vikir pointed Beelzebub toward him, black aura gathering at its tip.
“Do you want to continue?”
“….”
Mozgus clenched his teeth at the calm challenge.
Very few people in the Empire could pressure an upper-level Graduator like him so completely.
He balled his fists and drew out all the aura he could muster.
Crackle-crackle!
Rapidly spinning mana wrapped around his forearm.
“Hraaah!”
Mozgus charged with enough force to crush the person in front of him.
But—
Vikir did not meet the charge directly.
Thud!
He kicked the fallen holy book.
Boom!
A second kick sent the book spinning through the air.
It flew directly across Mozgus’s line of sight.
Mozgus instinctively turned his head around the obstacle.
“…!”
Then he realized Vikir had vanished.
For the fraction of a second in which the rotating book completely blocked his vision, the masked man disappeared from the space ahead.
“Where—?”
Cold sweat ran down Mozgus’s back.
A quiet voice came from beside his ear.
“…Looking for me?”
During the book’s brief rotation, Vikir had slipped completely outside Mozgus’s vision and taken his back.
Mozgus spun around.
BANG!
Vikir’s aura struck him across the body.
The blow shattered Mozgus’s plate armor and tore through the chainmail beneath it.
Crash! Thud!
Mozgus flew backward through three statues before hitting the floor.
The paladins on the upper terrace stared in disbelief.
None of them had ever seen Inquisitor Mozgus Quovadis, archbishop and commander of the Inquisition Knights, defeated so quickly.
“Am I dreaming?”
“Lord Mozgus lost?”
“How can that be…?”
After only a moment of shock, the Quovadis elites drew their swords and jumped down together.
“Seize the blasphemer!”
White aura rose around the hall.
Vikir adjusted his beaked mask.
“An impressive welcome for a lost lamb.”
The paladins completed the encirclement.
And then—
Grrrk.
Behind them, Mozgus forced himself upright among the broken statues.
He discarded the warped remains of his armor and gripped a heavy chunk of stone.
Blood ran down his body, but he ignored it.
“If you insist, we will see this through.”
Vikir extended Beelzebub to its full length.
Dense liquid aura boiled along the blade, marking his advanced Graduator strength.
Mozgus and the paladins stiffened under the pressure of the black-clad figure.
No one dared blink or even swallow carelessly.
They understood that a single opening could let the black hound tear through a throat or heart.
“Where did this monster come from…?”
Mozgus regretted charging so recklessly and reset his stance.
One-on-one, he now judged defeat almost certain.
Even with numbers, victory was far from guaranteed.
The masked intruder was strong enough that the gathered paladins could not be sure they could subdue him cleanly.
Under ordinary circumstances, Mozgus might have withdrawn and called for a formal crusade.
But—
“The saint is here.”
Dolores’s presence made retreat impossible.
Vikir, Mozgus, and the paladins faced one another in a tense deadlock.
“…Stop!”
A voice cut through the entire hall.
A figure appeared at the third-floor terrace.
Saint Dolores herself had finally come out.
