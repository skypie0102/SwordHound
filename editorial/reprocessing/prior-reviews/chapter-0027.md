## Hugo's scar

The scar across Hugo's nose is relatively recent in the current timeline but existed in Vikir's first-life memory as well. Hugo says a skilled female archer wounded him while he was fighting barbarians in the Black Mountains. He did not capture her, but says he left a similar mark on her face. Do not identify the archer beyond what this chapter establishes.

The MTL's wording around a Swordmaster being struck by a random arrow is cleaned up: Vikir's point is that Hugo would not be hit by an ordinary stray shot, so the archer must have deliberately targeted him.

## Literal graduation versus Sword Graduator

Chapter 27 continues the wordplay from Chapter 26. Vikir and his cohort have **graduated from seven years of training**, while `Sword Graduator` remains a separate combat rank. The two meanings must not be collapsed.

The standard post-training path for disposable hounds is roughly **twenty-one months** of dangerous frontier work. In Vikir's first life he would have entered the Pit Bull Knights as an apprentice and been sent into Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

## Elite civil-administration track

Hugo unexpectedly places Vikir on the family's elite civil-administration track instead of sending him directly into frontier combat.

Normalize the MTL's unstable modern-political wording as follows:
- `House Representative` = a lower-rank family civil administrator handling local executive/administrative, legislative and judicial matters;
- successful Representatives can rise into the upper administrative ranks / Senate-level work dealing with broader family affairs;
- this is still a warrior-house career path, not a retreat from combat;
- Hugo's **eldest and second sons** followed the same broad elite path. The recovered MTL's `nemesis and second son` is a machine-translation error.

The underlying class logic is important. Expendable hounds are seasoned early through lethal field work, while favored elites accumulate political knowledge, authority and resources before entering battle later with veteran subordinates beneath them. Restricting forms above the Baskerville Fifth Form helps preserve that hierarchy.

## Underdog City appointment

Use the established licensed terminology **Underdog City** and **Deputy Magistrate**.

Vikir is appointed:
- **Deputy Magistrate of Underdog City**;
- effectively **acting Magistrate / de facto ruler**, because the Magistrate's seat and deputy posts are vacant;
- over a metropolis of roughly **500,000 people**.

The previous Magistrate was Hugo's second son. After he withdrew for prolonged training, the deputies who remained were removed or suspended for bribery. Hugo therefore concentrates the vacant offices' practical authority in Vikir.

A Deputy Magistrate would normally be around thirty; even exceptional prodigies generally reach the post only in their early twenties. Giving it to Vikir at fifteen is intentionally extraordinary and places him on the same elite track that can later lead to the Imperial Academy.

## Rights and duties

Hugo's logic is reciprocal: Vikir's achievements are at least ten years ahead of the family children normally called geniuses, so he receives both unusually large authority and unusually heavy responsibility.

The appointment allows Vikir to build power more safely than immediate frontier deployment while keeping his true Intermediate Sword Graduator / Fifth-Form capability concealed.

## Barrymore and the Academy

Barrymore interprets the appointment correctly: Hugo is not raising Vikir as a disposable hound but cultivating him for larger use. He also expects that even respectable performance in Underdog City will place Vikir on the next Academy admission list.

Vikir's interest in the Academy remains instrumental rather than academic prestige: independence from Hugo, access to future opportunities, old debts and known resources.

The exact endpoint is Vikir deciding he must produce results quickly and choosing the **fastest and safest** available route. The recovered MTL's `quickest and most obvious` is corrected against the Korean witness.
