## What the champagne demonstration reveals

The seven heirs all have enough martial education to understand the top-down resonance from Chapter 32. Their conclusion is that Vikir has reached at least the **Sword Graduator** realm.

The inference comes from fine mana control and the liquid-like quality of his aura. Vikir does not formally announce his true rank, so the chapter preserves the distinction between what the heirs infer and what Vikir has publicly disclosed elsewhere.

Their remembered rumors are normalized to established continuity: seven minutes under the River Styx, two venomous snakes in the Cradle, Cerberus at age eight, and the childhood duel against Morgue's prodigy.

## Failed attempt to save face

The heirs had planned to intoxicate/entertain Vikir and then force him to pay the bill. Because the trap had not yet actually been carried out, they initially think they can still invent an excuse. Vikir sees through the intention immediately.

They then claim the party was merely a `test` of whether the new Deputy Magistrate could resist corruption. Vikir rejects the premise using Baskerville's master/hound hierarchy:

- a master may test a hound;
- a hound does not test its master.

This deliberately echoes Hugo's old training philosophy.

## Punishment

Vikir uses a wooden stake like the one from the public special-law demonstration and beats all seven heirs. The violence is punitive and coercive, not a formal judicial sentence yet; Chapter 34 determines the legal consequences.

The recovered MTL ending `Sweeten the deal` is rejected. Korean supports the sense:

**“If you already know, then accept the punishment.”**

Exact endpoint: Vikir continues the beating after they say they understand why they are being punished.
