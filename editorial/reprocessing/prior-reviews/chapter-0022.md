## Camus in Vikir's first-life memory

Vikir recognizes eight-year-old Camus as the future **Ironblood Empress of Morgue**, a prodigious mage and political figure from his first timeline. This future recollection is legitimate character memory and may remain, but it must not be confused with Camus's current eight-year-old knowledge or capabilities.

Normalize all pronoun and name corruption in the MTL: Camus is female; `Camus`, `camel`, and similar machine artifacts all refer to **Camus Morgue**.

The Korean and MTL both support that Vikir remembers an adult Camus who became a formidable magical/political figure. Avoid embellishing beyond what the chapter actually states.

## Ruby argument

Camus abandons childish indignation and argues the mine logically:

- only Morgue has the practical surface access needed to extract the ruby;
- Baskerville cannot exploit the vein independently without equivalent access;
- therefore Morgue proposes mining rights in exchange for compensation/territorial rent.

Vikir's counterargument is territorial rather than commercial. Baskerville may not value ruby itself, but it has no reason to sell access to sovereign land merely because Morgue wants the mineral beneath it.

The argument therefore remains irreconcilable: Morgue prioritizes the ruby; Baskerville prioritizes the territory.

## Paper-wall analogy

Camus draws two territories as circles divided by a large sheet of paper, then pushes her arm through the paper into Vikir's side and asks whose arm it is. Her analogy is that a resource crossing a boundary remains attached to its original owner just as her arm remains hers after crossing the paper boundary.

Vikir refuses the premise. He grabs her arm and answers, in effect, that once it has crossed into his territory it is his. His response is intentionally ruthless and shocks both children and adults.

The pressure Vikir emits here is not a public aura/rank reveal. It is killing intent / presence born from his Age-of-Destruction experience. Do not turn it into a Graduator aura display.

## Close-distance reaction

The source dwells on Camus's surprise at being pulled face-to-face with a boy outside her clan and her momentary embarrassment. Keep this as a brief childlike emotional reaction rather than sexualizing the scene.

Vikir then clarifies that **her arm**, not Camus herself, is what he is claiming under the analogy.

## Dagger cliffhanger

Vikir produces what appears to be a sharpened dagger and moves as though he may cut the arm he has claimed. The chapter ends at the adults' shock. Do not reveal yet that the weapon is a flexible rubber/toy blade; Chapter 23 owns that reveal.
