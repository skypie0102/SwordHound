## Immediate effect of the stake demonstration

The story of Judy receiving the promised fortune spreads through Underdog and the wider Baskerville territory. The administration even provides protection for Judy and her mother because the reward makes them targets.

Chapter 30 explicitly pays **10 billion gold**. The Korean opening locally says one billion, but the same witness and the recovered English have already established the ten-billion total in the previous chapter. Preserve **10 billion** for continuity rather than introducing a new contradiction.

The central public lesson is simple: break the law and receive the stated punishment; obey the law and receive the stated protection or reward. Crime drops by roughly half because citizens begin believing that statutes will actually be enforced.

## Replenishing the treasury

Vikir immediately turns from the ten-billion payout to recovering the money by attacking the criminal economy.

He marks **more than one hundred** current or future criminal hideouts on an Underdog map, drawing on first-life knowledge of:
- criminal locations and leaders;
- bribery and corruption methods;
- underground businesses and operating patterns;
- black-money channels for roughly the next twenty-five years.

His procurement principle is that resources should be taken from the enemy. Criminal assets will be seized, returned to the treasury, and used in part to strengthen relief for the poor.

The public demonstration has cut overt crime roughly in half, but remaining organizations are becoming quieter and more sophisticated. Vikir's prior-life knowledge makes concealment largely irrelevant.

Beelzebub twitches in response to Vikir's killing intent, signaling its hunger for blood.

## Youth Self-Governance Committee

The first organized backlash comes from the **City Hall Youth Self-Governance Committee**.

It is composed largely of second- and third-generation heirs of influential local families with long-standing power inside Baskerville territory. Their Baskerville ties may come through marriage, blood, schooling, business, or patronage.

Chihuahua dismisses the body as a self-satisfied **social club** rather than a meaningful civic organization. Its members object to the Vikir Special Law because anti-corruption measures threaten precisely the networks from which they benefit.
