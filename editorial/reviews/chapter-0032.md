## Burning Suspension

The chapter opens at **Burning Suspension**, an ultra-exclusive club on the 69th and 70th floors of a high-rise hotel in central Underdog. Its VVIP room costs ten million gold for six hours, and bottles of `Donquixote Perignon 666` cost more than one hundred million gold each.

The champagne tower contains **385 glasses** arranged by square-number layers from 10×10 down to 1×1. The deliberately grotesque `trickle-down` joke is preserved: the heirs let waiters lick spilled champagne from the table while throwing coins at them.

## The seven local families

Normalize the seven influential provincial families as:
- Montblanc
- Pierre
- Louis Vuitton
- Chanel
- Ferragamo
- Hermès
- Prada

Their heirs form the core of the City Hall **Youth Self-Governance Committee** introduced in Chapter 31. The committee is formally civilian/local, but the families have enough inherited wealth, influence and delegated authority to dominate ordinary citizens and pressure lower officials.

Historically these houses are descendants of families displaced from central imperial politics. Baskerville tolerates limited local autonomy in return for information, taxes/tribute, and day-to-day handling of local matters while the great house focuses on frontier expansion.

## Source of their black money

Their ordinary local revenues are not enough to sustain the club lifestyle. The major hidden source is **illegal slave trafficking**:
- frontier expansion displaces barbarian communities;
- people are lured, abducted, imprisoned and sold without lawful authorization;
- the local families profit from the human displacement created by Baskerville's military expansion while paying no lawful tax on the trade.

Because the proceeds cannot safely enter legitimate banks, the heirs burn enormous amounts of cash on luxury and vice.

## `Taming the new official`

Whenever a new Deputy Magistrate arrives, the heirs hold an extravagant party to overwhelm and compromise him. Their intended message is essentially: enjoy our world, accept our favors, and thereafter obey our interests.

They misunderstand Vikir because:
- he has the `Van` name and they assume he is merely an illegitimate/collateral child;
- he is fifteen and they know little of his family reputation;
- Chapter 28's deliberately staged banquet has convinced them he is already indulgent and corruptible.

Their plan is to invite him, lavish alcohol and women on him, then stick him with the enormous bill and later pass it off as a joke if necessary.

## Vikir enters personally

Vikir answers their requested audience by coming directly to Burning Suspension. He enters so naturally behind the incoming hostesses that the staff initially fail to challenge him.

A waiter tries to seize him; Vikir breaks the man's wrist/forearm and drops the other attacking waiters almost instantly. He identifies the committee members without respect or fear, then gives only his name: **Vikir**.

Once they realize he is the new Deputy Magistrate, the heirs immediately try to begin their usual `taming` routine.

## Champagne-tower demonstration

Vikir places one hand on the table and sends controlled mana resonance through it. The demonstration is deliberately technical:
- the top glass bursts first;
- the destruction then progresses **from the top downward**, rather than from the table upward;
- each square-number layer shatters in sequence until all lower layers collapse;
- this shows extremely fine vertical mana control rather than brute-force destruction.

The chapter ends under a rain of glass and champagne with the seven heirs saying, in effect:

**“We’ll pay for the drinks.”**

Do not import Chapter 33's inference about Vikir's Graduator rank or the later punishment into this chapter.
