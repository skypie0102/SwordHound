## The triplets' forgiveness and the eldest-son wordplay

Hugo says the triplets came before Vikir and offered forgiveness as his older brothers. Vikir's internal reading is harsher: they were terrified and likely wanted distance from him. The supplied Korean's next line is machine-like but explicitly concerns age inevitably increasing. A later callback in Chapter 254 remembers this exchange with the same age/eating wordplay before Hugo says he worked to become the eldest son. The reconstruction therefore uses **"Growing older costs nothing, after all."** Hugo answers that even "this father" had to work hard to become the eldest son when he became patriarch.

That dark joke is immediately explained: Hugo became patriarch by killing his older brothers. Vikir realizes that although he had assumed the eldest son must inherit, one could effectively *become* the eldest by removing everyone older. This reading follows the supplied Korean sequence and is corroborated by the later callback; the later chapter is used only to resolve the corrupted wording, not to import later events.

## Forgiveness maxim and Hugo's reaction

Vikir remembers the last surviving daughter of a family exterminated by Baskerville. After becoming a nun, she told Hugo she forgave him. Hugo's answer was that forgiveness is merely an excuse for the weak who lack the power to take revenge. Fandom independently summarizes the same incident and wording.

The single quoted paragraph in the chapter functions as Vikir turning Hugo's own words back on him after the recollection. Make that action explicit in surrounding narration rather than leaving the speaker ambiguous. Hugo immediately recognizes the line, laughs loudly enough to shake the windows, and says Vikir is acting like a true child of his—almost as though Hugo were looking at his eldest son. Do not turn this into reconciliation; Vikir's frozen resentment remains explicit.

## Food reward system and direct-line encouragement

Hugo rewards Vikir by ordering Barrymore to give him access to the food storage. Baskerville children eat water and haggis until age fifteen. The Korean witness describes the haggis as finely ground meat and offal with a small amount of vegetables: extremely calorie- and nutrient-dense, hygienically supplied without limit, but salty and unpleasant. Sweets and chocolate are therefore powerful low-cost rewards for good performance and competition.

Vikir asks for chocolate. Hugo and Barrymore read this as age-appropriate childishness. As Vikir leaves, Hugo unusually encourages him in the midterm and adds: **"Don't lose to the direct line."** Preserve the distinction between Vikir's lower-status `Van` line and direct descendants without naming later competitors. His eyes flare red "like two suns," echoing the previous chapter's omen.

## Cacao and Bloody Beans

Normalize **cacao beans**, not MTL `coca beans`. Vikir rejects processed chocolate and requests raw cacao with an extremely intense flavor. Korean has a separate line explaining that a strong-flavored variety would be especially bitter; this extra narrative line is exactly accommodated by repurposing the MTL divider slot after the scene break.

Barrymore obtains **Bloody Beans**, a red local cacao variety from farmland created after Baskerville campaigns on the western frontier. One bean is concentrated enough to make about 100 liters of chocolate. The pouch contains over one hundred beans, so the later statement correctly refers to the **pouch** producing roughly 10,000 liters—not a single bean producing 10,000 liters as the MTL says. Vikir bites one, finds it intensely astringent and bitter, spits it out, and is satisfied. `Crunch!` is used as a contextual bite sound rather than retaining an opaque sound-token mistranslation.

Barrymore assumes Vikir intends to enjoy chocolate for life and offers to process the beans. Vikir refuses. Exact endpoint: it becomes clear that he did not take the beans to eat. Do not import the practical-exam/Cerberus use from later chapters.
