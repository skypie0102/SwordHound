## Existing laws

The public banners list old Baskerville laws that are extremely harsh but selectively enforced. Important corrections from Korean include:
- illegal human trafficking — death;
- illegal gambling — wrist amputation;
- illegal usury — removal of an eye;
- illegal brothels — facial branding;
- illegal lobbying — exile beyond the territory;
- tax evasion — removal of the kneecap;
- smuggling — **death by drowning**, not MTL `waterboarding`;
- illegal drugs — tongue amputation;
- embezzlement of military supplies — **extermination of three generations**, not MTL `three legs`;
- adultery — six hundred lashes.

The point is not that these laws are lenient or unclear. The problem is unequal enforcement: commoners are punished while nobles and wealthy magnates have historically escaped consequences.

## Public distrust

Underdog citizens have watched officials, local power families and wealthy heirs bend law to their own interests for years. Vikir's age and apparent passivity make the crowd assume he will be corrupted just as easily as previous Deputy Magistrates.

Chihuahua is locally respected and therefore has more immediate public authority than Vikir at the start of the scene. Vikir deliberately does not retaliate when citizens mock or compare him unfavorably to his subordinate.

## The stake

Vikir appears in the central square with a wooden stake approximately **70 cm long**, **3 kg**, driven only about **10 cm** into the ground. It appears trivial to remove.

He then uses the Deputy Magistrate's legislative authority to promulgate:

**Vikir Special Law — Article 1, Section 1: Anyone who pulls out this stake receives 100,000,000 gold in cash.**

The chapter ends immediately after this apparently simple law is announced. Do not explain the trap or import Chapter 30's mechanism early.
