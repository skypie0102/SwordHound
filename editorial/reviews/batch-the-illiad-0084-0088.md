## Family continuity

- Recovery from the Red Death begins with a hunt for Ka’ah, the old A+ Mushuhushu previously driven away during the rainy-season attack.
- Vikir and Aiyen jointly kill Ka’ah, and Beelzebub acquires **Silent Heel — Mushuhushu (A+)**, replacing an older stored ability and giving Vikir near-silent movement.
- Ahmen's long-suspected betrayal is then made explicit: he collaborated with Leviathan and Bourgeois, used poison-and-cure cycles to manufacture shamanic authority, and knowingly helped release the Red Death.
- Leviathan exploited Ahmen in turn. The supposed antidote he carries is only flour, showing that the great house treated him as a disposable native collaborator rather than a trusted partner.
- Vikir and Akwilla deliberately manipulate the confrontation. Vikir forges correspondence in Ahmen's handwriting; Akwilla publicly protects Ahmen's honor only to make refusal of the traditional **Illiad** impossible.
- The review distinguishes Ahmen's **real guilt** from Vikir's **fabricated supporting evidence**. The staged narrative does not present the forged letters as independently authentic.
- Ahmen's own history mirrors Vikir's superficially: he entered Ballak as an outsider and slave. Unlike Vikir, years of humiliation and delayed acceptance eventually feed his resentment, entitlement, and desire to dominate the tribe.
- Explicit sexual-abuse material concerning young Ahmen is not reproduced; the fact of enslavement and abuse remains because it is necessary to his history.
- During the Illiad, Ahmen uses jungle traps and later corpse-working sorcery. Vikir's Silent Heel defeats the terrain advantage, and the corpse magic lets him identify Ahmen's original people as **Rokoko**.
- The family ends before the duel is resolved: an old female oxbear unexpectedly crushes Ahmen's undead shields and looms over him, handing directly into **The Ghosts of the Ancestors**.
