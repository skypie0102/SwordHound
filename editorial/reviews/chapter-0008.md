## Survival objectives and score ladder

The children receive two objectives: **survive**, and if possible **hunt large, powerful demonic creatures**. Survival alone used to be sufficient, but Hugo added the hunting objective because some children simply hid in safe caves or burrows and lived off their rations.

Preserve the five-step scoring ladder as separate paragraph slots:
- simple survival — 10 points;
- survival without serious injury — 30 points;
- outlasting the other children and surviving — 50 points;
- hunting a demonic creature and surviving — 70 points;
- satisfying the higher combat/outlasting condition while still surviving without serious injury — 90 points.

There is deliberately no 100-point score. Hugo's rule is that a swordsman who becomes complacent dies. Death always earns zero. The Korean/Mirror phrasing around `개죽음` carries the sense of a worthless or pointless death; **"a dog's death—a pointless death"** preserves both the idiom and its meaning.

## Restricted area, Cradle and Forbidden Zone

The Guardian Knights warn the children to remain inside recently secured territory. Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain still contains many unexplored forbidden regions.

Vikir has visited the mountain many times before: first as a young examinee, later as an adult instructor, and eventually during his years as a Baskerville hunting hound. Returning as an instructor made the region even more frightening because instructors also searched beyond the safe zone.

Keep the geography distinct:
- the children's survival-test area is comparatively safe, almost a comfortable cradle;
- the boundary is guarded by Guardian Knight instructors;
- beyond it lies the **Forbidden Zone**;
- beyond the ridge known as the **Cradle**, and farther across mountain ranges, powerful demonic creatures and barbarian tribes gather.

The supplied Korean's later line says the Cradle inside the ridge is especially familiar to Vikir. Reject the MTL's contradictory sentence claiming he was familiar with neither the Forbidden Zone nor the Cradle.

Baskerville continually pushes its frontier outward. Expansion of Baskerville territory also expands the Empire, so imperial support, tax privileges and military autonomy provide incentives for the campaign.
