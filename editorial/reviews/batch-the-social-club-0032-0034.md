## Chapter 32 — the club and the baited heirs

The Youth Self-Governance Committee is exposed as a social club controlled by heirs of seven influential provincial houses: **Montblanc, Pierre, Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Ferragamo, Hermès, and Prada**.

Their luxury is funded principally by illegal slave trafficking that profits from people displaced by Baskerville's frontier expansion. The heirs routinely `tame` new officials with expensive parties, favors, alcohol and women, then use debt and access as a leash.

Vikir answers their requested audience by entering the VVIP room personally. After disabling staff who try to seize him, he uses extremely fine mana resonance to destroy a 385-glass champagne tower **from the top downward**. The chapter ends with the seven heirs immediately promising to pay for their own drinks.

## Chapter 33 — rank inference and punishment

The seven heirs correctly infer from Vikir's liquid-like aura and mana control that he is at least a **Sword Graduator**, though Vikir does not formally announce his hidden rank.

Their attempt to pretend the party was only a `test` fails. Vikir invokes Baskerville's master/hound hierarchy: a master may test a hound, but a hound does not test its master.

He then beats all seven with a wooden stake. This is punitive coercion, not yet the formal statutory sentence. The Korean-backed endpoint is: **if they already understand why they are being punished, they should accept the punishment.**

## Chapter 34 — old law applied without exception

The seven families send apologies and expensive tribute, and citizens assume a political compromise will free the heirs. Vikir instead applies the existing Baskerville statutes without class exemption.

The next morning the seven heirs' **salt-preserved heads** are publicly displayed with records of the additional penalties imposed before execution. Important corrections include:
- `salt-preserved`, not MTL `salted and disembowelled` heads;
- facial branding under the illegal-brothel law;
- repeated gambling penalties, including one record of seventy-two wrist cuts;
- removal of an eye for illegal usury;
- death for illegal human trafficking;
- posthumous removal of the body beyond the city walls for illegal lobbying/exile.

The scene then rewinds. Vikir tells Chihuahua the seven families are tied to wider criminal networks and explicitly thinks that evidence can be found—or, if necessary, fabricated. Preserve this morally compromised policy rather than sanitizing it.

Vikir does **not** expect to remain Deputy Magistrate for long. He anticipates Academy admission or combat reassignment and therefore favors an extremely forceful short-term purge despite the burden it may leave his successor.

The heirs refuse to expose their families and criminal partners. Vikir brings in an interrogation specialist and more than one hundred custom tools based on Age-of-Destruction methods, entering the cell at the family endpoint.
