## Core continuity

This family follows directly from *Bared Teeth*. Publicly, Vikir is an eight-year-old **Intermediate Sword Expert** who can manifest **two Baskerville fangs**. Privately he is already a **Low Sword Graduator**, can reliably use four fangs, has an unstable Fifth Fang from the restored scripture, and possesses Beelzebub. Nothing in Chapters 20–25 publicly exposes that hidden state.

Chapter 20 formalizes the project’s sword/mage/age/fang comparison tables. Normalize all corrupted `Gradient`, `Gradator`, `junior`, and similar machine labels to Low/Intermediate/Advanced Sword Expert and Sword Graduator. The direct-line curriculum normally bars `Van` descendants from the Fifth Form and above; their taught ceiling is the Fourth Form.

Beelzebub’s third slot upgrades from Norvegicus regeneration to **Super Regeneration — Troll (C+)** after the Chapter 19 kill. The artifact remains private.

## Ruby-mine dispute

The ruby deposit lies in the disputed Baskerville–Morgue border / joint-security region associated with **Red Fang Mountain** and the wider Le Rouge et Le Noir mountain system.

The key geometry is:
- the practical surface entrance is on Morgue-controlled land;
- the valuable underground vein extends beneath Baskerville-controlled territory;
- following the vein from the entrance necessarily means mining below Baskerville land.

Morgue values ruby as a magical research material. Baskerville values territorial sovereignty far more than the gem. Covert Morgue tunneling has repeatedly been detected and raided by Baskerville hounds, producing unpublicized bloodshed.

Camus first argues Morgue’s claim herself, then demonstrates it with the paper-wall/arm analogy. Vikir rejects her premise by treating anything that crosses into his territory as subject to his control. His pressure in that scene is **killing intent / veteran presence**, not a public Graduator aura reveal.

The political solution in Chapter 24 is strategic rather than conciliatory: Vikir recommends granting Morgue practical mining rights so Morgue commits people and resources downstream while Baskerville pushes its frontier farther into monster/barbarian territory. In Vikir’s first timeline, Hugo used essentially the same arrangement to drive displaced enemies toward Morgue’s mining zone.

## Camus–Vikir friendly match

Camus demonstrates **Quadruple Casting** with four First-Circle spells:
- Fireball
- Ice Spear
- Lightning Ring
- Mud Wall

The feat is extraordinary even by Morgue standards. Her weakness is tactical: she splits attention, reveals her cards immediately, and repeatedly sacrifices visibility for defense.

Vikir fights entirely within the image of an exceptional eight-year-old. He breaks Mud Walls physically and uses forehead flicks instead of serious attacks. When Camus becomes too angry to manage cleanly, he maneuvers her toward collateral from the neighboring fifteen-year-old match rather than revealing more strength.

The resulting clothing accident is handled strictly as a child-safety/embarrassment scene. Vikir lends Camus his outer training garment; a fragment grazes Vikir’s forehead, and visible blood ends the friendly bout. Do not apply adult sexual framing to either child.

## Marriage discussion

Adolf proposes a marriage alliance to make the mining-access deal politically respectable. His first proposal is **Camus Morgue and Osiris Le Baskerville**.

The supplied Korean explicitly gives Camus as eight and Osiris as twenty: a **12-year difference**. The recovered MTL’s `seventeen-year difference` is arithmetic corruption and is corrected.

Hugo rejects that match partly because Osiris already has a betrothal arranged from before birth. Camus says she will not marry anyone weaker than her mother and prefers someone around her own age or younger; her attention shifts to Vikir. Adolf objects to Vikir’s `Van` status, while Hugo reiterates his merit-over-bloodline view.

Vikir’s answer—he will obey if Hugo orders a marriage—is hound-like obedience, not a romantic declaration.

## Adolf test

Adolf’s Chapter 25 test is a tactical puzzle, not a rank contest. He balances a magically made, water-filled jar on his head and declares that Vikir wins if even one drop spills.

Adolf’s shield magic is not broken by Vikir. Vikir repeatedly strikes the same point until his own short sword shatters. Adolf thinks the boy failed by pitting an inferior sword against superior shielding. In fact, Vikir deliberately engineers a broken blade fragment to ricochet into the jar, creating a leak and satisfying the stated victory condition.

No hidden Graduator aura, Fifth Fang, Lurking Fang, or Beelzebub is revealed.

Exact family endpoint: water runs down Adolf’s head and Vikir dryly notes that the jar had indeed been full to the brim.
