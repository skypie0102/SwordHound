## Opening report and omen

The Korean witness compresses the opening physical description into fewer lines than the MTL. Preserve the three English paragraphs for the seated man, his appearance, and his titles, with explicit shared-line ownership. Hugo gestures for Barrymore to continue rather than literally folding his hands. The ruby-mine dispute is with Morgue, one of the seven great families, over a western mine on the territorial boundary.

The MTL's `handwriting` is corrected to the **written portion/exam** of the midterm evaluation. The practical exam begins in five days and most Fang Castle guardian knights are away preparing. The two-suns report follows Chapter 4's exact endpoint. Korean line 38 says servants are whispering that a **little sun has been born to aid the heir**, not merely `the children`. Korean line 43 clarifies the omen: two suns or multicolored clouds have long been considered auspicious. MTL `Chae-un` is therefore treated as a corruption of the colorful-cloud idea, not a new proper noun.

## Mana concealment and the Age of Destruction

Vikir is summoned to Hugo's study and carefully conceals his mana. The Korean witness gives a stronger and more coherent explanation than the MTL: warriors who survived the war against demons and the long Age of Destruction became masters at suppressing their mana; present-era warriors who never lived through that age do not know the method. The distinction is **experience**, not raw strength. The technique still has limits, so Vikir recognizes that sufficiently stronger mana will eventually become noticeable to Hugo. The garbled MTL thought about needing to `show myself` is rendered conservatively as needing a solution before that happens.

## Hugo's changed attitude and form of address

Hugo's voice is normally blade-sharp but is unexpectedly subdued here. Vikir remembers Hugo previously viewing children's growth like livestock being judged for market, which makes today's warmer, expectant gaze unusual.

Retain Vikir's address **Patriarch** from the MTL and established English terminology. The Korean machine-like `master` wording is internally inconsistent. Hugo's reaction is reconstructed as **`Patriarch, huh? Not 'Father'?`**: the series reference explicitly identifies Hugo as Vikir's father, and that reading explains why the formal title bothers him without changing the subsequent topic. This is an explicit adjudication of corrupted witnesses, not a verbatim claim about the supplied Korean line.

## Broken spirit and family creed

Hugo is concerned with the triplets' mental collapse, not their healed bodies. The Korean witness adds that they now become so frightened of Vikir that they wet themselves; preserve this because it directly supports Vikir's next thought that a hound which has lost its fighting spirit is useless on the battlefield.

Vikir's sword metaphor is restored as a contrast between **three dull blades** and **one finely forged blade**, rather than the MTL's malformed `master sword`. The final exchange is the Baskerville creed in compact form: **strength is justice; weakness is sin**. Vikir asks how the strong can be wrong. Hugo's mouth twitches because the eight-year-old is echoing the family's own values back at him. The Korean endpoint says the child is adorable enough to make Hugo's chest ache; preserve that emotional beat without importing later reconciliation.
