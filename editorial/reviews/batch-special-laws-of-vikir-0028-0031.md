## Family sequence

### Chapter 28 — bait and recruitment

Vikir arrives at Underdog City Hall a day early, establishes Chihuahua Baskerville as a capable outsider-born administrator, and deliberately allows the extravagant `wine and women` welcome banquet to develop as bait. He identifies the city's underground economy and announces that he will preserve the existing legal code while adding only one short law.

### Chapter 29 — public distrust and the stake

Underdog's citizens openly distrust laws because old statutes were enforced against commoners but not the wealthy and powerful. Vikir refuses to punish their mockery. He drives an ordinary wooden stake into the ground and promulgates the first clause of the Vikir Special Law: whoever pulls it free receives one hundred million gold.

Several old legal-text errors are corrected from Korean, including **death by drowning** for smuggling and **extermination of three generations** for embezzling military supplies.

### Chapter 30 — literal enforcement

The reward escalates from 100 million to 1 billion and finally **10 billion gold**. The stake is genuinely easy to remove; fear of arbitrary elite punishment is what stops the crowd. Judy, a poor flower-selling girl caring for her sick mother, finally pulls it free. Rather than inventing an offense, Vikir pays her the entire amount exactly as the written law promises.

The family principle becomes explicit: a law has force only when citizens believe it will be applied as written regardless of status.

### Chapter 31 — trust becomes enforcement

The story of Judy's payment spreads, crime falls by roughly half, and public trust begins returning. Chihuahua writes a plaque normalized as **<Trust Established by Moving the Log (移木之信)>**, reflecting the same trust-building principle.

Vikir then marks more than one hundred criminal locations from first-life knowledge. He plans to seize illicit assets, replenish the treasury, and use part of the recovered wealth for poor relief. Remaining organizations are quieter and more sophisticated, but their locations, leaders, corruption methods, and financial channels are already known to him.

The first organized backlash comes from the **City Hall Youth Self-Governance Committee**, a social club of second- and third-generation heirs from influential local families with ties to Baskerville. The chapter ends on the Korean image **“The flies smelled it first.”** Vikir had laid bait from his first day precisely to draw this class of opponents into the open.
