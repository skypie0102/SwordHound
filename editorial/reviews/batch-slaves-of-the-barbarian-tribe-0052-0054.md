## Family continuity decisions

- Vikir’s attack on Madam Eight-Legs did not defeat her. He survived because the strike’s flash created an opening, Aiyen’s lasso pulled him clear, and Styx/Murcielago kept his catastrophically injured body alive.
- The apparent sacrifice successfully removes Vikir from Hugo’s active surveillance. Baskerville/Morgue have multiple credible witnesses who believe he died protecting Camus.
- Inside Ballak territory, hostile prisoners face brutal treatment, but Aiyen claims Vikir personally and explicitly classifies him as a **slave/helper rather than a husband candidate**.
- Aiyen has now repaid Vikir’s earlier rescue twice by her own accounting: saving him from Madam Eight-Legs and shielding him from Ballak’s ordinary captive fate.
- **Akwilla = current Ballak chieftain = Aiyen’s mother = the Night Fox.** This resolves the speculation raised during The Husband Hunt.
- Vikir’s noncombat survival skills become his safest route to usefulness while his body regenerates: construction, drainage, repairs, firewood, laundry, fieldcraft, and general camp labor.
- Ballak’s internal communal freedom and external brutality are deliberately contrasted. Accepted members share freely; enemies/captives do not receive those protections.
- Aiyen offers Vikir a practical route toward acceptance/freedom through a major hunt, creating the direct handoff to Chapters 55–59.
