Several days passed.

Notices appeared throughout every territory governed by Baskerville.

In city squares.

Town centers.

Administrative offices.

Everywhere people gathered.

> **NOTICE REGARDING THE INVESTIGATION AND COMPENSATION PROCESS FOR THE ‘MISSING INFANT CASES’ WITHIN BASKERVILLE TERRITORY**
>
> This notice publishes the findings concerning all infant-disappearance cases recorded between **Imperial Years B.C.10 and A.C.19** and establishes compensation procedures.
>
> Families and orphanages affected during the relevant period have been sent compensation guidance in accordance with **Article 1, Paragraph 4 of the Vikir Special Law**.
>
> Relevant parties are requested to submit the required documents to their municipal office within the designated period so that the remains of the deceased may be recovered and properly laid to rest…

Baskerville had opened a full investigation into the kidnappings and disappearances.

Compensation procedures followed.

The family did not publicly announce that a Demon King had descended into its territory.

But it did acknowledge something almost as shocking.

Its second son—

Seth Le Baskerville—

had been responsible for horrific crimes.

For families whose children had simply vanished—

families whose lives had collapsed overnight—

it was at least the beginning of an answer.

Some had spent years demanding an investigation.

Protesting.

Going on hunger strikes.

Refusing to let their children disappear into silence.

Now—

they were finally allowed to see the truth being processed.

Punishments were announced openly.

Proceedings were made visible.

The bereaved families were not the only observers.

Ordinary residents were allowed to participate in the trials as public jurors.

The first major defendant was Seth's mother—

**Noot**.

Surprisingly—

there was no evidence that a demon had brainwashed her.

Throughout the proceedings, she simply screamed that she wanted to see Seth.

Her indictment contained **1,642 charges**.

Among them—

releasing venomous snakes into children's quarters.

The murder of Baskerville's First Lady.

Conspiring in the abduction of the eldest daughter.

Assisting Seth's kidnappings of infants.

The sentence was immediate.

Execution.

Seth's former nannies followed.

Public executions.

Then the subordinates who had organized the kidnapping and transportation of children.

The same punishment.

For ordinary people living under Baskerville—

watching high-ranking members of the inner family machine stand trial and die in public was almost unbelievable.

One lesson was reinforced.

Baskerville law was brutal.

Absolute.

And even someone inside Baskerville could be crushed beneath it.

The purge continued.

Orphanage directors who had sold children were beheaded.

Brokers—

lobbyists—

civil servants who falsified documents—

were sentenced to have their limbs torn apart.

Countless others connected to the network were executed or permanently crippled.

For children who had no surviving family—

memorial monuments were erected.

Senior officials were assigned to maintain them and pay respects each year.

Annual memorial observances were established so the dead would not vanish from memory again.

The entire process was directed personally by Osiris Le Baskerville.

The young patriarch supervised it from beginning to end.

Almost.

There was one person to whom Osiris delegated part of his authority.

Vikir Van Baskerville.

The hero who had survived the war beyond the frontier—

and returned only to uncover one of the largest crimes in Baskerville history—

was officially recognized as the case's first discoverer and greatest contributor.

His authority increased.

So did his reputation.

Osiris and Vikir moved quickly.

Within days—

the necessary special laws existed.

Within a week—

enforcement had already begun.

The affair was large enough that it would ordinarily warrant intervention from an Imperial investigative commission.

Yet the aftermath produced remarkably little dispute.

The measures were too fast.

Too complete.

Too public.

◆◆◆

“That's right.”

Hugo bent down.

“Come here, Pomeranian.”

His mustache was gone.

The mustache Hugo had worn with pride for years—

shaved clean.

It did not necessarily make his face more appealing.

But the fact that Hugo had gone to such trouble to look better for his granddaughter was astonishing enough.

Pomeranian touched his newly bare chin.

Then laughed.

Brightly.

Hugo's eyes softened.

“Look at that smile.”

Happiness and grief mixed on his face.

“She looks like Roxana.”

His arms tightened around the girl.

“And Penelope.”

Vikir entered Hugo's office after being summoned.

He stopped briefly at the sight.

Osiris was there as well.

“It's been a while, little brother.”

Osiris looked exhausted.

The work of the last few days had hollowed the area beneath his eyes.

But when he looked at Vikir—

a faint smile appeared.

Awkward.

Small.

Far more human than before.

Vikir bowed.

Hugo spoke.

“You've been busy lately.”

“Yes.”

“Finish whatever remains quickly.”

Hugo adjusted Pomeranian on his knee.

“If you keep handling this affair, you'll miss the timing.”

His voice became firm.

“Go enroll in the Academy.”

Vikir shook his head.

“The family is still unstable.”

He looked toward the window.

“I should finish reorganizing things before I leave—”

He stopped.

Vikir still did not trust Hugo.

The trials were one thing.

The wider movement inside the family was another.

A purge.

A massive one.

Hugo appeared to be using the Seth affair to reorganize Baskerville's internal power structure.

Reality was not a fairy tale.

Vikir did not believe the trials and punishments existed only because justice had suddenly awakened inside Hugo.

Baskerville rarely admitted full responsibility for disasters of this scale.

It admitted even less often that its own blood had caused them.

And compensation—

public compensation—

was rarer still.

But Hugo had done all of it.

Why?

*Cold political judgment.*

That answer fit him better than morality.

*Officially acknowledge the family's responsibility.*

*Then use the case to remove every senior figure tied to the network.*

Vikir understood the structure.

*Eliminate political enemies in one sweep.*

*Strengthen the patriarch's authority.*

A reshuffling.

A new board.

The Seth affair had removed an extraordinary number of people from Baskerville's power structure.

Minor officials.

Major officials.

People buried inside administration.

People holding important posts.

When all of them were purged or demoted together—

entire factions lost their foundations.

Most visibly—

the Seven Counts lost much of the secondary political machinery they had used to restrain the patriarch.

Their claws had been cut back.

The knight orders changed as well.

Under the justification of punishing anyone linked to demonic infiltration—

senior and junior councilors were removed one after another.

Osiris directed much of that work on behalf of Hugo's faction.

Vikir participated too.

But not merely to demonstrate support for Hugo.

He had another reason.

Data.

Records of the people Seth had contacted in secret.

Vikir quietly extracted those records.

Then handed them to Cindywendy.

Her job—

analyze everything.

Find patterns.

Identify the disguises and current locations of the other Ten Corpses.

Naturally—

Cindywendy had objected.

*Absolutely not!*

Vikir could still hear her shouting.

*I'm already exhausted!*

*And now you want me to track Demon King-class enemies?*

*How many heads do you think I have?!*

She had no desire to accept work that dangerous.

So Vikir asked one question.

*Then you don't want the native trade deal?*

Silence.

The largest potential trade agreement Cindywendy had ever seen—

a massive commercial route involving the peoples of the Depths.

In the end—

she surrendered.

That matter was progressing.

But something else still bothered Vikir.

*Why is Hugo trying to send me to the Academy right now?*

Vikir had no reason to oppose the Academy itself.

The timing was the problem.

Too convenient.

During a political purge—

a figure like Vikir was useful.

Everyone knew he was now associated with Hugo's line.

He had discovered Seth's corruption.

He had exposed the crime.

He had provided the patriarch with the cleanest possible justification for drawing his sword against internal opponents.

Keeping Vikir visibly inside the family strengthened Hugo's political position.

*Sending me away now is inefficient.*

That was the problem.

From Hugo's perspective—

delaying Vikir's enrollment for a year or two should have been more useful.

Yet Hugo was doing the opposite.

Pushing him out the door.

*Is there another scheme?*

Vikir narrowed his eyes.

He could not simply ask.

So suspicion remained.

Then—

Osiris leaned close.

His voice dropped low enough that only Vikir could hear.

“Father is worried.”

Vikir looked at him.

“About what?”

“That you'll be caught in the backlash from the power struggle.”

Osiris's expression stayed serious.

“And get hurt.”

*Worried?*

Vikir almost laughed.

Hugo?

That heartless man—

worried about someone?

The idea still felt absurd.

Almost as strange as Osiris whispering an explanation into his ear.

*Isn't this normally Barrymore's job?*

Vikir glanced across the office.

John Barrymore was occupied with a different crisis.

“My lord.”

The old butler stared at Hugo's face.

“You look better without the mustache.”

“Do I?”

Hugo touched his bare chin.

“It feels empty.”

He was braiding Pomeranian's hair.

A feather braid.

Vikir had no idea where Hugo had learned how to do it.

Hugo smiled faintly while working.

*I still can't get used to this.*

Vikir stared.

Then—

Pat.

A hand came down on his shoulder.

Warm.

Unexpectedly so.

Vikir looked up.

Osiris stood beside him.

“Thanks to you,” Osiris said, “I realized something.”

Vikir waited.

“A person has duties.”

Osiris's hand remained on his shoulder.

“But so does an older brother.”

“…”

“You don't need to worry about what happens here.”

Osiris attempted another smile.

It was clumsy.

Unpracticed.

But real.

“Go to the Academy.”

He tightened his grip once.

“By the time you come home for your first vacation—”

Osiris looked toward the work waiting outside.

“I'll have everything settled.”
