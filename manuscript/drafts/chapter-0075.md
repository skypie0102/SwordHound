Established terminology controls **Saint Mecca**, **Quovadis**, **Hound of the Night**, **Akwilla / Night Fox**, **Pomeranian La Baskerville**, **Chihuahua Baskerville**, **Underdog City**, **Cindywendy**, **Messinadnaro**, **Judy**, **Bourgeois Clan**, and **Ballak Tribe**.

Southwestern Empire.

White stone.

Orderly streets.

Elegant buildings.

At the center of Quovadis territory stood the holy city of **Saint Mecca**.

Carriages rolled slowly over pale boulevards.

Pedestrians moved without hurry.

At the city entrance, paladins in white armor and cloaks inspected every traveler.

Then—

CLATTER. CLATTER.

A carriage drawn by three horses approached the checkpoint.

“Please wait. Inspection.”

The merchant inside produced a pass and identification.

A paladin read them carefully.

“Joseph Josta. Oil dealer. First floor, 108 Dortmund Street.”

He looked at the wagon.

“Cargo?”

“Oil. As written on the permit.”

The merchant stroked his mustache.

Large metal drums filled the back of the carriage.

“May we inspect them?”

“Of course.”

Several fully armored paladins climbed aboard.

The wagon sank under their weight.

They examined the drums.

Heavy lids.

Tight seals.

Each container sounded full when struck.

THUNK.

“Need us to open them?”

The merchant smiled nervously.

“What else would an oil drum contain?”

The paladins exchanged looks.

“Pass.”

The carriage entered Saint Mecca.

◆◆◆

Some time later, it turned into a deserted side street.

The merchant checked both ends of the alley.

Then he forced open one of the oil drums.

HSSSS—CLANG.

Black oil filled the container.

“……You can come out.”

The surface moved.

A figure rose from the oil.

Black broad-brimmed hat.

Heavy trench coat.

A long bird-beaked mask like those worn by plague doctors.

Oil streamed from every part of him.

The merchant recoiled.

The masked man reached into his coat and offered payment.

An Oxbear fang.

Rare.

Valuable.

The merchant accepted it with both hands.

“Th-thank you.”

That had been the bargain.

Smuggle one man through Saint Mecca's gate inside the oil drum.

Receive the fang.

The masked man stepped down from the carriage.

The merchant could not stop himself from asking.

“Who are you?”

The beaked mask turned slightly.

“The Hound of the Night.”

Then the stranger disappeared into the alley.

The merchant stared at the oil drum.

It was still almost full.

“……How does a man hold his breath in oil for more than two hours?”

◆◆◆

Vikir removed the mask once he was safely out of sight.

“Puhah.”

He drew a long breath and wiped oil from his clothes.

He had not held his breath this long since the River Styx trial immediately after his birth.

*Hound of the Night.*

The name was borrowed from Akwilla's title, **Night Fox**.

It still felt awkward.

But it was better than being known only as Baskerville's hound.

He reviewed the trip.

Leaving the flooded jungle had taken two days.

Crossing Baskerville territory without being noticed had taken another.

Reaching and infiltrating Quovadis lands took one more.

Four days.

The flood had actually helped him.

Fast water carried floating corpses and logs, letting him move several times faster.

The canopy provided alternate routes over rivers.

Hungry monsters had attacked along the way.

Giant catfish.

Old lizards.

Bears.

Wild dogs.

Venomous bats.

None had seriously threatened the fully recovered Vikir.

One exception remained unpleasant.

*Bone-sucking mosquitoes.*

Even the violent rain had not discouraged them.

A swarm had chased him for hours.

Pomeranian, whose body was still weak, caught a cold during the journey.

So the first place Vikir visited after leaving the jungle was not Quovadis territory.

It was **Underdog City**.

◆◆◆

Chihuahua Baskerville was staring at the moon when Vikir entered through his window.

Chihuahua fainted.

Recovered.

Looked again.

Then fainted a second time.

By the third collapse, Vikir was beginning to lose patience.

“Deputy Magistrate?”

Chihuahua finally sat upright.

“A ghost? No—how did you even get in?”

“I worked here every day. Why wouldn't I know the building?”

Vikir explained only what Chihuahua needed to know.

Two years among Ballak.

His eventual return.

Absolute secrecy.

Chihuahua stared at him as though Vikir had returned from another world.

Then he noticed Pomeranian.

“Who is this child?”

His eyes moved between their black hair and red eyes.

“Don't tell me—”

“She is blood-related.”

Chihuahua's expression changed.

“But not in the way you're thinking. Stop.”

Vikir entrusted Pomeranian to him.

“She's important. Don't interrogate her. Give her proper food, medicine, and basic education until I return.”

Chihuahua did not hesitate.

He summoned a city-hall physician.

Pomeranian was examined.

Medicine and food arrived.

“I'll protect her with my life until you return.”

Vikir believed him.

Chihuahua looked like a corrupt bureaucrat.

His nature was almost painfully straightforward.

Once he gave his word, he kept it.

Vikir handed him Oxbear hide and horn.

Chihuahua's jaw dropped.

“This much?”

“Sell it.”

“This could be worth more than ten years of my salary.”

“Then she won't lack anything.”

Chihuahua smiled knowingly.

“You're a thoughtful father. I have three daughters, so I understand.”

Vikir decided not to correct every misunderstanding.

Then Chihuahua suddenly remembered something.

“Deputy. Wait.”

He opened a cabinet and removed a thick stack of old documents.

Newspaper clippings.

Official notices.

Records of Baskerville-Morgue interactions.

Political changes from the two years Vikir had been absent.

“I kept these in case you came back.”

Vikir looked at him.

Chihuahua scratched the back of his neck.

Embarrassed.

Vikir said nothing for a moment.

Then he repaid that loyalty with something practical.

Crime had begun rising again in Underdog City.

“If administration becomes difficult, find **Cindywendy of Messinadnaro**.”

Chihuahua blinked.

“The woman you released?”

“She owes me.”

“How do I find her?”

“Judy.”

“The flower girl you gave ten billion gold?”

“Yes.”

Vikir knew Cindywendy's habits.

Making money was difficult.

Keeping it was harder.

A child with capital but no protection was exactly the kind of person an investment-minded survivor would approach.

Chihuahua considered that.

“But why would Cindywendy help city government?”

“Offer her business.”

“What business?”

“A monopoly on trade with the jungle tribes.”

Chihuahua went silent.

After the disaster involving Bourgeois merchants two years earlier, Ballak and nearby peoples had stopped trading with Imperials.

But Vikir was now a Ballak hero.

If he vouched for a merchant, the route could reopen.

Whoever controlled that route could become enormously wealthy.

Cindywendy also understood the slums in ways ordinary administrators did not.

Her advice could help stabilize Underdog City.

Within hours of Vikir's return, problems that had frustrated Chihuahua for two years suddenly had possible solutions.

Then—

WHOOSH.

Vikir went back out through the window.

“Deputy Magistrate! Where are you going?”

Vikir did not stop.

He had made Aiyen a promise.

And Aheul was still sick.

For that, he needed something only Quovadis could provide quickly.

Holy healing.

Saint Mecca awaited.
