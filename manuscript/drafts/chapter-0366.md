Four in the morning.

The moment Vikir woke—

he was dragged toward the worksite.

No matter how strong or vicious a prisoner was—

once a BDISSEM chain around the neck was pulled while they were still half-asleep—

there was little they could do.

Prisoners spilled into the corridor like rotting corpses.

Dragged.

Staggering.

Barely conscious.

Then the night-shift guards counted them from a safe distance.

Morning roll call.

Most prisoners were quiet.

Chronic sleep deprivation.

Malnutrition.

Exhaustion.

Even if they wanted to complain—

they barely had enough energy to stay angry.

And showing too much irritation around other inmates—

could get someone killed.

Vikir waited before the giant iron gate leading to the worksite.

Tool distribution had not finished.

Lower-level prisoners formed lines nearby.

“More nails.”

“This won’t last through today’s quota.”

“My pickaxe is too dull.”

“How am I supposed to dig with this?”

“The hammer handle is loose.”

“You got another one?”

Every prisoner fought quietly for better equipment.

The daily quota was fixed.

Failure—

meant brutal punishment.

The guards had incentives too.

If the prisoners under their supervision failed to finish assigned sections—

the guards lost wages.

Leave.

Performance credit.

So guards were surprisingly cooperative about tool requests.

Some even argued with each other—

trying to secure better equipment for their own teams.

Vikir watched the patrol density.

Command flow.

Shift spacing.

He estimated.

*Roughly three thousand combat-capable guards.*

*At least four thousand including support personnel.*

And—

from the information he remembered before regression—

five wardens stood above them.

More manpower than expected.

That meant—

more caution.

Then—

a bundle of work tools landed in front of him.

A familiar face.

**Garam Nord.**

*I keep running into him.*

Vikir accepted the equipment.

Garam spoke in a businesslike tone.

“Tools are strictly controlled.”

“To prevent prisoners from doing anything stupid.”

“You return everything after work.”

“If even one item is missing—

you go to solitary until it’s found.”

“If something breaks—

you need written confirmation from a guard.”

“Otherwise—

same result.”

Lose a tool—

and death followed quickly.

So prisoners guarded their work equipment like life itself.

Hiding tools for escape—

or fighting—

was practically unimaginable.

Before entering the worksite—

each prisoner signed an inventory.

Rope.

Nails.

Every item.

Everything counted.

Everything inspected.

“Hurry!”

“If the schedule slips again—

we all get punished!”

“Write your inventory and move!”

Prisoners rushed.

Not because they liked labor.

Because they feared what happened when the project fell behind.

Vikir checked his own tools.

“…Pathetic.”

One hammer.

A length of chain.

A handful of nails.

Level Nine prisoners were given almost nothing.

There was little point.

Their work environment was too extreme.

Wood burned.

Metal softened.

Iron melted.

So most of the work—

was done with bare hands.

Breaking rock.

Digging earth.

Climbing barefoot.

Sharp material.

Heavy material.

Burning material.

Everything—

through the body.

Vikir had expected that much from the moment he was assigned Level Nine.

Then—

“Huh?”

“What’s this little brat?”

“You’re Level Nine too?”

Laughter from the next line.

Huge body.

Ugly red blotches.

A monstrous face.

**Sakkuth de Leviathan.**

Yesterday—

he had been assigned **Level Eight**.

Now—

he was openly provoking Vikir.

Every laugh carried a foul odor.

Other prisoners backed away.

“Sulfur shower didn’t even disinfect him.”

“You get too close—

you catch something.”

“Even the guards don’t know what to do with him.”

“Shit.”

“You avoid shit because it’s filthy.”

“…That shit is dangerous.”

The whispers only encouraged him.

Sakkuth grinned through teeth ruined by poison.

“Kid.”

“What did you do to get sent here?”

“You look too clean.”

“Seduce some noble lady?”

“Happens sometimes.”

“Yesterday I met some pretty-boy prisoner.”

“Casanova?”

“Something like that.”

“Doesn’t matter anymore.”

“I ate him.”

“Alive.”

He mimed chewing.

Nobody intervened.

Prisoners were terrified of infection.

Guards wore masks and protective suits—

keeping their distance.

Only Vikir—

did not move.

He calmly finished recording his tool list.

Sakkuth’s grin twitched.

“Hey.”

“You deaf?”

“….”

“I saw you during admission.”

“You looked tough enough.”

“So you’re really Level Nine?”

“….”

“Listen.”

“Just because I’m Level Eight and you’re Level Nine—

doesn’t mean you’re stronger than me.”

Sakkuth jabbed a finger toward Vikir’s head.

“I came here voluntarily.”

“To serve **that person**.”

“….”

“But what kind of nonsense is this?”

“That person is in Level Nine.”

“So why am I stuck in Eight?”

“Something’s wrong.”

“Someone like me belongs in Nine.”

“Why do **you** get it?”

“Send me down there!”

“Let me serve that person!”

His body began leaking black fumes.

The BDISSEM restraints could stop mana.

They could not stop the poison physically accumulated inside his body.

“Why am I Level Eight?!”

“Because I surrendered voluntarily?!”

“If that’s the reason—

I’ll tear this whole place apart!”

“Want to taste my Red Death?!”

Sakkuth roared.

Then—

“…I know why.”

A short answer.

Sakkuth stopped.

Vikir finished his inventory.

Packed the tools.

Sakkuth stared.

“You say something?”

“Yes.”

“Hehehe.”

The laugh boiled out of Sakkuth’s throat.

He leaned close.

“Then tell me.”

“Why am I not Level Nine?”

“Because you’re missing the basics.”

“…Basics?”

Sakkuth opened his mouth.

Vikir’s hand moved.

WHOOSH.

A handful of nails—

straight into Sakkuth’s mouth.

Then—

BANG!

One punch beneath the jaw.

Sakkuth’s head snapped backward.

The nails collided—

piercing cheek.

Nose.

Jaw.

Neck.

Blood exploded from his mouth.

“Ghk—!”

Vikir gave him a faint smile.

“You should at least know whether you’re the predator—

or the prey.”

Then—

boots hammered down the stairs leading to the worksite.

Senior guards.

Fast.

Angry.

A voice thundered.

“What is this disturbance—

**Night Hound**?!”

Every prisoner went pale.

At the front—

**Lieutenant Bastille**.

Worksite commander.

The rumor had finally become visible.

Night Hound.

Three thousand twenty-one life sentences.

Level Nine.

And—

to everyone’s surprise—

he looked like a young man.
