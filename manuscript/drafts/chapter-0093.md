# Chapter 93: The Ghosts of the Ancestors (4)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/093.txt` — `7c4558df6c485c790389c25096cb5b1891b078bf`. Recovered English Chapter 92 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Akwilla**, **Aiyen**, **Divine Archer Adonai**, **Ah'Heman**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Death Knight**, **Madam Eight-Legs**, and **Vikir**.

Every arrow Adonai fired could kill.

Akwilla caught one and crushed it.

“Stay back.”

She stepped in front of Vikir and Aiyen.

Eight other Death Knights had already fallen to her.

Now only the summit remained.

Adonai.

Old era.

Akwilla.

New era.

Two archers standing at the peak of Ballak across different centuries.

Vikir swallowed.

Even during the Age of Destruction, fights between master-level monsters like this were rare.

“What happens?”

Aiyen answered immediately.

“My mother doesn't lose.”

She believed it.

Reality looked less certain.

Akwilla drew her great bow.

Silver solid aura gathered.

BOOM.

The arrow cut through Adonai's black aura.

He answered.

Black power exploded from his body.

The ground cracked.

Sulfurous gas rose.

His aura caught through it like hellfire.

A storm formed.

Dark clouds gathered overhead.

Silver arrows and black arrows devoured each other.

The speed exceeded what Vikir or Aiyen could fully track.

“Akwilla's speed matches his.”

Aiyen never looked away.

“Her archery is the best in the world.”

The duel became a lesson in impossible precision.

Prediction.

Counter-prediction.

Curved shots.

Shots using air currents from the opponent's own arrows.

High arcs dropping from above.

Side-angle trajectories that appeared to come from nowhere.

Akwilla ducked.

A black arrow broke a log behind her.

Adonai jumped.

A silver arrow exploded sand where he had stood.

Back and forth.

Then Akwilla began losing ground.

A black arrow grazed her side.

Another cut her shoulder.

Not because her technique was inferior.

Because she needed physical arrows.

Adonai did not.

Akwilla could form aura arrows in emergencies, but the cost was enormous.

Adonai drew endlessly on Death Knight power.

And the wind favored him.

Vikir frowned.

“Bad direction.”

Adonai shot with the storm at his back.

Akwilla fired into it.

Her arrows lost force.

His gained it.

The poison in Adonai's bow made every wound worse.

Akwilla's cuts darkened.

Her face became pale.

Then her own arrows began flying wide.

One.

Two.

Three.

Completely wrong directions.

Vikir's concern sharpened.

It looked as though the entire jungle had chosen Adonai.

Other Death Knights still pressured Ballak warriors.

Salt slowed them.

It did not end them immediately.

“We need reinforcements from the village.”

Aiyen covered Vikir's mouth.

“Quiet.”

He stared.

“We're almost winning.”

“How?”

Akwilla looked exhausted.

Adonai looked unstoppable.

But Aiyen saw what Vikir did not.

Her expression changed.

“I see it.”

She whispered.

“I see what Mother sees.”

Then an arrow struck Adonai in the back.

He stopped.

Another hit the back of his head.

Then dozens came.

From behind.

Ah'Heman flattened himself to avoid them.

Vikir finally understood.

They were Akwilla's earlier misses.

Not misses.

The arrows had entered the storm.

Circled through the wind.

Then returned toward the storm's center.

Toward Adonai.

The gale that had seemed to favor him was carrying Akwilla's arrows back with greater force.

THUD.

THUD.

CRACK.

Adonai's back filled with shafts.

A second arrow struck the first embedded in his thigh.

The combined force punched through.

Adonai dropped to one knee.

Akwilla raised her bow one final time.

“Great Ancestor.”

Her voice was tired.

“If you still had your mind, you would never make such a simple mistake.”

She drew.

“It saddens me.”

One arrow was already lodged halfway into the back of Adonai's skull.

Akwilla's final shot entered from the front.

The arrowheads met.

THUNK.

Adonai fell to his knees.

The legend of the old generation bowed to the living myth of the new.

Akwilla did not celebrate.

This was not Adonai in his prime.

He had died old.

His body was decayed.

His intelligence was gone.

She could not call the duel fair.

*If he had been alive at his peak…*

She refused to finish the thought.

Then Ah'Heman screamed.

With Adonai down, the outcome was decided.

The remaining Death Knights pulled close around him.

Ballak warriors surrounded them.

Aiyen raised her bow.

“Now die.”

Ah'Heman had one last card.

He dragged Adonai's corpse and the other ancestors close.

Blood covered his hands and mouth.

“Come closer and I'll defile them.”

Everyone stopped.

Ballak superstition held that an ancestor stained with a traitor's blood might be barred from the warriors' paradise.

Ah'Heman knew the fear.

And he knew enough witchcraft to make the threat credible.

Akwilla could not fire cleanly.

He hid behind the bodies.

Then Vikir spoke.

“Everyone move back.”

He drew a bow.

Not toward Ah'Heman.

Not toward the Death Knights.

Toward a distant structure built from stacked logs.

Akwilla frowned.

“What are you doing?”

“Get somewhere high.”

Vikir released.

The arrow arced.

It cut a vine knot holding the logs together.

SNAP.

Then another vine tore.

Then another.

The temporary barrier collapsed.

Behind it waited floodwater from the rainy season.

A river rich with dissolved salt.

The entire mass came down at once.
