# Chapter 93: The Ghosts of the Ancestors (4)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/093.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 92 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Akwilla**, **Aiyen**, **Divine Archer Adonai**, **Ah'Heman**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Death Knight**, **Madam Eight-Legs**, and **Vikir**.

Every arrow Adonai fired was a terrifying weapon.

Yet Akwilla had caught one with her bare hand—

and crushed it.

“Step back.”

She took one more step forward, placing herself between Adonai and Vikir and Aiyen.

Eight Death Knights had already tried to bar her path.

She had sent every one of them back toward the graves where they belonged.

Now only the summit remained.

Adonai.

The ultimate archer of Ballak's old age.

Akwilla.

The archer leading its new age.

Two peaks of the same tradition stood across from one another, separated by generations but meeting now on the same battlefield.

Vikir swallowed.

A battle between master-level aura users was never common.

Even during the Age of Destruction, when monsters capable of overturning entire battlefields appeared constantly, a true life-and-death duel between people at this level remained rare.

“…How do you think this ends?”

“There isn't even a question.”

Aiyen answered without hesitation.

Her expression was firm.

“Our mother does not lose.”

There was absolute trust in her voice.

Reality seemed determined to challenge that belief.

Creak—

Akwilla raised her enormous bow.

She drew the thick string until it groaned under impossible tension.

Then released.

BOOM!

Silver aura streaked toward Adonai.

Not liquid.

Solid.

A true master-level arrow.

It pierced through the black aura rolling off the Death Knight.

[…]

Adonai counterattacked immediately.

His mind might have been gone.

His instincts were not.

The combat sense of the greatest archer in Ballak history remained engraved into the corpse itself.

WHOOOM!

Black aura exploded from Adonai like pressurized steam.

The ground around him split.

Sulfurous gas poured from the cracks.

Adonai's aura rolled across it like black fire from hell.

The current twisted upward.

A vortex climbed into the sky.

Dark clouds gathered over the jungle.

Into that blackened sky—

Akwilla fired silver lightning.

Silver lines and black trajectories devoured one another.

The exchange happened so quickly that Aiyen, a new Graduator, could not follow it all.

Even Vikir, already a High Sword Graduator, lost pieces of the battle between blinks.

“Akwilla's speed is matching Adonai's.”

Aiyen never looked away.

“Mother's archery is the best in the world. Accuracy isn't the only reason.”

The two living myths—

one literally dead, one living—

gave the watching warriors a lesson in what mastery actually meant.

There was not a wasted shot.

Predict the direction an opponent would move.

Fire before the movement began.

Basic.

Use the air current generated by an enemy arrow to curve one's own shot.

Send another arrow high on a parabola, only for it to appear suddenly from above.

Create a side angle where none seemed possible.

Technique after technique appeared without pause.

BOOM!

Akwilla lowered her head.

A black arrow passed over her and snapped a massive log behind her at the waist.

THUD!

Adonai leapt.

A silver arrow buried itself in the sand where he had stood and blasted dust in every direction.

Advance.

Retreat.

Attack.

Counterattack.

Neither gave the other a clean opening.

And yet—

as time passed, Akwilla began to lose ground.

Fwoosh!

One of Adonai's arrows grazed her flank.

Black projectiles were slowly tightening around her.

But the difference was not skill.

Akwilla required physical arrows.

Adonai did not.

Akwilla could imitate his method and create projectiles from pure aura, but doing so consumed enormous amounts of mana.

She could afford it only in emergencies.

Adonai, sustained by the negative energy of undeath, could continue producing solid aura arrows at full purity again and again.

And there was another problem.

“…The wind is against her too.”

Vikir frowned.

Adonai had the wind at his back.

Akwilla faced it head-on.

The storm itself seemed to strengthen Adonai's arrows while cutting the force of Akwilla's.

The difference appeared gradually across her body.

More cuts.

More blood.

And Adonai's arrows carried the poison of Madam Eight-Legs.

Sssssss—

The wounds on Akwilla's body began turning black.

Her face, in contrast, grew pale.

Twang!

Twang!

Twang!

Then even Akwilla's arrows began missing.

One sailed wide.

Another vanished high into the storm.

Another curved far away from Adonai.

The wind carried them into completely wrong directions.

They never seemed to reach their target.

It looked as though the entire jungle had chosen to protect Adonai.

*Was the old man truly a god of the storm?*

Vikir thought furiously.

If this continued, Akwilla would lose.

And if Akwilla fell, the remaining Death Knights would overwhelm the rest of Ballak.

*Do we have to kill Ah'Heman first?*

That was the obvious answer.

It was also almost impossible.

Adonai's defense stood between them and the summoner.

And Ah'Heman had not poured all of his remaining power solely into Adonai.

Several other elite Death Knights still fought around the clearing.

The Ballak warriors could slow them with salt.

They could not simply ignore them.

“We need someone to go back to the village and bring every other warrior!”

Vikir raised his voice.

“At this rate—!”

A hand covered his mouth.

“Shh.”

Aiyen.

“We're almost winning.”

“…?”

Vikir stared at her.

Akwilla was exhausted.

Her wounds were dark with poison.

Her arrows were flying wildly in the headwind.

Adonai was still attacking.

From every obvious angle—

they were losing.

But Aiyen understood archery better than Vikir.

And she was seeing something he was not.

Her expression went blank with awe.

“I see it.”

Her eyes followed the storm.

“Yes.”

A whisper.

“I can see what Mother sees.”

“…?”

Vikir tilted his head.

Then—

THUD!

An arrow appeared from nowhere and buried itself in Adonai's back.

The shaft trembled.

It had not come from above.

Not below.

Not from the side.

From behind.

[…]

Adonai paused.

Behind him there should have been no enemy.

Only Ah'Heman.

Then—

THUD!

Another arrow struck the back of his head.

Then another.

FWOOSH-FWOOSH-FWOOSH!

A rain of arrows began arriving from behind.

“Ghk!?”

Ah'Heman flattened himself against the ground like an insect.

Several returning arrows passed close enough to graze the back of his neck.

“…!”

Vikir looked upward, mouth half open.

Now he saw them.

The arrows coming from behind Adonai—

were Akwilla's earlier “misses.”

Fwoosh!

Another storm of arrows returned.

Again—

all from behind.

The violent wind that had seemed to favor Adonai was carrying Akwilla's arrows back toward him.

Vikir finally understood.

Akwilla had never fired in the wrong direction.

Her arrows had entered the storm.

Ridden the circular current.

Traveled around the entire vortex.

Then returned to its origin.

Like salmon fighting upstream until they reached the place where they had been born.

The center of Adonai's black whirlwind.

The beginning of the wind.

THUD!

THUD-THUD!

CRACK!

Adonai's exposed back became a hedgehog of arrow shafts.

The arrows had ridden the wind through broad arcs and returned with even greater force than when Akwilla first released them.

They could not completely pierce Adonai's powerful corpse.

They buried themselves deep.

Then—

THUD!

Another arrow struck the back end of a shaft already lodged in Adonai's thigh.

The second impact drove the first arrow completely through.

Adonai staggered.

Dropped to one knee.

Akwilla lifted her head.

She looked exhausted.

“Great Ancestor.”

Her voice was quiet.

“If your mind were still intact, you would never have made such a simple mistake.”

She drew one last arrow.

“That is what makes this sad.”

Adonai's movement was already restricted.

Behind him—

blind arrows returned with the storm.

In front—

Akwilla aimed deliberately.

She released.

THUD!

One arrow entered from the back of Adonai's head.

Another from the forehead.

Both stopped halfway.

Then—

click.

The two arrowheads met inside the skull.

BOOM!

Adonai dropped fully to his knees.

The legend of the previous generation bowed before the myth of the new.

“…”

Akwilla looked at him.

She did not feel victorious.

She was alive and at the peak of her power.

The Adonai before her had already died from age.

He had been dragged back as a corpse.

His body had lost much of the strength it possessed in life.

His basic intelligence was gone.

This had not been a fair contest.

*If Lord Adonai had been alive in his prime…*

Akwilla shook her head.

She did not want to imagine the answer.

Then—

“AAAAAAAGH!”

Ah'Heman screamed.

He clutched at Adonai's corpse.

The strongest Death Knight had fallen.

The outcome was obvious.

The few remaining undead gathered tightly around Ah'Heman.

Far more living Ballak warriors closed in around them.

Aiyen raised her bow.

“Now die.”

Her eyes held only contempt.

But Ah'Heman still had not exhausted every gamble.

Almost all his life force was gone.

Almost all his mana was gone.

His lower body was ruined.

Still—

he refused to release his grip on life.

“Don't come any closer!”

He dragged Adonai and the other ancestral corpses around himself.

Blood ran from his mouth.

Blood covered his hands.

He gathered it deliberately.

“If you come near me—”

His eyes rolled wildly.

“I'll stain your ancestors with my blood!”

Everyone stopped.

Even Akwilla.

Among Ballak, there was an old superstition.

A warrior whose remains were defiled by the blood of a traitor might be unable to enter the paradise promised to warriors.

Ah'Heman knew it.

He was using that belief as a hostage.

If the warriors attacked carelessly, their ancestors' souls might not merely have been exploited through necromancy—

they might be denied rest forever.

And Ah'Heman was a skilled shaman.

That made it difficult to dismiss the threat as empty.

“…”

Akwilla raised her bow.

Ah'Heman immediately crouched behind Adonai's body.

No one could get a clean shot.

The warriors hesitated.

Then Vikir spoke.

“Everyone back away.”

Every eye turned toward him.

Vikir had drawn his bow.

But he was not aiming at Ah'Heman.

He was not aiming at the Death Knights.

He was aiming far away.

“…What are you doing?”

Even Akwilla could not understand it.

She had just performed an archery miracle using the wind itself.

Vikir's target still made no sense to her.

He looked completely certain.

“Get to high ground first.”

That was all he said.

Then—

TWANG!

His arrow flew in a high parabola.

It struck a stack of enormous logs.

More precisely—

the knot of vines binding those logs together.

SNAP!

The first vine broke.

Then another.

Then another.

Creak—

CRACK!

The heavy logs began to shift.

The crude wooden barrier collapsed.

And the thing held behind it surged forward.

ROAAAAAR!

Water accumulated throughout the long rainy season burst free.

Not ordinary freshwater.

A river saturated with dissolved salt.

An enormous wall of saltwater came crashing down.
