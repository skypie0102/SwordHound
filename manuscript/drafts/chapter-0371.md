# Chapter 371: Solitary Confinement (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/371.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 369 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical English **Souaré** is verified through the designated Fandom index; **Angajuman** is retained as the source-given alias because no accessible canonical Fandom entry was found. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Nouvelle Vague, BDISSEM, Garam Nord, Kirko Grimm, D'Ordume D'Orcdile, and the Five Wardens.

**Solitary confinement.**

A punishment cell used by one prisoner alone.

In ordinary prisons—

being locked alone was considered severe punishment.

Someone unfamiliar with prison might wonder why.

Then—

they imagined a room smaller than half a square meter.

Nothing to see.

No one to speak to.

No room to stretch.

A sealed box.

That—

was the ordinary meaning.

Nouvelle Vague—

was worse.

*I’d heard about it.*

Vikir had been sentenced to one hundred days.

For refusing labor.

For assaulting guards.

Now—

he would experience the punishment considered second only to execution.

CLACK.

CLINK.

A guard pulled the cart carrying Vikir’s battered body.

D'Ordume’s punch had left him covered in blood.

The guard spoke quietly.

“From this point onward—

you will be placed in solitary confinement.”

“You will not come out for one hundred days.”

Vikir raised his head.

Dried blood cracked loose.

A familiar face appeared.

**Garam Nord.**

“You again?”

Garam smiled awkwardly.

“No.”

“This time—

I volunteered.”

“…Why?”

“To bring you there safely.”

Garam explained.

Some guards enjoyed killing badly injured prisoners for entertainment.

So he had volunteered—

to ensure Vikir suffered only the punishment officially assigned to him.

Nothing extra.

Vikir tilted his head.

“Why do you care whether I live?”

“…I wanted to thank you.”

“For what?”

“For not killing me.”

Vikir fell silent.

The riot—

had been planned.

There had been no reason to make the incident larger than necessary.

So he had deliberately controlled his strength—

making sure no guard died.

Garam—

apparently—

had misunderstood that restraint as personal mercy.

Vikir considered correcting him.

Then Garam continued.

“And…”

“Thank you for not seriously hurting Kirko.”

“…Kirko?”

It took Vikir a moment.

Then he remembered—

the courageous young guard who had attacked him before D'Ordume arrived.

*Talented.*

*Far too talented to waste away in a place like this.*

Vikir glanced at Garam.

The tips of the young man’s ears—

were red.

“The other guards had shattered noses.”

“Broken teeth.”

“Broken arms and legs.”

“But Kirko was almost unharmed.”

“So…”

“I’m grateful.”

“If she wasn’t hurt—

why are **you** grateful?”

“That…”

Garam stammered.

Then shut his mouth.

Vikir understood.

*Too ordinary.*

In a place like Nouvelle Vague—

that was practically a defect.

Garam was—

at heart—

a normal young man.

So this abnormal place crushed him.

Other guards isolated him.

Prisoners bullied him.

Even the woman he quietly cared about—

looked down on him.

*Nouvelle Vague does not suit him.*

Garam was naturally kind.

Here—

kindness had no value.

Then—

Garam spoke again.

“…We’re almost there.”

“….”

“I recommend biting your tongue before they put you inside.”

“Solitary confinement is…”

“It’s really terrible.”

A sincere warning.

Vikir ignored it.

“I heard the same advice before I arrived at Nouvelle Vague.”

“No.”

“This is different.”

“Much worse than transport.”

“Much worse than admission—”

Garam stopped.

A long shadow had already fallen across the cart.

“Hehehe.”

“Well, well.”

“Who do we have here?”

“It’s been a while since I got a guest.”

A woman’s voice echoed through the blood-red cavern.

Garam’s body stiffened.

“Th-then I’ll stop here.”

“Good luck.”

He saluted whoever stood ahead.

Then—

left quickly.

Vikir looked up.

Sharp stone spires rose around the cavern—

resembling Red Fang Castle from House Morgue territory.

A woman approached through the grotesque landscape.

Tall.

Slim.

Curved figure.

Sunglasses.

And—

a magnificent mustache.

Vikir recognized her immediately.

**Souaré.**

One of Nouvelle Vague’s five great wardens.

A mixed descendant of dwarves and elves.

A rival of D'Ordume for future leadership of the prison.

Her dwarven blood—

gave her the beard.

And an extraordinary affinity for minerals.

“Hm~”

“You’re the new prisoner who fought D'Ordume?”

“Energetic little thing.”

Souaré smiled down at him.

CRUNCH.

One high heel crushed the cart.

Her hand closed around Vikir’s neck.

“Let’s see.”

“Which solitary cell should I give you?”

She shook him lightly—

almost playfully.

Vikir frowned.

There were no cells anywhere nearby.

No doors.

No rooms.

Nothing that looked like confinement.

Then Souaré answered the question herself.

“Maybe asphalt and concrete?”

“And a little BDISSEM powder.”

“That should shape nicely.”

She tapped the ground with one foot.

Then—

the earth melted.

Red.

Glowing.

Boiling like magma.

The stone became a viscous liquid—

almost like molten metal.

And began moving according to Souaré’s will.

“I’ll make you a custom cell.”

She smiled.

Set Vikir down.

Then pushed a thick tube into his mouth.

“This is your breathing hole.”

“Don’t drop it.”

The instant she finished speaking—

molten concrete and asphalt poured down.

Vikir disappeared beneath it.

A cocoon formed around his body.

Hot material flowed over the surface.

SSSSSSSS.

The shell hardened.

Temperature rose.

Space vanished.

Vikir closed his eyes.

*So this is Nouvelle Vague’s solitary confinement.*

Even Vikir—

who feared very little—

felt the oppressive horror of it.

Now he understood.

Why prisoners feared solitary almost as much as execution.

Ordinary sentences—

one or two days.

Severe punishment—

perhaps a week.

Vikir—

one hundred days.

He remembered Bastille’s reaction.

*A hundred days in solitary is practically execution.*

Now—

the meaning was obvious.

*At least they gave me a tube.*

Water.

Food.

Air.

Barely.

Everything necessary to remain alive—

and nothing else.

For someone as relentlessly active as Vikir—

the idea of doing absolutely nothing—

was almost its own punishment.

*Fine.*

*I can finally rest the body.*

*Organize the mind.*

He had spent years—

always moving.

Always fighting.

Always preparing for what came next.

Perhaps stopping—

for once—

would be useful.

The environment was miserable.

But meditation could turn the next hundred days into training.

Review memories.

Sort pre-regression information.

Plan future operations.

Vikir settled his breathing.

Three months—

and ten days.

He would endure.

At least—

that had been the plan.

Then—

a voice came from somewhere unexpected.

“Hisssss…”

“A young one this time?”

An old man.

The voice—

unpleasant.

Like fingernails dragging across glass.

“Not bad.”

“A cellmate in my old age.”

“Stay alive a while, young man.”

“Give me someone to talk to.”

Vikir moved his jaw carefully against the hardening concrete.

He could not afford to lose the breathing tube.

“Who are you?”

Silence.

The concrete continued solidifying.

Then—

“Hissss…”

The laugh returned.

From the cell beside him.

“Call me…”

“**Angajuman**.”
