Vikir fell silent.

*Esse, Non Videri.*

To be—

without being seen.

Two candidates competing to become the next head of House Bourgeois.

One—

Juliet.

Daughter of the second son, Damien.

The other—

the child of the eldest son, Bartolomeo.

Gender unknown.

Age unknown.

No public information at all.

Damien had once said:

*Coincidentally—

both my brother and I have only one daughter.*

Without that—

Vikir would not even have known Bartolomeo’s child was female.

Juliet’s identity had become public after she dropped out of the succession test.

Bartolomeo’s daughter—

nobody knew where she was.

Or what she was doing.

Some people were not even certain she existed.

*So Sinclaire was Bartolomeo’s daughter.*

Vikir touched his chin.

When he first met Juliet—

something about her had seemed familiar.

Now he understood.

Blood.

*And now I know why Sinclaire disappeared after graduation in the future.*

Perhaps—

after finishing Colosseo—

Sinclaire had become head of House Bourgeois.

Then vanished behind the enormous curtain that separated ordinary society from the people who truly moved the world.

A level of power so distant that the Vikir of his previous life—

a low-ranking soldier—

had never even known whether such people existed.

The world’s true VVIPs.

But the future tree—

that might one day tower over the world—

was still only a small leaf.

“Bro.”

“What do you think of me?”

Sinclaire’s voice pulled him back.

She was still crying in his arms.

Vikir thought carefully.

“…Why are you asking that now?”

A question like **what do you think of me**—

was obviously not really asking for an evaluation.

Vikir was not stupid.

In fact—

he considered himself fairly perceptive about these things.

Sinclaire pressed her lips together.

Wiped her eyes.

Then answered with unexpected firmness.

“Because dating Bro is the one thing I want to accomplish before I leave the Academy.”

“…Accomplish?”

Vikir’s mouth opened slightly.

Was a relationship really something to list beside achievements?

Sinclaire apparently thought so.

“Originally—

I planned to finish all four years.”

“Graduate.”

“Then leave this world behind.”

She counted with her fingers.

“Perfect grades.”

“Outside awards.”

“First place for all four years.”

“Student council president.”

“Colosseo graduate.”

“I thought those would be the most valuable things I could gain from school.”

A bitter smile.

“That’s why—

at first—

I couldn’t understand my cousin.”

“My cousin dropped out midway through school.”

Juliet.

Former Temisquira student.

Sinclaire hugged Vikir tighter.

“But now I understand her.”

“My thinking changed.”

“The most valuable things you take from school aren’t grades.”

“Or a diploma.”

“Or awards.”

“Or certificates.”

The memories—

of the people beside you.

Those mattered more.

Sinclaire looked at him with complete certainty.

Vikir asked:

“Why give me that much value?”

“I’m not someone worth that much.”

Sinclaire finished another beer.

Crushed the can in one hand.

“Then am I someone special?”

She laughed weakly.

“…When did I start liking Bro?”

“I’m curious too.”

“Should I think back?”

She closed her eyes.

Then began.

Her memories.

Her perspective.

The story of her first year.

◆◆◆

*Why do I have to leave?*

A little girl hugged a teddy bear.

She looked up at an old butler.

The butler bowed.

*When you grow up and return—

everything here will belong to you.*

And with those words—

the girl left House Bourgeois.

An orphanage.

The girl studied desperately.

Other children became discouraged.

Gave up.

She did not.

A prestigious elementary school.

The girl entered—

and immediately encountered discrimination over her background.

Performance evaluations that somehow went badly.

Cleaning duty that somehow returned too often.

Subtle exclusion.

Whispers.

Cruel words.

She endured it.

The world was not always fair.

But—

overall—

it was fair enough that excellence eventually forced people to recognize her.

The children who mocked her—

eventually wanted to be friends.

Teachers who had looked at her with prejudice—

eventually favored her.

As she grew older—

her appearance also began opening doors.

Finally—

after overcoming every obstacle—

the girl entered the Empire’s greatest academy unusually young.

Colosseo.

First place.

She had proven herself again.

Then came the freshman oath.

*Survive no matter what.*

*Climb.*

*Use useful people.*

*Discard useless people without hesitation.*

Her father’s words.

The girl carried them with her.

Then came the first class.

Even here—

she believed she was the smartest student.

Then—

a boy caught her attention.

Messy hair.

Ordinary name.

Unremarkable interview.

Nothing special at first glance.

Then—

during class—

the boy humiliated a professor who deliberately asked impossible questions.

*…I see.*

*His written score really was perfect.*

Even that harsh professor acknowledged it.

A score far above the girl’s own.

Her result—

in the seven hundreds—

had been second only to him.

The test had been brutal.

Yet someone had beaten her decisively.

From then on—

she became curious.

Maybe—

for the first time in her life—

she wanted to understand another person.

The girl knew she was attractive.

Smart.

Capable.

Used to being approached.

So when she decided to approach the boy—

she assumed he would welcome it.

But she had never really initiated with anyone before.

So—

awkwardly—

she spoke.

*Um… hello…*

Later—

during volunteer work—

she asked why he was there.

His answer was simple.

*Demerit points.*

The girl had expected the usual beautiful answers.

Fulfillment.

Service.

Sacrifice.

He gave none of them.

He looked bothered.

Moved away.

The girl—

unused to being dismissed—

followed.

*Funny coincidence.*

*We got assigned to the same volunteer site.*

*I suppose.*

It had not been coincidence.

The girl had begged the coordinator—

worked hard—

just to be assigned beside him.

She spent the day asking about his written score.

He gave her almost nothing.

She thought he was stingy.

But—

despite claiming he volunteered only because of demerit points—

he worked seriously.

Cleaning toilets.

Serving meals.

Repairing plumbing.

Laundry.

Playing with children.

Maintaining the grounds.

Work that should have required dozens of people—

he handled without complaint.

*…He’s actually a good person.*

That was the first time the girl sincerely praised someone.

After that—

the way she addressed him changed.

*Good morning!*

*…?*

*Bro!*

*Why are you ignoring me?*

*I didn’t realize you were talking to me.*

*And we’re classmates.*

*Don’t call me that.*

*Why?*

*I’m a year younger.*

*Bro is still Bro.*

*It’s uncomfortable.*

*Really?*

*Then I’ll think of something else before lunch.*

But she kept using it.

A special form of address—

that only she used for him.

Maybe that—

was when something began moving inside her.

She also noticed something else.

The boy—

despite looking detached—

was strangely awkward around girls.

And when his thick hair shifted away from his face—

he was far more handsome than she expected.

Still—

what drew her most strongly was not his face.

One day—

he said something about parents.

*You don’t really need them.*

*People have to move through the world on their own.*

*Parents matter when you’re too young to survive alone.*

*After that—

they’re unnecessary.*

The girl was shocked.

Every child she had ever known—

whether loved or abandoned—

wanted parental love.

She did too.

But the boy thought differently.

She began admiring him.

And—

at the same time—

feeling sorry for him.

Many things followed.

They drank together.

Worked part-time.

During the midterms—

she watched him save his friends.

Her heart raced.

On the University League train—

students from her cousin’s school confronted them.

Afterward—

she told him:

*Thank you.*

*I was scared.*

*Those seniors looked strong.*

And he answered:

*I was scared too.*

So calmly—

that she laughed.

Really laughed.

And she realized—

there was only one person who could make her emotions move this much.

The boy in front of her.

◆◆◆

“…Now that I say it out loud—

it doesn’t sound dramatic at all.”

Sinclaire rubbed her eyes and smiled.

Vikir remained silent.

The silence grew awkward.

Sinclaire spoke again.

“And now—

I’m telling Bro the biggest problem in my life.”

“This confession turned into something strange.”

She swallowed.

“Like I said.”

“Night Hound killed my father.”

“And—

I don’t know whether you’ll believe me—

but President Dolores was there with him.”

Vikir said nothing.

“The senior I trusted most at school—

was working with the person who killed my father.”

“So I don’t know who to believe anymore.”

“Friends.”

“Seniors.”

“Professors.”

“Anyone.”

“That’s why staying at school feels impossible.”

She looked up.

“I thought Bro would believe me.”

“You’ve written articles criticizing Night Hound.”

“You know what kind of criminal he is.”

Vikir remained silent.

Sinclaire lowered her head.

“Right now—

Bro is the only person I have who might understand me…”

She could not finish.

Her fingers closed around his sleeve.

Vikir answered.

“I’m sorry.”

“But I can’t accept your feelings.”

Sinclaire’s grip tightened.

Then she nodded.

“Yes.”

“I knew Bro would say that.”

She gave a small smile.

“I’ve only seen one other person with eyes like yours.”

“My father.”

Vikir looked at her.

Sinclaire continued.

“Someone chasing a goal ordinary people can’t imagine.”

“Always moving forward.”

“You can see it in the eyes.”

“Bro and my father are the same kind of person.”

Then—

almost as if presenting qualifications—

she spoke faster.

“I’m pretty.”

“I’m healthy.”

“I’m young.”

“I’m good with swords.”

“And magic.”

“More importantly—

I can understand Bro’s life.”

“Support it.”

“Handle the things around you.”

“I won’t become a burden.”

“I can do it well.”

Then her voice softened.

“…Still no?”

“What would I have to do?”

Vikir cut her off.

“Now is not the time for relationships.”

It was obvious to him.

A demon hunter talking about romance—

his dead comrades would have laughed.

A person who might die at any time—

had no business creating more things they could not afford to lose.

Family.

Loved ones.

Anything that had to be protected—

could become a weakness.

Vikir nodded seriously.

Sinclaire’s expression—

strangely—

brightened.

“…Now?”

“?”

She seized on the word.

“Not **now**.”

“So—

only for now?”

Vikir looked at her.

“After Bro achieves his goal—

wouldn’t you have more time?”

“My goal is far away.”

“Extremely difficult.”

“It will take a long time.”

“I know.”

“If Bro says that—

it must be enormous.”

Sinclaire’s gaze sharpened.

“Then someday.”

“After Bro has achieved everything you want.”

She took a breath.

“Will you accept me then?”

A troublesome question.

Vikir thought.

For a long time.

Then finally—

he nodded once.

“If that day comes.”

“That’s enough.”

Sinclaire pulled away.

Sat back on her knees.

Then lifted her beer can and finished it in one swallow.

Vikir stood.

“It’s late.”

“I should go.”

Sinclaire stood too.

Then asked quietly:

“…Bro.”

“Before you go—

can you hug me once?”

Vikir sighed inwardly.

She still seemed fragile.

Yet someday—

she might stand at the top of terrifying House Bourgeois.

How much hardship had the Sinclaire of his previous life endured—

before becoming a great unnamed power?

Vikir felt guilt.

And debt.

He closed his eyes.

Then—

rustle.

Sinclaire wrapped both arms around his waist.

“You can push me away for the rest of my life.”

“…”

“So just now.”

“Just for a little while.”

Her voice trembled.

Wet.

“Haha…”

“I wasn’t supposed to be this kind of person…”

The words disappeared against his chest.

Vikir held still.

And thought.

*I don’t have many days left here either.*

Like Sinclaire—

Vikir also planned to leave Colosseo.

His next destination would be far harsher than the Academy.

A place whose architecture resembled Colosseo’s monumental grandeur—

but represented something entirely different.

Pain.

Trial.

Endurance.

*Nouvelle Vague.*

And beyond that—

the Age of Destruction.

The curtain was rising.

Soon—

the full war against the demons would begin.
