Gas, oil, and fire boiled up from deep inside its three throats.

Cerberus.

The watchdog of hell.

Even Vikir, a veteran hunter who had survived the Age of Destruction, had seen a monster of this class only once from a distance in his previous life.

It had been during the opening days of the war.

The gates to the Demon Realm had suddenly appeared, and monsters poured out across the continent.

On the day that disaster began, a single Cerberus rampaging at the front had killed hundreds of warriors selected from the Seven Great Clans.

The damage had been so extreme partly because humanity still knew almost nothing about fighting such monsters.

If Camus Morgue, the young matriarch of the Morgue Clan later renowned as the Ironblood Empress, had not personally intervened, the losses would have been even worse.

And now one of those monsters stood directly before Vikir.

“…”

Even a hundred Hellhounds attacking together would struggle to equal the danger of the creature in front of him.

Vikir remained calm.

“So Cerberus has already come down this far. Why is something that belongs around the Seventh Ridge all the way down here?”

Cerberus was normally found in the high reaches of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Yet this one had descended into the lower ridges.

Something had driven it here.

Grrrrr!

Whatever the reason, the situation was troublesome.

“No choice.”

Vikir decided to reveal everything he had been hiding.

This was not an opponent he could face while pretending to possess only the strength expected of an eight-year-old.

Fortunately, he was already beyond the restricted boundary.

No Guide Dogs were watching.

Flash!

Vikir drew the blunt shortsword and poured mana into it.

Dense gaseous aura gathered along the blade.

That was the rough formula commonly used to compare martial and magical attainment.

At the tip of eight-year-old Vikir’s sword, gaseous aura burned so densely that it looked on the verge of turning liquid.

High Sword Expert.

Roughly equivalent to a Third Circle mage.

In his previous life, Vikir had not reached a comparable level until around twenty.

He gathered his mana and moved his hand rapidly.

The shortsword traced strange marks through the air.

Three sharp, fang-like arcs appeared.

The Baskerville 3rd Form.

A sword technique that created three fangs and drove them into an opponent’s neck.

In his previous life, Vikir had mastered the Baskerville swordsmanship through the 4th Form and had eventually possessed the mana and aura of a High Sword Graduator.

Compared with that former self, his current strength remained far lower.

But he had advantages now that he had not possessed then.

A smaller, lighter body.

Extreme mobility.

And flesh reinforced by the blessing of the River Styx.

His strengths were clear.

So were his limitations.

Vikir swung.

Three fangs formed from the path of his blade.

His child-sized arms were too short to reproduce the fourth fang he had once wielded naturally, but the three gaseous fangs were enough to meet Cerberus’s claw head-on.

Clang!

“…?”

Vikir felt something strange.

Cerberus had been difficult to face even with the power of his previous life.

Why could his far weaker child body hold against one now?

The answer had to lie with the monster.

“…!”

His sharp eyes caught it.

There was a depression along Cerberus’s flank.

Its movements were slightly unnatural.

Several ribs were clearly broken.

Near a patch of torn hide, Vikir also saw unmistakable arrow wounds.

“Someone already hurt you.”

He gave a short, incredulous laugh.

Then he remembered the barbarians living beyond the far side of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

For years, those tribes had been a persistent problem for both the Baskerville and Morgue Clans.

If barbarians had driven this monster away from the upper ridges, Cerberus itself was in poor condition.

Clang! Bang! Crash!

Vikir compressed his gas-like aura and repeatedly blocked Cerberus’s claws and teeth.

Every collision between blade, fang, and claw rang like metal striking bronze, scattering sparks through the forest.

GRAAAH!

Cerberus attacked with all three heads.

Each time, Vikir slipped away or intercepted the bite.

“I’m used to fighting triplets.”

The comparison was more literal than it sounded.

Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro’s Trident tactics had been modeled after the fighting pattern of Cerberus.

In Vikir’s previous life, those three had tormented him through countless practical exercises and real battles.

Their patterns had been burned into his memory.

Whoosh!

Baskerville 1st Form.

The first fang knocked aside Cerberus’s bite.

Clang!

Baskerville 2nd Form.

The second fang drove partway into the monster’s neck.

Thrust!

Baskerville 3rd Form.

The third fang struck the second and hammered it deeper.

Vikir’s swordsmanship resembled ordinary Baskerville technique.

Yet it was not quite the same.

Those who had survived the Age of Destruction possessed something swordsmen of the peaceful era could not imitate.

Old theories had been reinterpreted under conditions where anything unnecessary got people killed.

Every scrap of decorative movement had been cut away.

What remained was a sword that moved only to kill.

Years of extreme combat experience had sharpened it further.

If ordinary swordsmanship was richly marbled beef, Baskerville swordsmanship was dry chicken breast.

And Vikir’s current swordsmanship was hard jerky.

Nothing wasted.

Nothing soft.

Only killing intent refined to its purpose.

Thud!

The blunt shortsword struck Cerberus’s wounded flank.

Vikir’s gaseous aura burst against the impact, and one of Cerberus’s heads screamed.

But a Watchdog of Hell would not fall from a blow like that.

A claw drove through the opening beneath Vikir’s sword path.

Rip!

Vikir had broken the monster’s bones.

Now he paid with his own.

The claw smashed into his torso.

The body blessed by the River Styx miraculously kept the strike from tearing through his organs, but several ribs still broke under the impact.

“… A second hit like that would be bad.”

Vikir frowned.

Then—

Crack!

The blunt shortsword finally failed under the strain of his aura and shattered.

“Damn.”

The situation had become extremely unfavorable.

But an experienced hunter always looked for the best route available.

Sometimes that route led away from the enemy.

“The Thirty-Six Stratagems. When everything else fails, retreat is the best strategy.”

Among the military principles traditionally attributed to the Leviathan family, the final answer was often the simplest.

Run.

Vikir turned and kicked off the ground with all his strength.

Cerberus, believing it had gained the advantage, immediately pursued him.

GRAAAH!

The monster closed the distance rapidly.

Without the enormous wound in its flank, Vikir would already have been caught.

He silently thanked whatever nameless barbarian had wounded it and sprinted toward the examination boundary.

Soon the wire and stakes marking the restricted zone came into view.

No Guide Dog was nearby.

Vikir vaulted across the line.

Cerberus smashed directly through the barrier behind him.

Three enormous jaws lunged toward his back.

BARK! BARK! BARK!

What was more terrifying than a hound chasing prey it knew it could catch?

Cerberus opened its mouths triumphantly.

The distance vanished.

One more instant and its teeth would tear flesh and crush bone.

Then the ground disappeared beneath it.

Crash!

The apparently flat patch of earth collapsed.

Vikir’s pit.

Cerberus lost its footing and dropped into the trap.

It gathered itself almost immediately and tried to leap back out.

It never got the chance.

Thrust! Thrust! Thrust! Thrust!

A forest of sharpened wooden stakes drove upward into its body.

GRAAAH!

All three heads screamed.

Most of the stakes snapped against Cerberus’s hide without penetrating.

A few did not.

Those entered near the injured flank—close to the old arrow wound where its defenses were already broken.

Vikir looked down into the pit.

“Welcome to the Cradle of Needles.”

He gave a brief, self-mocking smile.

The trap had originally been built merely to slow Hellhounds or other pursuing monsters.

By accident, it had caught something far greater.

Cerberus shook violently and snapped several stakes lodged in its flank and mouths.

It was furious.

But rage was not the only reason it trembled.

Bloody Beans.

Vikir had boiled the concentrated cocoa beans in water and soaked the sharpened stakes in the resulting poison.

Cerberus was still a canine monster.

The chocolate compounds entered directly through its wounds.

A lower-ranked beast might already have collapsed.

Cerberus did not.

Foam spilled from its jaws.

Burning excrement dropped from beneath its body.

The thick smell of cocoa and coffee spread through the pit.

Still, all three heads remained fixed on Vikir.

So he emptied his pouch.

Flick! Flick! Flick!

The remaining Bloody Beans flew toward the monster’s open mouths.

Slowed by the stakes, Cerberus could not avoid them.

Most went straight inside.

Its movements gradually became heavier.

Crack! Crack!

Even poisoned and impaled, Cerberus snapped several more stakes and drove Vikir into the corner of the trap.

The pouch was empty now.

The monster was covered in wounds, but it still possessed enough strength for one final leap.

Vikir knew that.

He waited carefully.

Then Cerberus moved.

Vikir charged forward at the same time, gripping one last wooden spear.

Whoosh!

He threw it.

The spear grazed Cerberus’s body and clipped the wounded flank.

It seemed to catch for only an instant before bouncing away.

Against intact hide, it would not have penetrated at all.

Cerberus bared its fangs and lunged toward the now-unarmed child.

Its six eyes burned brighter at the thought of finally tearing apart this troublesome prey.

One step.

Then its body lurched sideways.

Cerberus corrected its balance and forced itself onward.

Two steps.

Its footing failed again.

Three.

Four.

One leg bent at an unnatural angle.

Five.

The monster nearly collapsed.

Six.

It dragged itself forward by sheer force.

Seven.

Its strength vanished.

Crash!

All three foaming heads slammed into the red earth.

The body did not rise again.

Only then did Vikir lean back against the side of the pit.

“… The work I did in secret finally paid off.”

His gaze shifted toward the spear he had thrown.

That spear had been different from the others.

Two small, sharp objects had been fixed to its point.

Dried venom clung to their blackened tips.

Fangs from a snake whose bite was said to kill before the victim could walk seven steps.

The lingering malice of the Bloody Mamba seemed to gleam from them.
