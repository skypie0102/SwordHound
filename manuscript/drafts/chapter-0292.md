# Chapter 292: The Successors (2)

“It was you.”

“You brought Night Hound and the Saintess into the vault.”

The accusation sounded like an arrest.

Damien turned—

and froze.

Large eyes like a young deer’s.

Clear pupils.

White hair shining silver.

For one instant—

he thought his dead daughter had returned.

Then he blinked.

Reality replaced the illusion.

The girl was younger than Juliet had been.

Her hair was much shorter.

And—

most importantly—

Damien knew exactly who she was.

“…Sinclaire Bourgeois.”

His niece.

Bartolomeo’s daughter.

A direct descendant of House Bourgeois.

Sinclaire stood in front of him.

Damien swallowed a faint sigh.

Her name on Oracle’s roster—

had been one reason he bothered attending a party organized by Academy students in the first place.

“This is a misunderstanding.”

Damien shook his head.

He had indeed cooperated with Night Hound.

But the demon matter—

was something he himself had only learned recently.

“I only noticed something was wrong slightly before the others.”

“So I hurried here.”

“In fact—

I only just returned to the main estate.”

Truth—

mixed with lies.

A politician’s answer.

Sinclaire watched him for a moment.

Then looked away.

“…Forget it.”

“That isn’t important right now.”

She stood in front of Bartolomeo’s corpse.

Then whispered:

“There was something I wanted to ask him.”

“Now I’ll never get the chance.”

She remembered the Academy.

Pigi—

excited about meeting the Empire’s richest man—

had prepared an entire list.

*Questions about the future of the Imperial economy.*

*Current financial indicators.*

*How new business opportunities are identified.*

*Promising small merchant guilds.*

*Recent mergers involving mercenary guilds.*

*And whether tycoons eat fried chicken.*

Sinclaire—

by contrast—

had prepared only one question.

Now the person who should have answered it—

was dead.

Then Damien spoke behind her.

“‘Why did you abandon me?’”

Sinclaire flinched.

“That was the question.”

Damien shrugged faintly.

“Juliet asked me the same thing.”

“When she was alive.”

“…Big Sister Juliet did too?”

Sinclaire’s eyes widened.

Damien bit his lip.

The expression—

was almost identical to Juliet’s all those years ago.

“Yes.”

“And the answer is—

your father and the family did **not** abandon you.”

House Bourgeois had a tradition.

Damien had already explained part of it to Night Hound.

Ordinary Bourgeois children grew up inside the family.

But the true successor candidates—

were treated differently.

Like lion cubs thrown from a cliff—

future leaders were sent into commoner society with their identities sealed.

They had to survive.

Succeed.

Rise inside the Imperial Capital—

without parents.

Without family assistance.

Without the Bourgeois name.

That was the proof.

**Esse, Non Videri.**

**To be—

without being seen.**

“House Bourgeois always raises at least two leadership candidates.”

“Competition.”

“Checks and balances.”

“In my generation—

it was your father and me.”

“In the next—

you and Juliet.”

Damien finally told Sinclaire the truth.

Then hesitated.

“…You should have learned this much earlier.”

“I don’t know why my brother never told you.”

THROB.

Pain struck Sinclaire’s head.

Blood trickled from a wound left by flying coin fragments.

The sight of it—

pulled up an earlier memory.

Belial’s voice—

through Bartolomeo’s body.

*It may be useful to cut away these useless attachments.*

*The performance ends now.*

Sinclaire gripped her head.

*Useless attachments?*

*Performance?*

She swayed.

“Are you all right?”

Damien stepped closer.

Sinclaire recoiled instantly.

Like a feral cat.

Both palms raised.

Backing away.

Eyes that trusted nothing in the world.

“If a demon really took over my father’s body…”

“Then who raised me when I was little?”

Damien frowned.

“What?”

“Which one was my father?”

“When did it change?”

“Was he never my father from the beginning?”

Sinclaire’s voice became more frantic.

“What if the one who died wasn’t the demon—

but my father?”

“Or the opposite?”

“What if my father was the demon?”

“What was real?”

“I don’t know anymore…”

She clutched her hair.

Then suddenly lifted her head.

“…Night Hound.”

“I need to find him.”

Whether Night Hound had killed the demon—

her father—

a demon wearing her father—

or her father wearing the demon—

the person at the center of the truth was Night Hound.

Sinclaire approached Bartolomeo’s corpse.

Reached down.

Closed his eyes.

“Goodbye, Father.”

“I’ll inherit the headship.”

She ignored whatever Damien said behind her.

Damien—

for his part—

only watched.

Sinclaire’s eyes slowly steadied.

A decision had been made.

◆◆◆

“Huff!”

“Huff!”

“Haa!”

Far outside the Bourgeois estate—

on a mountain road leading toward the Imperial Capital—

a middle-aged man ran desperately.

His formerly clean face was scratched.

His neat white robes—

covered in mud.

**Humbert L. Quovadis.**

A high-ranking cardinal of the Rune faith.

Yet he had abandoned his carriage and was fleeing on foot.

“Damn it!”

“Damn it all!”

“Bartolomeo was a demon!”

“I really didn’t know!”

He forced his way through the brush.

Shortly before Vikir’s dinner with Bartolomeo—

Humbert had eaten lunch with him.

The chain connecting Quovadis’s Old Testament Faction—

the cults—

and House Bourgeois—

had begun drawing Imperial scrutiny.

Humbert feared becoming the discarded tail.

So he confronted Bartolomeo.

Threatened him.

If Humbert were investigated—

or arrested—

he would expose everything.

That was when Belial revealed himself.

Humbert fell at the demon’s feet.

Begged.

And the capricious Belial—

amused—

let him live.

[When the crisis comes—

use this.]

[Poor human.]

And then—

a gift.

“…A demon’s gift.”

Humbert tightened his grip around it.

A golden sphere.

Round.

Shaped like an eye.

Power from Demon King Belial lingered inside it—

radiating an ominous presence.

Humbert broke through the final wall of brush.

Then—

“Aah!?”

A cliff.

He stumbled backward.

Rustle.

Branches moved overhead.

“AAAAH!”

Humbert threw himself to the ground.

Covered his head—

or tried to.

Something felt wrong.

He had lifted his arms.

But his hands never reached his head.

“…?”

He looked.

Both hands—

gone above the wrists.

Only hot blood sprayed from the stumps.

[Oh?]

[I only meant to take what you were holding.]

[I seem to have taken the hands too.]

A woman laughed overhead.

Humbert looked up.

A tree branch bowed beneath a figure with two horn-like projections.

**Ms. Ouroboros.**

The woman who had driven Humbert all the way here.

And now—

the woman who had cut off both his hands.

[Ohhh.]

[Pretty.]

[So this is it?]

[The holy relic the demon gave you?]

She played with the bloodstained golden eye.

“G-give it back!”

Humbert shouted.

Strangely—

the loss of the object terrified him more than the loss of his hands.

Ms. Ouroboros had no intention of returning it.

[A priest with a respectable title.]

[Selling children for money behind people’s backs.]

[And now—

colluding with a demon.]

[You really do everything.]

“Ugh…”

Her words seemed to see directly through him.

Humbert shook.

Ms. Ouroboros only smiled.

[Still—

I don’t mind.]

[I’ve been **collecting** criminals like you.]

“…Collecting?”

Humbert frowned.

Then—

WHIP!

Something serpent-like wrapped around his neck.

Ms. Ouroboros’s lash.

“Ghk!?”

The whip tightened.

Dragged him upward.

He could not fight back—

especially with both hands gone.

Ms. Ouroboros watched him struggle.

Then said pleasantly:

[I’ll even lend you a hand.]

[Come with me.]

[Let’s welcome the **Old Era** together.]

“?”

Old Era?

People normally spoke about welcoming a **new** era.

Even as consciousness faded—

Humbert found the wording strange.

Ms. Ouroboros leaned closer.

Whispered into his ear.

[The **Warring States Era**.]

That—

was the last thing Humbert heard while still conscious.
