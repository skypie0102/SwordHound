The infant Vikir—Vikir Van Baskerville.

<!-- source-p:002 -->
He lay in the darkness of the shared nursery.

<!-- source-p:003 -->
There was little he could do when he could barely control his own body.

<!-- source-p:004 -->
He could only nurse and reflect on his life before his return.

<!-- source-p:005 -->
Eight years old.

<!-- source-p:006 -->
He had sensed mana for the first time. The outside world would have called him a genius; among the Baskervilles, he was merely average.

<!-- source-p:007 -->
Fifteen years old.

<!-- source-p:008 -->
He had accumulated one circle’s worth of mana. A faint aura could now shine from his sword.

<!-- source-p:009 -->
Twenty years old.

<!-- source-p:010 -->
He had begun taking part in operations in earnest, mainly assassination, espionage, and the subjugation of barbarian tribes.

<!-- source-p:011 -->
Twenty-five years old.

<!-- source-p:012 -->
Perhaps because he had fought harder than others, his swordsmanship had reached a level that members of the Baskervilles’ collateral branches failed to attain even in their thirties.

<!-- source-p:013 -->
Twenty-nine years old.

<!-- source-p:014 -->
The limits imposed on an illegitimate child had become clear. Legitimate descendants of the direct line, once behind him, had rapidly overtaken him as they gained practical experience. Soon they were so far ahead that he could no longer see them.

<!-- source-p:015 -->
Thirty years old.

<!-- source-p:016 -->
The gates to the demon realm had opened, and countless demons had invaded.

<!-- source-p:017 -->
Thirty-five years old.

<!-- source-p:018 -->
He had endured the Age of Destruction, amassed combat experience, and killed more monsters than anyone else.

<!-- source-p:019 -->
Thirty-nine years old.

<!-- source-p:020 -->
The war had ended in a hard-won victory for the Empire and humanity’s alliance.

<!-- source-p:021 -->
Forty years old.

<!-- source-p:022 -->
He had spent his life as a loyal hunting dog. Yet the Baskervilles had repaid him with slander and false accusations of colluding with demons. Then came the guillotine.

<!-- source-p:023 -->
Grind—

<!-- source-p:024 -->
Hatred for Hugo Le Baskerville surged through him again.

<!-- source-p:025 -->
Vikir clenched his teeth, startling the nanny into pulling him away from her breast.

<!-- source-p:026 -->
“Oh, young master. You do grind your teeth sometimes.”

<!-- source-p:027 -->
She laid him in his cradle and stood.

<!-- source-p:028 -->
Her shift was over. It was time for another nanny to take her place.

<!-- source-p:029 -->
But—

<!-- source-p:030 -->
Rustle—

<!-- source-p:031 -->
Instead of leaving through the door, the nanny glanced around and went to a bed across the room.

<!-- source-p:032 -->
“Come now, young masters. Drink up.”

<!-- source-p:033 -->
She began nursing children she had already fed.

<!-- source-p:034 -->
The Baskerville children were raised together in one room.

<!-- source-p:035 -->
It was meant to ensure that they were all raised equally.

<!-- source-p:036 -->
But what mother would treat someone else’s child exactly like her own?

<!-- source-p:037 -->
Some mothers memorized their children’s features, described them to the nannies, and asked that they be nursed a little longer.

<!-- source-p:038 -->
Vikir did not even know who his mother was. He could expect no such favor.

<!-- source-p:039 -->
He had to grow as quickly as possible, at least until he could find food and feed himself.

<!-- source-p:040 -->
Just then—

<!-- source-p:041 -->
Something unexpected happened.

<!-- source-p:042 -->
After another glance around, the nanny returned to Vikir’s cradle.

<!-- source-p:043 -->
“Honestly, why would Madam ask me to put this in young master Vikir’s cradle?”

<!-- source-p:044 -->
Madam? Few people in this family were addressed that way.

<!-- source-p:045 -->
Fewer still had any reason to send him something.

<!-- source-p:046 -->
The nanny drew a fairly large box from inside her clothing. She did not seem to know what it contained.

<!-- source-p:047 -->
Clack—

<!-- source-p:048 -->
She placed the box in his cradle, tilting her head in puzzlement.

<!-- source-p:049 -->
Then she hurried out of the nursery.

<!-- source-p:050 -->
“…”

<!-- source-p:051 -->
Vikir stared at the box beside him.

<!-- source-p:052 -->
Had anything like this happened before his return? No.

<!-- source-p:053 -->
With an effort, he raised his head and looked out of the cradle.

<!-- source-p:054 -->
His cradle stood well apart from the others.

<!-- source-p:055 -->
Something felt wrong.

<!-- source-p:056 -->
His instincts proved right.

<!-- source-p:057 -->
Hiss… hiss…

<!-- source-p:058 -->
Soon something pushed its head out of the box.

<!-- source-p:059 -->
A gift for a newborn child.

<!-- source-p:060 -->
Snakes.

<!-- source-p:061 -->
Two black snakes with triangular heads emerged, exposing their thick bodies.

<!-- source-p:062 -->
Bloody mambas.

<!-- source-p:063 -->
They were also called seven-step snakes: anyone bitten would die before taking seven steps.

<!-- source-p:064 -->
They were a rare species, seldom seen in the world.

<!-- source-p:065 -->
Vikir’s mouth fell open in astonishment.

<!-- source-p:066 -->
Who had put these snakes here?

<!-- source-p:067 -->
The nanny seemed to have left without realizing what she had released.

<!-- source-p:068 -->
After a moment’s bewilderment, Vikir understood.

<!-- source-p:069 -->
‘Come to think of it, babies sometimes died mysteriously in the nursery.’

<!-- source-p:070 -->
Children with exceptional talent had often died young.

<!-- source-p:071 -->
Such things had once seemed remote from him. He had put them down to accidents, enemy assassins, or simple bad luck.

<!-- source-p:072 -->
Now he saw them in a different light.

<!-- source-p:073 -->
The hand reaching into the nursery must have come from within the family.

<!-- source-p:074 -->
Hiss—

<!-- source-p:075 -->
The two bloody mambas slithered toward him.

<!-- source-p:076 -->
Fangs gleamed in their red mouths, as though already stained with blood.

<!-- source-p:077 -->
If the snakes ran loose, they could kill every child in the nursery.

<!-- source-p:078 -->
It would be a catastrophe without precedent in Baskerville history.

<!-- source-p:079 -->
‘…Well, that wouldn’t be so bad.’

<!-- source-p:080 -->
He felt no attachment to the family.

<!-- source-p:081 -->
But revenge was pointless if it cost him his own life.

<!-- source-p:082 -->
Vikir reached out.

<!-- source-p:083 -->
He had killed countless monsters during the Age of Destruction.

<!-- source-p:084 -->
He had fought reptilian monsters, especially serpents, many times. He knew their weaknesses well.

<!-- source-p:085 -->
Even an infant’s body would be enough to kill these two.

<!-- source-p:086 -->
Clench—

<!-- source-p:087 -->
He thrust out both hands and seized a snake in each.

<!-- source-p:088 -->
His grip closed just behind their heads, where their fangs could not reach him.

<!-- source-p:089 -->
Caught off guard, the snakes thrashed.

<!-- source-p:090 -->
They tried to stab him with sharp scales, but his body had already been hardened by the Styx’s blessing. Their scales could not pierce it.

<!-- source-p:091 -->
‘Their fangs might be another matter, but their scales can’t hurt me.’

<!-- source-p:092 -->
At the same time, Vikir summoned his mana.

<!-- source-p:093 -->
His body, empty of mana at birth, was now filled with it.

<!-- source-p:094 -->
He had gained it by drinking the Styx’s water to his limit.

<!-- source-p:095 -->
Flash—

<!-- source-p:096 -->
Light flickered around his fists.

<!-- source-p:097 -->
The other infants in the nursery would need another eight years to reach this stage.

<!-- source-p:098 -->
Vikir had reached it before he was even eight months old.

<!-- source-p:099 -->
Shiver…

<!-- source-p:100 -->
The two snakes stiffened.

<!-- source-p:101 -->
It was the reaction of prey confronting a predator.

<!-- source-p:102 -->
Their scales stood rigid as they froze, as though the infant before them filled them with unprecedented terror.

<!-- source-p:103 -->
Vikir’s mana-filled hands savagely twisted their necks.

<!-- source-p:104 -->
Crack!

<!-- source-p:105 -->
Their skin and flesh remained intact, but the bones beneath broke.

<!-- source-p:106 -->
Both snakes’ jaws gaped.

<!-- source-p:107 -->
The pressure forced their eyes outward and their tongues from their mouths.

<!-- source-p:108 -->
With their necks broken, the snakes shuddered, voided their bowels and bladders, and died.

<!-- source-p:109 -->
Deadly venom dripped from their exposed fangs.

<!-- source-p:110 -->
The next morning.

<!-- source-p:111 -->
The first nanny to arrive screamed, bringing the family’s guardian knights rushing to the shared nursery.

<!-- source-p:112 -->
Vikir smiled brightly, a snake with a broken neck in each hand.

<!-- source-p:113 -->
The knights could scarcely believe their eyes when they recognized the bloody mambas, notorious venomous snakes of the Le Rogue Mountains.

<!-- source-p:114 -->
Dead though they were, finding such monsters in the nursery was a calamity in itself.

<!-- source-p:115 -->
Within minutes, word reached Hugo in the main castle. He rushed straight to Fang Castle.

<!-- source-p:116 -->
Afterward, every nanny who had worked the night shift was tortured and executed. The nursery was placed under strict guard.

<!-- source-p:117 -->
The person who had released the snakes was never found.

<!-- source-p:118 -->
Yet one person knew.

<!-- source-p:119 -->
“…”

<!-- source-p:120 -->
A young hound watched silently, seeing through it all.

<!-- source-p:121 -->
A child already becoming a legend: he had crossed the Cradle of Swords faster than anyone, remained in the Styx longer than anyone, and strangled two venomous snakes in his cradle.

<!-- source-p:122 -->
Vikir Van Baskerville.

<!-- source-p:123 -->
He lay quietly in his cradle and waited.

<!-- source-p:124 -->
For the chance to repay his debt. For revenge.

<!-- source-p:125 -->
…

<!-- source-p:126 -->
And eight years passed.
