# Chapter 3: Hellhound (3)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/003.txt`. Canonical English proper nouns checked against the user-designated Fandom wiki where an applicable entry exists. Recovered English Chapter 3 is an aligned secondary reference only.

Baby Vikir.

Vikir Van Baskerville lay at the edge of the darkened communal nursery.

There was not much an infant who could not even control his own body could do.

All he could do was suckle at a wet nurse’s breast and look back over the life he had lived before his regression.

Eight years old.

That was when he first felt mana.

In the outside world, such talent would have been enough to call him a genius. Within the Baskervilles, it was merely average.

Fifteen years old.

He reached the mana level of a one-Circle mage. He could make a faint aura rise from his sword.

Twenty years old.

He began taking part in real missions in earnest, mainly assassination, espionage, and the subjugation of barbarians.

Twenty-five years old.

By living far more fiercely than those around him, he attained a level of swordsmanship that members of the Baskerville collateral lines often failed to reach even in their thirties.

Twenty-nine years old.

The limits of an illegitimate child’s growth became painfully clear.

Once the legitimate direct-line members began accumulating real combat experience, those who had lagged behind him rapidly caught up and soon left him far behind.

Thirty years old.

The gate to the Demon Realm opened, and countless demons began invading the human world.

Thirty-five years old.

He survived the Age of Destruction by the strength of his own hands, piling up enormous combat experience and killing more monsters than anyone else.

Thirty-nine years old.

The war was won.

The Empire and the alliance of humanity seized a precious victory.

Forty years old.

He had lived his entire life as a loyal hound, yet the Baskervilles gave him no reward.

What came back instead were slander, a false accusation of colluding with demons, and the blade of the guillotine.

Grind—

Hatred for Hugo Le Baskerville surged up inside him again.

Vikir clenched his jaw, startling the wet nurse into pulling him away from her breast.

“Oh my. Young master grinds his gums like this sometimes.”

She laid Vikir back in his cradle and stood.

Her shift was over. It was time for another wet nurse to take over.

But then—

Shuffle—

Instead of leaving through the door as she should have, the wet nurse looked around and walked straight toward the beds on the opposite side of the room.

“Come now, young masters. Have some more milk.”

She began nursing several children she had already fed earlier.

The children of the Baskervilles were raised together in a single room.

Supposedly, that was so every child would be raised fairly.

But what mother would truly treat her own child exactly the same as someone else’s?

Some mothers memorized the distinguishing features of their own babies and privately asked the wet nurses to let those children nurse a little longer.

Vikir did not even know who his mother was.

There was no one to make such a request for him, so he had long since abandoned any expectation of special treatment.

At least until he could obtain food for himself, he needed to grow as quickly as possible before he could do anything meaningful.

… Just then.

Something unexpected happened.

After glancing around again, the wet nurse returned to Vikir’s cradle.

“Honestly, Madam… Why have me bring something like this to Young Master Vikir’s cradle?”

Madam?

There were not many women in this clan who could be addressed that way.

And someone of such standing had even less reason to ask a wet nurse to deliver something personally.

The woman pulled a rather large box from inside her clothes. She herself did not seem to know what it contained.

Clack—

She placed the box in Vikir’s cradle, shook her head several times, and hurried out of the nursery.

“…”

Vikir silently stared at the box beside him.

Had anything like this happened before his regression?

No.

With difficulty, he raised his head and looked beyond the cradle.

His cradle had been placed far away from all the others.

Something felt wrong.

And ominous premonitions had a habit of coming true.

Creak—creak—

Before long, something pushed its head out of the box.

A gift for a newborn baby.

Snakes.

Two black, triangular heads emerged, followed by thick bodies.

Bloody Mambas.

They were also known as the Seven-Step Death: venomous snakes said to kill a bitten victim before they could take seven steps.

They were an exceptionally rare species, almost impossible to find in the outside world.

Vikir’s mouth fell open in surprise.

Who had put these snakes here?

The wet nurse clearly had not known what was inside the box when she left it behind.

Vikir froze for only a moment before understanding the situation.

‘Come to think of it, babies used to die mysteriously in the nursery all the time.’

The more exceptional the child, the more likely they seemed to die young.

Back then, Vikir had assumed those deaths were accidents, assassinations by outside enemies, or simply bad luck.

Looking at this now, however, the truth appeared far uglier.

The hidden hand targeting the nursery clearly came from inside the clan.

Hiss—

The two Bloody Mambas slithered toward him.

Their blood-red mouths opened around gleaming venomous fangs, as if already stained with blood.

If nothing stopped them, every child in the nursery could be slaughtered.

It would become an unprecedented catastrophe for the Baskerville Clan.

‘… Well. That wouldn’t necessarily be a bad thing.’

Vikir felt no attachment to the clan whatsoever.

But if the catastrophe began with him, that was a problem.

Vikir reached out.

During the Age of Destruction, he had killed countless monsters.

That included many serpent-shaped creatures, so naturally he knew their weaknesses.

Even the body of an infant was enough.

Grab!

Vikir thrust out both hands and caught one venomous snake in each.

His fingers locked around their necks, exactly where their fangs could not reach him.

Caught before they could strike, the snakes thrashed wildly.

They raised their sharp scales and tried to drive them into him, but Vikir’s body had already received the protection of the River Styx.

Even their scales could not leave a wound on him.

At the same time, Vikir drew on the mana throughout his body.

The mana pool that had been empty since birth was now brimming with power.

It was the result of drinking the waters of the Styx.

Flash!

Both fists began to shine with dazzling light.

It was a level the other children in this nursery would not reach for another eight years.

Vikir had accomplished it before he was even eight months old.

Sssssss…

The two snakes’ bodies went rigid.

A weaker creature freezing before an apex predator was simply a law of nature.

Perhaps the snakes sensed an unprecedented terror from the nursing infant before them. Their scales stood on end, and they froze stiff.

Then Vikir’s mana-filled hands twisted viciously.

Crack!

The skin and flesh of the two snakes remained intact.

The bones supporting them did not.

Both snakes opened their mouths wide.

The pressure forced their eyes to bulge outward and their tongues to hang long from their jaws.

Their broken bodies convulsed.

They voided feces and urine where they lay, then died.

Deadly venom dripped from the tips of their exposed fangs.

◆◆◆

The next day.

The scream of the first wet nurse to report for work brought every guardian knight in the vicinity rushing to the communal nursery.

Vikir lay there with a radiant smile, gripping one broken-necked venomous snake in each hand.

The knights stared at the two notorious Bloody Mambas in such shock that their eyes nearly popped from their sockets.

By then their venomous fangs had already been removed, but the appearance of such dangerous creatures in the nursery was still an enormous disaster.

Within minutes, word reached Hugo at the main castle, and he immediately hurried to the castle where the clan’s infants were being raised.

Afterward, every wet nurse who had been on duty that night was tortured and executed, and extraordinarily strict security was established around the nursery.

The person who had released the snakes was never found.

But there was one person who knew.

“…”

A little hound that could not yet speak had witnessed the entire truth.

Since his birth, he had become a child of legend: the fastest ever to cross the Cradle of Swords, an infant who had plunged into the River Styx, and now a baby who had snapped the necks of two venomous snakes in his own cradle.

Vikir Van Baskerville.

He lay quietly in his cradle and waited for the right moment.

The moment to repay what he was owed.

The time for revenge.

…

Eight years passed.
