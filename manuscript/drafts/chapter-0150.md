# Chapter 150: Not Brother, But Uncle (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/150.txt` — `8e30ee75b8d75d9ad9100526fc9cea333d9f3b02`. Recovered English Chapter 149 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Quilt Rune Indulgentia, Hugo Le Baskerville, Sinclaire, Tudor Donquixote, Sancho Barataria, Pigi, Bianca Fo Usher, Cindywendy, Peri, Night Hound, and Indulgentia. Sinclaire's later family identity remains spoiler-guarded.

Golden Week volunteer service was over.

Tomorrow morning—

everyone would return to the Academy.

On their final night at Indulgentia Orphanage—

the volunteer students gathered in the great hall.

At the front stood the orphanage director.

**Quilt Rune Indulgentia.**

His face resembled a famous older stage actor.

His voice was warm.

Convincing.

Designed to move people.

“…And even now, your parents are working hard for your sake.”

“But the children here have no parents.”

“So people like you—”

“young men and women capable enough to serve as examples to others—”

“must become guardians for these children.”

“The kindness you showed them over the last ten days will remain in their memories for the rest of their lives.”

“For these ten days—”

“you were no different from the parents who gave them life.”

Sweet words.

They reached their full value only when spoken by a demon.

Quilt's speech did exactly what it was designed to do.

Students throughout the hall began wiping at their eyes.

His words reminded them of their own parents.

The debt they owed.

The affection they had received.

Then—

that feeling was redirected toward the orphans.

*Ridiculous.*

Vikir alone remained unmoved.

He understood the structure of the speech.

Quilt had deliberately stirred the students' sense of gratitude toward their parents—

then converted it into responsibility toward the children.

The proof appeared immediately after the speech.

A huge donation box.

Quilt spread his arms.

“Now!”

“Show the children of this orphanage how much love you felt for them during these ten days!”

“Share with them some of what your parents have given you!”

Wallets opened.

Clink—

clink—

clang!

Gold coins struck one another.

The pile grew.

A small golden hill formed inside the donation box.

The more the students respected and loved their parents—

the more generously they gave.

Vikir—

of course—

gave nothing.

He held no particular respect or affection for **Hugo Le Baskerville**.

And even if he had—

he had no intention of donating money to a demon.

Then—

someone grabbed lightly at his clothes.

Sinclaire.

“Big Brother.”

“You're not donating?”

“…No money.”

“No money?”

Sinclaire stared at him.

“How can an Academy student have no money?”

Colosseo's annual tuition was enormous.

Sinclaire poked Vikir in the ribs.

“That's cold.”

“These children don't have parents.”

“We should help them.”

That was the ordinary reaction.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Even Bianca.

All of them were crying hard enough to have tears and runny noses—

and all of them were emptying their money into the box.

Vikir shook his head.

“Parents aren't necessary.”

“…What?”

Sinclaire's eyes widened.

Vikir explained in the same flat tone.

“In the end, you have to make your way through the world by your own strength.”

“Parents are useful during childhood, when you need someone else's help.”

“After that, they aren't necessary.”

Vikir had never thought the orphanage children were especially pitiful merely because they lacked parents.

He himself had been raised inside Baskerville as a hound.

The orphanage—

in theory—

could provide the children with what they needed to survive childhood.

That was enough.

To Vikir—

the world was a place full of suffering.

Something to fight.

Something to endure.

Something to overcome.

Parents were guides for the earliest part of that long battle.

Nothing more.

It was a functional view.

Not an emotional one.

But within Baskerville—

and in the Age of Destruction Vikir had lived through for half his life—

it was not unusual.

For ordinary people of the present era—

who had never seen that ruined future—

his thinking was difficult to understand.

People who had lived through war—

and people who had not—

could fail to understand one another for their entire lives.

Sinclaire's expression changed.

The playful curiosity disappeared.

Something sadder replaced it.

“…Big Brother.”

After a moment—

she spoke again.

“Come visit my home sometime.”

“During a festival.”

“Or a holiday.”

“…?”

“We can eat together.”

Sinclaire rose onto her toes.

Then—

without seeming to realize what she was doing—

she reached up and patted Vikir on the head.

“…What are you doing?”

Vikir looked genuinely confused.

Sinclaire froze.

Then abruptly pulled her hand back.

Her face reddened.

The gesture had clearly been unconscious.

“A-Ah…”

“Well—I'm going!”

“I'm going to see the children!”

She backed away.

Then waved.

“Big Brother!”

“Seriously!”

“Let's eat together sometime!”

“I'll pay!”

“After volunteer service is over, make time for me!”

Then she disappeared into the crowd.

“…Strange.”

Vikir frowned.

The Sinclaire from his previous life surfaced in his memory.

Quiet.

Usually smiling.

Friendly enough with everyone—

but never deeply close to anyone.

A Hot Department prodigy.

For all four years at Colosseo—

she had never surrendered first place in either written or practical examinations.

Then—

after graduation—

she disappeared.

No one ever saw her again.

She was said to be a commoner.

Yet almost nothing was known about her origins.

Vikir's brow tightened.

*Come to think of it—*

*one of Cindywendy's reports said someone of royal blood had entered this year's twentieth class.*

*Could it be…?*

Sinclaire's appearance.

Talent.

And strangely empty background.

The possibility was not impossible.

But it remained only a suspicion.

Then Quilt's voice filled the hall again.

“Good!”

“Now the children will thank all of you who became their parents during these ten days!”

A spotlight shifted toward the opposite side of the stage.

Rows of orphanage children stood there.

Cleanly dressed.

Awkward.

Embarrassed.

Each held a rough handmade wreath.

Quilt smiled broadly.

“Come now, children.”

“Thank the people who became your parents.”

The intention was obvious.

Use the children—

and squeeze one more round of donations from Academy students with little experience of the world.

But the feelings between the children and the volunteers—

were real.

The children had made the wreaths themselves.

They wanted the older students to like them.

The students—

in turn—

were genuinely happy to receive the gifts.

Tudor hugged several children tightly.

“Uwaaah!”

“I'm Tudor!”

“I swear I'll come visit every week until the day I become a great hero!”

“And after that too!”

Sancho spoke with complete seriousness.

“In the North, children are precious.”

“They are the seedlings of the future.”

“You carry the Empire's tomorrow.”

“Grow strong.”

Pigi was openly crying.

“I-I'll come again too!”

“Next time I'll bring lots of good food!”

Bianca tried to maintain her composure.

“…Ten days.”

“If it's short, it's short.”

“If it's long, it's long.”

She looked away.

“Leaving like this is harder than I expected.”

The children placed wreaths around the students' necks.

Then kissed their cheeks.

But—

few approached Vikir.

That was not only because of his distant atmosphere.

For most of the ten days—

the children had barely seen him.

Cleaning drains.

Repairing pipes.

Laying dining-hall tiles.

Maintaining the playground.

Vikir had quietly taken on the filthy and difficult jobs.

Work done in the background.

Work the children rarely noticed.

As a result—

his visible volunteer evaluation was surprisingly ordinary.

Then—

one child slowly approached.

**Peri.**

“…”

“…”

She stared at Vikir.

Vikir stared back.

For once—

he broke the silence first.

“…What are you looking at?”

Peri hesitated.

Then brought out the hand she had been hiding behind her back.

A small handmade wreath.

Crude.

Uneven.

Carefully made.

“…Thank you.”

Vikir accepted it.

Most volunteers bent down—

allowing the children to place the wreaths around their necks personally.

Vikir did not think to do that.

So Peri reached for his hand instead.

Then—

with one small finger—

she wrote on his palm.

**Big Brother, thank you.**

She probably meant the sewer incident.

Vikir nodded.

“You're welcome.”

He had jumped into the sewage to save Madam Eight-Legs's egg.

Not for the sake of being praised.

So he felt no particular pride in the gesture.

But Peri was not finished.

Her fingertip moved across his palm again.

**Will you come again?**

Vikir's pupils trembled slightly.

*Again.*

Would there be an again?

Hounds prepared for death at all times.

The prey might kill them.

The master might discard them.

Tomorrow was never guaranteed.

That was why Vikir did not casually say things like—

*I'll come next time.*

Especially not to a child who had already learned to distrust volunteers whose visits slowly became less frequent—

until they stopped entirely.

Vikir thought for a moment.

Then nodded.

“As long as both of us still have a tomorrow.”

That was the strongest promise he could make.

Peri's eyes widened.

Then—

she smiled.

Her fingertip moved again across Vikir's dry palm.

**I'll wait.**

A damp little finger left the promise behind.

Soon—

it was time for the farewell kisses.

The other children kissed the volunteers on the cheek.

Peri and Vikir simply stood facing one another.

Neither leaned closer.

Vikir turned first.

“No kiss is fine.”

“I don't particularly like them either.”

“…”

Peri visibly panicked.

Her hands moved uncertainly.

As if she had wanted to do something—

but had lost the chance.

Vikir added one final correction.

“And…”

“not ‘Big Brother.’”

“‘Uncle.’”

Peri was thirteen.

Being called her big brother felt strangely embarrassing to him.

Leaving a clearly disappointed Peri behind—

Vikir headed toward the hall exit.

“Wait!”

He would have kept walking—

if Saintess Dolores had not stepped directly into his path.
