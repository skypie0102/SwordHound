The ground trembled.

Hooves thundered through the darkness—

kicking up a cloud of dust.

The rider racing toward the rear checkpoint was Viscount Baldini.

[Where is she!?]

[Where did she go!?]

[Find her!]

He shouted at the guards.

The longer he thought about the pass he had issued to a stranger without identification—

the worse he felt.

So he had ridden after her.

But there was no trace.

No record of any high-ranking visitor at the rear Poison Human depots.

Nothing.

Only corpses—

the inevitable leftovers of war.

[Damn it.]

[Nothing had better have happened…]

At last—

Baldini turned his horse around.

◆◆◆

A short while later—

the wasteland he had just crossed moved.

Several Poison Human corpses shifted.

Then—

“Ugh…”

Tudor pushed one corpse aside and sat up.

“That was the worst blanket I’ve ever used.”

Sancho rose beside him.

“I’m soaked in cold sweat.”

“If he’d found us—

we’d have been finished.”

Pigi clutched his chest.

“I think my heart shrank to the size of a bean.”

Bianca shoved a body away.

“Disgusting.”

When the pursuit force had approached—

Vikir ordered everyone to lie flat and cover themselves with Poison Human corpses.

Now—

one by one—

they climbed out.

“…That was close.”

Vikir rose too.

Then three women followed.

Camus’s head appeared first—

from beneath one arm.

She narrowed her eyes toward Vikir’s other side.

“You’re usually hopelessly slow.”

“But when something like this happens—

you react quickly.”

“I-I didn’t do anything!”

Dolores lifted her head from Vikir’s side.

She had simply jumped there in the emergency.

Camus glared.

“They say the quiet cat gets into the kitchen first.”

“Old sayings exist for a reason.”

“T-that isn’t what happened!”

“There were only Poison Human bodies around.”

“And during that drinking game in the dorm before—

something kind of similar happened—

so I moved without thinking!”

Camus’s eyes narrowed.

“Dorm?”

“Drinking game?”

She turned toward Vikir.

“What happened, boyfriend?”

“Who are you calling boyfriend!?”

Camus and Dolores glared across Vikir.

Then a gentle voice interrupted.

“We’re still inside enemy territory~”

“Do you really want to fight while buried under corpses?”

Sinclaire raised her head.

From between Vikir’s legs.

Camus nearly exploded.

“Why are **you** coming out from there!?”

“And don’t act innocent after choosing that spot!”

Dolores sighed.

“…Sinclaire.”

“You really shouldn’t be the one saying that.”

Camus.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

A strange contest began around Vikir.

Vikir ignored all of it.

His eyes remained forward.

Soon—

figures appeared through the dense red fog.

Poison Humans.

Some stood motionless inside the fence.

Others wandered without purpose.

“We infiltrate the barracks from here.”

Tudor looked over.

“Straight through the middle?”

“Yes.”

“We could go around.”

“But there are too many of them.”

Vikir studied the enemy lines.

“These are probably tomorrow’s siege forces.”

“If we reduce their numbers here—

the people defending Tochka will have an easier time.”

Everyone’s expression hardened.

Once they broke this encirclement—

the water source was close.

They crouched behind a raised dirt mound.

*If only Baby Madam were here.*

Vikir remembered his old Night Hound operations.

With her—

movement like this would have been easier.

Then Camus stepped forward.

“What about going underground?”

Before anyone answered—

she pulled something from inside her clothes.

The **Picaresque Mask**.

The Dantalian relic Vikir had given her before entering Nouvelle Vague.

“Hack, hack—”

Camus instantly became a red-haired dog.

Dolores’s eyes lit up.

“S-so cute…”

She reached out.

Camus slapped her hand away.

Then started digging.

SCRATCH-SCRATCH-SCRATCH—

The mound rose higher as the tunnel sank deeper.

Soon—

Camus had dug all the way beneath the fence.

She emerged on the far side—

tail wagging.

Obviously waiting for praise.

Vikir silently stroked her head.

“You’re using the mask well.”

“Of course.”

Camus returned to human form.

The eight infiltrators crawled through.

Inside—

the red fog was thicker.

Visibility nearly disappeared.

Dark crimson silhouettes moved in scattered groups.

SWISH.

Night Hound bared his fangs.

Several nearby Poison Humans fell silently—

their necks opened before they could react.

Thick venom oozed from the wounds.

Vikir frowned.

“…There’s an absurd amount of poison inside each one.”

“How are they producing enough for this many?”

The army was enormous.

And every body held a huge reserve.

Producing this many Poison Humans should have required impossible quantities of poison.

*They aren’t weaker than the ones I remember from before regression.*

*This quality didn’t appear until late in the war.*

The question returned.

*How did Leviathan manufacture so many?*

In his previous life—

the answer had never been found.

This time—

something had changed.

“Bro.”

Sinclaire poked Vikir’s shoulder.

“Look.”

Beyond the red mist—

a black tent stood alone.

Vikir narrowed his eyes.

Slowly—

the scene behind the fog came into focus.

Poison Humans stood in line.

One by one—

they entered the black tent.

And from the other side—

they emerged.

Larger.

Longer-limbed.

More grotesque.

Stronger.

A massive Poison Human covered in burns and sword wounds limped inside.

A survivor of the day’s fighting.

Several minutes later—

it crawled out the opposite side.

Its injuries—

gone.

Its limbs—

twisted into insect-like shapes.

Its face—

even less human than before.

[OOOOOOO—]

It disappeared into the fog—

radiating enough vitality and poison to make the air distort.

Sinclaire touched the Money Hat.

“My hat is reacting.”

“There must be an extremely valuable artifact inside.”

“Maybe they’re using it to recharge the Poison Humans.”

Vikir nodded.

Sinclaire had never existed in his previous timeline.

If her instinct was correct—

this discovery could change the future.

“The water source is close.”

“But we destroy this facility first.”

If they could stop Leviathan from restoring and strengthening tomorrow’s assault force—

the defenders at Tochka would gain precious breathing room.

The objective did not conflict with reaching the water.

Everyone agreed.

Tudor frowned.

“But how do we destroy it?”

“Getting through is already dangerous.”

The black tent stood on high ground.

High-ranking Leviathan forces were almost certainly inside.

And the surrounding Poison Humans made a frontal attack nearly suicidal.

Vikir remained calm.

“I prepared a secret weapon for exactly this.”

Then—

rustle.

He reached under his heavy cloak.

And pulled something out.

Everyone stared.

“Vikir!”

“When did you get so big!?”

The thing he had been hiding—

was enough to make every pair of eyes widen.
