With the first-, second-, and third-year Ranking Battles complete—

Colosseo Academy’s final exams were effectively over.

All that remained was the closing ceremony.

After that—

vacation.

Students would receive brief counseling on their performance over the year—

then leave with their families.

The grand auditorium filled quickly.

Students.

Professors.

Parents.

And among them—

several people impossible to overlook.

One man stood nearly three meters tall.

A fierce tusked sea-lion hide hung around his shoulders.

A spear as large as a whaling harpoon rested across his back.

His blond hair spread like a beast’s mane.

And he laughed loudly enough for half the hall to hear.

“Son!”

“Congratulations on winning!”

“This father can’t stop smiling!”

**Cervantes Donquixote.**

Head of House Donquixote.

The Spear King.

He slapped Tudor’s shoulder repeatedly.

Then—

a gloomy voice came from beside him.

“Are you saying that for my benefit?”

The second man was thin—

but almost as tall.

His face looked corpse-pale.

Large, shadowed eyes.

Bloodless lips.

Long hair fine as spider silk.

**Usher Poe Roderick.**

Head of House Usher.

The Bow Demon.

He glanced once at Bianca.

Then back at Cervantes.

“I congratulate your son.”

“Although I’m not sure that victory deserves celebration.”

“What?”

“All victories deserve celebration!”

“Are you picking a fight because your daughter lost?”

“A fight?”

“Of course not.”

Roderick turned.

“Daughter.”

“Explain why you withdrew from the final round.”

Bianca answered calmly.

“I dislike performing before crowds.”

“I am not a monkey.”

Tudor’s face twisted.

“What?!”

“Then why fight seriously until the final?!”

“Are you stupid?”

“Before the final—

we fought privately.”

“Once the parents arrived—

outsiders started watching.”

“I had no intention of displaying House Usher’s secret archery for them.”

At the decisive moment—

Bianca had simply avoided Tudor’s spear—

stepped outside the arena—

and forfeited.

Roderick smiled faintly.

“Don’t use such insulting language, daughter.”

“Imperial law includes defamation based on true statements.”

“Yes, Father.”

“The law is solemn.”

“We must obey it.”

The father and daughter walked away together.

Behind them—

Cervantes and Tudor nearly exploded.

“Hey!”

“Roderick!”

“Come back and fight me!”

“And Bianca!”

“Why would you forfeit like that?!”

The resemblance between parent and child—

on both sides—

was obvious.

But the crowd’s attention soon shifted again.

Too many important people had gathered.

**Hobbes De Leviathan**, head of House Leviathan, with Granola.

**Nabokov I Quovadis**, head of Quovadis, with Dolores.

**Osiris Le Baskerville**, acting young head of Baskerville, with Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro.

Each Great House—

powerful enough to resemble a country.

The Empire’s pillars.

Only House Bourgeois and House Morgue lacked their family heads.

◆◆◆

The crowd followed a road lined with newly planted trees and magic barriers.

Eventually—

everyone entered the main auditorium.

The program was simple.

Vice Headmaster’s speech.

Headmaster’s speech.

Awards for the top students of each year.

Then dismissal.

Professor Banshee Morgue stepped to the podium.

[Students.]

[Faculty.]

[Parents and guardians.]

[I am Banshee Morgue, Vice Headmaster of Colosseo Academy.]

His snake-like eyes swept the audience.

*The Headmaster faction is weak.*

*Winston was absent too long.*

*His influence is nearly gone.*

*The next Headmaster seat will be mine.*

That conclusion seemed natural to Banshee.

Since becoming Vice Headmaster—

he had steadily gathered ordinary professors under his banner.

An unofficial Vice Headmaster faction.

The Headmaster faction—

by contrast—

had very few professors left.

That struggle spread downward into the student body.

The Noble Faction.

The Great-Clan Faction.

Informal student organizations linked to professors aligned with Banshee.

Opposite them—

the formal Student Council—

more naturally aligned with the weakened Headmaster faction.

Banshee could read the entire structure at a glance.

Then his eyes found Dolores.

Student council president.

Third-year leader.

She looked exhausted from days of work.

*Unfortunately—

children are always affected by the conflicts of adults.*

His gaze moved.

Then stopped.

“…!”

An empty position among the already sparse Headmaster-aligned faculty.

Only one person could be missing from a ceremony this important.

*Professor Sadi.*

Banshee’s jaw tightened.

*That woman…*

Not even attending the speech of the man who had protected her?

Hopeless.

Then he remembered the private report.

*Winston’s attachment to Sadi may be connected to the Forty-Seven People Riot.*

The report had offered only theories.

Still—

even the possibility was disturbing.

Banshee looked toward Winston.

The Headmaster smiled innocently at the students.

Impossible to read.

Banshee exhaled.

Then began his speech.

He spoke about Colosseo’s graduates.

Their careers.

Achievements.

Scholarly success.

Then his voice softened.

[You may resent strict parents.]

[Strict teachers.]

[But they are trying to give you the strength and wisdom required to survive the difficult road ahead.]

[To those who entered this year—

and those preparing to leave—]

[you worked hard.]

Several students wiped their eyes.

Banshee continued.

[May there always be work for your hands.]

[May there always be something left in your purse.]

[May a road always open before you.]

[May you be slow to make enemies and quick to make friends.]

[May your neighbors respect you.]

[May wicked people fail to recognize you.]

[May the wind remain at your back.]

[May sunlight fall before you.]

[And when rain does come—

may a rainbow follow quickly.]

[May you be poor in misfortune—

and rich in blessing.]

[May your saddest future day still be better than the happiest day you have known so far.]

[May peace, love, hope, prosperity, achievement, and fulfillment accompany your fate.]

Applause shook the auditorium.

Especially from the factions aligned with Banshee.

Then Winston approached the podium.

His smile was bright.

[The Vice Headmaster spoke so well that I feel pressured now.]

[Perhaps I should have prepared something more impressive.]

The audience laughed.

Then Winston became serious.

[What I’m about to say may sound ordinary.]

[Live fully in the present.]

He looked across the hall.

[Most of you know I spent several months away from the Academy.]

[A mana rampage badly damaged my health.]

Because Winston had been absent so long—

Banshee had risen rapidly—

and nearly taken operational control of the Academy.

Everyone important knew the political situation.

Winston continued.

[When you suffer—

you understand pain.]

[I learned how precious health is.]

[How precious each moment is.]

[Life can end in the next second.]

[Minute.]

[Hour.]

[So live each moment completely.]

The students listened.

[This applies to everyone.]

[Not only soldiers or firefighters who risk their lives.]

[A person can slip while bathing and strike their head.]

[Become ill from favorite food.]

[Be hit by a falling pot.]

[Or suffer a sudden mana rampage.]

The hall grew quieter.

[Humans can die at any time.]

[So during the coming vacation—

remember how valuable life is.]

[Live so that even if tomorrow—

no—

even if this very moment were your last—]

PFT.

A tiny sound.

Skin tearing.

Something soft rupturing underneath.

Winston stopped speaking.

The audience stopped breathing.

“…Cough!”

Dark-red blood sprayed from his mouth.

Something had come through the curtain behind him—

passed through his back—

and pierced his heart.

Too long for a knife.

Too flexible for a spear.

Long.

Thin.

Twisting like a snake.

SWISH.

Blood burst from Winston’s chest.

And everyone finally saw it.

A **whip**.
