# Chapter 392: The Worst Torture (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/392.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 390 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Garam Nord, Kirko Grimm, Sakkuth de Leviathan, Lieutenant Colonel Bastille, BDISSEM, and Nouvelle Vague.

“Let’s talk.”

Garam—

Vikir—

stood in front of Sakkuth.

Everyone stared.

The guards forming the encirclement.

Kirko—

still held hostage.

Even Sakkuth himself.

Silence spread across the labor zone.

Then Sakkuth laughed.

“What is this?”

“Stupid Garam?”

“Have you lost your mind?”

Vikir had survived the Age of Destruction.

He had spent years hiding killing intent—

mana—

presence.

Even masters far beyond Sakkuth had failed to read him properly.

There was no chance Sakkuth would recognize the man beneath Garam’s face.

Vikir shrugged.

“My mind is fine.”

“But didn’t I almost kill you before?”

“You almost did.”

A person who escaped death could change.

That—

at least—

was plausible.

Sakkuth narrowed his eyes.

“Same face.”

“Different eyes.”

“You’ve become a little interesting.”

“If you understand that—

release my colleague.”

“Then let me return to work.”

Sakkuth scoffed.

“Don’t get ahead of yourself.”

“I only answered because your eyes changed.”

“You’re still garbage.”

Vikir stayed where he was.

Guards shouted behind him.

“Second Lieutenant!”

“Get back!”

“You’ll just give him another hostage!”

“Know your place!”

Sakkuth cleared his throat.

Then—

SPIT.

A foul glob shot toward Vikir’s face.

Everyone flinched.

Vikir shifted his head slightly.

The projectile passed beside him.

Far too cleanly.

Far too fast for the Garam they knew.

“…?”

Sakkuth’s expression changed.

Vikir lowered his voice until only Sakkuth could hear.

“I have something you’ll want to discuss.”

“Aren’t you curious?”

Sakkuth snorted.

“What could you possibly have to say to me?”

But the hook had landed.

A hostage-taker understood better than anyone—

promises from senior guards meant nothing.

Yet one low-ranking guard had walked out alone.

No visible fear.

No support.

What could someone like that possibly want to say?

For an instant—

Sakkuth became curious.

Then he shook it off.

“Forget it.”

“I have nothing to discuss with you.”

“The Queen.”

“…!”

Vikir needed only one word.

Sakkuth’s face froze.

His entire riot—

his despair—

his rage—

had begun with the belief that the Queen abandoned him.

Killing intent rolled outward.

The guards farther away instinctively stepped back.

Vikir did not.

Sakkuth tightened his grip on Kirko’s neck.

“You’re brave.”

“Suddenly you have my attention.”

“Good.”

“Then listen.”

Sakkuth grinned.

“You’re in love with this woman, aren’t you?”

“Fine.”

“Let’s talk.”

“If you’re lying—

I break her neck first.”

“Then I tear her apart—”

“Wait.”

Vikir raised one hand.

Sakkuth stopped.

“What now?”

“This conversation contains sensitive information.”

“Absolute secrecy.”

Sakkuth stared.

“What classified information could a Second Lieutenant possibly have?”

“My rank is irrelevant.”

“You’ll understand when you hear it.”

Sakkuth’s interest deepened despite himself.

“Then what do you want first?”

“Give Kirko back.”

Sakkuth blinked.

“…Are you stupid?”

“You want a hostage-taker to release his hostage before negotiating?”

“You can keep a hostage.”

“…What?”

“Use me.”

Silence again.

Vikir continued.

“Exchange me for Kirko.”

“Otherwise—

we cannot speak.”

Sakkuth looked at him as if studying a strange animal.

“You understand what position you’re in?”

“Yes.”

“I’m a weak junior guard making an unreasonable demand of a Level Eight prisoner with a hostage.”

“I could simply take both of you.”

“Then there will be no conversation.”

Vikir’s shoulders rose faintly.

“Pity.”

“You would have liked what I had to say.”

Sakkuth’s confusion only grew.

“How much nerve do you have?”

“You know I’m not naturally brave.”

“I’m not good enough at acting to fake this much confidence either.”

“….”

“So if I can stand here and speak like this—

shouldn’t you wonder why?”

That argument—

somehow—

worked.

Sakkuth tilted his head.

“Fine.”

“But before I exchange hostages—

prove you’re worth taking.”

“A hostage needs qualifications?”

“Of course.”

He shook Kirko by the throat.

“This woman is about to die and she still refuses to beg.”

“She has courage.”

“Guts.”

“But you?”

“Stupid Garam.”

“Rotten Dog.”

“If I grab you—

maybe you piss yourself and die of fright.”

“Then you’re worthless.”

“You’re being ridiculous.”

“So are you.”

Vikir sighed.

“How do I prove it?”

Sakkuth looked around.

Then held a hand toward a nearby Major.

“Hey.”

“Cigarette.”

The Major happened to have one between his lips.

After a moment—

he threw another cigarette over.

Sakkuth caught it.

Put it in his mouth.

Inhaled.

Blood from his split lips soaked into the paper.

Dark.

Wet.

Foul-smelling.

Then Sakkuth held it out.

“Smoke it.”

“Do that—

and I’ll accept your courage.”

Every guard fell silent.

Kirko’s eyes tightened.

*He won’t.*

Sakkuth’s blood had been treated like a highest-grade biological hazard.

If even one drop landed in the wrong place—

guards disinfected the surrounding area violently and completely.

Nobody sane would willingly touch a cigarette soaked in it.

Lieutenant Colonel Bastille gritted his teeth.

*He bought us time.*

*But this is the limit.*

Behind the line—

guards had been creeping closer.

Sakkuth had given them almost no opening.

Then—

Vikir took the cigarette.

And placed it between his lips.

“Fire.”

Sakkuth stared.

He had not expected that.

Vikir tilted his head.

“You’re the one who offered it.”

“Light it.”

Still half-dazed—

Sakkuth turned toward the guards.

A Major threw him a match.

Scratch.

Flare.

Sakkuth held the flame out.

A massive Level Eight prisoner—

bending toward a junior guard less than half his size—

to light a cigarette.

The scene was absurd.

Vikir inhaled.

Slowly.

Then exhaled smoke.

Everyone stared.

Sakkuth’s expression became serious.

“…How did you know?”

Vikir looked at him.

“Know what?”

“That there isn’t actually any plague in my blood.”

The guards froze.

For two years—

Sakkuth had fooled everyone.

The terrifying disease covering his body—

the symptoms—

the contamination scare—

all of it had been theater.

He had used substances available inside Nouvelle Vague to imitate severe skin disease.

Medical injections and his knowledge as a poison specialist helped maintain the deception.

And his reputation did the rest.

A Leviathan assassin.

The creator of Red Death.

Everyone had believed what they expected to believe.

But now—

one question remained.

How had Garam known?

Even prison doctors had failed to expose the bluff.

Sakkuth.

Kirko.

Bastille.

Every guard nearby looked at Vikir.

He took another drag.

Then answered calmly.

“I didn’t.”
