# Chapter 107: The Returned Hero (3)

The next morning.

Vikir rose from the shabby bed in his small inn room.

Highsis had offered him a guest room in the fortress the night before, but he had refused.

He had already paid for this room.

Leaving it unused felt wasteful.

When he descended the creaking wooden stairs, the guards posted outside the inn saluted him in booming voices.

“Loyalty!”

“…I’m not a member of Morgue. You don’t need to salute me.”

“Lord Vikir is the hero of Red Salt Castle! We will never forget that you saved our lives in the Depths two years ago. Please accept our respect!”

Vikir stopped trying to argue.

He would have to get used to salutes and praise.

Feeling awkward every single time was not sustainable.

A female server from the night before hurried over with a parcel.

“Um… If you don’t mind, please take this for the road…”

Inside were a skewer made from good cuts of beef, a packet of herbal anti-inflammatory medicine, and a bottle of chilled dark beer.

The guards stared.

Then sighed.

“What happened to that shameless woman?”

“Nancy, the coldest innkeeper in the fortress, is suddenly being nice?”

“Did she discover hospitality overnight?”

“What a rotten world. In the end, looks really are everything.”

Nancy was not the only one behaving differently.

Clop, clop, clop—

Soon after sunrise, three horses approached the inn.

A line of carriages, attendants, and additional riders followed them.

At the front rode the three Morgue sisters.

Highsis Morgue.

Middlesis Morgue.

Lowsis Morgue.

They looked very different from the night before.

Their skin appeared brighter. Their lips were vivid red, their brows carefully shaped, their features sharper.

The effect looked natural.

It was anything but.

A remarkable amount of time, money, and effort had clearly gone into the makeup.

They were also wearing formal dresses they would never normally choose.

The three sisters dismounted to greet Vikir.

Even Highsis, famous for her temper, thanked the servant who helped her down in a gentle voice.

Vikir looked them over.

“Is it all right to travel unarmed while the region is still on a war footing?”

It was an innocent question.

The sisters answered with deadly seriousness.

“This *is* armament.”

“I’m fully armed too. You could call it complete combat readiness.”

“Ho ho ho. My sisters’ makeup is closer to camouflage than armor.”

The two elder sisters’ expressions stiffened at Lowsis’s comment.

“Oh? By camouflage, do you mean the powder you used to hide your cheekbones?”

“I only covered my cheekbones. What about you? You erased your entire jawline. And the shading you used to build up your nose? That’s practically civil engineering.”

“All right, move your bangs. Your forehead is wide enough for me to play football on it.”

The three Morgue sisters began smiling at one another.

Cold mana swirled around them.

The surrounding air chilled.

Vikir ended the fight with one question.

“Did you send word to Baskerville?”

Highsis raised a hand first.

“Yes! I sent an owl yesterday. I told them you had returned.”

Her form of address had already shifted from the reverent *lord* to the more familiar *sir*.

It was an obvious attempt to close the distance between them.

Vikir did not care.

“Good. I’ll go alone from here.”

They were still standing directly in front of the inn.

By *from here*, Vikir simply meant the rest of the journey.

The sisters looked openly disappointed.

“…Are you sure you don’t want us to guide you?”

“The border post is still quite far.”

“The roads are confusing. You could get lost.”

“I know you must be busy.”

“We’re not busy! Not at all!”

“We’ve had so little to do lately that we sleep all day! The tribes are quiet, and so are we.”

“If you’re going anyway, let us come along. We can show you the sights and—”

“No, thank you. Just lend me a horse.”

Vikir waved them off.

*When I return to the main house, I should report that Morgue’s people are slacking.*

Sleeping all day while stationed on someone else’s land was hardly diligent service.

Highsis hurried around the carriage and returned leading a pale white horse.

Only its ears had been dyed pink.

“This is my most precious horse. His name is Poppy. I’ve raised him since he was a foal. When you ride him, please imagine that you’re riding me— No! I mean, imagine that you’re riding *with* me. Ah…”

“Sister, you bought that horse in a panic at the market last night.”

“Right, right. You picked the best horse you could find and harassed the dealer until he sold it.”

Highsis smiled at her sisters with clenched teeth.

Vikir mounted Poppy.

The sisters were still arguing over which one should accompany him to the edge of Red Salt Castle when he turned.

“…There’s something I want to ask.”

All three stopped.

Meeting his eyes, they stared up at him as though momentarily hypnotized.

His next words snapped them back to reality.

“Where is Camus?”

Camus.

Camus Morgue.

The name hit the triplets like a bucket of cold water.

“R-right. Right. Camus. Of course.”

“…We may actually be in trouble, you know.”

“That’s why I told you two not to act like this!”

The sisters finally seemed to realize what they had been doing.

Highsis recovered first.

“Camus is in closed-door training.”

“…Closed-door training?”

Vikir repeated.

Highsis nodded and explained what had happened.

After Vikir disappeared, Camus organized search parties and spent years combing the forests of the Depths.

She failed to find him.

She did not even find a body.

Then, only recently, she abruptly ended the search and entered seclusion to train.

What Highsis said next was more surprising.

“She broke with Uncle Adolf.”

Even Highsis sounded bewildered.

Camus had changed after giving up the search.

Her once-brash, cheerful personality had quieted.

She spoke much less.

Then she announced that she was leaving the **Light Faction**.

Camus had always followed Adolf like a parent, and Adolf was the leader of that faction. Her defection shook Morgue.

Adolf made no public statement.

He simply watched in silence as Camus crossed over to the opposing **Dark Faction**.

“…The Dark Faction.”

Vikir frowned.

Morgue contained two broad factions.

The Light Faction used magic in visible work both inside and outside the family.

Its members moved through the territory hunting monsters, constructing facilities, and handling practical operations.

The Dark Faction rarely appeared in public.

Its members stayed deep within Morgue territory and concentrated on research.

They took a particular branch or field of magic, tested it again and again, and through failure and experimentation discovered or recreated spells.

In simple terms—

the Light Faction produced practitioners.

The Dark Faction produced researchers.

*The Light Faction is currently led by Adolf Morgue. The Dark Faction should be led by Snake Morgue.*

Snake Morgue.

An archmage who led Morgue’s Dark Faction and held the imperial title of Marquis.

A close relative of Matriarch Raspane Morgue, he was not well known outside the family.

At court, what little reputation he had was that of an exceptionally difficult and exacting man.

Vikir, of course, remembered Snake Morgue from the Age of Destruction.

*This is unexpected.*

Before regression, Camus and Snake had gotten along terribly.

In this life, however, she had suddenly joined his side.

Morgue’s internal council consisted of one senior councillor, twenty middle councillors, and four hundred junior councillors.

Given Camus’s position, she was likely at least at the middle tier.

Or perhaps she held no formal council seat at all.

Vikir considered this for a moment.

“Can you pass a message to Camus?”

Highsis nodded immediately.

“Of course. A favor from a friend is easy.”

“Good. Keep it short.”

All three sisters produced paper and pens as if expecting a long dictation.

What Vikir said was only one sentence.

“When you have time, let’s meet face to face.”

…?

That was all?

After two years apart?

Highsis, Middlesis, and Lowsis stared blankly.

Vikir turned his horse.

Then, as if remembering one final detail, he added—

“Oh, and tell her this too.”

The sisters looked up.

“She must leave the Dark Faction.”
