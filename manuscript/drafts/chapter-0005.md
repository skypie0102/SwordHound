# Chapter 5: The Baskerville Dog (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/005.txt`. Canonical triplet names checked against the user-designated Fandom wiki. Recovered English Chapter 5 is an aligned secondary reference only.

“I don’t like you. Follow me.”

“Follow me.”

“Follow me.”

The Baskerville triplets—Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro, all nine years old—made their challenge openly.

“…”

Eight-year-old Vikir considered them in silence.

Before his regression, meeting those three as a child had felt like encountering death itself.

Decades later, they had also played a part in the humiliating death that ended his first life.

More importantly, in another ten years or so, they would become outstanding warriors known as the Baskerville Trident—or Hugo’s Trident.

They were future pillars who would win countless achievements for the clan.

Which meant there was value in crushing them early.

If Vikir intended to destroy the Baskervilles one day.

“… Sniff. You’re scary, big brothers.”

Was this convincing enough?

Vikir whimpered like a frightened little boy while quietly checking his surroundings.

Then he looked at the arrogant triplets and said,

“Why don’t we go somewhere no one can see us and settle this there?”

The triplets sneered.

“At least you understand honor.”

“You understand.”

“You understand.”

Beating someone in front of too many witnesses would be troublesome even for them.

So they twisted Vikir’s arms behind him, covered his mouth, and dragged him toward a secluded part of the nursery castle.

The old structure had been built according to an ancient architectural style and was full of isolated rooms, blind corners, and forgotten passages.

They passed a flooded cellar entrance, an abandoned granary, and an unrepaired crack in the masonry before climbing a spiral staircase.

At the end of an unused corridor, the triplets shoved Vikir into an empty room.

Highbro, their leader, grinned.

“If you make a sound, we might cut something off. Don’t worry, though. If you reach the infirmary quickly enough, the priests can probably put it back on.”

“Put it back on.”

“Put it back on.”

Highbro drew a dagger from his waist and smiled darkly.

Looking at him, it was difficult to believe he was only nine years old.

Were children simply crueler by nature?

Ordinary children could laugh while tearing the heads or wings from insects, then crush them underfoot.

If even ordinary children could be like that, the children of the Baskervilles needed no explanation.

The three little devils closed in around Vikir.

“I heard you stayed under the River Styx for seven minutes. We laughed ourselves sick when the wet nurses told us that ridiculous story.”

“Laughed.”

“Laughed.”

Highbro gestured to Lowbro.

Lowbro nodded with a grin, slipped behind Vikir, and clamped both hands over his nose and mouth.

Highbro stared into Vikir’s face and smiled viciously.

“Seven minutes, huh? If you can even last three, I’ll put my hand on the line.”

“My hand too!”

“Mine too!”

Lowbro pressed down harder over Vikir’s mouth and nose.

There was a strangely innocent malice in those small hands, as if he were absolutely determined not to let even a breath of air through.

Highbro and Middlebro held Vikir’s arms in place, smiling as they waited to restrain him if he struggled.

… But.

“…?”

“…?”

“…?”

The triplets tilted their heads.

Vikir had not moved at all.

He simply stood there, watching them.

“Hehehe. Trying to hold your breath? You’ve got some nerve. Let’s see how long you last.”

“Let’s see.”

“Let’s see.”

They tightened their grip.

One minute passed.

Vikir was not circulating mana. A child his age should already have been struggling for air.

“…”

But he remained exactly as he had been at the beginning—calm and motionless.

“… He can hold out pretty well.”

“This kid.”

“This kid.”

The smiles on the triplets’ faces slowly faded without them noticing.

Two minutes passed.

Their chatter diminished.

The silence surrounding Vikir began to feel impossibly long.

“… What’s wrong with him? Is he actually not breathing?”

“Isn’t he?”

“He isn’t!”

They checked again. Vikir’s nose and mouth were completely sealed.

Three minutes.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Seven.

Eight.

Nine.

Ten minutes.

Vikir still had not moved.

A graveyard silence filled the room.

By now, cold sweat had soaked the backs of the three boys who were supposed to be tormenting him.

Ten minutes sounded brief until one actually counted six hundred seconds.

Throughout all of it, Vikir stared at them with the same composed expression, barely even blinking.

The silence became oppressive.

At last, Highbro forced a smile and signaled Lowbro.

“… Boring. Let him go.”

“Let him go.”

“Let him go.”

Lowbro began pulling his hand away from Vikir’s mouth.

“Aaaagh!?”

A scream suddenly tore through the room.

It was not Vikir gasping for air.

Blood sprayed from Lowbro’s index finger.

“… Ptui.”

Vikir chewed what was in his mouth a few more times, then spat it out.

He smiled contemptuously at the triplets.

“What happened to putting your hand on the line?”

Blood coated the corners of Vikir’s mouth as he grinned.

The triplets went white.

A moment later, Highbro spat out his words as though forcing them between clenched teeth.

“H-hmph! That doesn’t scare me at all!”

“I’m not scared!”

“I’m scared!”

The last answer broke the pattern.

Highbro and Middlebro turned.

Lowbro had raised his injured hand and was sobbing.

“My finger! My finger’s gone!”

The triplets’ perfect rhythm immediately collapsed.

Baskerville children were accustomed to seeing blood.

It was different when the torn flesh and broken bone belonged to them.

The three boys who had always moved together like the prongs of a single trident suddenly began acting separately.

Highbro tried to calm Lowbro.

“Stay calm. We’ll go to the infirmary and have a priest reattach it.”

“Right.”

“S-sniff… okay. Hurry, hurry!”

Middlebro hoisted Lowbro onto his back.

Highbro lunged for the door.

“Who said you could leave?”

Vikir stepped in front of him.

Highbro’s face twisted.

“You—”

That expression did not last.

Crunch—!

Vikir’s fist smashed into his face, breaking his nose and teeth.

Highbro collapsed, clutching his face.

Crack!

Middlebro stood there in shock for barely an instant before Vikir’s next kick dislocated his jaw.

“Ghh!?”

“Ugh—”

“S-sob…”

Each of the three brothers made a different sound—screaming, groaning, crying.

Vikir quietly sat down beside the door.

“Those injuries will trouble you for the rest of your lives if you don’t receive proper treatment within an hour.”

“…”

“But judging by the state you’re in, I don’t think you’ll be leaving this room today.”

“…”

“Whenever your teeth, jaw, or finger ache on a rainy day, remember what happened here.”

The three boys glared at him as if they wanted to kill him.

Nine years of Baskerville upbringing had not erased their cruelty.

‘… But in the end, they’re still children.’

Vikir smiled faintly.

There was no such thing as someone who kept talking back after being beaten properly.

If someone still talked back after a beating, then the only question was whether they had been beaten hard enough.

That was Hugo’s philosophy of child-rearing.

It was also the Baskerville Clan’s code of conduct.

“Aaaah!”

Highbro, the eldest, gathered what courage he had left and charged again.

A dagger was clenched in his hand.

But—

Thud!

The blade struck Vikir squarely and failed to draw even a single drop of blood.

“… What?”

Highbro stared blankly.

For an instant, the patch of skin on Vikir’s chest touched by the dagger darkened before returning to its normal color.

The blessing of the River Styx.

At only eight years old, Vikir’s body had already become all but impervious to ordinary blades.

Crack!

A sound similar to the impact of the dagger rang out.

The result was entirely different.

The few teeth Highbro had left snapped apart, and he crumpled to the floor.

That was the result of only two punches.

Clatter… crunch… grind—

Tears, snot, saliva, blood, bubbles of spit, and broken teeth mixed together inside his mouth.

Vikir’s shadow fell over the three brothers as they trembled, clutching their faces, jaw, and hand.

Clang!

He threw the dagger into the space between them.

No one reached for it.

They all understood instinctively.

‘… Whoever grabs that first is only going to get beaten worse.’

The triplets did not dare raise their heads.

They crouched on their knees, bleeding and crying, drooling and sweating coldly.

All three had wet themselves in terror.

Then Vikir spoke.

“Only one of you gets to leave this room alive.”

The moment they heard there might be a way out, all three expressions changed.

“Who?”

“Who?”

“Who?”

Vikir’s answer was simple.

“That’s for you brothers to decide.”

At once, all three pairs of eyes shifted toward the dagger lying in the center of the room.

… Scrape.

The Baskerville Trident began to split apart from within.
