# Chapter 266: The Season of Redemption (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/266.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 264 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Existing repo terminology controls Dolores L. Quovadis, Old Testament Faction, New Testament Faction, Quovadis, and Decarabia.

A peaceful weekend morning.

Vikir stepped into the city.

By noon—

the streets were packed.

Ladies talked over drinks on café terraces.

Businesspeople held meetings.

Young couples walked arm in arm.

The smell of flowers drifted from one shop.

Sweet egg bread from another.

Carriages rushed past.

At the expanding train station ahead—

hammers rang continuously.

Vikir stopped.

A narrow alley between red-brick buildings.

Imperial Guard seals blocked the entrance.

Only hours earlier—

he had assassinated Edward Bourbon Jr. there.

Two things had changed.

First—

the corpse was gone.

Only a white outline remained.

Second—

there was a deep trail carved across the ground.

“….”

Vikir examined it from a distance.

It looked almost like the mark left by a giant snake.

That trail had not existed when he left.

People nearby whispered.

“Someone died there last night.”

“They say he was tortured and poisoned.”

“Was it the Night Hound again?”

“No.”

“They’re saying it was Ms. Ouroboros.”

“What?”

“There’s another criminal like Night Hound?”

“You’re behind on the news.”

Vikir blended into the crowd and listened.

He had left no trail.

His route had been deliberately complicated.

He had even switched into dog form to cross terrain no ordinary pursuer could follow.

So he was not worried about being traced.

But Ms. Ouroboros appearing afterward—

that was another matter.

*Strange woman.*

She had moved beyond being merely a copycat.

She had some unknown agenda.

And she appeared to be hunting the Night Hound himself.

Vikir would need to watch her.

Still—

that was not why he had come out today.

He pulled his cap low and entered the busiest district of the Imperial Capital.

“…Crowded.”

People packed the streets like sprouts growing too close together.

*Good.*

*If you want to hide a tree, hide it in a forest.*

Vikir let the crowd carry him forward.

Soon—

the white-shield emblem of Quovadis appeared.

A grand temple.

Beautiful.

Expensive.

Maintained by an Old Testament branch.

A long line stretched from the entrance.

A priest in white shouted through a horn.

“Come repent!”

“Special discount today!”

“Half-price forgiveness for minor sins!”

“Thirty percent off major sins!”

“For truly serious crimes, consult the bishop!”

“Indulgences!”

“Today only!”

“Buy now and receive tax benefits!”

“Who knows when a sale like this will happen again!”

His voice mixed with street vendors selling sweets, roasted corn, and children’s toys.

People exited the temple smiling.

Each carried an indulgence.

A couple nearby caught Vikir’s attention.

“I feel so much better after buying one.”

“I’ve been miserable with guilt.”

“Was that because you drove drunk and hit that child?”

“Yeah.”

“I haven’t slept properly since.”

“I heard indulgences were discounted, so I came to repent.”

“But the child survived, right?”

“Did you apologize to the family?”

“Or compensate them?”

“No.”

“Why would I?”

“I bought an indulgence.”

“A pretty expensive one too.”

“So I should be fine now.”

“I guess so.”

“Anyway, we’re still good people.”

Vikir watched them for a moment.

*No demonic scent.*

They were ordinary humans.

That—

somehow—

made the scene worse.

The Old Testament temple was full of people like them.

People entering with sins.

People leaving with paper forgiveness.

Vikir felt suddenly old.

*What is this world becoming?*

He was fighting to prevent the Age of Destruction.

Today—

that goal felt heavier than usual.

Eventually—

Vikir joined the line.

After a long wait—

he entered the temple.

Massive white pillars rose overhead.

Everything looked expensive enough to make valuation meaningless.

Neatly dressed priests approached visitors.

“Welcome.”

“What sin have you committed?”

“What kind of indulgence do you require?”

A young priest came to Vikir.

Inexperienced.

Eager.

He began explaining before Vikir asked anything.

“We have indulgences for minor offenses.”

“For major offenses.”

“And special indulgences covering crimes punishable by death.”

“But you look quite young.”

“I doubt you’ve committed anything that serious.”

“So perhaps a minor-sin indulgence?”

The priest was trying to estimate the proper price.

Vikir interrupted.

“If someone can repent for past sins—”

“can they also repent in advance for future ones?”

The priest smiled.

“Of course.”

“If you purchase the indulgence beforehand.”

“What are you planning to do?”

“…Something serious enough for execution.”

The priest’s expression hardened.

For a moment—

Vikir wondered whether this would finally produce moral outrage.

Instead—

the priest became professionally serious.

“If the crime is of that level, the indulgence will be very expensive.”

“Can you afford it?”

“If so, I can call a bishop.”

“In the meantime, perhaps you’d like to wait in the VVIP chapel and have tea?”

No warning.

No attempt to stop the future crime.

Only pricing.

Like a luxury sales consultation.

Vikir almost laughed.

The priest lowered his voice.

“…What exactly are you planning to do?”

Vikir answered.

“I intend to kill my neighbor’s pig.”

The priest blinked.

“A… pig?”

“That qualifies as a capital crime?”

“Yes.”

“A pig is alive.”

“Is taking a life not a sin?”

The priest sighed deeply.

He looked at the enormous line behind Vikir.

Then back at Vikir.

“Listen.”

“Just go.”

Vikir did not move.

The priest tore off a blank indulgence.

Stamped it.

Then shoved it into Vikir’s hand.

“Fine.”

“You came all this way over the life of one pig.”

“Take it.”

“…Thank you.”

“Don’t sin again.”

“Live properly.”

Vikir looked at the paper.

“The payment…”

“Tsk.”

“Forget it.”

The priest reached directly into Vikir’s pocket.

Pulled out a coin.

A one-gold copper coin with trace gold content.

He dropped it into the donation box hanging from his neck.

Clink.

“Congratulations.”

“You have repented.”

And that was that.

◆◆◆

Vikir left the temple holding the indulgence.

**<Indulgence>**

**‘May all sins of this faithful believer be forgiven.’**

**—Issued and guaranteed by the Old Testament Faction. Counterfeiting is punishable.—**

*So this is how it works.*

Vikir organized what he had seen.

And smelled.

The demonic presence inside the temple was faint—

but real.

Decarabia opened its eye beneath his clothes.

[…Yes.]

[I can sense the Sixth Corpse here.]

[Very weakly.]

Vikir followed the rotten trace back toward the main road.

*The Sixth isn’t inside this temple itself.*

*But it is connected.*

*And it is somewhere in the Imperial Capital.*

Then—

“…!”

A familiar face appeared.

*Damn.*

Vikir pulled his cap lower and moved behind a pillar.

Even beneath a deep hood—

he recognized her immediately.

Dolores.

Moving suspiciously.

Checking her surroundings.

Then quietly entering the Old Testament temple.
