# Chapter 294: Confessions (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/294.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 292 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Sinclaire, Bianca, Dolores L. Quovadis, House Bourgeois, Belial, and Night Hound. Direct Fandom verification remains robots-blocked.

The night slowly faded toward blue.

A quiet knock sounded in the girls’ dormitory corridor.

Knock.

Knock.

Click.

The doorknob turned softly.

But the door did not open immediately.

Vikir looked around.

The corridor was almost unnaturally quiet.

A faint sour smell drifted from somewhere—

feet.

Badly dried laundry.

Maybe both.

Snoring leaked through one closed door.

Several shared lockers showed signs of damage.

Overall—

not much different from the men’s dormitory.

Once Vikir confirmed no one else was awake—

creak.

The door opened a crack.

“Bro?”

Sinclaire peeked out.

Once she saw it was Vikir—

she smiled.

“Come in.”

The door opened.

Warm air washed over him.

The room smelled faintly sweet—

some kind of berry fragrance.

The decorations were simple.

But small objects scattered around the room gave it a cozy, old-fashioned atmosphere.

“Where’s Bianca?”

“Don’t you share this room?”

“She’s sleeping in another friend’s room.”

“We haven’t been getting along lately.”

“I thought you two were close.”

“What happened?”

“…Nothing.”

“It’s probably my fault.”

Sinclaire gave an awkward laugh.

She wore a white T-shirt and pink shorts.

A damp towel hung around her neck.

Her short hair—

still not completely dry—

smelled faintly of shampoo.

“Here!”

She tossed something.

Vikir caught it.

A cold can of beer.

Condensation covered the metal.

Sinclaire brought out a few snacks.

“It’s too late for real food.”

“So don’t complain that the snacks are cheap.”

“I won’t.”

“Good.”

Then she clapped.

“Do you play board games?”

From under the bed—

she pulled several boxes.

Jenga.

Rummikub.

UNO.

Simple games.

“Want to play?”

“…It has been a long time.”

Vikir nodded.

Board games reminded him of countless nights before regression—

playing with comrades in military camps.

Strangely—

tonight—

another memory came first.

The orphanage.

Volunteer work.

Children laughing over simple games.

*Have I grown dull?*

Since regression—

far more than the world around him had changed.

Vikir himself had changed too.

CRASH.

The Jenga tower collapsed.

Sinclaire grabbed her hair with both hands.

“Ahhh!”

“Why do I always lose?”

“Center of gravity.”

“If you maintain balance—

it won’t fall.”

“Tch.”

“Easy for you to say.”

She started again.

Then knocked it down again.

“Seriously.”

“I can’t play board games with Bro.”

“Why are you so good?”

“You act like you used to do nothing but this.”

“There was a time when that was almost true.”

“Huh?”

Sinclaire’s eyes widened.

Vikir closed his eyes.

No explanation.

Soon—

empty beer cans accumulated.

As the alcohol took effect—

Sinclaire’s expression loosened.

A reddish color spread across her pale face.

Then—

almost casually—

she said:

“I want to see the Milky Way.”

Vikir remembered something she had said before.

*Whenever I feel bad—

I come here and look at the Milky Way.*

The rooftop.

Beer.

Stars.

That had probably been one of Sinclaire’s few ways to relieve stress.

Now—

the best rooftop viewing spot was off limits.

Dolores had closed it personally.

*Because it was one of our contact points during the Belial operation.*

Vikir rested a hand against his chin.

Then asked:

“You must be annoyed that President Dolores closed the rooftop.”

Sinclaire froze.

Only slightly.

But enough.

The clear, lake-like eyes darkened.

Vikir noticed immediately.

“Did something happen between you and the student council president?”

No answer.

Vikir already knew about the Bourgeois incident.

What bothered him—

was Sinclaire’s reaction afterward.

*She isn’t the kind of person who would collapse just from seeing a demon fight a demon hunter.*

He watched her.

Sinclaire looked down.

Silence filled the room.

Vikir waited.

Eventually—

she answered.

“I’m thinking about dropping out.”

Vikir’s eyes widened.

That—

he had not expected.

Before regression—

Sinclaire had completed all four years.

Top-ranked student from first year through graduation.

Vice student council president in second year.

Student council president in third.

Then—

immediately after graduating—

she vanished completely from the world.

But now—

before even finishing first year—

she wanted to leave.

“I haven’t told anyone.”

“You’re the first.”

Sinclaire smiled weakly.

Vikir asked:

“Because of President Dolores?”

“No.”

“It has nothing to do with her.”

“I’m just tired.”

“Burned out.”

“I’ve wanted to leave school for a while.”

“That’s why I started acting cold toward everyone.”

A lie.

Vikir knew it.

The Sinclaire he remembered had never taken a leave.

Never surrendered first place.

He cut directly through the excuse.

“Something happened between you and President Dolores.”

“No.”

“It’s just—”

“Is it because of what happened at Bourgeois?”

Sinclaire’s face went rigid.

*There it is.*

Vikir had found the wound.

The Sixth Corpse.

Belial.

The Bourgeois vault.

Sinclaire had clearly been hit harder than he realized.

“No.”

“Nothing happened that day.”

She waved both hands quickly.

Vikir reached out.

Grabbed both wrists.

“What happened?”

“…!”

“What is hurting you this much?”

Sinclaire’s pupils widened.

Then trembled.

Vikir had seen this before.

Countless comrades after demon hunts.

Nightmares.

Guilt.

Shock.

The aftermath of surviving battles against things humans were never meant to see.

*If this is post-battle trauma—

I can help.*

He did not want someone who might become a major future strength of the Human Alliance—

to disappear into isolation.

But Sinclaire’s answer—

was not what he expected.

“…He killed him.”

“What?”

Her voice shook so badly that the words slurred.

“Night Hound killed my father.”

For a moment—

Vikir felt as though lightning had struck his head.

Night Hound had killed only one being inside House Bourgeois that night.

Belial.

And the human body Belial occupied—

was Bartolomeo.

*So that means…*

Before he could finish the thought—

whump.

A weight hit his chest.

Sinclaire leaned forward and wrapped both arms around him.

His shirt became damp.

She was crying.

Vikir did not push her away.

He did not embrace her either.

He simply stood still.

Silence.

Minutes passed.

Eventually—

Sinclaire’s shaking weakened.

The hound stood with his mouth half-open—

still unable to find words for the lamb curled against him.

Then—

Sinclaire lifted her face.

Wet eyes.

Trembling voice.

“Now it’s Bro’s turn to answer.”

Vikir looked down.

Sinclaire asked:

“…What do you think of me?”
