A man's tears.

They were a rare thing to see.

The older a man became, the rarer they seemed.

Society did not look kindly on men who cried easily.

That was true even in ordinary households.

It was far more true in Baskerville.

A heart like a furnace filled with boiling iron.

A swordsman seemingly stripped of ordinary human emotion.

Hugo Le Baskerville—

the supreme head of the Ironblood Sword House.

The word *tears* did not suit him at all.

He looked like the sort of man who would not cry even if someone drove a needle into his eye.

So when Vikir saw Hugo crying that morning—

he had been surprised.

Extremely surprised.

Even after surviving the Age of Destruction and ten years of brutal war, Vikir could remember very few moments that had shocked him so deeply.

Which led him to one conclusion.

*Surely nothing can surprise me more than this.*

He had seen Hugo cry.

What could possibly top that?

Vikir genuinely believed it.

And then—

he was proven wrong.

Again.

More thoroughly than before.

“Peekaboo! Ta-da!”

Pomeranian sat awkwardly in front of him.

More precisely—

she sat on someone's lap.

And the person twisting his hands, tongue, and eyes into ridiculous expressions to make her laugh was—

was—

*I don't want to admit it.*

*But that really is Hugo Le Baskerville.*

Vikir covered his forehead with one hand.

The head of Baskerville had Pomeranian seated on his knee and was making faces at her like a village grandfather entertaining a toddler.

Everything Vikir believed he knew about Hugo was collapsing.

Anyone in Baskerville would have reacted the same way.

“???”

“???”

John Barrymore and Osiris Le Baskerville stood beside Vikir with their mouths hanging open.

Even Vikir had a disturbing thought.

*Has a demon brainwashed him?*

Hugo, however, carried on without the slightest concern for the people staring at him.

“Heh heh heh. Is this old man's mustache funny? Go on, pull it. There's more over here too.”

“Musta…che… dirty…”

“Hm?”

Hugo looked wounded.

“Dirty?! This old man shampoos and conditions his mustache every day. I even used essence on it because you were coming today.”

“Old… man… beard… prickly…”

“Hm? Old man?”

Hugo's face brightened instantly.

“Oh! Right, right—Grandpa! Say it again. Go on. Heh heh. That's right, this old man is your grandpa.”

It was so far outside anything Vikir had expected that he could only stare.

*When did this happen?*

His mind went back to a short while earlier.

The instant Hugo first saw Pomeranian, his eyes had trembled.

*Th-this child… is really Penelope's daughter?*

*Yes.*

Vikir had explained it as plainly as possible.

*Black hair and red eyes are Baskerville traits. And judging from the portrait, she looks almost exactly like Lady Roxana and Penelope.*

*Almost exactly? I thought Penelope herself had come back to life! How can this be?!*

Pomeranian's very existence provided one piece of evidence after another.

A tiny mole near her neck.

A bluish birthmark on her lower leg.

Both were physical traits Hugo himself possessed.

More importantly, Pomeranian had been found among the Rokoko Clan that had abducted Penelope.

And she carried the pendant Hugo had made with his own hands.

Pomeranian also remembered her mother clearly.

“Mommy… was always happy… smiled a lot…”

Hugo had laughed and cried at the same time.

From Pomeranian's account, Penelope seemed to have lived a life that was happier than Hugo had feared.

“But Mommy… always missed… Father…”

The little girl struggled to put the rest into words.

“She tried… many times… to come back… but couldn't… Madam Eight-Legs… wouldn't let us through…”

Hugo tilted his head.

Pomeranian spoke remarkably well for a five-year-old, but her explanation was still difficult to follow in places.

Vikir, who had spent two years in the Depths, translated.

“Penelope always missed you. She tried more than once to return to Baskerville, but a powerful monster controlled the route and prevented her from crossing.”

“What?!”

Hugo shot upright.

“You're saying a monster kept my daughter from returning to me?!”

“Yes.”

Vikir nodded.

“It was one of the most dangerous monsters in the Depths. I killed it.”

Hugo stared at him.

Then his expression filled with fierce approval.

“Well done.”

His voice rang through the room.

“You avenged her for me. Well done. Truly well done.”

Repay harm with harm.

Even in a moment like this, Hugo remained unmistakably Baskerville.

Then he turned toward Vikir again.

His eyes were still red and wet.

“To hear how my daughter spent her last years…”

Hugo's voice trembled.

“And to learn that she was at least able to live with some happiness…”

He swallowed.

“That alone is a blessing compared with knowing nothing at all.”

His arms tightened around Pomeranian.

“And now I have the granddaughter she left behind.”

A faint smile appeared.

“I do not think there will be a better day in the rest of my life.”

Praise.

Joy.

Open gratitude.

Coming from Hugo, the display was so extreme that both Barrymore and Osiris looked stunned.

Barrymore had served Hugo for decades.

Even he seemed unable to process it.

Osiris was worse.

The man who normally displayed almost as little emotion as his father stood with his mouth open and his eyes wide.

Then Hugo carried Pomeranian toward Vikir.

Vikir instinctively tensed.

His body prepared to counterattack before his mind could stop it.

But Hugo halted in front of him.

And then—

“Thank you, son.”

Hugo bowed.

Not a nod.

Not a shallow bend at the waist.

A full ninety-degree bow.

Barrymore and Osiris' mouths fell open all over again.

Silence lasted for several seconds.

Finally Osiris managed to speak.

“My life has not been very long…”

He stared at his father.

“But this is the most surprising day I have ever lived through.”

“I have lived quite a long time…”

Barrymore slowly closed his mouth.

“And I agree with you, Young Master.”

Osiris' gaze remained on Hugo.

Parents were mirrors for their children.

Children were mirrors for their parents.

For most of his life, Osiris had tried to imitate the side of Hugo everyone knew—

strict.

Impartial.

Cold.

Unshakable.

So Osiris had suppressed his own emotions whenever they surfaced.

He restrained himself almost as a form of discipline.

A small desire to eat something delicious.

The impulse to smile.

Even his feelings for a woman he liked—

he deliberately pushed them away.

It had become a form of self-punishing training.

But now—

for the first time—

Osiris saw his father differently.

*So Father has a side like this too.*

And if Hugo possessed that side—

then perhaps Osiris did as well.

He learned something that day.

There was no need to crush every trace of awkward warmth inside himself.

*Can I allow myself to do some of the things I want?*

Perfection was not necessary.

A flaw here.

A little humanity there.

Even the coldest man in Baskerville could smile peacefully because of them.

“…”

Osiris fixed Hugo's smiling face in his memory as though looking into a mirror.

Then Barrymore noticed Osiris' expression.

His eyes widened.

*Good heavens.*

*Young Master is smiling.*

Osiris had almost never smiled openly in his life.

Barrymore had certainly never seen him do it this naturally.

The old butler's own face softened in response.

Master.

Young master.

Both smiling.

For the first time in years, something like a warm breeze seemed to pass through Baskerville.

Then—

one voice erased every smile in the room.

“Father. I have something to say.”

Vikir.

The atmosphere tightened at once.

Hugo and Osiris both turned toward him with serious expressions.

This was the person who had brought their family an impossible piece of happiness.

However sternly they looked at him, neither could hide the gratitude in their eyes.

Hugo understood first.

“Ah.”

He nodded.

“The reward.”

Clear rewards.

Clear punishments.

That was one of Hugo's principles.

Vikir had discovered Penelope's fate.

He had killed the monster that prevented her from returning.

And he had brought home a granddaughter whose existence none of them even knew about.

It was difficult to imagine a greater service to the family.

Hugo nodded.

Whatever Vikir asked—

he was prepared to hear it.

Osiris felt the same.

“Tell me, son.”

Hugo's voice was firm.

“What do you want?”

Osiris added,

“If it's something I can help you with, brother, I'll do everything I can.”

Vikir nodded.

Everything had gone far more smoothly than expected.

Now it was time to aim at his real objective.

Before entering the Academy, there was still one matter inside the family that had to be resolved.

Revenge for the miserable end of his previous life.

And the first step toward preventing the coming Age of Destruction.

“I want to hunt a monster.”

“…?”

“A very dangerous one.”

“…!”

Hugo and Osiris' expressions hardened immediately.

They were the head and heir of the Ironblood Sword House.

Swords of the Empire.

Men whose family existed to fight monsters.

Hugo asked,

“What kind of monster?”

“If you're asking for its species, I don't know exactly.”

Vikir paused.

“But…”

“But?”

This time, Osiris asked the question.

Vikir answered briefly.

“I know where it's hiding.”

Hugo and Osiris nodded at the same time.

“Where?”

Vikir answered as though he had been waiting for the question.

“Inside Baskerville.”

“…!”

Both men's eyes widened.

Then Vikir stated what he truly wanted.

“Half a day.”

His voice remained flat.

“Lend me all seven Baskerville Knight Orders for half a day.”

It was a request for roughly half the military power of Baskerville.

Enough force—

if fully unleashed—

to erase a small country from the map within a single day and night.
