# Chapter 13: The Gluttonous Flies (2)

‘Only one who carries the blood of Baskerville may draw this sword, Beelzebub.’

The inscription contained both the Baskerville name and the weapon’s name.

“… So this is the relic that was found here.”

Using the faint light from shattered ruby fragments, Vikir felt along the weapon’s shape.

Three rounded, reddish ornaments sat around the hilt like small ruby beads.

From them extended a long, narrow black blade, more like an enormous spike or awl than an ordinary sword.

A faint green sheen glimmered around the rough hilt.

Its overall silhouette resembled a gigantic fly.

Vikir knew this relic.

In his previous life, when he was still young, he had once seen an illustration of it in an old mythology book.

‘The Fly of Gluttony, Beelzebub.’

Explaining the weapon’s origin in full would have taken a long time.

The short version was enough.

In the distant age of myth, seven unprecedented demonic monsters had invaded the continent.

They became known as the Seven Calamities.

Humans across the continent united to bring those calamities down.

The remains they left behind—whether material or something less tangible—continued to exist in the world afterward.

They could no longer interfere with cause and effect as they once had, but some portion of their former power remained.

Beelzebub was one such relic.

Vikir had spent his previous life becoming accustomed to hunger.

There had been times when he ate nothing for an entire week, then consumed everything he could in a single meal.

On long operations, he had sometimes hidden for a month while surviving on little more than water.

A stomach trained under conditions like that did not normally complain.

Even after regression into a child’s body, Vikir should not have been this weak against hunger.

Growl—!

His stomach twisted violently again.

Vikir frowned.

This was not ordinary hunger.

“Are you asking for something?”

The answer came immediately.

Bzzzzzz—

Beelzebub vibrated with a sound like a fly beating its wings.

Vikir’s nose twitched.

His sense of smell seemed sharper than before.

An irresistible scent drifted toward him from somewhere outside the dungeon.

Demonic energy.

To Beelzebub—and somehow to Vikir as well—it smelled almost delicious.

He climbed out of the dungeon and made his way back through the tangled root tunnels.

The closer he came to the examination boundary, the stronger the scent became.

The first corpse he encountered was the Hellhound he had killed earlier.

Beelzebub reacted before Vikir even called on it.

Like a hound baring its teeth without its master’s command, the relic lunged eagerly toward the body.

Ordinarily, an unruly hound deserved punishment.

This time Vikir made an exception.

The hunger Beelzebub shared with him was overwhelming.

‘You must have been starving for a very long time.’

The stone where Beelzebub had rested and the surrounding soil had dried and warped.

For a moment, Vikir wondered whether the relic’s insane hunger might even have contributed to the lifeless, scorched condition of the surrounding mountains.

If so, how much would he have to feed it to satisfy that appetite?

“…”

He shook away the thought.

The power Beelzebub offered was worth the price.

Before long, the relic demonstrated exactly why.

Gulp. Gulp. Gulp. Gulp.

Beelzebub drove its stinger into the Hellhound’s neck and began drawing blood at an astonishing speed.

Blood and the juices of the monster’s organs flowed through the weapon and into Vikir.

The painful emptiness in his stomach began to ease.

His knotted insides slowly relaxed.

Flash!

One of Beelzebub’s three eye-like red ornaments suddenly shone brighter than the others.

‘It gained a skill.’

Vikir’s intuition was correct.

When the original Fly of Gluttony had still lived, it possessed the power to steal the traits and specialties of others.

Its capacity for absorption had once been nearly limitless.

Countless victims had been stripped of the achievements of their entire lives and reduced to helpless wrecks.

This relic was only a remnant of that creature, so its power was far more limited.

It could hold only three stolen abilities.

By drinking the Hellhound’s blood, Beelzebub had acquired its first.

The ability manifested only when Vikir willed it.

Anyone wounded by Beelzebub’s stinger would suffer the Hellhound’s deadly bleeding effect.

Even a shallow injury that normally would not bleed much would eventually begin pouring blood, and the bleeding would last far longer than an ordinary wound.

Meanwhile—

Bzzzzzzzz—

Beelzebub continued feeding.

It did not stop until the Hellhound’s corpse was nearly mummified.

The skill had been acquired almost immediately.

The rest was simple hunger.

Vikir’s own hunger eased somewhat as well.

Growl—!

Not enough.

That delicious scent still drifted from farther away.

Vikir hurried back toward the familiar boundary line.

The Cradle.

The broad region where Baskerville children were undergoing their practical examination spread out beyond it.

Vikir slipped across the boundary.

His pit beneath the distant rocks soon came into view.

Fortunately, the Guide Dogs had not returned to watch him closely.

Even the so-called Cradle covered a large area, too broad for the instructors to observe every corner at once.

And Vikir had spent so long doing nothing but sitting in his pit that they had already classified him as a harmless, low-priority examinee.

He reached his hiding place and brushed away the soil and dead leaves concealing what lay beneath.

A very appetizing mass of meat appeared.

Cerberus.

Its corpse remained where he had left it.

BZZZZZZZZ—!

Beelzebub went wild at the scent of flesh and demonic energy.

The fly-wing buzzing grew louder.

The relic plunged into Cerberus’s body and began greedily drawing out blood and fluids.

Vikir stopped it almost immediately.

“No. Stop. This corpse is evidence for the practical examination. If you ruin it too badly, explaining the autopsy later will become a pain.”

Beelzebub ignored him and tried to feed more deeply.

Vikir’s expression hardened.

“I said no.”

Smack!

He slapped the back of his own hand hard.

Naturally, striking his own hand hurt Vikir.

And if Vikir hurt, Beelzebub hurt with him.

Bzz…

Only then did the relic reluctantly withdraw its fang from Cerberus.

Vikir looked down at the veins along his wrist.

Fresh power seemed to pulse through them.

Perhaps Cerberus’s greater strength had forced the abilities into a new order.

Cerberus occupied the first slot.

The Hellhound had been pushed down to the second.

And, absurdly, the third now contained the ability of a Brown Rat Norvegicus.

Vikir glanced beneath Cerberus’s corpse.

Scattered bones and scraps of rat hide lay underneath it—the remains of something Cerberus had eaten earlier.

Apparently, feeding Beelzebub stronger monsters could displace weaker abilities already stored inside it.

Among the three veins standing out on Vikir’s wrist, the one associated with the rat seemed noticeably thinner than the others.

He gave a quiet laugh.

Something was still better than leaving the slot empty, so for now he let it remain.

More importantly, acquiring Cerberus’s **Incinerate** was an enormous gain.

Anyone pierced by Beelzebub’s black fang could now be afflicted by the hellfire carried by Cerberus.

The resulting burn would not naturally heal.

Once struck, the victim would have to endure searing pain until death unless some other means removed or overcame the effect.

And burning pain was among the worst agony a human being could experience.

In that respect, Incinerate was even more terrifying than the Hellhound’s Hemorrhage.

“All right. I’ve taken everything this mountain can give me.”

There was little else he needed to do.

He could simply wait for the practical examination to end, present Cerberus’s corpse to the Guide Dogs, and claim first place.

And perhaps receive another reward from Hugo.

“…”

Vikir closed his eyes and assessed his condition.

His mana reserves had already reached roughly the quantity expected of a Fourth Circle mage.

As for Baskerville swordsmanship, he could clearly draw three Fangs.

Perhaps now he could manage four.

Conservatively, his practical level remained **High Sword Expert**.

Judged generously, he was already approaching **Low Sword Graduator**.

In real combat, the limitations of his present swordsmanship still dragged his performance down by roughly half a step, so High Sword Expert remained the safer assessment.

In his previous life, no matter how much mana or combat experience Vikir accumulated, the swordsmanship he had been taught prevented him from drawing more than four Fangs.

Hugo was different.

The Hugo of the present era could draw seven Fangs.

The Hugo Vikir remembered from before his regression could draw nine.

The Baskerville 9th Fang.

Swordsmanship of that level was passed only within the clan’s privileged direct line—to the legitimate eldest son who would inherit the house and to selected illegitimate sons meant to support him.

Compared with it, the four-Fang swordsmanship Vikir had learned in his first life was crude and incomplete.

But this time things would be different.

“… The 10th Fang.”

The Baskerville 10th Fang.

Ten Fangs.

An ultimate realm no living Baskerville of this era could reach.

A legendary swordsmanship manual left by the first patriarch contained the key to it, together with knowledge born from the ancient struggle against one of the Seven Calamities.

Vikir knew where that treasure was.

‘I don’t even have to go far.’

It was absurdly close.

The priceless knowledge was rotting unnoticed among ordinary miscellaneous books that anyone could have picked up and read.

And who would give that book to Vikir?

Hugo Le Baskerville, patriarch of the Baskerville Clan.

Hugo himself would place it in Vikir’s hands.

Without ever imagining what the seemingly worthless book was truly worth.
