# Chapter 87: The Illiad (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/087.txt` — `f954b592f541c39249d6785ae49baff72188738a`. Recovered English Chapter 86 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Ah'Heman**, **Akwilla**, **Aiyen**, **Ahun**, **Aheul**, **Leviathan Clan**, **Vikir**, **Chihuahua Baskerville**, **Underdog City**, and **Illiad**.

Ah'Heman froze.

Akwilla wanted the bag.

The bag containing Leviathan's supposed antidote.

“Show us.”

The chieftain's command was absolute.

Ah'Heman panicked.

“I can't!”

The moment he said it, he knew he had made a mistake.

Every Ballak warrior stiffened.

Young and old alike respected the chieftain.

Refusing her directly was almost a confession by itself.

Aiyen started forward.

“That old bastard—”

“Hand it over.”

Someone moved before her.

Ahun.

Ah'Heman's grandson held out his hand.

“The chieftain ordered you.”

He drew an arrow.

“If you refuse—”

“Ungrateful brat!”

Ah'Heman shouted.

Ahun did not move.

Watching Aheul nearly die had changed something in him.

The other warriors joined in.

“Open the bag!”

“Show us!”

“Traitor!”

Ah'Heman's face twisted.

Then he ripped the bag loose.

“Fine!”

He swung his arm—

and threw it into the river.

Silence.

Then Ah'Heman smiled.

“Oh dear.”

He spread his hands.

“My old hands slipped.”

Akwilla did not react.

She looked toward Vikir.

“Just as you said.”

Ah'Heman's smile vanished.

A head broke the river surface.

“Chieftain! Got it!”

A Ballak hunter had been waiting underwater.

The soaked leather bag was recovered before the current could take it.

Half its contents remained.

White powder.

Akwilla examined it.

“This is the Red Death antidote?”

Ah'Heman said nothing.

“Why do you have it?”

Nothing.

Akwilla scattered some powder across the ground.

“Vikir was right again.”

Ah'Heman looked at him.

Akwilla continued.

“This isn't medicine.”

She rubbed it between her fingers.

“It's flour.”

“What?”

Ah'Heman stared.

“No. That is the antidote.”

Vikir stepped closer.

“Leviathan lied to you.”

They had understood his ambition.

His desire for authority.

They had given a convenient native collaborator a plague with no real antidote and let him believe he remained in control.

Ah'Heman began backtracking.

“I didn't know anything!”

“Then why did you refuse to show the bag?”

Vikir asked.

“And why did you run directly to the water source where the outbreak began?”

“Why should I answer you?”

Vikir reached into his clothes.

“Because there's more.”

Two letters.

Different handwriting.

One rough.

One neat.

“These are communications between you and Leviathan.”

The warriors stared.

Several elders recognized Ah'Heman's handwriting.

Ah'Heman erupted.

“Forgery!”

He pointed at Vikir.

“I never wrote that!”

He was right.

At least about the letter.

Vikir had forged it.

Two years as Deputy Magistrate in Underdog City had taught him many things.

One of them came from Chihuahua.

“Your handwriting is unusually good.”

“Of course. I sign things for the magistrate constantly. I've become excellent at copying other hands.”

“Teach me.”

“With pleasure.”

Vikir had practiced.

A useful administrative skill.

And now—

a useful weapon.

*Incitement is easy.*

Vikir watched Ah'Heman rage.

*Clearing your name afterward is hard.*

The accusation itself was false evidence.

The underlying crime was real.

Akwilla examined the letters.

“We would need to find the Leviathan correspondent to verify this.”

Impossible in practice.

Ballak could not investigate one of the Seven Great Clans.

The crowd's anger rose.

“Traitor!”

“My wife almost died!”

“My children too!”

“Hang him!”

Even Ahun looked at his grandfather with disgust.

Ah'Heman understood the situation.

There was still no decisive legitimate proof.

The powder was flour.

The letters could be denied.

If he refused to confess, they lacked clean grounds under their own customs.

Then Akwilla surprised everyone.

“Enough.”

The crowd fell silent.

“There is no conclusive proof.”

Aiyen stared.

“Mother—Chieftain! You're going to let this go?”

“Silence.”

Akwilla's voice hardened.

“Show respect to the shaman.”

She praised his age.

His years of service.

His status.

His dedication to tradition.

Ah'Heman could hardly believe it.

Akwilla—

the woman who had spent years opposing his authority—

was defending him.

He bowed.

“I am humbled by the chieftain's wisdom.”

“Naturally.”

Akwilla smiled.

“How could ignorant young people be allowed to stain your honor?”

Ah'Heman's relief began turning into unease.

“You are a famous shaman.”

“Yes……”

“Your pride must be heavy.”

“……Yes?”

“And to be falsely accused without evidence must wound that pride terribly.”

The trap closed.

Akwilla raised her voice.

“So I will give my old friend and honored spiritual guide the chance to defend his innocence!”

The warriors shouted in agreement.

Vikir stepped forward.

Ah'Heman stepped back.

Akwilla's smile disappeared.

“If you are innocent, protect your honor.”

Ah'Heman swallowed.

“What……kind of chance?”

Akwilla answered.

“I order an **Illiad**.”

Ballak's ancient method for an irreconcilable dispute.

A contest settled by force.

The winner's position would decide the matter.

Ah'Heman went pale.

Vikir smiled faintly.

The evidence had never been the point.

It had only been the road leading here.
