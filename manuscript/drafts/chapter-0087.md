# Chapter 87: The Illiad (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/087.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 86 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Ah'Heman**, **Akwilla**, **Aiyen**, **Ahun**, **Aheul**, **Leviathan Clan**, **Vikir**, **Chihuahua Baskerville**, and **Illiad**.

Ah'Heman's thoughts spun in confusion.

His plan had been simple.

Finish the ritual.

Release the antidote.

Cure everyone.

Become the hero of Ballak.

Instead, the plan had failed before he even had the chance to put it into motion.

He had expected every warrior in the tribe to look at him with respect.

Most of them were staring at him with contempt.

“…?”

That part still made no sense to him.

He did not understand how the Red Death had been cured, but surely the failure of his ritual alone could not explain this kind of hostility.

Even if the ritual had done nothing, their reaction was far too cold.

Then Akwilla repeated her demand.

“None of that matters right now. I want to see what's inside that pouch at your waist.”

Ah'Heman felt his heart drop.

He barely managed to keep his thoughts together.

Why?

Why did the chieftain want to see that particular pouch?

And why now?

She would not have asked unless she knew something.

*…Did they figure it out?*

For the man who had personally helped spread the Red Death through the water source, that possibility was enough to make his insides twist.

Akwilla watched him hesitate.

“Well? Take it out. Let us see what's inside.”

The authority of the Ballak chieftain was absolute.

Once the chieftain gave a direct order, no one in the tribe simply refused.

Yet Ah'Heman was so frightened that he did exactly that.

“I-I can't show you this!”

The words left his mouth before he even seemed to understand what he was saying.

The consequences were immediate.

Every warrior's face hardened.

The younger hunters sighed or shook their heads.

The older warriors frowned deeply.

Respect and loyalty toward the chieftain ran through every generation of Ballak.

Openly denying Akwilla's command could only make Ah'Heman look worse.

“That old bastard… I'll—!”

Aiyen started forward in anger.

But someone moved before she could.

“Hand over the pouch. Now.”

Ahun.

Ah'Heman's own grandson stepped to the front and held out his hand.

Aiyen stopped short, surprised.

Ahun kept his eyes fixed on his grandfather.

“You cannot disobey the chieftain. If you don't open that pouch immediately…”

He drew an arrow from his waist and nocked it.

Ah'Heman's heavy brows shot upward.

“You ungrateful brat!”

“…”

Ahun did not flinch.

Watching his younger sister Aheul nearly die from the Red Death had changed something in him.

Soon the other warriors began shouting as well.

“Hand it over!”

“Show us what's inside!”

“Traitor! You're the one who did this!”

The accusations came like a wave.

Ah'Heman's expression twisted further with each one.

Finally—

“Fine! Look! Go ahead and look!”

He tore the pouch from his waist.

Then, before anyone could stop him, he hurled it into the river beside them.

The warriors froze.

A strange silence followed.

Ah'Heman looked at Akwilla and forced a smug smile.

“Oh dear. This old man got dizzy and accidentally dropped the pouch into the river…”

The excuse died halfway through.

Something was wrong.

The warriors were whispering among themselves and looking to one side.

Vikir stood there with his arms folded, saying nothing.

Akwilla looked at him.

“As expected. Just as you said. He really did throw it into the river.”

“…What?”

Ah'Heman's mouth fell open.

Then—

“Chieftain! I found it!”

A head suddenly broke the surface.

A Ballak warrior had been hiding underwater in advance.

He snatched up the leather pouch Ah'Heman had thrown away.

It was soaked through, but about half of its contents remained.

“Ah!? No!”

Ah'Heman lunged forward.

Too late.

The dripping pouch was already in Akwilla's hands.

“…”

She looked inside.

White powder, partly dissolved by the water, filled the bottom.

Akwilla raised her eyes.

“This is the antidote for the Red Death, isn't it?”

“…”

“Why do you have it?”

Ah'Heman opened and closed his mouth.

No answer came.

Akwilla nodded slowly.

“Again. You were right.”

She was looking at Vikir.

Ah'Heman followed her gaze in bewilderment.

Akwilla poured some of the white powder onto the ground.

“This powder does nothing. It's ordinary flour.”

“What!?”

“Vikir said you would believe it was the antidote. Judging by that expression, he was right again.”

Terror spread across Ah'Heman's face.

“N-no. That can't be. That's the antidote. It has to be…”

“Leviathan deceived you,” Vikir said as he stepped forward.

The Leviathan Clan had used a foolish local collaborator to spread a plague that had no antidote.

They had seen through Ah'Heman's hunger for power and authority.

They had simply given that ambition a direction.

And now every prediction Vikir had made in advance was coming true in front of the Ballak warriors.

Their mood darkened further.

Ah'Heman desperately tried to retreat from his own story.

“I-I didn't know! Like he said, it's just flour!”

“Then why did you hesitate when we asked to see it?”

Vikir's voice remained even.

“And why did you come straight to the water source where the plague first appeared?”

“Shut up! Why should I explain myself to you!?”

Vikir questioned him point by point.

Ah'Heman answered with anger instead of explanations.

It did nothing to improve his position.

Because Vikir had prepared something more.

Two letters appeared in his hand.

The handwriting on them was different.

One was rough and difficult to read.

The other was neat and controlled.

Vikir held them up.

“These are letters exchanged between you and the Leviathan Clan.”

A murmur went through the warriors.

One of the letters unmistakably resembled Ah'Heman's handwriting.

Eyes moved between the paper and the shaman.

Ah'Heman's face flushed with fury.

“That's a lie! I never wrote a letter like that!”

No one believed him.

Several elderly Ballak stepped forward and confirmed that the writing looked exactly like Ah'Heman's.

“Aaaargh! It's true! I'm innocent! That bastard is framing me!”

Ah'Heman pointed at Vikir and nearly jumped in place with rage.

Vikir merely smiled.

Because this time—

Ah'Heman really was being framed.

*Good thing I had Clerk Chihuahua around.*

Vikir remembered Underdog City two years earlier.

> *Clerk Chihuahua, your handwriting is excellent.*

> *Of course! I've never met anyone better with a pen. I sign for the magistrate so often that I've become quite good at imitating other people's handwriting too…*

> *Could you teach me?*

> *Of course! It would be an honor!*

> *Thank you. Then teach me whenever there's time.*

Vikir had practiced the technique diligently while serving as Deputy Magistrate.

Now the effort was paying off.

Forgery could be surprisingly useful in agitation and political work.

*Incitement is easy. Clearing your name afterward is hard.*

He quietly watched Ah'Heman rage over an accusation that was, for once, genuinely fabricated.

Akwilla inspected the letters.

“We would have to determine whether anyone in the Leviathan Clan writes like this.”

In practice, that was almost impossible.

How could Ballak investigate one of the Seven Great Clans of the Empire?

Naturally, the mood turned against Ah'Heman.

“Traitor!”

“My wife almost died because of you!”

“My children too!”

“Kill him!”

“Hang him!”

Even Ahun was looking at his grandfather with disgust.

Not one person openly stood on Ah'Heman's side.

He clenched his teeth.

*There is still no real proof.*

The white powder had been exposed as flour.

The letters could be denied.

As long as he kept refusing to confess, Ballak lacked clean evidence with which to condemn him under its own customs.

It was shameless.

Cowardly.

But it was still his best chance to survive.

Then Akwilla spoke.

“…Enough. Everyone is overreacting.”

All the warriors turned toward her.

So did Ah'Heman.

Akwilla coughed lightly before continuing.

“When you look at it properly, there is no decisive evidence. I do not want this incident to stain the shaman's honor any further. He has served this tribe for many years. He is a man of merit.”

Whispers spread through the crowd.

Aiyen stared at her mother in disbelief.

“Mother—no, Chieftain! You're really going to cover this up?”

“Silence!”

Akwilla's voice cracked like a whip.

“Respect the shaman's authority! His age, seniority, and years of service to this tribe far exceed yours. Show proper manners!”

Aiyen shut her mouth under the force of the rebuke.

Many warriors still looked dissatisfied.

But the chieftain's word was absolute, so no one argued.

Ah'Heman himself looked dazed.

Akwilla had always treated his growing influence as a thorn in her side.

Why was she defending him now?

He did not understand.

But it was the only opening he had.

He bowed deeply.

“I can only admire the chieftain's ocean-like generosity and wisdom.”

“It is nothing. If one respects the traditions left by the ancestors, this is only natural.”

Ah'Heman nodded as though deeply moved.

Then Akwilla's eyes sharpened.

“How could I allow ignorant young people to smear your lofty honor?”

“Yes. Thank you, Chieftain.”

“No thanks are necessary. You are a man of great reputation, not merely in Ballak but throughout the peoples of this region.”

“Yes…”

“The weight of such honor and dignity must be heavy indeed.”

“…Yes?”

“And to be falsely accused today, without proper evidence, must have wounded you deeply. Naturally so. Your noble pride itself has been injured.”

“…?”

The praise had become so thick that Ah'Heman finally felt uneasy.

Akwilla raised her voice so that everyone could hear.

“I will now give my old friend, my treasured companion, and the spiritual teacher of every young person gathered here a chance to defend his innocence!”

She swept one arm toward the crowd.

“A chance to defend his honor!”

Her eyes returned to Ah'Heman.

“A chance to defend his pride!”

Then she shouted—

“Does anyone object?”

“NO!”

Every warrior who had already understood Akwilla's intention answered at once.

Vikir stepped forward without expression.

“…?”

Ah'Heman instinctively took a step back.

He still did not understand what was happening.

Akwilla's voice turned firm.

“Make way.”

She stared directly at the old shaman.

“If you truly are innocent, this opportunity will let you protect the honor and pride you have built over so many years. Surely you would not refuse such an arrangement?”

“…What opportunity are you talking about?”

Ah'Heman asked cautiously.

Anxiety flickered in his eyes.

Akwilla answered.

“I order an **Illiad**.”

The Illiad was Ballak's traditional answer to a dispute neither side would yield on.

The two sides settled the matter through force.

The winner's will decided the outcome.

Ah'Heman's face turned white.

Vikir, on the other hand, smiled quietly.

Evidence.

Proof.

None of it had ever been the real objective.

Everything until now had merely been the road leading to this duel.
