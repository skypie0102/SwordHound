# Chapter 365: Ghost Castle of the Ultra-Deep Sea (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/365.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 363 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Nouvelle Vague, BDISSEM, and Garam Nord. The Level Nine prisoner names remain source-local witness forms pending direct Fandom access.

The admission ceremony ended.

Bodies of dead prisoners—

were thrown into the volcanic abyss like garbage.

Because none of them had been formally **executed**—

surface records would continue listing them as alive and incarcerated.

Nobody would investigate.

Nobody would care.

Even after death—

they would remain prisoners on paper forever.

The survivors—

received floor and cell assignments.

Vikir had been sentenced to more than three thousand life terms.

And throughout the search—

health examination—

and disinfection—

he had remained almost unnaturally calm.

So the result was obvious.

**Level Nine.**

CLANG.

A narrow solitary cell closed around him.

Level Nine—

by principle—

was single occupancy.

The cell itself was surprisingly plain.

Black brick.

A perfect stone cube.

Bars made from BDISSEM—

the material that suppressed both mana and physical force.

“This is why even digging with a spoon is impossible.”

The bars were narrow.

And BDISSEM covered more than the front.

Ceiling.

Walls.

Floor.

Escape was almost meaningless anyway.

Even if someone somehow dug through—

outside waited the sea floor—

ten thousand meters beneath the surface.

Drip.

Drip.

Cold water seeped through gaps in the stone.

Highly concentrated brine.

The air was wet enough to cling to skin.

Dark.

Cold.

Damp.

The atmosphere filled not only Vikir’s cell—

but the entire corridor.

There was nothing inside.

No bedding.

No bowl.

No furniture.

Only empty cubic space.

Vikir evaluated it.

“Not bad.”

Compared with battlefields where he had fought demons without rest—

this was almost comfortable.

Then—

BOOM.

A heavy gate far down the corridor sounded.

CREAAAAK.

A door so massive—

more than a hundred guards were required to open it.

Prisoners entered.

They had just returned from forced labor.

“Huff…”

“Ghk…”

“Uuugh…”

Their condition was terrible.

Burned.

Exhausted.

Half-dead.

Vikir remembered the information he already knew.

Every prisoner in Nouvelle Vague—

from Level One through Level Nine—

was sent to the labor site at dawn.

The work—

digging farther into the dormant volcano beneath the prison.

An expansion project.

Nouvelle Vague was built inside an old volcano.

Old—

but not cold.

The geothermal heat trapped inside had never truly disappeared.

So prisoners spent every day—

boiling.

Burning.

Drying out.

Digging.

“The new inmates begin work tomorrow.”

A voice beside Vikir’s cell.

Garam Nord.

The lieutenant had appeared without Vikir noticing.

He walked alongside the line of returning prisoners.

“Wake-up at four in the morning.”

“Work until one in the afternoon.”

“Ten minutes for lunch.”

“Then work again until midnight.”

“Return to your cells.”

“One hour of prayer and repentance.”

“Bed at one.”

Three hours of sleep.

One meal.

Ten minutes to eat it.

Black bread.

Jerky.

Nothing more.

And the same brutal labor—

every day.

Garam’s voice echoed upward.

Nouvelle Vague’s vertical layout carried sound clearly.

Vikir listened to the waves of sound—

and reconstructed the prison around him.

*Huge cylinder.*

*Cells arranged around the walls in rings.*

*Worksite in the center—

deep below Level Nine.*

The strongest prisoners—

those near Level Nine—

worked deepest.

Breaking rock.

Digging farther down.

The weaker prisoners—

near Level One—

worked higher up.

Moving debris.

Carrying stone.

Each floor corresponded to a work zone.

The deeper the level—

the hotter.

The more dangerous.

The closer to Level One—

the cooler.

The safer.

Complicated in words.

Simple in structure.

Garam spoke again.

“You are performing the glorious labor of expanding the great Nouvelle Vague.”

“Work with pride.”

Expansion.

More specifically—

they were building something beneath Level Nine.

A new area.

**Level Ten.**

A massive underground construction project.

That explained why the deepest worksite sat beneath Level Nine.

“The new Level Ten will house criminals too dangerous even for Level Nine.”

“For your own safety—

cooperation is recommended.”

The logic was not entirely false.

Occasionally—

a prisoner too dangerous for Nouvelle Vague to control—

went on a rampage.

And the first people to die—

were usually other prisoners.

So from the weaker prisoners’ perspective—

the sooner the truly dangerous inmates could be moved farther down—

the better.

Vikir nodded.

*There really are plenty of monsters here that exceed normal standards.*

He knew several Level Nine inmates by reputation.

**Cystus Ent Megidio.**

**Lord Griffin.**

**Aurora of Sleeping Beauty.**

**Snowwhite of the Assassin.**

**Cinderella of the Glass Heel.**

**Marquis de Sade.**

Each was said to possess enough power—

to destroy a nation alone.

Most had received more than five thousand life sentences.

Even Level Nine—

apparently—

was not enough to contain them forever.

Garam looked toward Vikir.

“One more warning.”

“Refuse labor—

or resist guard control—

and you’ll be sent to solitary.”

“Don’t assume nothing exists below Level Nine.”

Vikir nodded slowly.

The “solitary cell” Garam meant—

was not this ordinary Level Nine room.

*Solitary confinement.*

*A place people rarely survive.*

*And supposedly—

only one prisoner in all of Nouvelle Vague can endure it.*

Memories from before regression stirred.

Then—

“…Huh?!”

“They’re coming!”

“Move!”

“Get out of the way unless you want to be eaten!”

Lower-level prisoners returning from labor—

suddenly scattered.

Even the guards tightened their grips on their weapons.

Beyond the open gate—

shadows appeared.

Small.

Large.

Some—

monstrously huge.

The **Level Nine prisoners**.

Returning from the deepest part of the worksite.

Everyone stared.

Vikir—

did not.

He turned his head toward Garam.

“Hey.”

“Do you have a newspaper?”

Garam blinked.

“…A newspaper?”

“Why would a prisoner ask for something that valuable?”

“Newspapers are valuable?”

“I didn’t know.”

“Anything containing information from the outside world is valuable here.”

“Especially newspapers.”

“Even guards rarely see them.”

“Only the warden—

and a few privileged people—

have regular access.”

Outside information.

Rare.

Dangerous.

Precious.

At least—

inside Nouvelle Vague.

Vikir clicked his tongue.

“Then guards are prisoners too.”

Garam put both hands on his hips.

He looked irritated.

But he did not draw his three-section staff.

Instead—

he thought:

*That attitude won’t survive tomorrow.*

Even demons locked in Level Nine—

eventually broke beneath Nouvelle Vague’s labor.

The prison shared its name with the volcano that once burned hotter than any other.

It was dormant now.

But the heat trapped beneath it—

was still hellish.

Starting tomorrow—

Vikir would work in the deepest—

hottest—

most dangerous part.

A place where even Level Nine monsters—

sometimes died.
