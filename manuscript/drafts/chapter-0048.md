# Chapter 48: The Husband Hunt (3)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/048.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 48 is an aligned secondary witness only. Established terminology controls **Ballak Tribe**, **Camus Morgue**, **Adolf Morgue**, **Staffordshire Baskerville**, **Highbro / Middlebro / Lowbro**, **Sword Graduator**, and **Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain**. Raw-layer nephew/masculine Camus references are normalized to her established female identity. Camus's captivity is rendered nonsexually and without embellishment.

*I've seen her somewhere before.*

Vikir was certain he had met the female warrior in front of him.

There was no time to search his memory.

THUD!

One of her long legs whipped toward Vikir's abdomen.

The impact sent a numb shock down his spine.

The weight behind the kick was astonishing.

No ordinary body could produce that kind of force without aura.

Vikir flipped several times through the air and landed on his feet.

*She's strong.*

The Ballak woman in front of him was no ordinary warrior.

Her face was covered in charcoal ash, making her difficult to identify precisely, but she looked somewhere between her late teens and early twenties.

Fighting her while continuing to hide his true strength would be difficult.

Vikir lowered his stance and prepared to fight seriously.

The woman spoke first.

“Didn't we say we'd meet again?”

Her Imperial was broken.

That was enough.

Vikir finally remembered her.

She was the feral girl who had been locked in a cage like a beast at the illegal slave auction in Underdog City.

The nobles there had intended to butcher and consume her.

Vikir had overturned the auction, opened her cage, and helped her escape.

He had even given her medicine before letting her go.

“This is how you repay a favor?”

The Ballak girl shook her head.

“Me. Enslaved. Revenge. Morgue. Not you.”

So her capture by the slave traders had been connected, at least in her mind, to Morgue.

That was not impossible.

Morgue's development of the ruby mines had kept the family in constant conflict with the mountain tribes.

This large raid appeared to be retaliation for the fact that she had nearly been enslaved and sold.

Vikir narrowed his eyes.

*Then she's fairly important among the Ballak.*

The woman gestured with her chin toward a wolf in the distance.

Camus was bound to its side by the noose.

“You hurt us. We return hurt. She. Future leader.”

Ballak and Morgue.

An exchange of retaliation between enemies.

The woman swept her bow through the air, signaling the wolves around her to fall back.

Retreat.

The Ballak warriors began withdrawing almost at once. Capturing Camus had apparently accomplished their objective.

The woman hesitated for a moment, then looked at Vikir.

“If you want your woman back, follow me.”

“...So you can speak Imperial?”

Vikir kept her talking in hopes of gaining another clue and buying a little time.

That was as far as the conversation went.

The Ballak woman turned her wolf and vanished into the jungle.

Vikir moved to pursue.

SWISH! SWISH! SWISH!

A rain of arrows forced him to stop.

*Should I reveal more of my strength?*

If he used everything he had hidden, he could probably force his way through the barrage.

But—

“Camus!”

Adolf charged onto the battlefield with reinforcements.

That made revealing his full power impossible.

Vikir retreated while avoiding the arrows.

Too many eyes were watching him.

“You bastards! Give me back my niece!”

Adolf's rage erupted in a large-scale spell that churned up the ground.

The Ballak warriors were fast and experienced.

They evaded the spell, crossed the water, and disappeared into the jungle while their rear guard scattered in multiple directions to confuse pursuit.

In the end, the raiders escaped into the trees almost intact, leaving only a handful of casualties behind.

“This... This is absurd!”

Adolf stood there in disbelief.

The mages who had arrived with him looked much the same.

Less than three minutes had passed between Morgue detecting the intrusion and mounting a response.

Yet the Ballak raid had been so swift and methodical that the fortress had still suffered severe damage.

The attackers had come prepared, and Camus's lack of real battlefield experience had made the situation worse.

Damage to crops and slaves could be replaced.

The abduction of a leading candidate to inherit Morgue could not.

“What are we supposed to do now!?”

Adolf stamped his foot as he looked toward the sinking sun.

The flooded jungle of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain was dangerous even in daylight.

After dark, it became far worse.

Entering it at night bordered on suicide. Even Morgue's mages hesitated to pursue.

One mistake could wipe out the entire rescue force.

Then—

“We can still catch them.”

Vikir stepped forward.

He stared into the dark flooded forest.

“I went in there when I was a child.”

That was misleading at best.

At eight, Vikir had only entered the region briefly.

But before his regression, he had conducted countless searches, scouting missions, and punitive expeditions in these mountains.

He knew the terrain almost by heart.

The Morgue mages stared at him as though he had lost his mind.

One finally spoke.

“Going into Le Rouge et Le Noir after dark is suicide—”

He never finished.

“Anyone who doesn't want to go can remain here. There will be no punishment afterward.”

Adolf Morgue stood before Vikir with bloodshot eyes.

He had already realized that there was far more to Vikir than his age suggested.

After a moment, Adolf bent until they were at eye level.

GRAB!

He seized Vikir's hand in both of his.

“Please. Help me save my niece.”

Vikir nodded.

The raiders' ropes had originally been aimed at him.

Camus had been taken because she pushed him out of the way.

A debt had to be repaid.

Without another word, Vikir entered the jungle tangled with countless roots.

*This may also be my chance to slip out from under Hugo's control.*

A crisis often concealed an opportunity.

Vikir had already begun considering how this one might serve him.

Darkness settled over the mountain.

◆◆◆

The flooded jungle was filled with monsters, poisonous plants, and terrain that functioned like a collection of natural traps.

Bone-sucking mosquitoes.

Thorny vines carrying lethal venom.

Spiders that moved without a sound.

Narrow ravines hidden beneath fallen leaves and mud.

An ordinary person could not survive here for more than a few hours.

Even a powerful warrior was not safe.

And at night, the darkness grew so deep that even the tribes avoided traveling whenever possible.

Creatures that slept during the day awakened after sunset.

No wonder even Morgue's bravest warriors had been reluctant to enter.

Yet one figure moved through all of those dangers at astonishing speed.

Vikir Van Baskerville.

He burned certain leaves to drive away poisonous insects, stepped over ravines hidden beneath the mud, and rubbed animal dung on his clothes to mask his scent.

Every movement was practiced.

The Morgue and Baskerville warriors following him could only stare.

“...Are all Baskerville children like this?” Adolf asked.

“Of course not. The Deputy Magistrate is exceptional,” Staffordshire replied with a shrug.

Vikir, walking at the front, remained perfectly calm.

*These were ordinary tracking methods during the Age of Destruction.*

Swordsmen in that era had not been allowed the luxury of knowing only swordsmanship.

Foraging. Scouting. Tracking. Concealment. Ambush. Assassination. Medicine. Treatment. Cooking.

To survive, they had learned everything they could.

SWISH!

Vikir cut through a rotting log blocking the path.

Dense Sword Graduator aura vibrated rapidly along the blade and sliced through the obstruction in an instant.

Large obstacles or tiny signs of danger, Vikir found and removed them all himself.

Everyone behind him was genuinely impressed.

Especially the Baskerville triplets.

“Cool.”

“Cool.”

“Delicious.”

Lowbro's speech still occasionally went strange after his right index finger had once been severed, but the three of them clearly meant the same thing.

Then—

STOP.

Vikir suddenly halted.

He studied bare footprints left in wet mud, checked the direction of the wind, then crouched behind a thick bush and suppressed his presence.

The Morgue and Baskerville warriors followed him into cover.

A faint light glimmered ahead.

Ballak hunters were gathered around a small fire.

RUSTLE. CRACK.

At night, even the smallest human sound seemed as loud as thunder.

The Ballak had also scattered dry leaves and twigs around their temporary camp so that approaching footsteps would betray themselves.

Vikir briefly considered how to cross without making noise.

Then—

“...Silence.”

Adolf spoke a short incantation and made a compact gesture.

A translucent layer of mana spread beneath everyone's feet.

Their footsteps vanished completely.

This was magic.

More specifically, the support magic in which Adolf excelled.

Vikir had once worked as an assassin. He understood exactly how absurd an advantage it was to approach a target without producing a sound.

Soon, the pursuers were close enough to see the camp clearly.

The Ballak hunters rested, completely unaware that they had become the hunted.

Vikir used the remaining firelight to identify the figures ahead.

At the front was the same woman who had blocked his sword and kicked him earlier.

She was clearly leading the group, issuing orders to the others.

The man who had fired the poisoned dart and thrown the noose earlier followed her instructions while muttering complaints.

And at the center of the Ballak warriors was their objective.

Camus Morgue.

She was kneeling naked beside a wooden stake, wrists and ankles bound behind her and a collar around her neck.

Fortunately, there were no signs that she had been beaten or otherwise abused.

Camus was still cursing the Ballak warriors without the slightest trace of fear.

Vikir let out a quiet sigh.

*Why do I keep seeing her naked?*

Just then—

GRIP.

Someone placed a hand on Vikir's shoulder.

Adolf Morgue stood behind him with a rigid expression.

“I will never forget this favor, nephew-in-law.”

The form of address was strange, but this was hardly the time to correct him.

Morgue's second-highest political figure and one of its greatest Sixth-Circle mages was about to show what he could really do.
