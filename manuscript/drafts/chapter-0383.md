**BDISSEM.**

The mysterious material that suppressed the mana and physical strength of Nouvelle Vague prisoners.

Vikir had long suspected it might be plant-derived.

The reason—

one title.

**Blood Daylily Lumberjack.**

A Hell Tree title that inflicted enormous additional damage against plant-form enemies.

It also prolonged certain status effects.

Taunt.

Confusion.

Blindness.

Bleeding.

Burning.

Poison.

And others.

Restraints nobody else could break—

had become breakable under that title.

Now—

Vikir knew his guess had been correct.

The girl before him outranked every guard he had met.

Souaré.

D'Ordume.

Both colonels.

This one—

brigadier general.

**BDISSEM.**

The being who produced the substance carrying the same name.

Her appearance resembled a young girl.

Her true nature—

something much older.

Ancient plant power.

Ancient cunning.

An **Ent**.

A **Dryad**.

If beastfolk existed between animals and humanity—

Ents existed between plants and humanity.

*As expected.*

*The Five Wardens are all nonhuman humanoids.*

D'Ordume—

saltwater-crocodile beastfolk.

Souaré—

elf and dwarf blood.

And BDISSEM—

a dryad.

Meanwhile—

BDISSEM stared at Vikir with fascination.

“So you’re the one.”

“The one breaking the restraints I make.”

Apparently—

she could feel when one was destroyed.

Whoosh.

Her five fingers elongated.

Vines.

Then blackened.

Hardened.

Became chains.

“Show me.”

“How do you break them?”

Chains flew like snakes.

CLANG!

CLANG!

CLANG!

Handcuffs and shackles snapped at their ends like jaws.

If they caught Vikir—

mana and physical strength would be sealed immediately.

He could break free.

Still—

even one second of interrupted mana flow was unpleasant.

So he moved backward.

Dodged.

Again.

Again.

BDISSEM grew irritated.

“Fight properly!”

“Take me seriously!”

She misunderstood.

She thought Vikir’s refusal to counterattack meant condescension.

Creak.

Bark rubbed against bark inside her body.

Dense, elastic vines whipped toward Vikir.

The initial strike was dangerous.

The entanglement afterward—

worse.

“This is all Orca’s fault!”

“That old man makes everyone overprotect me!”

“They treat me like a child!”

“Why does Flubber get different treatment?!”

Vikir did not understand every detail.

But he understood the frustration.

He continued dodging.

And deliberately—

made it worse.

“Call your guardian.”

“You’re too young for this.”

“What?!”

“I’m over a hundred years older than you!”

“I don’t fight women.”

“Leave.”

“I’m both male and female!”

“….”

Then why—

exactly—

was everyone so protective?

Vikir thought.

Then spoke.

“You’re an important resource for Nouvelle Vague.”

“You should take care of yourself.”

“What happens if production or maintenance of BDISSEM restraints stops?”

“…!”

“And if you die—

do all existing restraints lose power?”

“…!”

“…!”

Her face answered before her mouth could.

BDISSEM became furious.

“So what?!”

“Stop telling me to protect myself!”

“I’m sick of hearing it!”

“I need freedom too!”

Apparently—

BDISSEM spent most of life confined.

Protected.

Maintained.

Used as the source of prison restraints.

Vikir understood the pressure better than most.

He had lived as a prisoner here.

He knew how Nouvelle Vague crushed people.

He evaded another whip.

Flipped.

Landed on the far side of the aquarium.

Then asked:

“If you are guarded so closely—

why are you here alone?”

“Hmph.”

“Because Orca left.”

Warden Orca.

The actual head of Nouvelle Vague.

Vikir had never seen him once since imprisonment.

“Things on the surface are apparently a mess.”

“So many major criminals are being sent down here that the old man went topside for a long assignment.”

“Something about responding to a huge ‘event.’”

“No idea.”

BDISSEM had spent too long isolated.

She talked constantly.

Even with Vikir barely responding.

“Demons.”

“Gates.”

“All kinds of nonsense lately.”

“The surface sounds complicated.”

“…!”

Information.

Vikir suppressed the impatience rising inside him.

*Demons and gates.*

*The Age of Destruction is close.*

Expected.

Still dangerous.

He could not save everyone.

Only those he refused to leave behind.

Great Demons.

Gate of Destruction.

Long Rain of Terror.

Poseidon.

Nouvelle Vague.

The pieces connected.

Then Vikir moved.

“First—

the enemy in front of me.”

BOOM.

Beelzebub awakened in his hand.

The demonic sword—

unused for a long time—

shone crimson.

There was no need for Sword Master solid aura.

Hiss.

Instead—

Vikir converted everything into gaseous Expert-class aura.

Spread it.

BDISSEM scoffed.

“What?”

“Expert?”

“That weak attack—”

Then froze.

Gas was weak compared with solid aura.

But scale—

was another matter.

Vikir’s gaseous aura spread like mist covering an entire mountain.

Far beyond anything an ordinary Expert could create.

CRACK.

CRACK.

Anchors split.

Cables snapped.

Every particle of aura—

a cutting edge.

*Solid aura is better for one opponent.*

*Gaseous aura is better for controlling an enormous field.*

Vikir did not intend to kill BDISSEM.

Now that he understood what she was—

her usefulness was obvious.

*Keep her alive.*

He had experienced her power firsthand.

Enough to know how to handle it.

So the cutting fog spread.

Not to destroy.

To herd.

A basic technique of hunting hounds.

“Ugh!”

BDISSEM looked around.

Every escape route—

filled with cutting mist.

Only one remained.

The aquarium.

The saltwater pools holding the Gulper Eels.

Splash.

She dove.

Vikir thought—

*Good.*

*Plants hate salt.*

Ordinary vegetation exposed to excess salt blackened at the tips—

then withered.

Dryads—

plant-dependent beings—

should be similar.

Dense.

Concentrated.

Almost sticky with salinity.

Splash.

Vikir jumped after her.

Several Gulper Eels approached.

His aura tore them apart.

*Where?*

He searched for a weakened BDISSEM.

Then—

“Hehehe.”

“Looking for me?”

A woman’s voice.

Seductive.

Close.

WHOOSH!

Vines—

far thicker than before—

Much faster underwater.

Hair floating.

Body changed.

gone.

Mature.

Powerful.
