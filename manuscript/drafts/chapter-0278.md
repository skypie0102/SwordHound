# Chapter 278: Money War (6)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/278.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 276 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Night Hound, Beelzebub, House Bourgeois, Damien Bourgeois, Baskerville, Hugo, Sword Graduator, and the Grave of Swords. Bahamut and the Submerged Deep-Sea Fish style remain source-local pending direct Fandom access.

Vikir had deliberately touched Damien’s most sensitive wound.

“…Seeing your dead daughter again.”

No life was flawless.

For Damien—

that flaw had a name.

**Juliet.**

His daughter.

Dead young.

Publicly—

people said she had been caught in a scandal and drank poison from shame.

The subject was Damien’s reverse scale.

His one forbidden point.

As expected—

he attacked.

FLASH.

The golden stiletto came fast.

Vikir felt a flicker of admiration.

*So this is the strength of the tycoon house.*

Baskerville had swords.

Donquixote—

spears.

Usher—

bows.

Leviathan—

poison.

Morgue—

magic.

Quovadis—

divine power.

House Bourgeois seemed different.

Its defining weapon—

was money.

That enormous wealth was what made it one of the Seven Great Houses.

But that did not mean Bourgeois lacked military strength.

Quite the opposite.

If one believed money could buy anything—

then money could also buy training.

Weapons.

Techniques.

Bloodlines.

Teachers.

Elixirs.

House Bourgeois had purchased swords.

Spears.

Bows.

Shields.

Magic.

It bought promising mercenary groups.

Absorbed smaller martial families.

Arranged marriages with talented houses.

Then raised second—

third—

fourth—

and fifth generations from those mixed bloodlines.

A child with sword talent—

received sword training.

A child with magical talent—

received magical training.

A child without obvious talent—

received tutors and elixirs until something usable emerged.

House Bourgeois used money to collect strengths from everywhere.

Damien was one result.

His inherited talent—

combined with expensive training from childhood—

made the golden stiletto dangerous.

But—

CLANG!

Beelzebub deflected it.

Red blade.

Hidden presence.

Impossible strength.

Damien’s expression shattered.

“…N-Night Hound?”

Vikir was surprised too.

*Stronger than expected.*

The ornate weapon was not decoration.

And Damien’s technique—

was familiar.

An old sword style.

An ancestor of Baskerville swordsmanship.

**Submerged Deep-Sea Fish Style.**

Strictly speaking—

not fully Baskerville.

It came from the old **Bahamut** family—

one of the ancient iron-blooded sword houses that Baskerville later absorbed.

*Fish scales woven between hound fangs.*

*This is from before the two systems fully fused.*

*A restricted military technique.*

*Did Bourgeois buy this too?*

Damien’s version still had flaws.

Baskerville had never absorbed everything from Bahamut.

Different foundations could not simply become identical.

Baskerville had taken the useful parts—

like a dog eating the flesh of a fish and spitting out the bones.

Forcing the remaining systems together created friction.

*Hugo only fully completed the fusion much later.*

*So the version Damien learned still carries weaknesses.*

Vikir leaned back.

The stiletto missed.

Damien immediately recovered.

Attacked again.

Smooth lines—

like deep-sea fish scales.

Sharp points—

like hound teeth.

PFFT.

PFFT.

PFFT.

The curtain behind Vikir filled with holes.

Damien stared through them.

Then—

“…?”

Vikir was gone.

*Where?*

Damien closed his eyes.

Extended mana outward.

A searching web.

Nothing.

Only wind.

Close to his ear.

Damien spun.

“There!”

Vikir stood—

seemingly in midair.

A nearly invisible strand of Baby Madam’s silk ran beneath his feet.

Damien attacked again.

Brown aura flared around the broken-gold light of his weapon.

This time—

the structure was more recognizably Baskerville.

A fourth-form technique.

Sword Graduator aura.

Lethal.

But Vikir knew Baskerville swordsmanship too well.

*Too shallow.*

A deep-sea fish could not swim properly in shallow water.

Vikir caught the stiletto.

Forced it down.

Then stepped on it.

CRACK!

The blade shattered.

The aura burst apart like droplets.

“…!”

Damien retreated immediately.

Night Hound’s reputation was already terrifying.

A criminal strong enough to kill upper-level Graduators.

A threat often mentioned beside Ms. Ouroboros.

Damien knew one-on-one combat was a losing proposition.

He moved backward without showing his back.

Still fighting.

Still using the broken half-blade.

Again—

Baskerville movement.

Again—

Vikir read it.

A hound’s fang flying at someone who had spent a lifetime studying hound fangs.

Easy to catch.

Easy to swallow.

CLANG!

Vikir knocked away the final weapon.

Damien’s eyes widened.

*Of course he’s surprised.*

This was a technique Vikir trusted completely.

A level of handling even Cane Corso in the Grave of Swords had recognized.

The broken stiletto landed behind Damien.

He stood unarmed.

Vikir remembered one of Hugo’s rare long lectures.

*Rich people always say the same thing.*

*“Money is power.”*

*But when people say “A is B,” they’re usually trying to elevate A to the value of B.*

*“Time is money.”*

*“Silence is golden.”*

*If they were truly equal, who would trade time or silence for gold?*

*Everyone knows which side is worth more.*

*So the people who say “money is power” already understand the truth.*

*Money is below power.*

Hugo had disliked Bourgeois enough to become unusually talkative.

*If Hugo knew Damien had learned Baskerville’s fourth form—*

*there might actually be a war.*

Vikir stepped forward.

Grabbed Damien by the face with one hand.

Then slammed him to the floor.

THUD!

Money might become power.

But in a direct hierarchy—

power came first.

Wealth looked fragile when pure force stripped away the systems surrounding it.

Damien lay on the floor.

Eyes wide.

Then—

slowly—

he closed them.

No screaming.

No resistance.

No plea.

Vikir spoke through the mask.

“No begging?”

“No threats?”

“….”

“Most wealthy people tell me they’ll pay.”

“Or say I’ll regret touching them.”

Damien waited a moment.

Then answered:

“My back hurts.”

“I can’t talk.”

“….”

“Joking.”

Even now—

he remained strangely composed.

The calm of someone who had survived enough dangerous situations to recognize when panic would accomplish nothing.

Damien looked up.

“From the eyes behind that mask—

you are not someone persuasion will move.”

“You have a clear purpose.”

“….”

“If you intend to kill me—

do it.”

“There is no negotiation with a terrorist.”

Vikir shook his head.

“You read me well.”

“But not completely.”

“I never came here to kill you.”

Damien did not answer.

Vikir continued.

“You—

on the other hand—

seem to want death.”

For the first time—

Damien looked genuinely blank.

His eyes drifted toward the night sky.

One bright star.

Far.

Too far.

Its light seemed unable to reach him.

Then Damien spoke dryly.

“You read me well.”

“Completely.”
