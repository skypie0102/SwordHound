Hugo Le Baskerville stood before the Gate of Destruction.

His body—

now transformed into that of a Death Knight—

was covered in pitch-black armor forged from hellfire.

The dark-crimson aura he had once wielded had turned completely black.

His pledged life receded like an outgoing tide.

Skin became pale and bluish.

The colors of his eyes inverted—

as though the horizon between life and death itself had turned over.

A contract.

Life—

in exchange for power.

In the final battle—

Hugo had driven every drop of mana in his body past its limit.

And finally crossed the threshold he had gazed toward for so long.

The Ninth Form.

BOOOOM!

Hellfire from the Gate of Destruction collided with the aura of one Death Knight.

Even Balmung—

a sword famed throughout the world—

could not endure the recoil.

The blade slowly began to melt.

That Balmung.

Sharp.

Hard.

A crystallization colder and sharper than almost anything else in the world.

Slowly—

hotly—

it melted.

Collapsed.

Vikir watched.

And before he could stop himself—

asked:

“…Why?”

The question came out heavy.

Slow.

Like a piece of metal dropped into a furnace.

And because the voice was so heavy—

the faint tremor inside it was impossible to miss.

For the first time—

Vikir asked something because he genuinely wanted the answer.

Absurd paternal love?

A false sense of responsibility?

A small amount of guilt?

Belated atonement?

What—

exactly—

had made Hugo choose this?

Vikir left most of the question unsaid.

Hugo did not turn around.

As always—

he simply faced forward.

Walked forward.

His broad back slowly dissolved into the blinding light.

Then—

a faint voice reached Vikir.

Distant.

Almost lost in the roar.

“…I don’t know either.”

Vikir froze.

*There are things even he doesn’t know?*

The thought came almost automatically.

He stared at Hugo’s back.

Confused.

Estranged.

Even after living more than one life—

even after becoming older in experience than the man before him—

Vikir still could not understand him.

Could not read his heart.

Hugo’s back moved farther away.

Broad.

Steady.

Then disappeared into blackness and light.

SNAP.

Vikir’s vision cleared.

Black sky.

Red dimensional gate.

The first catastrophic blast from the Gate of Destruction—

gone.

Everyone in Tochka had seen the man who stopped it.

The supreme swordsman of the Iron-Blooded Sword House.

Master of all Baskerville hounds.

And in truth—

a hound who himself had spent his whole life chained.

**Hugo Le Baskerville.**

Age sixty-four.

Burned away on the battlefield.

A very Baskerville death.

There was no better description.

Even Orca and Marquis de Sade stood speechless.

“…That was Baskerville Ninth Form.”

“So someone really reached that realm.”

“Cane Corso wasn’t senile after all.”

It was overwhelming.

Tragic.

Magnificent.

Hugo’s final strike left everyone inside Tochka with their teeth clenched and their hearts heavy.

The fortress survived.

So did Vikir.

“….”

Vikir watched pale ash scatter into the wind.

White residue—

the remains of complete combustion.

He could not know with certainty whether it was Hugo.

But somehow—

he felt that it was.

Still—

Hugo’s sacrifice had not ended the crisis.

RUMBLE!

The Gate of Destruction remained suspended in the sky.

The first firestorm was gone.

The Long Rain of Terror—

was not.

Tiny red sparks began to fall from the gate.

Patter.

Patter.

Patter.

Like sparks thrown from clashing iron.

Then more.

And more.

Until countless red drops rained down.

“It’s fire!”

Someone among the refugees screamed.

They were right.

Fire fell from the sky.

Every drop ignited what it touched.

No one could stand beneath it.

The apocalypse had begun casting its shadow from Tochka outward.

People screamed.

Refugees fled from the walls.

Tents.

Wooden poles.

Everything flammable became fuel.

Red smoke rose from the ground.

“…It’s over.”

“We can’t stop that.”

“Heh.”

“So this is how we burn.”

Orca and Sade looked upward helplessly.

Then—

near Tudor—

Shammua wiped a drop from her face.

“Huh?”

There was no pain.

No despair.

Only surprise.

Then delight.

“This is water.”

Tudor and Bianca turned.

“What?”

“That isn’t water!”

“It’s fire!”

“Get under the stone wall!”

Shammua looked puzzled.

“No!”

“Big brother, big sister—”

“it really is water!”

She held out her hand.

A clear drop rested on her palm.

Ordinary rainwater.

“…What?”

“Huh?”

People who had been fleeing the fire looked upward.

The Gate of Destruction continued releasing burning red droplets.

But between them—

other drops were falling.

Water.

Sssssss—

Fire and water met in midair.

White steam burst across the sky.

The flames spreading across the ground began to weaken.

Then the rain thickened.

Harder.

Heavier.

Until—

SHHHHHHHHHHH!

It became a downpour.

“Rain!”

“It’s really raining!”

“Water!”

“Water is falling!”

Everyone stared upward.

The Gate of Destruction.

And above it—

thick storm clouds.

WOOOOOSH—

A powerful southeast wind drove the clouds directly over Tochka.

Rain became a torrent.

Water poured down the fortress walls.

The sudden storm was so violent—

it began overwhelming even the fire rain from the Gate.

Camus stared.

“I’ve never seen rain like this.”

Aiyen shook her head.

“Not even in the jungle.”

Dolores looked stunned.

“How can the timing be this perfect…?”

Sinclaire’s eyes widened.

“This solves the drinking-water problem too.”

Kirko looked upward.

“The climate above ground really is unpredictable.”

No refugee in Tochka had ever experienced a storm like this.

Sssssssss—

The burning Gate of Destruction began to cool.

A rainfall powerful enough to suppress even the Long Rain of Terror.

A flood sweeping away the flames that had barely begun to spread.

“Everyone inside the fortress!”

Orca gave the order.

Tochka stood on a high plateau made of hard rock.

Floodwater could not reach it easily.

The ground itself—

huge stone masses packed with sand—

drained quickly.

A fortress built to endure enormous floods.

One that fire and water both seemed to avoid.

Dolores stopped.

“…Like an Ark.”

Then—

realization.

*Fire and water alone will avoid this place.*

*Only here will true salvation be found.*

The false myth Dolores herself had spread.

And the man who had ordered its creation long ago.

*The Great Flood will come.*

*Prepare the Ark.*

*Endure.*

*That is all I can ask.*

*Wait a little longer and everything will resolve itself.*

*I promise.*

A man who had built the foundation here long before anyone understood why.

Who endured the lack of water.

The demons.

The siege.

And kept insisting that salvation would come.

Vikir.

He stood at the front of the fortress—

watching the Gate of Destruction.

Most raindrops evaporated before reaching it.

But the rain behind them kept falling.

Again.

Again.

Again.

The heat lost ground.

The gate cooled rapidly.

Vikir looked toward the southeast.

A familiar constellation hung there.

Seven guiding stars.

A navigation marker used since ancient times.

Except now—

there were eight.

RUMBLE!

The eighth star shone brighter than all the rest through the storm.

Vikir stared at it.

“…Poseidon.”

All that suffering in Nouvelle Vague—

had finally paid off.

The Long Rain of Terror.

One hundred fifty days of fire.

And almost simultaneously—

the Great Flood.

One hundred fifty days of rain.

The flood completely cooled the furnace sustaining the Gate of Destruction.

Its mana fluctuations weakened.

The summoning formation faded.

The Gate of Destruction stopped.

The Age of Destruction—

would not begin.

Vikir looked down.

At the dead Poison Humans.

At the plains below—

now becoming an ocean.

Then gave one final order.

“Tudor.”

Tudor raised his spear.

Ready.

Vikir spoke immediately.

“We need House Donquixote’s fleet.”

Everyone nearby understood where this was going.

The Imperial Capital.

The Emperor’s city.

The center of the world—

where so many of them had spent their youth.

And there—

the final enemy.

The First Corpse.

The last demon.
