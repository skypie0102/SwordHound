# Chapter 271: Rich Friends (2)

The next morning—

Vikir received unexpected news from the dorm supervisor.

“Student Vikir.”

“You have a visitor.”

Vikir stopped packing his bag.

An unscheduled visit.

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, Bianca, and Sinclaire were all heading toward first period.

Every one of them looked over.

Vikir almost never talked about his family.

His childhood.

Where he came from.

Or who belonged in his life.

“Someone came to see you?”

“Of course Vikir has family, Tudor.”

“That’s rude.”

“Lucky!”

“You get to miss first period!”

“That’s unusual.”

“You’re normally so predictable.”

Sinclaire narrowed her eyes.

“Could it be…”

“that woman?”

Everyone turned.

Vikir looked blank.

Sinclaire continued.

“Remember when we were drinking on the roof?”

“An owl brought you a letter.”

“Was it from your girlfriend?”

Chaos.

“What?!”

“Vikir has a girlfriend?!”

“With his face, it would be stranger if he didn’t.”

“Traitor!”

“Someone outside the Academy?”

“Another school?”

“Did you meet her at the University League?”

Then—

“If you intend to make noise—

do not block the hallway.”

Professor Banshee Morgue stood behind them.

Her expression—

pure disgust.

“Student Vikir.”

“An unscheduled visitor requires a visitation confirmation showing the start and end times.”

“It also requires the dorm supervisor’s signature.”

“Otherwise, you will be marked absent.”

“You know this.”

“Yes.”

“You already lose too many conduct points.”

“So I’m reminding you.”

Banshee walked straight through the group.

“…Children.”

The contempt was obvious.

◆◆◆

Vikir entered the visitation room.

A woman sat cross-legged inside.

Black hair.

Red eyes.

Smoking.

“Long time no see~”

“My little brother~”

She waved brightly.

Vikir narrowed his eyes.

“When did I get an older sister?”

The woman ignored the dryness in his voice.

**Messinadnaro Cindywendy.**

She had changed her hair and eye color before coming to the Academy in person.

“This message was important enough that I became the owl myself.”

“How do I look?”

“Black hair and red eyes suit me, don’t they?”

“Ohhh~ little brother~ I missed you!”

Cindywendy grabbed Vikir’s cheeks.

Pinched.

Pulled.

*For the supervisor outside?*

Vikir played along.

“It’s good to see you, big sister.”

“Thank you for visiting.”

“Of~ course~”

“Big sister had to come see her adorable little brother~”

“Look how cute you’ve gotten~”

“…Don’t overdo it.”

“Fine.”

She finally released his face.

Vikir rubbed his reddened cheeks.

“How is the trade?”

“With the jungle tribes?”

“Smooth.”

“Three large-scale exchanges are already complete.”

“Good.”

“Then—”

Cindywendy already knew the next question.

She closed her eyes.

Shook her head.

“No trace yet.”

“…I see.”

Ballak.

The tribe that had vanished without explanation.

The complete loss of contact continued to bother Vikir.

But he could not simply abandon the Academy and cross the distant jungle immediately.

He changed subjects.

“What about the other request?”

“The sponsorships?”

“Done—or in progress.”

Before—

Vikir had given Cindywendy a list.

Artists.

Soldiers.

Mercenaries.

Students.

Small shopkeepers.

Low-ranking officers.

People with no obvious connection to Vikir.

Cindywendy had visited them one by one.

“If they had children, I arranged education support.”

“If they needed work or training, I set up help.”

“Some were struggling financially.”

“Some needed medical expenses for themselves or family.”

“Everything necessary has been handled.”

“Well done.”

Cindywendy tilted her head.

“But who are they?”

“I investigated.”

“You don’t know them.”

“Not one.”

That was true.

In this life—

Vikir had never met most of them.

Before regression—

they had been comrades.

Benefactors.

People who had helped him survive.

“…I hope I never meet them again.”

Vikir smiled bitterly.

Cindywendy watched him in silence.

Then—

thump.

She pushed a bag across the table.

Vikir opened it.

Gold bars.

A jewelry box.

“The military funds you asked for.”

“Only as much weight as you said you could carry.”

“Thank you.”

“Convert it at the designated black market.”

“Use it gradually.”

Vikir set the bag aside.

Then Cindywendy gave him the real reason she had come personally.

“The head of House Bourgeois is a demon.”

Vikir had expected it.

Hearing it from the investigator herself—

made it real.

“I understand.”

“That’s enough.”

Cindywendy frowned.

“That?”

“Against Bourgeois?”

“That amount of money is nothing.”

“Do you need more?”

Long ago—

Cindywendy had promised Vikir:

*Whatever you do, I’ll make sure you never lack money.*

She wanted to fulfill that promise now.

“Wasn’t this why you built me up?”

Money against money.

Cindywendy was ready for war with House Bourgeois.

Vikir shook his head.

“This is enough seed capital.”

Her eyes narrowed.

“Because of the source?”

“Yes.”

Bourgeois elites auctioned lunches and dinners.

But raw money alone was not enough.

They cared where it came from.

How it was earned.

Whether the buyer was interesting.

An unknown person carrying unexplained wealth—

would be rejected.

“The path by which the money is made must be credible.”

“And profitable enough to attract them.”

“That’s why I can’t simply use your fortune.”

“Not yet.”

“This isn’t the place to deploy you.”

Cindywendy shrugged.

“You really are impossible to understand.”

“You have a sponsor with this much money—

and never ask for it.”

“Unlike all the other annoying men.”

Vikir answered simply.

“Money is a means.”

“It cannot become the objective.”

Cindywendy went still.

“…My father used to say that.”

“He was a good merchant.”

Vikir nodded.

Bourgeois was a mountain.

But larger mountains waited behind it.

The blade called Cindywendy—

was not ready to be drawn yet.

Cindywendy still looked skeptical.

“Then how will you fight Bourgeois without my money?”

“I’m not refusing all help.”

“Then?”

“Instead of giving me fish—

teach me how to catch them.”

She blinked.

Vikir leaned forward.

“I have a plan.”

“There are holes in it.”

“I need you to identify them.”

“And fill the missing manpower and information.”

“…You want investment advice.”

“Yes.”

Vikir placed a flyer in front of her.

**<Mock Investment Competition>**

House Bourgeois.

All Academy students eligible.

A competition promising entry into the financial world.

Vikir tapped the paper.

“I’m going to use the money you brought—

and do this.”

Then he explained the investment strategy.

The one designed to make House Bourgeois notice him.

Cindywendy stared.

Then—

“…You’re insane.”

Her face twisted in disbelief.

“That’s fraud.”
