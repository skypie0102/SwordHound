Vikir had already stood to leave.

“…Then I’ll take my leave.”

Turned.

“Ah…”

Kirko opened her mouth.

Then stopped.

Black Tongue spoke first.

“You can go.”

“Your adjutant’s interrogation won’t take long.”

“Yes.”

Vikir left.

Kirko watched his back.

Expression complicated.

Then turned toward Black Tongue.

“…!”

His face was suddenly right in front of hers.

“Hehehe.”

“It’s been a long time.”

Black Tongue took Kirko’s hand.

Patted her shoulder.

“…?”

Kirko frowned.

She had never met him before.

More immediately—

his skin felt cold.

Elastic.

Wrong.

She leaned back.

“A pleasure to meet you for the first time, Lieutenant Colonel.”

“Hehehe.”

“There you go again.”

“Always talking coldly every time we meet.”

“I worked so hard to court you too.”

“…What are you talking about?”

“Today is the first time I’ve met you.”

“Awww.”

“Again?”

“All right.”

“Since you finally showed your face—

I’ll forgive you.”

“I missed you.”

“How could I forget that cute face?”

“You haven’t aged at all, we—”

The parasite-like muscles of Black Tongue’s face stretched.

“**Lieutenant Valentine Grimm.**”

Kirko froze.

Her lips barely moved.

“…My name is Kirko.”

“Captain Kirko Grimm.”

“Hm?”

Black Tongue looked genuinely blank.

Then hit his own head lightly.

“Ah.”

“My memory.”

“You’re Major Garam’s adjutant.”

“You look so much like her.”

“I mixed you up.”

“That was years ago…”

He flipped through the file.

“Yes.”

“Yes.”

“Promoted to Captain today.”

“Is Captain that easy to reach now?”

“….”

“I haven’t seen a guard rise that fast since D'Ordume and Souaré.”

“Major Garam is an exception.”

His gaze moved between Kirko and the papers.

“Twenty-two.”

“Birthplace…”

“What?”

“Nouvelle Vague?”

“You were born here?”

“Then your parents must have been guards.”

He rolled his eyes.

Thought.

Then slapped one knee.

“Ah!”

“No wonder you looked familiar.”

“You’re **their** child.”

Kirko bit her lip.

The incident.

Twenty-three years ago.

The story everyone had told her.

A prisoner attacked a guard.

A child was born.

Kirko.

But Black Tongue’s next words—

did not fit that history.

“Hehehe.”

“Yes.”

“There was a model couple inside Nouvelle Vague back then.”

“Everyone praised them.”

“Superiors were even discussing special parental leave.”

“…?”

Kirko tilted her head.

That made no sense.

Her birth was supposed to be a taboo.

A product of criminal violence.

Something shameful.

Black Tongue remembered something entirely different.

“Yes.”

“They used to hold hands in secret.”

“Two model junior guards.”

“Made me sick with envy.”

His voice became wet with strange desire.

“So I arrested them.”

“Military-discipline violation.”

“D'Ordume and Souaré were cracking down hard on prisoners at the time.”

“We needed the guards disciplined too.”

Kirko’s pupils trembled.

Before she could ask—

Black Tongue grinned.

“So we hurriedly invented a shocking incident.”

“Your parents.”

A male guard.

A female guard.

Two junior officers.

Secret lovers.

Their consensual relationship—

rewritten as a prisoner attacking a guard.

A grotesque story—

useful for deepening hatred between prisoners and staff.

Kirko’s voice became small.

“…My father wasn’t a prisoner?”

“My mother wasn’t assaulted?”

She had imagined her unknown parents countless times.

Hated a father whose name she did not know.

Pitied the mother whose name she did.

Now—

two facts replaced everything.

Her parents had loved each other.

And her mother had looked almost exactly like her.

“…Then I might have been loved.”

Kirko closed her eyes.

The feeling that entered her—

was relief.

“My father wasn’t evil.”

“My mother wasn’t only a victim.”

“I was just a terrible child.”

She no longer had to hate the man she had never met.

Or reduce the woman she had never met to pity.

For the first time—

something that had bound Kirko since birth loosened.

But one question remained.

“Where are they?”

Kirko’s gaze hardened.

“You took my parents.”

“They disappeared after that.”

“Why are you asking me?”

“Because you took them.”

“Hm?”

Black Tongue rolled his eyes.

Thinking.

Then—

“…Ah!”

He slapped his knee.

“That’s right!”

“I did take them!”

“I remember them protesting their innocence.”

He sounded delighted to recall it.

“But you’re saying they disappeared?”

“They never came back?”

“How strange.”

“Maybe they escaped.”

He rubbed his chin.

Then—

“Ah!”

“I remember!”

“I forgot to let them out.”

“I meant to interrogate them for a few days.”

“Then I got busy.”

“I forgot.”

“Where did I put them…?”

His eyes moved around the chamber.

Then stopped.

“There.”

Black Tongue pointed.

At the black sphere beneath Kirko.

The leech she had been using as a seat.

“Oh dear.”

“You’re sitting on her.”

“Your mother.”

A memory flashed.

*There’s probably someone inside this cushion too.*

*Probably.*

*Sit on the ground if you don’t like it.*

*…No.*

*I don’t mind.*

“Your mother might feel bad.”

“I really am sorry.”

“Then forgot them in a corner.”

“Completely.”

“Say hello.”

“Your mother and father.”

“My forgetfulness delayed the family reunion.”

“But at least you meet now.”
