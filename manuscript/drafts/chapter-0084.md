The Hound of the Night returned.

By the time Vikir reached Ballak village, Red Death had spread across the region.

Ballak had suffered less than many neighboring tribes.

Raised houses.

Flood barriers.

Drainage.

The preparations Vikir had ordered before the rains had mattered.

Elsewhere, entire communities were collapsing.

Ahun reached him first.

He looked thinner.

Exhausted.

The moment he saw Vikir, he grabbed him.

“You came back!”

Ahun had barely slept since Aheul became sick.

Barely eaten.

His life had become watching his only sister breathe.

He clutched Vikir's hand.

“Thank you.”

Tears ran down his face.

“You kept your word.”

His voice shook.

“Even if you couldn't find medicine, coming back is enough.”

Vikir looked at him.

They thought he had returned empty-handed.

He had not.

He had brought a miracle.

“Come.”

Ahun blinked.

Vikir pulled him toward the shelter near the village entrance.

Aheul lay inside.

Of all the infected Ballak, she was in the worst condition because she had been sick the longest.

Yet the area around her was clean.

No waste.

No foul smell.

Ahun had cared for her obsessively.

Vikir removed a small glass vial.

**Saintess's Tears.**

He did not even open it.

Light leaked through the glass.

Aheul's red spots began disappearing.

Her fever eased.

Her expression relaxed.

For the first time in days, she fell into peaceful sleep.

Ahun froze.

His hand trembled against his sister's face.

Then he looked at Vikir.

“Vikir.”

Again.

“Vikir.”

Again.

“Vikir!”

He repeated the name carefully and reverently, like a devout believer invoking a god, his voice full of trust, affection, and joy.

Soon the other warriors joined him.

The anxiety of the past days broke all at once.

“Vikir!”

“Vikir!”

The floating structures shook under their cheering.

Vikir remained calm.

One patient had recovered.

Many had not.

“How many?”

“About thirty, including Aheul.”

Manageable.

For now.

“What about the other tribes?”

Ahun's expression darkened.

“Bad.”

He swallowed.

“Some are nearly destroyed.”

The tribes that had ignored the advice about raised houses and flood preparation suffered worst.

Those that copied Ballak did better.

“How many sick?”

“I don't know.”

Ahun looked toward the jungle.

“Thousands.”

Vikir decided immediately.

“Bring them here.”

Ahun stared.

“All of them?”

“Yes.”

“Do we have enough medicine?”

“Enough.”

Vikir looked at the vial.

“And helping them benefits Ballak too.”

The young warriors understood.

Neighboring tribes fought.

Over prey.

Territory.

Marriage.

But they also shared blood, friendships, and common enemies.

When disaster threatened everyone, the jungle tribes could still move together.

Ballak hunters scattered to spread the news.

◆◆◆

Vikir watched the holy light inside the vial.

*This can change more than the plague.*

Baskerville's greatest long-term enemy was not another Great Clan.

It was the people of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Ballak.

Rokoko.

Other tribes scattered across the jungle.

People who ran barefoot across terrain that cut Imperial soldiers apart.

Archers who could hit a willow leaf from thousands of paces away.

Warriors who split giant beasts with axes and blades.

If Vikir could make those tribes owe him a debt—

if he could use that debt to bring the mountain tribes under his influence—

then he would gain an independent force capable of standing against Baskerville itself.

More importantly, stopping Red Death early prevented the tribal collapse that had strengthened Baskerville in his previous life.

Leviathan's crime was becoming Vikir's opportunity.

“Move quickly.”

He closed his hand around the vial.

“Before the death toll rises.”

He cured Ballak's remaining patients first.

Then went for the real target.

The water source.

A river beginning high in the mountains and spreading down through the jungle.

A lifeline shared by tribe after tribe.

Vikir knew the terrain after two years here.

The message spread ahead of him.

Ballak arrived.

Rokoko arrived—the shamanic tribe infamous for its cannibalistic customs.

Other tribes followed.

Even old enemies.

Among them were berserker tribes such as **Renaissance**, whose warriors were said to rival Imperial companies when armed with axes.

Akwilla led the gathering to the source.

Vikir stepped into the river.

“You can drink after this.”

He opened the vial.

A drop of **Saintess's Tears** entered the water.

Light spread.

First around his hand.

Then across the stream.

Then outward through the entire river.

The jungle glowed as though the Milky Way had fallen from the sky.

People knelt at the water.

Scooped it in their hands.

Drank.

The Red Death began disappearing.

A roar rolled across the jungle.

Akwilla laughed.

“Vikir!”

The Night Fox pointed at the Hound of the Night.

“I should make you Ballak's shaman!”

Every tribe had a different language.

Different gestures.

Different customs.

Their meaning was the same.

Gratitude.

Respect.

Love.

Former patients knelt.

Parents knelt.

Children knelt.

Elders who had once wanted Vikir killed bowed to him because their families were alive.

Vikir accepted the gratitude without celebration.

He was looking for someone.

*Where is she?*

He had heard Aiyen was sick.

Yet she was nowhere among the patients.

Then—

THUDTHUDTHUDTHUD.

Rapid footsteps.

Vikir recognized them before he turned.

Something hit him at full speed.

WHAM.

Aiyen wrapped both arms around him.

Hard.

His bones complained.

Vikir reinforced his body with mana before his lower back gave out.

He looked down.

Then lifted her face from his chest.

“I heard you had Red Death.”

Aiyen looked perfectly healthy.

No red spots.

No sweat.

Normal breathing.

Only a faint flush across her face.

Vikir frowned.

He flicked a trace of holy water onto her.

Nothing changed.

The blush remained.

Aiyen looked down at herself.

Then back up.

“……Huh.”

She scratched her head.

“Looks like I'm completely cured.”
