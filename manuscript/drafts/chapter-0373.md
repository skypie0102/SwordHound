Three months later.

“Hehehe—

you’re still alive?”

At Souaré’s astonished voice—

the concrete shell around the solitary cell cracked.

CRACK—

CRUNCH!

RUMBLE!

The hardened casing split apart.

Vikir opened his eyes.

His body had wasted away until almost nothing remained but skin and bone.

Yet his eyes were still sharp.

Alive.

Souaré stroked her beard.

“Oh my.”

“If I’d known you’d recover this quickly—

I should have brought you out a month earlier.”

“My room is better than solitary confinement, you know.”

“These nights have been rather lonely lately.”

“No.”

“Hehehe.”

“What an arrogant brat.”

“I like you.”

“Next time you land in solitary confinement—

I’ll take good care of you~”

“Bye now!”

Souaré waved him off.

Vikir staggered away.

When he looked back—

he saw heaps upon heaps of shattered concrete and asphalt.

That was probably the solitary cell where old Angajuman had been imprisoned.

*Those were exhausting days.*

Angajuman had constantly demanded sword matches from the neighboring cell.

Even in solitary confinement—

Vikir had not exactly enjoyed silence.

He returned to the stone chamber on Level Nine where he had originally been confined.

CLANG.

The door shut.

Darkness fell immediately.

In a few hours—

at dawn—

he would be taken back to forced labor.

Vikir decided to conserve energy until then.

Shrrk—

ptoo.

Decarabia spat out the Ring of Andromalius from somewhere inside itself.

Vikir had stored a substantial quantity of food inside the ring.

Now—

he withdrew it little by little.

Ate slowly.

“Food really does make you feel alive again.”

He looked down at the ring.

Ever since it had spent so much power creating the enormous barrier at the Academy—

its strength had recovered poorly.

At the moment—

the only reliable use left was extracting small amounts of stored food.

That was disappointing.

[Human.]

[What will you do now?]

“Work.”

“I finally have a reason to work hard.”

[The other humans will probably assume you are working hard because solitary confinement frightened you.]

“Exactly.”

“I need the Level Ten construction to advance as quickly as possible.”

“So I can find what I’m looking for.”

Decarabia blinked.

It had more questions than usual today.

[What are you even looking for this deep beneath the sea?]

[Is there some treasure hidden under this extinct volcano?]

“….”

Vikir was silent for a moment.

The thing he sought—

had become even more important now that only four of the Ten Corpses remained.

*The remaining three have joined forces.*

*Something will happen.*

They would almost certainly try to force the Age of Destruction to arrive ahead of schedule.

If that happened—

Vikir could not stop everything alone.

The future war—

the war that would kill countless humans—

would erupt again.

The battle against the Ten Corpses had entered its final phase.

Vikir would have to lose what needed to be lost—

and take what needed to be taken.

*Maybe this could end the war in one stroke.*

That was why he had come to Nouvelle Vague.

To find a weapon capable of opposing the Ten Corpses’ final weapon.

Even if it meant cutting away every bond he had made with his friends.

[The final weapon of the Ten Corpses…]

[You mean the **Long Rain of Terror**, don’t you?]

“You know it well.”

[Of course.]

[I was once one of them.]

Decarabia narrowed its eye.

Then asked:

[…But does humanity possess any weapon capable of opposing the Long Rain of Terror?]

[As far as I know—

no.]

“The demons think the same thing.”

Vikir’s eyes gleamed in the dark.

“But one exists.”

“Deep beneath the Nouvelle Vague volcano.”

“Far below even the reach of demon eyes…”

“An ancient weapon humanity has completely forgotten.”

“‘The One Who Roars and Shakes the Earth.’”

“**Poseidon.**”

Poseidon truly existed.

The demons’ final weapon—

the Long Rain of Terror.

And humanity’s ultimate weapon—

Poseidon.

Only Poseidon could suppress it.

That was why Vikir had entered Nouvelle Vague personally.

Even if doing so meant abandoning every emotion and friendship he had built outside.

*They’re only school friends I knew briefly in my teenage years.*

*People like Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, Bianca, Sinclaire…*

*They’ll forget someone like me soon enough.*

Their faces passed through his mind.

Then he pushed them away.

What mattered now—

was surviving Nouvelle Vague.

And pushing the excavation forward fast enough to beat the demons.

*The three working together should be Cimeries, Andrealphus, and Flauros.*

The captured demon-aligned escorts had confirmed that much before his imprisonment.

Cimeries—

inside Donquixote.

Andrealphus—

inside Usher.

Flauros—

inside Leviathan.

*Then the last one really is inside the Imperial Family?*

The Emperor had not shown his face even as the Seven Great Families fractured.

That alone was suspicious.

So once Vikir escaped Nouvelle Vague—

his first destination would be the Imperial Family.

*If necessary—

I’ll kill every last member of the Imperial bloodline.*

Then—

a voice interrupted his calm thoughts of treason.

“All prisoners up!”

“Labor begins now!”

Bastille’s wake-up call.

◆◆◆

Every Nouvelle Vague prisoner began labor at four in the morning.

There were no exceptions.

Not even for someone who had just returned from solitary confinement.

CLANG!

THUD!

CRACK!

Vikir swung a pickaxe while wearing multiple layers of BDISSEM restraints.

He did not stop.

The guards began whispering.

“I’ve never seen a Level Nine prisoner work that hard.”

“Suspicious, isn’t it?”

“Maybe he has another plan.”

“What plan?”

“That’s the bastard who refused labor before.”

“Warden D'Ordume crushed the riot and had him thrown into solitary.”

“Oh.”

“Right.”

“He spent three months in there, didn’t he?”

“And came back alive?”

“That’s insane.”

“He survived, sure…”

“But ever since then he’s said nothing.”

“He just works.”

“Guess even a Level Nine monster can be scared by solitary.”

“Of course.”

“I once saw someone spend half a day in there and come out with white hair.”

“Another lasted one day and died of heart failure.”

No one—

prisoner or guard—

found Vikir’s new diligence suspicious.

From then on—

Vikir became famous as the hardest-working prisoner on Level Nine.

“All right!”

“Meal break!”

“Ten minutes!”

The guards shouted.

Vikir sat.

His lunch—

half a heavily salted little fish.

And black bread hard as stone.

CRUNCH.

The bread was hard enough to break teeth.

Even smashing it against rock barely damaged it.

The fish was so heavily salted that its sour smell could not be hidden.

Still—

the prisoners devoured the garbage completely.

That alone showed how brutal the Level Ten construction was.

The ground belonged to a dead volcano.

Dig only a little beneath the cooled layers—

and terrifying heat burst upward.

Lava flowed through deep channels and falls.

The air dried everything.

Standing here—

felt like slowly becoming jerky.

An ordinary person would not last seconds.

Vikir turned.

Several prisoners were staring down into a lava pit.

They were tired of rotten fish and brick-hard bread.

They wanted something else.

“…See anything?”

“Yeah.”

“A shadow.”

“Let’s fish it.”

“Pull before the line and hook melt!”

Using chains and improvised hooks made from work tools—

which carried a death sentence if lost—

the prisoners lowered a line.

SPLASH.

Something came up.

A lava shark.

Eight meters long.

More than a ton.

“Got one!”

The shout had barely left the man’s mouth before prisoners swarmed it.

The already-starved monster—

nothing but bone and skin—

was torn apart by hands and teeth in moments.

The prisoners searched for more food.

But Nouvelle Vague was not a place where prey thrived.

Desperate hunger eventually forced them to look elsewhere.

“…Huff.”

“Huff.”

Vikir noticed a prisoner sitting in the distance.

His skin was pale.

Soft.

His body—

fat and swollen.

Other prisoners were making sure he did not work.

“You don’t need to work.”

“We’ll do your share.”

“Don’t move.”

“Just lie down.”

“Hey.”

“Stop moving.”

“You’ll lose meat.”

Then—

Drooling.

CRUNCH.

The victim screamed.

Then gave up resisting.

Tears ran down his face.

“Hehehe.”

“Treat him properly.”

“We need to enjoy this slowly.”

“Good thing we put ointment on him.”

“My turn tomorrow.”

“His flesh better heal quickly.”

Here—

Vikir had no reason to participate.

He still possessed stored food inside the Ring of Andromalius.

“…They really are desperate to survive.”

He watched silently.

At this point—

the line between human and demon looked very thin.

Then—

“…!”

Vikir noticed a third method of obtaining food.

Not hunting.
