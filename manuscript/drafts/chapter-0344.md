# Chapter 344: Ballak (2)

Tudor.

Bianca.

Vikir stared at the two of them—

now visibly older.

And remembered another life.

The Fifth Battle.

One of humanity’s darkest moments.

Amdusias had led a legion of demonized trees.

Their roots blocked the Human Alliance on every side.

And among the worst—

were the Daylilies of Blood.

They grew anywhere water existed.

That made them dangerous in battle—

and even more dangerous during retreat.

Vikir remembered one retreat in particular.

His body had been filled with arrows.

A sudden storm swelled the river.

Daylilies covered the escape route.

Then—

one great hero stepped forward.

**Bianca Usher.**

Her burning arrows detonated the Daylilies.

She opened a route for the survivors.

But roots caught her before she could escape.

And when she was about to be left behind—

another hero appeared.

**Tudor Donquixote.**

*Run!*

*You’ll be killed too!*

*…How can I run while you’re trapped?!*

*Go!*

*Get help!*

*No!*

*I’m not leaving!*

Exactly like now.

They had argued until the end.

Tudor ultimately used his own body to shield Bianca and the others from the explosion.

He died.

Bianca lived—

holding his burned body.

The man she had spent her entire life fighting with.

Competing with.

Playing beside.

Loving without ever saying it plainly.

Vikir still remembered the rain.

The burned ground.

Bianca sitting there—

holding Tudor and crying.

None of the survivors had known what to say.

*Back then—*

*I was only one low-ranking soldier watching two heroes from a distance.*

Now—

those same two people stood in front of him.

They had encountered Amdusias far earlier than in the old future.

And this time—

Vikir was not a powerless observer.

*Don’t worry.*

*This time, Amdusias dies by my hand.*

If that happened—

Tudor and Bianca would not reach the same ending.

At least—

Vikir hoped so.

“What was that earlier?!”

Tudor snapped at Bianca.

“I told you to run!”

“Why didn’t you run?!”

“Because you were caught!”

“And whose fault was that?!”

“You were just getting in the way!”

“That’s rich coming from the one who had his neck grabbed!”

“I wasn’t caught because I’m weak!”

“Vikir is just ridiculously strong!”

“You would’ve been caught too!”

“I’m an archer!”

“Don’t compare that to someone fighting with a spear!”

“You are impossible!”

“Next time I tell you to leave—

leave!”

“That’s my decision!”

The two continued bickering.

Exactly as always.

Vikir watched them in silence.

Perhaps—

if things went well—

they could keep doing this forever.

Then he returned to the present.

Tudor and Bianca had changed physically.

Taller.

Heavier bones.

More developed muscle.

Tudor’s jaw was broader.

His voice deeper.

Bianca looked older too.

More importantly—

their auras had grown dramatically.

“…How long have you been on this floor?”

Tudor scratched his head.

“Feels like a little over a week.”

Bianca answered more precisely.

“If you count every sunrise and sunset—

today is day 2,555.”

“Seven years.”

Vikir stared.

Outside sensation—

one week.

Inside biological time—

seven years.

Tudor grinned.

“That means a little training gives you an entire day’s worth of growth.”

“We’ve gotten seven years of results.”

Bianca glared at him.

“It also means you age faster.”

“So?”

“I’m still young.”

“You idiot.”

“We don’t know how much longer we’ll be trapped here.”

“That’s future Tudor’s problem.”

“Right now—

we train!”

Vikir nodded.

Their improved strength made sense.

Harsh environment.

Constant survival.

Constant training.

And time flowing like a river.

*The Tower of Opportunity.*

The tower’s trials were cruel.

But humans had a habit of turning cruelty into growth.

That was one reason humanity had survived the Age of Destruction at all.

Tudor slapped Vikir’s shoulder.

“Come on!”

“Let’s go back to the shelter.”

“There are more people waiting.”

“Who?”

“Sancho and Pigi!”

Tudor laughed.

“They’re going to lose their minds when they see you.”

“We’ve got food stored too.”

“Tonight we barbecue!”

He pushed Vikir toward the jungle.

Then—

“Oh, right.”

“Sinclaire was with us on the previous stage too.”

“The mission went badly.”

“So we sent only Sinclaire downward.”

“The four of us were penalized and dropped here.”

Tudor waved toward the river.

“The Daylilies don’t attack much as long as you stay away from the water.”

“So we managed.”

“The real problem is the **other side**—”

He stopped.

The smile vanished.

Beyond the jungle—

their shelter was destroyed.

Stone-and-log huts—

collapsed.

Storage jars—

smashed.

Fruit and grain—

scattered across the ground.

In the center sat two men.

Beards longer.

Bodies larger.

But instantly recognizable.

“Sancho.”

“Pigi.”

Both looked up.

Then their eyes came alive.

“Vikir?!”

They ran over.

Sancho had grown much taller.

Pigi now looked fully adult.

“How are you here?!”

“Did you get assigned to us this time?”

They grabbed Vikir’s hands—

shaking them repeatedly.

Behind them—

Tudor and Bianca were already examining the damage.

“…Them again?”

“All the food.”

“Gone.”

Sancho nodded grimly.

“We fought.”

“Numbers were against us.”

“And individually—

they were far stronger than we expected.”

“Keeping our lives was already fortunate.”

Both Sancho and Pigi were wounded.

Tudor clenched his teeth.

“Damn it.”

“That’s the ‘other problem’ I was talking about.”

“They’re worse than the Daylilies.”

“We finally get Vikir back—

and we can’t even have the barbecue.”

Vikir looked at Pigi.

Something had caught his attention.

“Pigi.”

“…Was your blood always black?”

Pigi blinked.

“Oh.”

“This?”

“Yes.”

“It’s always been like that.”

“Seriously.”

“I’m not becoming a Majin or anything.”

“Doctors said it has something to do with unusually high iron in my blood.”

He glanced down.

“Although…”

“Ever since entering the Tower—

it does seem darker.”

“Hm.”

Bianca returned from checking the damage.

“That can wait.”

“They took everything.”

“At this rate—

forget the mission.”

“We don’t even have food for tomorrow.”

“…And definitely no barbecue.”

Vikir looked at them.

“What mission?”

Four windows appeared.

**<Mission — Kill or Be Killed!>**

**Every one of the ‘predecessors’ possesses powerful strength!**

**Survival is difficult by nature~**

**Recognized only when everyone completes the mission!**

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca had arrived first.

And had already fought the established inhabitants of this floor.

“We don’t know what they are.”

Tudor frowned.

“Barbarians, maybe.”

“They’re openly hostile.”

Sancho added.

“They keep stealing our food.”

Pigi nodded.

“Fast.”

“Strong.”

“And their arrows are ridiculous.”

Bianca looked toward the trees.

“Comparable to famous master archers.”

Deep spiral holes scarred stone and thick trunks.

Shots from long range.

Heavy rotation.

Vikir recognized that kind of archery.

“…I have an idea.”

He followed the combat traces into the jungle.

Tudor grabbed his arm.

“That direction goes straight into their territory.”

“Going head-on is dangerous.”

Bianca narrowed her eyes.

And Sancho—

for some reason—

grinned.

“Still…”

“I suddenly feel like we can win now.”

Pigi nodded too.

“We’ve only had the four of us until now.”

“With Vikir here…”

The four fell in behind him.

Heading directly toward the base of the veterans who had lived on this floor far longer than they had.
