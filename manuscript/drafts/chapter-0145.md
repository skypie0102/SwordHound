# Chapter 145: Volunteering for the Golden Week (2)

“Um…”

Sinclaire had appeared out of nowhere.

Her speech toward Vikir was stiffly polite.

Vikir looked at her as if asking what she wanted.

Sinclaire hesitated before speaking.

“Um… Vikir, are you here as a regular volunteer too?”

“No.”

“Then… why are you here?”

“Demerits.”

“…Oh.”

Sinclaire nodded at the short answer.

“I was planning to volunteer regularly.”

“I do need the service credit, but helping people who are less fortunate feels good too.”

“It gives me a sense of accomplishment.”

“I see.”

Vikir nodded.

He had no interest in Sinclaire's private motivations.

Then he turned to leave.

Sinclaire followed.

“Still, it's a coincidence that we were assigned to the same volunteer location.”

“Is it?”

“Actually, I saw you submit your volunteer form last week.”

“I just didn't realize we were coming to the same place.”

“I see.”

“Vikir…”

Sinclaire smiled for some reason—

despite receiving nothing but short replies.

Vikir cut her off.

“You can speak casually.”

“You look about my age.”

“…Ah.”

Sinclaire blinked.

“I'm actually seventeen.”

“One year younger than you.”

“It doesn't matter.”

Vikir nodded.

Sinclaire's face brightened immediately.

“Then… I'll speak casually from now on!”

“Fine.”

“Do that.”

Vikir believed the conversation was over.

He left to rinse and wring out his cleaning rag.

Sinclaire followed him.

Even when Vikir entered the men's washroom—

Sinclaire stood at the doorway and watched him work.

*At least she didn't follow me inside.*

She waited quietly until he came back out.

Then attached herself to him again.

“Hey.”

“When I saw you submit a volunteer application during the holiday, I thought you were managing your service points in advance.”

“For your grades.”

“I was forced to submit it.”

“I had too many demerits.”

“Oh.”

Sinclaire nodded.

“I knew you were a good student, so I thought there had to be some special reason.”

She kept glancing at him as she spoke.

Vikir sighed.

“I'm busy.”

“If you have questions, ask them quickly.”

“Then go.”

Sinclaire's eyes lit up.

“Really? I can ask?”

She stepped closer—

then unleashed everything she had apparently been holding back.

“How do you study?”

“How many hours a day?”

“How do you divide your time between preparation and review?”

“Did you buy the new genealogy supplement for *Introduction to Magic*?”

“I can show you mine.”

“What's your major in the Cold Department?”

“Swordsmanship?”

“Is the Cold Department swordsmanship professor good?”

“You got every question right on the last Cold Department quiz.”

“I missed one.”

“How did you solve that problem?”

“It was beyond normal undergraduate level.”

“Oh—and did you see the question about ‘Ethics During Dissection’ on the *Monster Physiology Experiment A* quiz?”

“The answer is controversial.”

“What do you think?”

“Um… what else…”

“I had so many things I wanted to ask, but now that I have the chance I can't remember them.”

“How are you so good at everything?”

“Did you receive advanced education before entering the Academy?”

“You didn't?”

“Wow.”

“Then what kind of private instruction did you have before admission?”

“Ah—I don't know whether I should ask this.”

“You don't have to answer if it bothers you.”

“I heard you're a commoner.”

“What region are you from?”

“And is your eyesight bad?”

“Your glasses look strong.”

“Oh, they're not?”

“Then why do you wear them?”

“Is your hairstyle intentional?”

“Oh. It just grew like that.”

“Have you ever thought about cutting your bangs?”

“Or brushing them back?”

“No?”

Most of the questions—

were about studying.

Vikir answered in his usual dry tone.

At the same time—

he kept working.

Sweeping corridors.

Washing clothes.

Burning trash.

Sinclaire followed closely and helped with whatever he was doing.

When the chores finally slowed—

she rummaged through her bag.

Then held out a bottle of milk.

“Here.”

“Drink this while you work!”

“…”

Vikir accepted the bottle.

He looked down at Sinclaire.

“…?”

Sinclaire looked back up at him with the same bright expression.

Vikir reached a conclusion.

If he left things as they were—

she would follow him around all day.

“I'll drink it.”

“Goodbye.”

“Oh! Where are you going?”

“I'll help!”

“The toilet.”

Sinclaire's face turned bright red.

“A-Ah.”

“I probably can't help with that.”

“Go ahead~”

She leaned against the wall outside the washroom.

Apparently—

she intended to wait for him to finish.

◆◆◆

Vikir eventually managed to send Sinclaire somewhere else.

Finally—

he was alone.

“…A model student.”

“Her enthusiasm for studying is impressive.”

Carrying a bucket and rag—

Vikir entered a quiet corridor.

Most of the Academy volunteers were outside eating with the children—

or sharing snacks with them.

Vikir had deliberately chosen mealtime to continue inspecting the building.

That was when everyone's attention was most scattered.

Then—

“…!”

A child appeared in the corridor.

“…”

A girl around ten years old.

Beautiful blond hair.

Fair skin.

Slightly downturned eyes that made her look melancholy.

An old, crude golden necklace hung around her neck.

A name had been engraved into it.

**Peri.**

The girl stared at Vikir.

He raised the bottle of milk Sinclaire had given him.

“Want it?”

“…”

Peri stared back.

Swish—

Vikir flicked his wrist.

The milk spun visibly inside the bottle.

The girl's eyes shifted toward it for a moment.

Then she turned and ran down the corridor.

Vikir put the bottle back in his pocket.

He did not think much of it.

Then—

“Her name is Peri.”

A voice came from behind him.

Vikir turned.

Dolores stood there.

A knitting basket in one hand.

Several dolls in the other.

Student council president.

Head of Ryukeion.

Saintess of Quovadis.

She also volunteered here every weekend.

Dolores narrowed her eyes at Vikir.

“Are you doing your service properly?”

“You'll have to work hard if you want those demerits erased.”

“…I'm working hard.”

“Good.”

Dolores nodded.

Her gaze remained stern.

Then she looked in the direction Peri had gone.

“You shouldn't be too kind to that child.”

“…?”

Vikir tilted his head.

Dolores explained.

“She was born here.”

“Over the last thirteen years, she's watched countless volunteers come and go.”

“…”

“At first, she was close to them.”

“She called them big sisters and big brothers.”

“…”

“But as time passed, those same people came less and less often.”

“It can't really be helped.”

“As students advance through the Academy, they have more to study.”

“Then they graduate.”

“Then they find work.”

“I'm reaching that stage myself.”

“…”

“Out of a hundred first-year volunteers, perhaps one will still be coming by third year.”

“And even that person usually disappears after graduation or employment.”

Dolores's expression became complicated.

“For a volunteer, a child here is one person among many people in their life.”

“But for a child in this orphanage—”

“those volunteer brothers and sisters can become a huge part of their world.”

She was right.

Most Academy students came because volunteer service was a graduation requirement.

Their time at the orphanage—

however important it might feel to the children—

was temporary.

Dolores watched Peri disappear down the corridor.

“She grew tired of brief closeness.”

“Long periods of missing someone.”

“And permanent goodbyes.”

“So she made a promise to herself.”

“She wouldn't give her feelings to people from outside anymore.”

“…I see.”

“Yes.”

Dolores sighed.

“Every time I see her, her guard is higher.”

“Eventually, she stopped speaking at all.”

“It's as though she's given up expecting anything from other people.”

“It hurts to watch.”

“I wish she didn't see the world so negatively.”

Dolores had volunteered here for more than three years.

Even now—

Peri did not return her greetings.

Then—

“…!”

Vikir's expression changed.

Dolores did not notice.

“I hope Peri starts speaking again soon.”

“Her aphasia was caused by emotional trauma.”

“She needs warmth and affection from the people around her—mmph!?”

Dolores could not finish.

Vikir's hand snapped out.

He covered her mouth.

Then—

with one motion—

pulled her into a narrow recess between the walls.

The alcove was the result of a poor architectural layout.

Cabinets protruded on both sides.

Shadow covered the gap.

From the corridor—

it was extremely difficult to see anyone inside.

“Mmph!?”

Dolores pushed against Vikir's chest.

She tried to pull his hand away from her mouth.

Vikir did not let her.

Instead—

he pressed her more firmly into the recess.

“Shh.”

His low voice sounded beside her ear.

Dolores's thoughts went blank.

*What?*

*Why so suddenly?*

*No—what is happening?*

She had never been this physically close to a man before.

Faced with a situation this abrupt—

and this rude—

her body froze.

Vikir was not looking at her.

His eyes narrowed toward the corridor.

The muscles in his face tightened.

A foul odor was approaching.

Thick.

Rotten.

Like corpse-stench stirred through the inside of his nose.

Thud.

Thud.

Thud.

Footsteps struck the marble floor.

A man was walking toward them from the far end of the corridor.

A gentleman in his fifties.

A devout believer.

A successful businessman.

A benevolent father.

On the surface—

there was nothing wrong with him.

Beneath that skin—

the stench of death was almost suffocating.

The man approaching—

was **Quilt Rune Indulgentia**.

Vikir's current assassination target.
