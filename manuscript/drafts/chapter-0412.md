# Chapter 412: End game (4)

Ballak.

A warrior people of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Alongside Baskerville—

one of the strongest powers in the western jungle.

And Aiyen—

Akwilla’s daughter—

had become its young leader.

At only seventeen—

she had already entered the Sword Graduator realm.

In the original future—

Red Death should have erased her.

Vikir’s interference had changed that.

*But meeting her here…*

That—

he had never expected.

Aiyen elbowed him lightly.

“I knew you’d be here.”

“….”

“Though I didn’t expect you to still be wearing a collar.”

She grinned at the old restraint around his neck.

Vikir looked back at Souaré.

Aiyen had been the one who kicked her unconscious.

“What happened?”

“What part?”

“Why I’m here?”

“Or how I became this strong?”

“Both.”

Aiyen pointed forward with her thumb.

“We should move.”

“You’re in a hurry, aren’t you?”

She was right.

They left immediately.

Aiyen knocked out two guards—

stole their bows and arrows—

and followed.

Then she said:

“I entered the Hell Tree too.”

“…!”

Vikir remembered Amdusias.

The arrow marks once visible on the demon’s horns.

Aheul’s old testimony.

But Aiyen shook her head.

“Not the same Hell Tree you entered.”

“Mine was a different individual.”

“It called itself the **prototype**.”

Vikir understood.

The Hell Tree he had cleared—

was an improved version.

A version adjusted so challengers had at least some chance to survive.

Aiyen—

alone—

had entered an abandoned early model.

A tower discarded because its survival rate had been too low.

“I killed fairies.”

“Monsters.”

“Ate candies.”

“Survived.”

“But the tower was broken.”

“There was no proper completion route.”

“Even after I killed the Dragon Horse at the top—

it told me I would stay trapped forever.”

Vikir looked genuinely surprised.

The Dragon Horse had not been weak even in the more refined Hell Tree.

Aiyen had repeatedly challenged it—

and won.

“So that’s how you became strong enough to reverse things without me.”

“Hehehe.”

“But I only escaped because Andromalius was defeated in the west.”

One day—

after Aiyen had already conquered everything her prototype tower could offer—

the exit simply opened.

Later—

she learned why.

Vikir had destroyed the other Hell Tree—

and killed the tower’s master.

“After that I left the western lands and reached the central continent.”

“And everything was already ruined.”

The sky had split.

Gates appeared overnight.

Fire fell.

Monsters poured out.

The beginning of the world’s destruction.

“Four signs are happening.”

Aiyen counted.

“Endless darkness.”

“Burning hail.”

“Swarming flies.”

“Frogs.”

Vikir frowned.

“Four?”

“Not ten?”

Aiyen looked confused.

“Yes.”

“Four.”

Then Vikir understood.

*Six Demon Kings are already dead.*

In the future he remembered—

ten disasters had preceded full destruction.

Water turning red.

Insects multiplying.

Livestock and wild beasts dying.

Locust storms annihilating crops.

Deaths among newborn life.

And more.

Those missing disasters—

had belonged to Demon Kings Vikir had already eliminated.

“…So killing six of them reduced the calamities to four.”

It was progress.

But not salvation.

Four Demon Kings were still enough to destroy the world.

The Gate of Destruction would open completely eventually.

Before that—

they had to die.

Aiyen’s expression hardened.

“That’s why I went to Cindywendy the moment I reached the Imperial Capital.”

Vikir’s eyes sharpened.

Cindywendy.

One of the people he trusted completely.

And someone Ballak had traded with for years.

“She told me what happened.”

“That you had been sent here.”

“Wrongfully imprisoned.”

“Not exactly wrongfully.”

“Continue.”

“So I decided to finish the work you left behind.”

Aiyen’s eyes glowed blue.

“Pedro Donquixote.”

“Usher P. Isolde.”

“Thomas de Leviathan.”

“And their masters.”

“Pasamonte.”

“Madeline.”

“Hobbes.”

“I tried to kill all six.”

Vikir immediately remembered old newspaper fragments.

The Usher attack.

Madeline badly injured.

The Donquixote attack.

Pasamonte lightly wounded.

Then—

the Leviathan incident.

Hobbes attacked.

One suspected assailant.

The same person.

Aiyen.

“You could have waited for me.”

“I was going to leave Nouvelle Vague soon.”

“I knew you’d come back.”

Aiyen shrugged.

“But what reunion gift is better than your enemy’s head?”

“I care about atmosphere too.”

She looked sincerely disappointed that she had failed to bring the remaining demon heads.

Vikir tried to reassure her.

“Going in alone was already impressive.”

“That’s enough.”

“Compared to the Dragon Horse?”

“Or fighting a whole Daylily colony in quicksand?”

“Not especially.”

“What happened in the end?”

“I failed.”

“I attacked the Leviathan head.”

“He was stronger than expected.”

“I got captured.”

“And they sent me here.”

“That one is Flauros.”

“A powerful Demon King.”

“You couldn’t handle him alone.”

“Did Cindywendy not stop you?”

“No.”

“She didn’t stop me.”

“I wasn’t alone.”

“…?”

Aiyen skipped the details.

But the amount of planning and resolve behind her revenge was obvious.

“Thank you.”

“Hmph.”

“Then treat me well.”

A massive man blocked their route.

Bastille.

He raised his two-handed greatsword.

“Whatever you’re planning—

you are not going any farther!”

He knew he could not defeat the pair directly.

So—

BOOM!

Bastille smashed the sword into the ground.

The passage cracked.

Rocks fell.

He intended to collapse the route—

even if he died with it.

“…Troublesome.”

Vikir frowned.

Then Aiyen whistled.

WHOOSH!

Her hair flared like fire.

A huge wolf—

made of flame—

appeared.

Vikir’s face changed.

“Bakira!”

The wolf that had once crossed the jungle beside him.

Aiyen smiled sadly.

“When his lifespan ended—

he didn’t go on.”

“He stayed.”

“We made a phantom-beast contract inside the Hell Tree.”

Bakira struck Bastille once.

BOOM!

The lieutenant colonel flew away.

Then the fire wolf carried Aiyen and Vikir across the collapsing gap.

“Fast.”

Vikir had never imagined the wolf that once lost its hind legs during Madam Eight-Legs’ attack could move like this.

Aiyen looked ahead.

“Isn’t the Gulper Eel kennel on Level Five?”

“Why are we going to Level Ten?”

Vikir leaned closer—

and quietly told her the next part of the plan.

Aiyen’s eyes widened.

“…You planned all of this?”

“Even being imprisoned?”

“Yes.”

“So far—

everything is proceeding as intended.”

D’Ordume and Souaré were down.

Bastille had been removed.

The prisoners were rioting across every floor.

The guards were overwhelmed.

Everything—

almost—

had gone according to plan.

Then—

BOOM!

A tremendous sound came from above.

Chains shrieked.

Metal winches collapsed.

The **Gate of Good and Evil**—

began opening.

“Who opened the Gate?!”

Bastille shouted from the rubble.

Nobody answered.

Cold seawater poured from above—

cooling the overheated battlefield.

Prisoners and guards alike looked upward.

An old man walked through the opening.

He had forced the gate open with his own arms.

Then dropped into Nouvelle Vague.

THUD.

Wrinkled face.

Closed lips.

Hard white beard.

Uniform sharp as a blade.

Everyone went pale.

Nouvelle Vague’s symbol.

The one man feared by every prisoner—

and obeyed by every guard.

**Orca Montreuil-sur-Mer Javert.**

Warden of Nouvelle Vague.

Had returned.
