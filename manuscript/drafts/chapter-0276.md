# Chapter 276: Money War (4)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/276.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 274 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Oracle, Dolores L. Quovadis, Pigi, Sinclaire, Night Hound, Vikir, House Bourgeois, and the Sixth Corpse. “This isn’t the main hall.”

“It’s the restroom.”

Viscount Saltsail stared at Puss in Boots.

“…You’re joking.”

“How could I be?”

Puss in Boots pointed toward the wall.

Only now did the golden washbasin make sense.

A toilet stood slightly farther to the side.

“You said earlier that the cocktail made you want to use the restroom.”

“So I brought you here first.”

“That was just a comment.”

“I see.”

“Then shall we proceed to the main hall?”

Puss in Boots motioned toward the attendants.

“The guest has visited the restroom.”

“Please provide him with fresh clothes and perfume.”

“Huh?”

“There’s no need—”

“Please don’t worry.”

“In case a guest feels unclean after entering a restroom, this is simply part of our service.”

The attendants approached.

They replaced Saltsail’s red cloak.

“There are many designs.”

“Choose whichever you prefer.”

Saltsail’s mouth fell open.

More than fifty premium cloaks were available.

He owned only two.

*This cloth comes from raw materials deep in the western jungles.*

*Even money doesn’t guarantee access to it.*

Then—

clink.

The cocktail glass in an attendant’s hand made a sharp sound.

Saltsail looked.

The cubes inside—

still had not melted.

“Wait.”

“Those aren’t ice cubes.”

“That is correct.”

Puss in Boots nodded.

“They’re diamonds.”

“We chill them in cold storage.”

“They keep the drink cool without diluting it.”

Saltsail said nothing.

Puss in Boots finally led him onward.

And then—

the real main hall appeared.

The center of the Imperial Capital.

The peak of excess.

Crystal chandeliers threw jewel-like light everywhere.

Gold dust coated decorative spiderwebs stretched between the ceiling and walls—

sparkling whenever the lighting shifted.

Servants moved through the crowd in expensive black cloaks.

The ceiling rose high enough for indoor fireworks.

Spiral staircases twisted upward through multiple levels.

Rare ingredients were displayed everywhere.

More than one hundred fifty chefs cooked continuously in front of the guests.

Music came from elaborate keyboards and instruments made of jade, pearl, and precious stone.

Moderation did not exist here.

Neither did restraint.

“Unbelievable.”

“How can a banquet hall be this extravagant?”

“I want one of these too.”

“What have I been doing with my money until now?”

“Yes!”

“This is what money is for!”

A huge swimming pool occupied part of the hall.

It was not filled with water.

It held cherry-colored fruit liquor with low alcohol content—

light enough to evaporate from skin without leaving a sticky film.

Seven bridges of ivory and gold crossed over it.

The pool floor was transparent glass.

Below it—

massive live fish swam in a separate chamber.

Every one of them was premium food.

If a guest pointed—

a chef would retrieve it and prepare it immediately.

Shark.

Whale.

Whale shark.

Everything associated with pleasure seemed to have been dragged into one room.

Food.

Drink.

Music.

Acrobats.

Models.

Dancers.

Fireworks.

Balloons.

Men.

Women.

Noise.

Color.

Heat.

All of it burning together beneath the banner of hospitality.

And supposedly—

this entire spectacle had been prepared by three compassionate Colosseo students.

“I’ve never seen a party like this.”

“What kind of students can afford this?”

“How much money did they make?”

The guests were stunned.

Then the alcohol and atmosphere swallowed them.

Dancers in bright feathered costumes performed.

Tens of thousands of balloons rose.

Fireworks burst.

BANG.

BANG.

BANG.

The flames struck balloons—

which burst again—

scattering petals and gold dust.

This was Oracle’s party.

A spectacle designed to overwhelm judgment.

Then the hosts stepped forward.

“H-hello.”

Pigi appeared first—

smiling awkwardly.

“Thank you for waiting.”

Sinclaire followed—

polite and composed.

Finally—

Dolores stepped out.

“We are the members of Oracle hosting tonight’s gathering.”

Applause filled the hall.

The guests were mostly older.

No screaming.

No whistles.

But their welcome was enthusiastic.

Dolores addressed them.

“Thank you all for attending Oracle Investment Club’s gathering.”

“For us younger students—

this is a rare opportunity to meet seniors in the financial world.”

When she identified herself as the Saintess of Quovadis—

the room reacted immediately.

*Sinclaire.*

*What do we do?*

Pigi looked nervous.

Sinclaire whispered back.

*Stay calm.*

*If we don’t say anything strange, they’ll assume we’re nobles.*

Pigi and Sinclaire had not introduced their backgrounds.

As Sinclaire predicted—

most guests simply assumed the two Colosseo prodigies came from important families.

Dolores continued.

“I hope the relationships begun tonight will continue.”

“Not merely continue—”

“but advance steadily.”

“Like that powerful horse.”

She pointed toward a giant coral sculpture.

One huge piece of expensive coral—

carved into a magnificent animal.

It was not a horse.

It was a deer.

Antlers and all.

No one corrected her.

“Run like horses, young ones!”

“Excellent spirit!”

“What bold students!”

**Calling a deer a horse.**

By now—

the guests were drunk on the party itself.

Whether the sculpture was a deer or horse no longer mattered.

The social elite crowded around Dolores, Pigi, and Sinclaire.

Praise.

Flattery.

Questions.

Curiosity.

Meanwhile—

a servant boy quietly moved toward the edge of the hall.

Puss in Boots.

Behind the cat mask—

red eyes surveyed every guest.

*The aesthetics of humanity near the end of the world worked exactly as expected.*

Night Hound.

Vikir.

Tonight—

he had become Puss in Boots.

Before regression—

as the war against the Demon Army approached—

humanity had developed strange habits.

One was extreme luxury.

Again and again—

societies nearing collapse turned toward excess.

Rich people who believed the world might end—

spent fortunes chasing sensations no ordinary person could imagine.

Taste.

Beauty.

Fashion.

Pleasure.

As wealth passed through generations—

the extravagance became increasingly absurd.

Vikir had seen plenty of it.

*People in peaceful times have money.*

*But they don’t know how to spend it.*

*They lack imagination.*

The decorations tonight—

the pool.

The bridges.

The diamond ice.

The gold dust.

All of it came from things Vikir had seen before.

There was one problem.

*The investment profits still weren’t enough.*

So Vikir had borrowed more.

A lot more.

He poured the debt into the party.

A strong impression mattered more than thrift.

Of course—

he had no intention of repaying some of those lenders.

The wealthy people who had loaned him money—

were demon collaborators.

They had trusted Oracle’s reputation.

The loans themselves had been obtained under false identities—

so Dolores, Pigi, and Sinclaire had no repayment liability.

*I’ll bankrupt them.*

*Let them choke on the money they chose.*

These were people who—

when contracting with demons—

had chosen wealth rather than combat power.

When the great war came—

they would betray humanity early.

If Vikir killed the Sixth Corpse—

their borrowed demonic influence would collapse anyway.

Then—

“…!”

Vikir found what he had been waiting for.

Long brown hair.

Pale skin.

Black eyes.

Red lips.

Androgynous features.

Black suit.

Red cloak.

A look better suited to a funeral than a party.

*Found you.*

**Damien Bourgeois.**

Director of the Imperial Money Manufacturing Bureau.

Outside director of the Bourgeois Landowners Guild.

Vikir closed his eyes once.

Opened them.

Then walked toward him.

Like a hound that had finally found the trail.
