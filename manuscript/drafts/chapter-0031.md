# Chapter 31: Special Laws of Vikir (4)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/031.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 31 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English forms for Underdog City, Vikir Van Baskerville, Chihuahua Baskerville, Deputy Magistrate, and established Baskerville terminology follow current project/Fandom evidence. The recovered English incorrectly substitutes Camus for the separate Morgue figure Kamu; Chinese Chapter 31 and later Red Death chronology support **Kamu Morgue**. The epithet rendered here as **Heaven-Tilting Martial Marquis** is source-descriptive/provisional because no current Fandom canonical English form was located.

The legend of a girl turning her life around by pulling up a wooden stake and receiving ten billion gold spread through all of Underdog City in no time.

Before long, the story had traveled across the entire Baskerville territory.

It was even said that Underdog City had assigned guards specifically to protect the girl's prize money.

‘This is the law. It will be upheld.’

The young Deputy Magistrate's short declaration was repeated from mouth to mouth.

Naturally, the law Vikir had enacted—the so-called **Special Laws of Vikir**—became even more famous than Baskerville's existing territorial statutes.

People talked everywhere.

“The new Deputy Magistrate really is different.”

“Whatever Deputy Magistrate Vikir says, he follows through on it no matter what.”

“I heard Underdog City's crime rate has already fallen by half since that day.”

The old saying that *the law is far away, but the fist is close* had become obsolete.

Now the law was close enough to keep the fist at a distance.

Break the law and you were punished.

Follow the law and you were rewarded.

That simple principle struck the citizens with unexpected force.

A society with rules it actually stood by.

The distrust that had built up over years of laws being ignored began to wash away after that day.

From the very next day after the Special Laws of Vikir were proclaimed, illegal activity visibly decreased.

The crime rate was cut in half.

That alone said enough.

Much of the darkness that had been rooted in Underdog City for years had been swept away by a single carefully staged demonstration.

◆◆◆

Underdog City Hall.

General Secretary Chihuahua Baskerville was smiling as he wrote across a wooden plaque.

<div class="info-window">
<p>Trust Won by Moving the Stake</p>
</div>

It would hang in the Magistrate's office from that day forward.

Vikir silently watched Chihuahua's brush move across the plaque.

Then he spoke.

“Your handwriting is excellent, General Secretary Chihuahua.”

“Oh, it's nothing. I've never met anyone better at calligraphy than I am, if I may say so myself. I also became quite skilled at copying other people's handwriting after years of signing documents in place of Magistrates...”

Chihuahua trailed off.

Vikir had just said something he had never expected.

“Could you teach me?”

Chihuahua's eyes widened.

A proud member of Baskerville's main family had asked a servant-born subordinate for something rather than ordering him.

And a swordsman, of all people, had shown interest in calligraphy.

“Of course! Of course I can teach you! It would be my honor!”

“Thank you. Then I'll ask you when we have time.”

After receiving the compliment, Chihuahua hummed to himself as he moved the brush with renewed enthusiasm.

Vikir watched him closely.

Chihuahua Baskerville.

A commoner from outside the clan who had worked for Baskerville for three years before formally receiving the family surname.

Swordsmen who joined the clan could become Guardian Knights and gain access to Baskerville swordsmanship.

Administrators were different.

Their compensation was smaller and they received few special privileges.

For a swordsman, becoming part of Baskerville came with the obvious incentive of learning swordsmanship that could elevate his entire martial path.

For an administrator like Chihuahua, there was little beyond honor to make the arrangement attractive.

‘If I remember correctly... he joined Baskerville because he wanted to serve his hometown.’

Vikir had encountered Chihuahua several times in his previous life.

The man looked like a scheming court official, but his nature was rigidly upright.

He had always spoken plainly to Magistrates and Deputy Magistrates.

That honesty had repeatedly gotten him pushed out to remote posts.

He eventually died old without ever being given a real chance to show what he could do.

Vikir was thinking about that when Chihuahua spoke again.

“By the way, Deputy Magistrate, that was incredible.”

Having finished the plaque, Chihuahua looked over at Vikir.

“Who would have imagined such a performance? I truly admire you. I've worked here as General Secretary for twenty years, but this is the first time I've ever felt anything like it. My heart is still pounding.”

Vikir gave a small nod.

“Baskerville's laws are actually well designed. But simply posting laws isn't enough. What matters is whether the people believe those laws will be enforced and choose to obey them.”

“Exactly. You're absolutely right. The previous Deputy Magistrates never thought that way.”

“You can't just get angry because the people distrust the law or look down on it. I needed something that would seize their attention and leave an impression they couldn't forget.”

In fact, Vikir had borrowed the underlying idea from a performance Kamu of the Morgue Clan had used in his previous life.

Kamu Morgue, later remembered by the epithet **Heaven-Tilting Martial Marquis**, became a terrifying ruler almost as soon as taking power and transformed the territory under Kamu's control into an uncompromising domain of law and order.

People there would not even pick up money dropped on the road.

Disputes between residents all but disappeared.

The crime rate approached zero.

‘...Though such brutally harsh rule never earned the people's affection.’

Even so, during the war against the Demon Realm, Kamu's iron-fisted administration greatly improved humanity's survival rate.

After the Generation of Destruction, the Morgue territory was said to have retained the largest surviving population.

...But none of that mattered right now.

“The problem is what comes next.”

Vikir looked down at a map of Underdog City.

“With ten billion gold paid out as prize money, we need a way to fill that hole.”

He picked up a pen and began drawing red X marks across the map.

As though he knew every alley in the city by heart, he marked one secluded corner after another.

X. X. X. X. X. X. X....

The number of marks soon passed one hundred.

“These are places where illegal organizations are hiding now, or places they'll use in the future. We'll raid them one by one and return their black money to the treasury.”

“How do you know? Are all of these really criminal hideouts?”

“I can smell them.”

Vikir pinched his nose and smiled.

Baskerville hounds had sharp noses.

Leaving Chihuahua staring blankly at him, Vikir continued laying out the plan.

“Military doctrine says the best way to provision yourself is to take supplies from the enemy. Sweep these places clean and our revenue will rise. Then we'll use that money to strengthen welfare and support the poor.”

“Oh... remarkable. But there are so many X marks. Are there really that many hidden organizations?”

“Yes. Even after crime fell by half, this many remain. The spectacle reduced open crime. What's left has simply become quieter and more cunning.”

That was fine.

Vikir knew where those organizations were.

More importantly, he knew where the core organizations were.

‘Before my regression, I patrolled and raided these places more times than I can count.’

‘The memory of chasing rats through the dark like a mad hound during the war on crime is still clear.’

He also remembered the methods used by colleagues who had accepted bribes.

How criminals thought.

How they moved.

Where they hid.

What methods they used.

How far their arrogance would go.

Vikir knew all of it.

He even remembered the channels through which underground money would circulate for at least the next twenty-five years.

Bzzzzzz—

Beelzebub, hidden near the artery in his wrist, began to tremble faintly as though it had sensed his killing intent.

It wanted blood.

Just then—

“Deputy Magistrate!”

The door opened.

A clerk stepped inside wearing a troubled expression.

“Someone is requesting an audience.”

Vikir and Chihuahua both turned toward him.

Chihuahua spoke first.

“Who requests an audience during working hours? The Deputy Magistrate has only been in office for two days.”

“W-Well... the Youth Self-Governance Committee from the local township office wishes to invite him...”

Chihuahua's expression immediately hardened.

The look on his face said that the thing he had been expecting had finally arrived.

Vikir spoke lightly.

“So a group of local people with a little status wants to see me?”

“...Yes. The township youth association is made up of second- and third-generation heirs from influential local families.”

The request came from young members of smaller families within Baskerville territory—old local houses that had exercised influence in Underdog City for generations.

Most had direct or indirect connections to Baskerville.

Many were linked by marriage.

Others were tied through blood or shared schooling.

Chihuahua could not quite hide his contempt.

“They call themselves the township's Youth Self-Governance Committee, but in reality they're nothing more than a decadent social club. It seems the young masters have something they want to say about the ‘Special Laws of Vikir.’”

“...I see.”

Vikir leaned back against the sofa and rested a hand on his chin.

‘When you make a law meant to eliminate corruption, the place that reacts most violently is usually the most corrupt one.’

The first organized objection to the Special Laws of Vikir had come from this youth committee.

Vikir looked at Chihuahua, who was watching him uneasily.

Then he said only one thing.

“The flies smelled it first.”

This was the reason Vikir had begun laying bait from his very first day in office.

Now it was time to clean out the pests eating away at the city.
