# Chapter 44: The Fiancée (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/044.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 44 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Current canonical/project terminology controls **Camus Morgue**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Rokoko Clan**, **Adolf Morgue**, **Hugo Le Baskerville**, and **Rosie Morgue**. Chinese semantics control Rosie's relationship to Camus in this chapter: Camus calls her a foster/adopted younger sister; later Fandom cousin/resurrection material is not imported.

Before long, the fortress built by Morgue came into view.

Massive earthen walls formed a broad circle, with watchtowers of timber and iron rising at intervals along them.

Camus and Vikir walked through the fortress as they talked.

“The tribes raid the ruby mines, seize the indigenous slaves, and steal crops. They don't take the slaves because they want to rescue their own people. They sell them somewhere else.”

“Right. There are many different tribes, and they don't consider every other tribe their kin. They'll sell criminals from their own tribe or captives from another without hesitation.”

“You know the situation well. Good. That saves me from having to answer the ridiculous question of why tribespeople fight one another.”

Camus raised a hand and pointed toward the nearest outer wall.

“Earth mages and iron mages built that wall in a month.”

The scale was enormous.

If ordinary laborers had built it, one year would not have been surprising.

Even then, hundreds of workers would have been needed.

Vikir approached the earthen wall.

Something about it looked strange.

Inside the hardened earth, an iron framework had been embedded in a lattice.

He could see portions of that framework because the entire wall was riddled with holes.

Hundreds of them.

The surface looked almost like a biscuit repeatedly pierced before baking.

Vikir inspected one of the holes.

Roughly three centimeters across.

“...Arrow marks.”

His thoughts immediately went to the most notorious warrior tribe beyond Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Camus nodded.

“The **Ballak Tribe** is the worst of them. Their arrows carry tremendous force. More sentries have been sniped to death than I care to count. They can punch straight through an earthen wall two or three meters thick. How are we supposed to stop that?”

“Even shield magic would struggle. The arrows are too fast.”

“Exactly. Uncle nearly died to one not long ago.”

Camus gave a short, humorless laugh.

Adolf Morgue himself had recently gone out on a search operation and been targeted by a sniper.

“An arrow broke through his shield magic and struck his chest. He survived, fortunately, but the injury was serious. His pride probably hurt almost as much.”

“Come to think of it, Hugo had a similar experience. He still has a scar across the bridge of his nose.”

Vikir remembered it clearly.

If Ballak archery could wound Sword Master Hugo and Sixth-Circle mage Adolf, it deserved every bit of caution Morgue was giving it.

“There must be an exceptional archer among them. Do you know who?”

“Apparently a woman. The shots came from too far away to identify her, though. They also paint their faces black, so even seeing them doesn't help much.”

Camus waved one hand irritably.

“Anyway, the Ballak are the most dangerous even though there are only about three hundred of them. That's roughly one tenth the population of the **Rokoko Clan**, which is dangerous in a different way.”

The Ballak were a fiercely independent warrior tribe.

They lived beyond the borders of ordinary rule, raiding and fighting wherever opportunity took them.

For reasons no one understood, they had shifted eastward over the past seven years.

That movement had increased their clashes with Baskerville territory.

And now that portions of Baskerville land had been leased to Morgue for ruby-mine development, Morgue had inherited the same problem.

Camus looked toward the distant horizon.

“Morgue watches them closely, but their raids are too quiet. They're difficult to detect. Worse, our defenses have a weak period once every month.”

“A weak period?”

Camus frowned at Vikir's question.

“Morgue is matriarchal. Women greatly outnumber men, and most of the mages guarding the frontier are women.”

“What does that have to do with a monthly weakness?”

“...There are a few days every month. And mana makes it more troublesome.”

“But you're mages, aren't you?”

Camus opened her mouth, stared at him for a moment, then burst out laughing.

“You really are slow about certain things, aren't you?”

“...?”

“Fine. I like that. A man who's too clever is boring. Bonus points.”

Only after Camus patted him on the shoulder a few times did Vikir finally understand what she meant.

Just then—

“Deputy Fortress Commander!”

Someone called for Camus from a distance.

A female mage hurried over and dropped to one knee.

“The Dark Faction search party captured a tribal scout alive!”

They had taken a prisoner.

◆◆◆

The man dragged forward in ropes had brown skin and black hair.

One look at the tattoos covering him was enough for Vikir to identify his origin.

*Rokoko.*

A shamanic tribe.

Whatever circumstances had led to his capture, his fate here was already nearly decided.

Camus Morgue faced the prisoner with an oppressive presence.

“Did you get anything out of him?”

The mages beside her looked uncomfortable.

“He refuses to speak.”

“Mind magic?”

“It doesn't work. A powerful shamanic spell is protecting his memories.”

Camus turned toward the captive and slowly approached.

“You attacked a Morgue fortress before and carried away several slaves.”

“...”

“One of them was a girl of Morgue. My foster younger sister. Her name is **Rosie**.”

Camus stared at the Rokoko captive with sharpened eyes.

“What did you do to her?”

At last, the man's tightly closed mouth opened.

He gave a short answer in his own language.

Camus's brow furrowed.

“Translate it. Where are the defectors who can interpret tribal languages?”

No one answered.

The surrounding mages only exchanged uneasy looks.

Finally one of them spoke apologetically.

“They were all killed or taken away during the last raid, Deputy Fortress Commander.”

“Then there's nobody here who can translate for me?”

“Not at the moment.”

It was an awkward problem.

While everyone stood there at a loss, Vikir stepped forward.

“I know a little Rokoko.”

Camus turned on him in surprise.

“You know that too? Is there anything you *can't* do?”

“I'm not fluent. I only know some basic words.”

Vikir moved beside her.

Camus said,

“Ask where my younger sister is. The girl they took in the last raid. Red hair, red eyes, unusually pale skin. Around twelve years old.”

Vikir nodded and addressed the prisoner in Rokoko.

The man answered briefly.

Vikir's expression hardened.

After a moment, he looked at Camus and shook his head.

“She died.”

Every Morgue face nearby darkened.

They had understood from the moment of Rosie's abduction that death was possible.

Actually hearing it confirmed was different.

Camus stepped forward.

Her voice came out low and vicious.

“When this war ends, your language will be spoken only in hell.”

Those were the last words the prisoner heard.

Camus addressed the others.

“The Light Faction's Grand Senator is receiving treatment. The Dark Faction's Grand Senator is inspecting the territory on the opposite side. That leaves the decision here to me, Camus Morgue, Deputy Fortress Commander.”

The summary trial ended there.

Camus moved both hands and traced a magic circle through the air.

Then—

PUK!

A huge iron spike erupted from the earth.

Iron hidden among the soil gathered together in an instant, growing into a single brutal skewer.

It drove through the Rokoko prisoner from groin to crown before he could even scream.

The body was lifted high into the air.

Then flames ignited below it.

ROAR!

Iron-spike execution by fire.

In moments, Camus burned the prisoner to death.

Every slave who witnessed it stared at her in terror.

Even the Morgue mages looked much the same.

Crackle. Crackle. Pop.

The body burned around the iron spike.

The smell of scorched flesh spread through the air while black ash drifted away on the wind.

Camus smiled calmly in front of the terrible flames.

“Let's go.”

She took Vikir away with her.

Everyone around them watched their backs with a quiet trace of fear.

◆◆◆

Once they had returned behind the earthen wall, Vikir found himself surprised.

Not because he had watched a living man impaled and burned.

Before his regression, Vikir had spent decades on battlefields and witnessed things far worse than that.

What surprised him was Camus's expression.

“...Hic... ngh...”

She had found a place where no one else could see her.

Now she was crying.

Her face twisted as her eyes reddened. Tears rolled down her still-round cheeks.

Vikir's mouth opened slightly in disbelief.

To think he would see Camus Morgue—the future Queen of Fire and Skewers—crying like this.

He had seen her cry when she was eight, but seeing it now at fifteen felt different.

*She really is still fifteen.*

Vikir stared awkwardly for a while before finally attempting to comfort her.

“...Why are you crying?”

“Why do you think I'm crying!?”

Camus snapped, then hurriedly looked around to make certain nobody had heard her.

Vikir fell silent for a moment before trying again.

“You were very close to your younger sister.”

“Yes. She always followed me around. She was innocent and kind—almost too kind for Morgue.”

Camus leaned against the wall and crouched down.

They were nearly the same height, but at that moment she somehow looked much smaller.

Vikir searched for something useful to say.

“Don't grieve too much. She should have gone peacefully.”

Camus glared at him through tears.

“Do you think I'm an idiot?”

There was anger in the question, and grief underneath it.

Vikir understood at once.

Camus had understood the Rokoko captive.

“I'm a genius. I can't speak the language, but I can understand some of it.”

“...”

“Tell me directly. Did I hear him correctly?”

Vikir could only nod heavily.

The captive's answer had not meant *dead*.

It had meant—

*Eaten.*

The Rokoko were a shamanic clan that also practiced cannibalism.

Eating captives was one of their customs.

Once Vikir confirmed it, tears ran down Camus's face again.

“...I'm sorry. I'm sorry I couldn't protect you. I'm sorry, Rosie.”

Camus wept openly.

Vikir simply stood beside her in silence.

He had not expected this side to exist behind the mask of Camus Morgue, the future Queen of Red and Black, fire and iron spikes.

After some time passed, Camus finally stood.

She wiped her cheeks with her sleeve and erased the dried tracks of tears.

Her usual cold expression returned.

Then she looked at Vikir, who had remained quietly nearby.

“Not bad.”

“...?”

“If you'd tried to offer me some cheap sympathy, I would have killed you.”

A threat from a grieving fifteen-year-old girl was hardly frightening to a veteran who had survived decades of war, but Vikir nodded anyway.

Sometimes simply remaining beside someone was its own kind of comfort.

Vikir had chosen silence because he had no idea what else to do with a crying fifteen-year-old girl.

This time, it had happened to be the right answer.

Camus gave his chest a light thump.

“There's no time to stay sad. We need to pull ourselves together and take revenge as soon as possible.”

“...”

“Come with me. There's something we need to do together.”

Her voice had become firm again.
