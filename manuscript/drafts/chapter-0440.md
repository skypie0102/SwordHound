# Chapter 440: Dreaming the Impossible Dream (4)

House Donquixote had been recovered.

The knights who once crossed vast lands and seas behind a single spear—

were freed from demonic control.

And once again—

served the rightful successor.

**Tudor.**

Son of Cervantes.

The legitimate head of House Donquixote.

The lion cub once driven from his home—

had returned grown.

The evil uncle was dead.

The father avenged.

In another era—

the entire Empire would have shaken.

Now—

almost no one noticed.

Civil war killed thousands.

Sometimes tens of thousands.

Every day.

Even the restoration of one of the Seven Great Houses—

could disappear beneath that noise.

For the Night Walkers—

however—

the victory changed everything.

“The supply lines are open again.”

Dolores sounded relieved.

Routes the demons had severed—

were restored.

Food.

Water.

Supplies.

All could move toward **Tochka** again.

The growing refugee population had been approaching disaster.

Vikir looked through the conference-room window.

Tochka.

High walls.

Thin air.

Sheer cliffs.

A fortress built like a solitary iron wall.

Dolores studied the map.

“The problem is still water.”

“If we could solve that locally…”

“There should be a source beyond at least one of these mountains.”

Vikir showed no concern.

“The water problem will solve itself soon.”

Everyone looked at him.

“…How?”

Vikir did not answer.

At the other end of the table—

Tudor spoke.

“We now have a complete assessment of House Donquixote’s remaining military strength.”

As the new family head—

he had chosen full cooperation with the Night Walkers.

“We removed the demon-linked personnel.”

“Those purged or driven out earlier are being restored.”

Vikir asked:

“How many can still fight?”

Tudor slid over documents normally kept secret even from allies.

“Better than expected.”

“About sixty percent of our overall strength is intact.”

“But most of that is the fleet.”

“The cavalry…”

He hesitated.

Then glanced toward Camus.

She sat with her arms crossed.

“What?”

“Are you blaming me for destroying your cavalry?”

“No.”

Tudor answered quickly.

“The Invincible Cavalry suffered because the demons had them charging around the civil-war front while brainwashed.”

“My own failures and the demons are enough to blame.”

He smiled awkwardly.

No one laughed.

House Donquixote’s famed cavalry had been nearly ruined.

Killing Chimeries—

even wearing the demon’s hide as a trophy—

could not erase that loss.

A strip of Chimeries’s skin now hung from Tudor’s Gungnir.

Vikir looked at it.

“Keep that.”

“It will strengthen the morale of the knights around you.”

Tudor nodded.

“…Thank you.”

Then continued.

“The Invincible Armada is different.”

“It suffered almost no damage.”

“The sea level fell too far for the fleet to approach the coast.”

“So it barely participated in the civil war.”

Vikir’s gaze sharpened.

“That is enough.”

The fleet—

not the cavalry—

had always been his greatest concern.

Sinclaire leaned forward.

“Bro.”

“Why do we need the Invincible Armada so badly?”

“The civil war is happening in the center of the continent.”

“There’s almost no naval warfare.”

“And with sea levels this low—

the fleet isn’t even useful for coastal supply.”

Everyone shared the question.

Vikir looked up from the map.

“Soon—

the fleet will become extremely important.”

“And when that happens—

Tochka’s water problem will disappear too.”

Before anyone could ask—

“Puhishishi.”

Sade groaned loudly.

“Meeting.”

“Meeting.”

“Meeting.”

“Do you people never get tired of this?”

Few people interrupted Vikir casually.

Marquis de Sade—

was one of them.

He raised two fingers.

“Vikir.”

“You remember, right?”

“Two left.”

When they escaped Nouvelle Vague—

Sade owed Vikir a life.

In repayment—

he had promised to kill three people Vikir selected.

Orca—

sitting nearby with folded arms—

had made a parallel promise.

“I am not like this criminal.”

“I honor agreements.”

“You asked me to protect three people.”

“Two remain.”

Sade’s first fulfilled debt—

Passamonte.

Orca’s—

Tudor.

Indirect or not—

the obligations had counted.

“I remember.”

Vikir nodded.

Then looked around.

“Is Cindywendy still here?”

“We need information on House Usher before deciding the next move.”

“I’m not omnipresent.”

The conference-room door opened.

Cindywendy entered with a stack of documents.

She sat down.

“Working your future sister-in-law a little hard lately, aren’t you?”

Vikir looked at her.

“I thought you hated connections to Baskerville.”

Cindywendy smiled.

“People change.”

“I let go of my hatred toward House Baskerville some time ago.”

Then—

“What about you?”

“Still carrying yours?”

Vikir went silent.

Cindywendy had reconciled with the family that destroyed hers.

She claimed she would become its mistress—

rule from inside—

but in practice—

she had abandoned revenge.

Chosen compromise.

“Enough.”

Vikir’s voice came sharper than usual.

Everyone at the table flinched.

Even Sade and Orca.

That tone appeared rarely—

usually only around demons.

Cindywendy smoothly changed direction.

“Oh, scary.”

“You sound exactly like when you stood outside my prison bars.”

“Fine.”

“Main points only.”

She pushed the papers forward.

**1. Signs the Imperial civil war is escalating.**

**2. House Morgue and House Baskerville have allied.**

**3. House Quovadis and House Bourgeois have allied.**

**4. House Leviathan and House Usher have allied.**

**5. House Donquixote has declared neutrality and closed its gates.**

Cindywendy continued.

“Quovadis has also declared war on Leviathan over the old Red Death incident.”

“And Donquixote’s neutrality is public knowledge now.”

Tudor himself had announced it.

The great institutions of the Empire moved slowly.

But once moving—

they accelerated with terrifying momentum.

House Donquixote would officially close itself.

Repair civil-war damage.

Stabilize the succession.

Quietly—

it would support the Night Walkers.

Camus rested her chin on one hand.

“Morgue and Baskerville together…”

“Looks like Mother really decided to back the Second Prince.”

“The prince nobody even knows exists.”

Dolores answered from across the table.

“Maybe not support.”

“Maybe balance.”

“Quovadis is doing the same thing.”

The two women’s gazes crossed.

Sinclaire reached from behind Vikir and pointed at the map.

“Bourgeois is searching for the Second Prince too.”

“The strongest lead remains the rumor that an Imperial-family student enrolled at the Academy.”

Civil war—

in the end—

was a fight over legitimacy.

Demons united under one objective.

Humans—

split beneath many flags.

Vikir remembered the mysterious Second Prince.

Before regression—

the man never appeared.

Nobody had ever even confirmed whether he truly existed.

*If he is alive—*

*maybe humanity can unite sooner this time.*

Vikir looked at Cindywendy.

“Among the Seven Great Houses—

which has the strongest intelligence network?”

“House Usher.”

She answered immediately.

“They specialize in assassination.”

“Infiltration.”

“Reconnaissance.”

“No one else invests as heavily in that kind of talent.”

The door opened.

Bianca entered.

Excitement flushed her face.

“The brainwashing on the God-Killing Sniper Squad has been broken.”

“They told me everything happening inside the family.”

Vikir’s eyes darkened.

House Usher—

still under demonic influence.

Still one of the centers of the civil war.

With Donquixote restored—

the greatest immediate threats were Leviathan and Usher.

And the current leader of House Usher—

**Madeline**—

was suspected of housing the **Third Corpse**.

Tudor stood first.

When House Donquixote fell—

Bianca had saved him.

Now—

Tudor.

Vikir.

All the others.

Reached toward her.

“Come with us.”

Tudor spoke softly.

“Your family can still be saved.”

Bianca froze.

Then—

light returned to eyes that had been dull for years.

Her eyes reddened.

Vikir stood.

“We move quickly.”

“Before news of Donquixote’s recovery spreads.”

Every Night Walker rose with him.

Determination burned in their expressions.

Sade grinned.

“So the second target is decided?”

Orca stood too.

“This should reduce what I owe.”

The next demon hunt—

would begin immediately.

For Vikir—

who usually left long gaps between hunts—

that alone showed how little time remained.
