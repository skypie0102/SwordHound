Vikir left the inner castle where the Great Banquet had been held.

He passed through the main gate and reached the fork between the training grounds and the surrounding castle buildings.

Then stopped.

“…”

Killing intent leaked from beyond a hedge.

Too obvious to be accidental.

A deliberate greeting.

A heartbeat later—

a dark red sword aura slithered toward him like a snake.

Thick as honey.

Red as blood.

A strike only a **Peak Sword Graduator** could release.

Six serrated Fangs tore through the air.

*Should I counter?*

Vikir had enough power to block the attack and answer in kind.

But the strength he had publicly revealed was only Intermediate Sword Graduator.

That limited what he could safely show.

He drew the longsword at his waist.

Blocked.

Redirected.

Rolled with the force.

In that brief exchange, Vikir released **four Fangs**.

The incoming attack contained six.

Four collided and destroyed one another.

Two remained.

Vikir chose not to evade.

They struck him.

The blessing of the Styx transformed his body into something like black iron.

Clang!

Crash!

The ordinary sword reinforced with his publicly limited Intermediate Sword Graduator aura broke in two.

A piece of his cloak tore away.

The deflected force carved a deep scar through the ground.

The two Fangs that actually reached Vikir's body shattered before they could even meaningfully test the Styx's protection.

“…!”

That told him something.

The attacker had never intended to kill him.

Laughter came from behind the hedge.

“My nephew really has grown.”

Boston Terrier stepped into view.

One of the Seven Counts.

He smiled at Vikir.

Vikir reset his stance.

Half a broken sword remained in his hand.

He was ready to fight with it.

Boston's eyes lit with even greater approval.

Then a new voice cut between them.

“Who draws a sword like a lunatic inside the family estate?”

A massive figure swept into the gap, black cloak snapping behind him.

Great Dane.

He glared at Boston.

“Attempted murder of a direct relative. If Brother Hugo learns about this, you'll be confined for months.”

“Don't slander an uncle's love for his nephew.”

“Love?”

Great Dane's expression flattened.

“You hid in a bush and fired a sword aura at him.”

“Children grow through fighting.”

“You're the child.”

Boston bared his teeth.

“Stay out of the next Pit Bull's affairs.”

“The next Pit Bull?”

Great Dane snorted.

“He'll be the next Mastiff. Why would our precious nephew join your pack of mongrels?”

“Your people puff themselves up until a blade opens their bellies. Then they beg.”

“At least we have bellies to open.”

The two Counts glared at one another.

Hugo was no longer present.

There was nothing to restrain them.

Boston drew his sword.

“Fine! Whoever wins gets Vikir!”

Great Dane drew his own.

“For my beloved nephew's future, gladly.”

Steel faced steel.

“…”

Vikir had already walked away.

*Exhausting people.*

He had expected Boston's blunt personality to drive him into demanding a direct display sooner or later.

Fortunately, Great Dane existed to absorb the problem.

As Vikir left, he considered the power behind the attack.

*Stronger than I expected.*

Before regression, the Seven Counts had all stood at the summit of the Sword Graduator realm.

The current Seven Counts were much the same.

Vikir's own hidden realm was also **Peak Sword Graduator**.

But realm alone did not decide swordsmanship.

Boston and Great Dane had mastered the **Sixth Fang**.

Vikir had reached the **Seventh Fang**.

Then there was the Styx.

And Beelzebub.

*One of the Seven Counts should be manageable.*

He considered it honestly.

*Two at once would be difficult.*

An absurd level of combat power for a seventeen-year-old body.

Vikir continued arranging future plans.

Then—

“Little brother!”

Another voice approached from behind.

Vikir turned.

A foul smell struck him immediately.

Seth Le Baskerville.

Hugo's second son walked toward him with a friendly smile.

“Where are you sleeping tonight?”

“…”

“If you haven't decided, come stay at my castle. It's nearby.”

Vikir watched him chatter.

What kind of thing had to be hiding beneath that skin to produce a stench like rotting corpses?

Whatever it was—

it was not ordinary evil.

Between Cindywendy's investigation and Chihuahua's reports, Vikir already had evidence connecting Seth to multiple kidnappings and the disappearance of adopted children.

Those crimes had formed the most decisive part of the false case used against Vikir before regression.

And there was more.

Cindywendy had uncovered facts Vikir never expected.

Two venomous snakes had been sent to his cradle when he was an infant.

The order came through Seth's side of the family.

And Penelope La Baskerville's disappearance into Rokoko hands had not been a random accident.

Evidence indicated that Seth had arranged it.

Seventeen years earlier, Seth's people bought two **Bloody Mambas** from natives of the Depths.

They also leaked Penelope's walking route and schedule to Rokoko.

Cindywendy had found the traces while investigating tribal trade for Vikir.

*And Ah'Heman was the one who sold the Bloody Mambas.*

Vikir's mouth curved dryly.

By killing Ah'Heman in the Depths, he had unknowingly collected a debt dating back to his own infancy.

Seth escaped suspicion at the time for two reasons.

He cultivated the image of a kind, weak young man.

And he himself had still been a child.

No one thought to put him on the suspect list.

Vikir looked at the figure in front of him.

At Seth's skin.

At whatever had worn it for years inside Baskerville.

“So, little brother, what will you do now?”

Seth remained close.

“Return to Underdog as Deputy Magistrate?”

His smile did not falter.

“Or enter the Academy?”

Before Vikir could answer—

“I told you to stop, Seth.”

Osiris arrived on horseback.

Once again, he placed himself between Seth and Vikir.

His voice was severe.

“Do not make noise in front of the main residence.”

He looked from one brother to the other.

“It is late. Continue this conversation another time.”

To anyone without context, Osiris would have looked like a rigid, unpleasant elder brother.

Before regression, Vikir had thought exactly that.

Now he simply bowed.

“Then I will visit and greet my brothers another time.”

Osiris's face eased slightly.

Seth's expression stiffened.

Vikir's answer gave neither of them grounds for complaint.

The eldest son had spoken.

The reason was proper.

Vikir had accepted without insulting the second son.

A neutral response.

An experienced one.

Even Osiris seemed to approve.

◆◆◆

Baskerville possessed many old castles.

Centuries of construction had left the estate filled with forgotten passages, abandoned corners, and secluded rooms.

Vikir chose one of those older buildings for the night.

The castle where he had spent much of his childhood.

He passed a flooded cellar entrance.

An abandoned storehouse.

A spiral stair threading between unrepaired cracks in the stone.

Memories from more than a decade ago guided him down the dark corridor.

Then—

a familiar feeling stopped him.

Vikir turned.

The scene ahead seemed to belong to his childhood.

Three figures waited.

The Baskerville triplets.

Sometimes called Baskerville's Trident.

Highbro Le Baskerville.

Middlebro Le Baskerville.

Lowbro Le Baskerville.

His half-brothers had not spoken to him once during the banquet.

Now they were eighteen.

Tall.

Broad.

Their jawlines harder than before.

“…”

“…”

“…”

And yet all three still stiffened slightly in front of Vikir.

A dry smile crossed his face.

They had swords.

They had chosen a remote corridor.

Their purpose seemed obvious.

“What?”

Vikir's hand was already ready to draw.

Shing—

The triplets unsheathed their swords together.

No bloodlust.

But their posture carried absolute resolve.

*Then I'll kill them.*

A hound that openly showed its fangs without knowing when to hide them was useless.

Better to silence all three here.

It would also bury what they had witnessed in the Depths two years ago.

Vikir prepared to move.

Then—

Thud.

All three brothers dropped to one knee.

Their heads bowed.

Each sword turned inward.

The tip rested against the top of its owner's right foot.

The hilt faced Vikir.

“…?”

For once, Vikir did not immediately understand.

Then recognition came.

This was not a challenge.

It was an oath.

The formal oath used by knights of the Empire—

when they stood before the master they had chosen to follow.
