Established terminology controls **Ballak Tribe**, **Ahun**, **Aiyen**, **Ah'Heman**, **Camus Morgue**, **Baskerville Fang Sword Style**, **Sixth Fang**, **High Sword Graduator**, **Peak Sword Graduator**, **Sword Master**, **Beelzebub**, **River Styx**, **Hugo Le Baskerville**, and **Rokoko**.

The jungle's seasons were brutally distinct.

Summer.

Mad summer.

Winter.

Mad winter.

Beyond the endless water and the sea of trees, the seasons had turned more than once.

Now the jungle had reached one of those quiet moments between them.

THUD.

A dull impact sounded across a meadow.

A young Ballak warrior dropped backward, clutching his nose.

“My nose!”

Blood ran between his fingers.

It was Ahun.

A short distance in front of him stood Vikir, face expressionless, one fist still extended.

He had grown taller.

His black hair had been cut short without much care.

His gaze was still cold.

His skin remained noticeably pale beside the Ballak hunters.

Vikir lowered his hand.

“That's enough.”

He turned and walked away.

Ahun grabbed a companion's arm and pulled himself upright.

“Damn monster.”

He spat into the grass.

“He's gotten worse since his body recovered.”

The hunters around him laughed.

“He was already strong.”

“His archery is ridiculous now too. They say he's almost at Captain Aiyen's level.”

“Come on. No one's catching Captain Aiyen that easily.”

“And that punch wasn't anything special. Ahun only got a nosebleed.”

Ahun's voice dropped.

“……It didn't hit me.”

The laughter stopped.

“What?”

“His fist.”

Ahun looked at Vikir's back in the distance.

“He stopped before touching my face.”

Only then did the others understand.

The blow had ended several centimeters short.

The pressure alone had struck Ahun hard enough to make his nose bleed and his bones feel as though they had shifted.

One hunter swallowed.

“That was just the wind from his fist?”

No one laughed after that.

◆◆◆

Vikir had just turned seventeen.

Much of the childish softness had left his face.

His height had risen sharply.

The roundness in his cheeks had thinned.

Twenty-four months in the jungle had changed more than his appearance.

His injuries were gone.

His body had become denser and stronger.

His instincts had sharpened.

His spirit had hardened further.

Once Ahun and the other hunters were out of sight, Vikir stopped holding back.

PSSHT.

The artery at his wrist opened.

**Beelzebub** emerged.

The blood-forged blade had grown thicker and longer.

Nearly a meter of dark edge extended from his wrist.

Vikir tested a slash.

CRACK.

A boulder split.

He thrust.

THOOM.

A second stone gained a clean hole through its center.

Then Vikir moved again.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Six boulders shattered almost at once.

Torn.

Split.

Pierced.

Impaled.

Cut.

Crushed.

Six different wounds.

Six predatory teeth.

*Sixth Fang.*

Vikir had fully mastered the **Sixth Fang of the Baskerville Fang Sword Style**.

The six streams of aura withdrew.

Mana churned through his body in six stable rotations.

He had crossed into the realm of a **High Sword Graduator**.

*Still not enough.*

His target was higher.

Before regression, forty-year-old Vikir had never crossed the wall into Sword Master.

He had died at the level of a **Peak Sword Graduator**.

That realm lay between High Sword Graduator and Sword Master.

Aura at that stage became so dense and viscous that it seemed almost solid.

Vikir had only just reached High Sword Graduator in formal rank.

But rank and combat ability were not identical.

River Styx had altered his body.

Beelzebub gave him abilities ordinary swordsmen did not possess.

Ballak had taught him archery, stalking, and killing methods foreign to Baskerville's normal curriculum.

When Vikir used everything at once—

*I can fight at Peak Sword Graduator level.*

That did not make him one.

Forcing out combat power above one's true realm was not the same as owning that realm.

And his real target remained Hugo Le Baskerville.

A Sword Master.

Vikir dismissed Beelzebub and returned to the village.

At the entrance, young hunters waited for Ah'Heman's blessing before leaving.

Each had charcoal smeared thickly across the face.

Ballak believed that if prey could not remember a hunter's face, its spirit would have more difficulty taking revenge.

Ah'Heman saw Vikir approaching late and glared.

He clearly disliked him.

But refusing to bless the tribe's most successful young hunter would create another problem.

If Vikir kept bringing back the best prey without the shaman's blessing, people might begin wondering whether the blessing mattered at all.

Ah'Heman therefore dragged charcoal across Vikir's face with minimum effort.

Pale skin still showed through.

“The god of the hunt will be with you.”

“Thank you.”

Neither man meant much by the exchange.

Nearby, Aiyen was listening to a returning patrol.

Her expression was unusually serious.

Vikir walked over.

“Not hunting?”

“Later.”

That was unusual enough to make him look twice.

Aiyen usually found an excuse to accompany him before he even asked.

Today she looked distracted.

Vikir did not press.

He took a newly forged longsword, a bow, and arrows and entered the forest alone.

No wolf.

No hunting party.

He no longer needed either.

Only one person watched him go.

Aiyen.

When Vikir finally disappeared among the trees, she looked back at the scout.

“Again. From the beginning.”

The scout nodded.

“Four reports.”

He raised one finger.

“First. The rainy season is coming.”

Patrols had begun seeing lungfish.

They possessed lungs as well as gills and could survive for long periods outside the water.

During dry periods they buried themselves deep in wet mud.

As humidity rose before the rains, they emerged and crawled across the ground on their fins.

Ballak hunters considered scaleless flesh unclean and rarely ate them.

That made lungfish less important as prey—

but extremely useful as a seasonal warning.

Rain meant floods.

Disease.

Shifting hunting grounds.

Preparation had to begin early.

The scout raised a second finger.

“Second. Suspicious outsiders.”

Aiyen's eyes narrowed.

The intruders had pale skin.

Imperials.

At first the patrol assumed they might be remnants of the merchant and mercenary force destroyed two years earlier.

They were not.

These people entered quietly.

Went directly to the river's source.

Emptied red liquid from glass containers into the water.

Then attempted to leave without being noticed.

Ballak captured one.

The prisoner swallowed poison hidden inside his mouth and died before interrogation.

The only useful object recovered from him was a dagger.

A huge serpent had been engraved into the metal.

Aiyen kept it.

Vikir knew an irritating amount about Imperial affairs.

He would probably recognize the mark.

“Third?”

“Rokoko.”

Ballak's rivals.

Where Ballak produced archers, Rokoko produced shamans and curse-workers.

Imperials grouped much of their craft under the term **black magic**.

Recently, however, Rokoko hunters had almost vanished from their own territory.

That was strange.

Their population was nearly ten times Ballak's.

A tribe that large did not simply disappear.

The scout lifted his last finger.

“Fourth. Morgue search parties.”

That was the report Aiyen cared about most.

“They came again?”

“Yes. More often than before.”

“The commander?”

“The same woman.”

Aiyen clicked her tongue.

For two years, Morgue search teams had repeatedly entered the jungle.

Their stated purposes varied.

Suppression.

Surveying.

Territorial searches.

But the field commander never changed.

**Camus Morgue.**

And now her search line had come dangerously close.
