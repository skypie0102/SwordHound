“What's this?”

“The Cold Department idiots?”

**Granola de Leviathan.**

Tall.

Dark-haired.

Handsome in a cold, unfriendly way.

A Hot Department first-year—

and third son of House Leviathan.

He looked toward Tudor and smiled mockingly.

“What are you scared of?”

“You're shaking hard enough for me to feel it from here.”

“You lunatic.”

Tudor cut him off with the ease of someone used to this behavior.

Granola continued anyway.

“I heard you talking about the Night Hound.”

“You're Colosseo students and *this* frightened?”

“Embarrassing.”

Tudor pointed at Vikir.

“He saw the Night Hound himself.”

Granola's eyes shifted.

“Vikir?”

“He saw the Night Hound?”

“His name is Vikir.”

Granola looked at Tudor.

“You're boasting because you memorized a commoner's name?”

His gaze swept over Vikir—

half curious—

half contemptuous.

“Anyway.”

“Stop trembling.”

“If the Night Hound appears at the Academy—”

“I'll personally protect you useless people.”

Tudor scoffed.

“You're putting on a show.”

“We talked like that before too.”

“Then we saw what happened.”

“The news says the Night Hound is at least Graduator-level.”

“How exactly are you dealing with a Graduator?”

Granola laughed.

“You believe newspapers?”

“Naive.”

“Rumors inflate everything.”

“A coward hiding behind a mask can't possibly be that impressive.”

“And besides—”

He gestured for the students behind him to step away.

Psssh—

a faint black mist spread from Granola's body.

Paralytic poison.

Mana carried it outward like water vapor.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

All jumped back.

“Granola!”

“You're using force inside the Academy!?”

“This?”

Granola smirked.

“Hardly.”

“A small demonstration.”

The poison itself was weak by Leviathan standards.

Skin contact would produce numbness—

perhaps stiffness—

for several minutes.

Nothing more.

But the reputation of House Leviathan was enough to make students retreat.

Everyone—

except Vikir.

“…?”

He had been about to step back too.

Then—

[Hoo—]

Something inhaled.

The black mist moving toward Vikir—

vanished.

Baby Madam—

wrapped around his left wrist like a watch—

had opened her mouth.

Gulp.

The poison disappeared so quickly—

it looked as though it had never existed.

Like someone slurping a mouthful of noodles.

[Hurk—hic.]

Baby Madam swallowed the weak paralytic—

burped—

then stuck out her tongue.

Vikir looked down.

*Perfect.*

*Food.*

Then back at Granola.

*Leviathan.*

*He probably has plenty of poison.*

Vikir's attention toward the boy sharpened.

Tudor, Sancho, and Pigi—

meanwhile—

had no idea what happened.

“Where did the poison go?”

“It just disappeared.”

“That scared me.”

“Was it fake?”

Even Granola looked around.

“…Huh?”

“Where did my poison go?”

Tudor stared.

“You released it.”

“And now *you're* surprised?”

“Are you stupid?”

Granola stiffened.

Then recovered with heroic desperation.

“I merely displayed mercy deeper than the abyss.”

“Using poison on muscle-brained Cold Department fools would be wasteful.”

His gaze moved to Vikir and Pigi.

“Commoner trash.”

“Neither of you even looks properly Expert-level.”

“If that's the level of the Cold Department—”

“this Academy is becoming boring.”

Colosseo's freshmen—

on average—

stood between lower and intermediate Expert.

Graduating students—

around upper Expert.

Tudor—

two years younger than most of his class—

had already reached upper Expert.

Sancho stood at intermediate Expert.

Pigi—

lower Expert.

Outside Colosseo—

Pigi would still be regarded as impressively talented.

Inside—

he fell below the freshman average.

Tudor stepped forward.

“Don't insult my friends, Granola.”

Granola did not flinch.

“You can measure a person by their companions.”

“Looking at yours tells me enough.”

The Hot Department elites behind him laughed.

“Don't disgrace the Academy with mediocre strength.”

“Midterm practical evaluation will prove everything.”

Tudor's expression hardened.

“It's our first real evaluation.”

“It exists to record progress.”

“Not to prove someone's worth.”

“Shut up.”

Granola's eyes flashed.

“For me—”

“the midterm is life or death.”

“Take it seriously.”

“Then I'll crush someone wasting mediocre talent like you.”

At that moment—

“Vikir!”

“What are you doing over there?”

A familiar voice approached.

**Sinclaire.**

A commoner—

and the top-ranked first-year of the Hot Department.

She smiled the moment she reached Vikir.

“Did you finish the books from the library?”

“The spider-raising books?”

“Yes.”

“I read some too.”

“Want to compare notes later?”

“Sure.”

Sinclaire brightened.

“Were you studying ahead for Practical Monster Strategy?”

“No.”

“Then why study it in advance?”

“Tell me too.”

“I want to score well on the exam.”

Her eyes were sincere—

curious—

fixed on Vikir.

Granola's expression became complicated.

“Of course.”

“Useless commoners only have one tactic.”

“Cling together and combine their tiny strength—”

He stopped mid-sentence.

Then called toward Sinclaire.

“H-Hey!”

“Sin… Sin… Sinclaire!”

“Are you trying to disgrace the Hot Department?”

“Come over here!”

“If you need someone to teach you—”

“I'll do it!”

“N-Not because I particularly care.”

“But you're temporarily the top student of our department.”

“If you keep associating with Cold Department people—”

“it damages our image—”

Sinclaire blinked.

Tilted her head.

“…Who are you?”

Granola froze.

“What?”

“You don't know me!?”

“I'm Granola de Leviathan!”

“Vice representative of the Hot Department first-years!”

“Third son of House Leviathan!”

“Your future senior!”

“How can you remember some commoner named Vikir or Vekir or whatever—”

“and not me!?”

Sinclaire looked genuinely apologetic.

“I… really don't.”

“…”

“Sorry.”

“I'll remember next time.”

“Mr. Kurukuru.”

Granola took visible damage.

“…Unbelievable.”

His companions hurried to him.

“Calm down.”

“She's using psychological warfare.”

“Obviously she knows you.”

“Commoners are like that…”

Granola's shoulders sagged anyway.

“See you later, Vikir.”

“That arrogant Granola.”

“What about her?”

“Sinclaire.”

“Did something happen between you?”

“Why is she only that friendly with you?”

“The way she looked at you was practically dripping honey.”

“No.”

“He's terrifying.”

“If he's Graduator-level, he could join a knight order.”

“Hey.”

“You aren't the Night Hound, are you?”

“You were the only one who fell behind on the way back to the dormitory.”

“only you don't have an alibi.”

“Come on.”

“I was joking.”

“Sorry.”
