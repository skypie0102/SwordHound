# Chapter 418: End game (10)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/418.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 416 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Poseidon, Nouvelle Vague, Gate of Good and Evil, Flubber, Aiyen, Decarabia, Tzersi, and Vikir. The creature hatched from Poseidon remains intentionally unnamed in this chapter; do not prematurely assign a species beyond source description.

BOOM!

A storm erupted.

Then—

emptiness.

A huge crater remained.

Dust—

gone.

Smoke—

gone.

Water.

Steam.

Groundwater.

Lava spray.

Sulfur gas.

Everything in the blast zone had been swept away.

The ground looked polished.

The air—

unnaturally dry.

No flame remained.

No drifting gas.

At the center of that perfectly cleared wasteland—

two men knelt on one knee.

Clothes torn.

Blood running from their foreheads.

Weapons driven into the ground to anchor their bodies.

They had endured the light—

heat—

and pressure.

One—

Warden Orca.

A man sustained by the will to protect and maintain.

The other—

Marquis de Sade.

A man sustained by the will to destroy.

Opposite purposes.

Opposite lives.

Yet—

the same result.

Neither had fallen.

Both began standing again.

Then—

“…What?”

“What in God’s name did you throw into that clash?”

They finally saw it.

RUMBLE—

Blue flame rose from the crater.

A thick pillar.

Straight upward.

It tore through Nouvelle Vague.

Level Nine.

Eight.

Seven.

Six.

Five.

Four.

Three.

Two.

One.

And beyond.

At the top—

the Gate of Good and Evil blocked the pillar.

Chains snapped one by one.

Winches burst.

The enormous door began glowing red from heat.

Anyone looking at it—

could see that something catastrophic was happening.

Flubber’s remaining mucus protection evaporated before the flame even reached it.

Incoming seawater turned to vapor.

“What is that?”

For once—

Orca looked genuinely stunned.

No one else understood either.

D’Ordume.

Souaré.

Sadi.

Aiyen.

Marquis de Sade.

All stared at the pillar.

Only Vikir moved.

BOOM!

He walked toward its source.

The heat was unbelievable.

Blue fire.

Far hotter than ordinary flame.

A violent updraft tried to lift his body.

Even the burns he had repeatedly endured while maintaining Garam’s scarred disguise—

were nothing compared with this.

*This much burning is irrelevant now.*

Vikir pushed through.

He gripped the ground whenever the updraft threatened to carry him away.

Finally—

through fire and smoke—

Poseidon appeared.

The sphere had split into two halves.

Blue flame erupted from the opening.

The root of the pillar extended downward—

too deep to see.

Only periodic explosions—

and earthquakes from far below—

revealed the truth.

*Nouvelle Vague is not built on an extinct volcano.*

The volcano had never died.

It had only stored power.

Waiting.

And Poseidon—

that blue seed—

had rooted itself into the deepest heat of the mountain.

Drawing strength from it.

*Parasite?*

Vikir reconsidered.

*Or perhaps Nouvelle Vague itself was created around Poseidon.*

*Built as a place for this thing to hatch.*

Interesting.

But not important now.

Vikir reached into the flame.

At first—

the fire flared defensively.

Then his hand touched it.

And the flame calmed.

Like a wild animal allowing itself to be stroked.

The impossible heat became warmth.

Vikir regenerated damaged skin as he moved deeper.

Then—

he parted the blue fire.

A tiny circular hollow waited inside.

And two eyes stared back at him.

[…!]

Black scales.

Blue fire glowing between them.

A body smaller than Vikir’s palm.

Something resembling a lizard—

with wings on its back.

Born from flame.

Fed by flame.

Wrapped entirely in flame.

Vikir stared.

The creature stared back.

First meeting.

Then—

hic.

A tiny blue spark puffed from its nostrils.

Aiyen had followed him.

She looked delighted.

“Pretty.”

“So that came out of the egg?”

“A salamander?”

“No.”

Vikir answered shortly.

“Something much higher.”

He did not know its true identity either.

Only one thing.

Among the ancient beings that had opposed demons—

this thing belonged to the highest order.

A life with enormous potential.

Decarabia’s reaction confirmed it.

[Impossible!]

[This still exists?!]

[I thought they vanished ages ago!]

[I heard Mage Emperor Tzersi personally exterminated them one by one!]

[Could this be the last one in the world?!]

The inverted pentagram trembled violently.

Meanwhile—

the tiny black-and-blue creature approached Vikir.

Rubbed its cheek against the back of his hand.

Instant affection.

Trust—

toward the first being it had seen.

But the moment was brief.

FLAP.

It opened its wings.

Prepared to rise.

Its eyes met Vikir’s.

A meaning passed directly between them.

*We will meet again.*

No spoken language.

No ambiguity.

Vikir answered the same way.

*Human lives are short.*

*By the time you are grown enough to return—*

*I will already be gone.*

*We will not meet again.*

The creature looked deeply sad.

Then—

the moment came.

Its wings opened fully.

From the deepest part of the volcano—

blue fire surged upward.

Power gathered into those tiny wings.

Brighter.

Brighter.

Ready to pierce the abyss—

the ocean—

and the sky beyond.

Vikir watched.

Then turned away.

“That’s done.”

Aiyen blinked.

“What?”

“My objective in Nouvelle Vague.”

“The egg hatched.”

“There is nothing more I need here.”

“Then we can leave?”

Vikir paused.

“…That is the new problem.”

Before entering Nouvelle Vague—

he had accepted a simple conclusion.

If he found Poseidon—

activated it years early—

and completed the mission—

then even dying afterward would be acceptable.

Now—

standing here after succeeding—

another desire had appeared.

He wanted to live.

Longer.

He wanted to see humanity’s future.

How this terrible war ended.

What happened after the point his previous life had never reached.

Many people would die.

Many would survive.

And those survivors—

would keep living.

A future beyond the future Vikir remembered.

THUMP.

He grabbed Aiyen by the wrist.

“Huh?”

Vikir looked upward.

“Let’s go.”

Now—

at last—

it was truly time to leave Nouvelle Vague.

Faces surfaced in his mind.

People he missed.

People waiting above.

And a surface world—

that might have changed beyond recognition.
