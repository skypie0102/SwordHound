Parent visitation began.

The Ranking Battle arena filled with families.

Tudor and Bianca—

Cold Department.

Granola and Sinclaire—

Hot Department.

Top-performing second- and third-years also took the stage.

Dolores—

student council president and third-year leader—

was among them.

The Academy displayed its best students in front of the visiting families.

Parents reunited with children.

Some hugged.

Some praised.

Some immediately started applying pressure.

“My son!”

“I missed you!”

“Are you eating properly?”

“Daughter—

why aren’t you standing over there?”

“That arena?”

“That decides the year leader.”

“Oh.”

“That’s not a place ordinary students stand.”

“Then next year—

make sure you do.”

“Our child studies beside students like that?”

“That’s reassuring.”

“You should make friends with people like them.”

“That’s part of why we sent you here.”

Encouragement.

Pressure.

Praise.

Comparison.

New alliances.

New rivalries.

That social effect—

was one of the reasons parent visitation existed.

◆◆◆

Tudor and Bianca were already fighting—

and the match had not even started.

“My father is watching.”

“I’m not embarrassing myself today.”

“Sorry.”

“I’m stepping on you.”

“My father is here too.”

“So I’m not letting you off either.”

“You always talk like that.”

“You ranked below me in the midterms.”

“No.”

“I ranked above you.”

“You were sixth.”

“I was third.”

“That was before practical scores, attitude points, and extracurricular bonuses.”

“Overall—

I beat you by one point.”

“You’re counting all of that?”

“Doesn’t matter.”

“I’ll overturn it today.”

“Overturn?”

“Sounds more like capsizing.”

“Why does a girl talk like that?”

“Boring.”

“What does being a girl have to do with it?”

“If we’re doing this—

why is a boy like you so weak?”

“I’m not weak!”

“Just wait for the match!”

“Boring.”

“You’re boring.”

The argument continued.

Nearby—

the Hot Department final felt completely different.

Cold.

Silent.

Granola finally broke it.

“Hey, commoner.”

“Today—

I, Granola—

the new hope of the noble faction—

take back first place.”

Sinclaire did not answer.

“And the year leader position.”

“And the future student council presidency.”

“Those can’t go to a commoner either.”

Nothing.

Granola tried to maintain dignity.

He desperately wanted a reaction.

Sinclaire stared toward the distant mountains.

Then muttered:

“…Childish.”

Granola froze.

The bright—

innocent—

almost childlike atmosphere Sinclaire once carried was gone.

Now—

she somehow felt older.

Like an older sister rather than a playful girl.

Granola did not understand what had changed.

For some reason—

his heart beat faster.

Then the announcer’s voice filled the arena.

[Parents and guardians—

please pay attention!]

[The grade-level final matches are about to begin!]

The final stage of the exams—

had arrived.

◆◆◆

Far from the arena—

near an old well beside a bench beneath an old oak—

Dolores stood alone.

Her own final match would come later.

After the first- and second-year matches.

Right now—

she cared about something else.

Sinclaire.

*What am I supposed to say to her?*

Nobody had come to watch Sinclaire during parent visitation.

Dolores intended to wait until after the final—

after the crowd’s attention moved elsewhere—

then speak with her.

But how?

Since the Bourgeois incident—

Sinclaire refused to meet Dolores.

She seemed to be distancing herself from everyone.

But with Dolores—

the wall was especially high.

*Of course it is.*

*After what happened…*

Dolores sighed.

Then—

“…!”

A familiar figure crossed her vision.

Vikir.

Walking toward the old well.

Immediately—

Dolores remembered Sinclaire talking about him during the early Oracle planning.

*Should we ask Bro to join too?*

Dolores had asked whether Vikir was interested in finance.

Sinclaire’s face had brightened.

*Bro is good at this kind of thing.*

*He reads every newspaper.*

*He knows how society moves.*

*Lately he’s been especially interested in economics.*

*Western trade.*

*Old financial papers.*

*He’s really serious about it.*

Then—

*He comes to the library a lot.*

*I’ll ask him when I’m on duty.*

Sinclaire had clearly liked Vikir.

A lot.

So the two were probably close.

Dolores stood.

Maybe Vikir could help arrange a conversation.

Then—

a small child ran past her.

Black hair.

Red eyes.

Pale skin.

Round cheeks.

Adorable from head to toe.

The girl sprinted toward Vikir.

Then shouted—

“Daddy!”

Dolores froze.

Her mind went blank.

*What?*

*Already?*

*Who is the mother?!*

Before she recovered—

Vikir grabbed the girl under one arm.

Then ran.

“…Huh?”

“Wait!”

Dolores rushed down the slope.

Too late.

Vikir had already disappeared along the path near the well.

◆◆◆

Vikir moved toward a quieter route.

At the old well—

he finally set the child down.

“Pomeranian.”

“How did you get here?”

“Where is your guardian?”

“Hehehe.”

“I snuck away.”

“Big Uncle and Grandpa never let me play.”

Vikir pressed one hand to his forehead.

Cindywendy had recently told him that **they** were coming to the Imperial Capital for parent visitation.

Apparently—

Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro had been managing things well enough that everyone relaxed.

Too much.

Once the others discovered Pomeranian missing—

chaos would follow.

He needed to return her before that happened.

Vikir took her hand.

“Come on.”

“I’ll take you back.”

“Okay!”

Pomeranian still obeyed him readily.

As they walked down the path—

Vikir asked quietly:

“How is the Wraith Tree?”

“Growing well!”

“But sometimes I have nightmares.”

“Nightmares?”

“What kind?”

“Demons keep appearing.”

“They’re scary.”

Pomeranian shivered.

Vikir patted her head.

“The demons in your nightmares have nightmares too.”

“What appears in **their** nightmares?”

Vikir raised one wrist.

Beelzebub emerged.

“…This uncle.”

He made his voice deliberately ominous.

Pomeranian’s eyes sparkled.

“Wow.”

“Uncle is amazing.”

“So don’t be afraid of demons.”

“You have this uncle.”

Pomeranian nodded happily.

Good.

She could continue developing the Wraith Tree without fear.

Someday—

that power might become an enormous strength for humanity.

Then—

someone grabbed the back of Vikir’s coat.

No approach.

No footsteps.

Nothing.

Vikir turned sharply.

“Oh dear.”

“I’m thirsty.”

“Where’s the well, old man?”

An elderly woman held one corner of his coat.

Worn white priest robes.

Bent back.

Round face.

Glasses resting low on her nose.

Eyes narrowed almost shut.

At first glance—

an ordinary grandmother.

But Vikir recognized her.

And inwardly stiffened.

*This person…*

The greatest variable in today’s parent visitation—

had just appeared.
