“It’s been a while.”

The voice was lower than they remembered.

Dolores.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Sinclaire.

Every one of them stared.

Vikir.

Night Hound.

The friend who had been imprisoned in Nouvelle Vague four years ago—

stood before them.

Taller.

Broader through chest and shoulders.

Jawline sharper.

The androgynous-looking boy they remembered had become a solid young man.

And the pressure around him—

had changed even more.

It felt less like facing one person—

and more like standing before a mountain range.

The changes were so great that he almost seemed like another person.

That—

more than anything—

made him real.

Four years had passed for Vikir too.

“…How?”

Dolores and Sinclaire could only stare.

Tudor recovered first.

“Vikir!”

“Is it really you?”

“My friend Vikir?!”

“At least I believe so.”

“You bastard!”

“You made us worry ourselves sick!”

“How are you here?”

“What happened?!”

“It’s a long story.”

“Then give us the short version!”

Vikir thought for a moment.

“All right.”

He subtly stepped away before Tudor could hug him.

“I was imprisoned in Nouvelle Vague.”

“I escaped.”

“I came straight here to kill Passamonte.”

“I spent the last few days around the castle waiting for a chance to infiltrate.”

“Then you went in first.”

“So I followed.”

“…You skipped a lot at the beginning, didn’t you?”

Everyone thought the same thing.

But there was no time.

[…Kill demons.]

The Death Knight moved.

BOOM!

**Baskerville Fourth Form.**

Four fangs rushed toward Vikir.

Each one could tear open ground and sky.

And the remnant’s mastery—

was far beyond ordinary master-level execution.

Vikir remained calm.

“Fourth Form.”

“I’m confident in that one too.”

His own blade moved.

**Fourth Form.**

The swordsmanship Vikir had practiced for most of his life.

Natural as breathing.

Four fangs met four.

RUMBLE!

Hair scattered.

Blood droplets flew.

Neither side yielded.

Then the Death Knight snarled.

ROOOAR—

Black aura surged.

**Fifth Form.**

Vikir answered with the same.

Five against five.

Like fighting a mirror.

Flesh split.

Blood sprayed.

Tiny differences in left-right balance opened shallow wounds on both sides.

[KILL.]

The remnant climbed again.

**Sixth Form.**

Predatory violence.

Vikir answered—

Sixth Form.

Blade to blade.

Point to point.

Sparks exploded.

Still even.

Then—

Seventh Form.

Then—

Eighth.

Each escalation—

matched.

Until finally—

the Death Knight revealed its last card.

**Baskerville Ninth Form.**

Absolute killing intent.

RUMBLE—CRACK!

Dark-red aura shredded the surrounding hills.

Vikir watched the nine-fang net.

“…Ninth Form.”

“So this is the realm beyond the threshold of death.”

Inside this dream-world—

life and death were already blurred.

The remnant also carried Vikir’s pre-regression experience of dying once.

And because it had no rational self—

it had no ordinary fear of death.

Those impossible conditions—

combined by chance—

had produced a Ninth Form Baskerville.

Vikir himself—

had mastered the Eighth.

He could not simply match the number.

That was the limit of the living.

Or—

more precisely—

of someone who still had things to lose.

“…But this is still only residue.”

“A scorch mark left where the wheel of fate passed.”

Vikir extended Beelzebub.

FLASH.

**Baskerville Eighth Form.**

Eight fangs met nine.

“Vikir!”

Dolores stepped forward.

“Let me help!”

“No.”

“I’m enough.”

He refused her divine support.

They still had to fight the remaining Ten Corpses.

Her sacred power needed to be preserved.

And—

Vikir wanted to know how far his Eighth Form had matured.

A Ninth Form Baskerville—

even an imperfect one—

was the perfect test.

BOOM!

Eight and nine collided.

Their paths differed—

but their nature did not.

Fangs existed to bite.

CRACK.

The first side to lose balance—

was the Ninth Form.

[…!]

The Death Knight stepped back.

For the first time—

confused.

Vikir’s conclusion solidified.

*This is nothing like Cane Corso’s Ninth Form.*

The nine attacks were individually devastating.

But they moved separately.

No unified intent.

Nine hounds fighting alone.

Eight hounds hunting together—

could bring down larger prey.

That was the difference.

RUMBLE!

Vikir’s eight fangs began rotating.

A sphere.

A **Black Sun**.

The killing method he had learned indirectly from Cane Corso.

Every trajectory—

every force—

converged.

The disordered Ninth Form broke against it.

CRACK—BOOM!

One fang shattered.

Then another.

Then all nine.

Vikir’s eight remained.

They crashed into the remnant.

[…!]

The Death Knight vanished into the Black Sun.

Its body was shredded.

Still—

it did not scream.

Only glared.

[…Demon.]

Then—

it crawled out through the gaps.

[KILL.]

Vikir watched it.

“He who fights demons should be careful not to become one.”

Another Black Sun waited beyond the first.

“It’s unfortunate.”

“You already have.”

A second Black Sun overlapped the first.

RUMBLE!

Two enormous spheres rotated like interlocking gears.

The Death Knight—

crushed between them—

finally screamed.

[AAAAAAARGH!]

Its body became a bloody ruin.

But the greater agony—

was inside.

[…DEMONS.]

[DIE.]

[DIE.]

[DEMONS DIE.]

Even ruined—

it clawed at the ground.

Trying to reach Vikir.

People who survived the Age of Destruction—

carried hatred.

Resentment.

And those born as Baskerville hounds—

forced through that same era—

carried something even worse.

A collapsed self.

Hatred hardened into doctrine.

Anger compressed for years—

until nothing remained but eruption.

Vikir stared down at it.

Dolores watched his profile.

*What is he thinking?*

The Death Knight—

was Vikir.

A broken fragment of his unconscious.

A symbol of hatred toward demons—

made stronger by endlessly chewing old wounds.

Surely—

looking at himself like this—

he must feel grief.

Pain.

How could anyone see such a ruined self-portrait—

hear such despair—

and remain unaffected?

Then—

Vikir smiled.

“This is excellent.”

Everyone froze.

“The best material I could have asked for.”

“Coming into the Hell Tree fragment was worth it.”

Dolores stared.

Vikir untied a pouch at his waist.

Then shook its contents onto the ground.

RATTLE.

RATTLE.

RATTLE.

Small black objects.

Like beans.

“Eat.”

Everyone looked down.

The black lumps began crawling immediately.

Toward the Death Knight.

Drawn by blood.

**Doppelganger leeches.**

Black Tongue’s copy-form parasite strain.

Vikir’s secret weapon from Nouvelle Vague.
