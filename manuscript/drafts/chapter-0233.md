# Chapter 233: Tuition (9)

The raw award name 妮姆菲特 corresponds to the same legacy orphan-girl name slot already canonicalized in this project to **Peri**, so production uses **Peri Award** rather than reviving recovered-English “Nymphet.” The Academy blocs are kept descriptive as noble faction / power-family faction.

Colosseo's **Thrifty Bazaar**.

Cute name.

Enormous scale.

Students sold—

used equipment.

handmade goods.

gathered materials.

family antiques—

of questionable provenance.

Outside bidders—

allowed.

The event—

ranked among—

the Empire's top ten auctions.

And inside the market—

two student blocs—

held major power.

The **noble faction**.

Old aristocratic houses.

Seven Great Houses—

and connected bloodlines.

Small—

elite—

politically powerful.

The **power-family faction**.

Rich agricultural families.

merchant houses.

mercenary-guild heirs.

mine owners.

shipyard heirs.

Not old nobility—

but—

money.

networks.

industry.

Both factions—

recruited—

talented juniors.

Those connections—

continued after graduation—

into Imperial politics—

and finance.

This year—

both wanted—

Vikir.

A commoner—

apparently alone.

No patron.

Yet—

first place in midterms.

Academically brilliant.

Combat-capable.

Perfect—

to recruit.

Letters—

ignored.

Meeting invitations—

ignored.

Beautiful upperclassmen—

ignored.

Martial intimidation—

ignored.

Eventually—

two rival factions—

agreed—

on one thing.

“This arrogant freshman—

needs discipline.”

“Break him first.”

“Recruit him after.”

Then—

Vikir entered—

the Thrifty Bazaar—

with Gnoll hides.

The factions smiled.

“Tuition money.”

“Perfect.”

“We can suppress—

the auction.”

They warned—

other students—

not to bid.

The first lot—

received—

silence.

Vikir—

looked at—

the mountain of hides.

*Strange.*

*Useful material.*

*Why no buyers?*

Then—

another thought.

*Too much supply?*

He shrugged.

Lit—

a torch.

WHOOSH!

Half the hides—

burned.

Everyone—

stood.

Stunned.

Vikir watched—

the fire.

Then asked—

“Still—

too many?”

Torch—

again.

Half—

of the remaining stock—

burned.

Students—

began—

to panic.

Gnoll hides—

were class materials.

Armor reinforcement.

Winter practical gear.

Research reagents.

Limited supply.

Vikir—

raised the torch—

again.

“No!”

“I need those—

for next class!”

“I need—

cold-weather armor lining!”

“I'll buy!”

“Stop burning them!”

Vikir lowered—

the flame.

“Minimum bid—

four times—

the original opening price.”

The market—

erupted.

Students—

paid.

And—

blamed—

the factions—

who had created—

the artificial boycott.

The noble faction—

and power-family faction—

could only—

stare.

◆◆◆

“…That's—

how the auction ended?”

Professor Banshee—

looked—

almost offended—

by the economics.

Vikir nodded.

“Yes.”

“So—

tuition—

is covered.”

CRACK.

The heavy leather money sack—

split.

Coins—

and notes—

poured—

over Banshee's desk.

Clink.

clatter.

clink.

Banshee—

put on—

small reading glasses.

Counted.

“…You need change.”

He selected—

the amount.

“Your tuition—

resolved.”

“All four years—

paid upfront.”

Vikir did not take—

the remainder.

“The rest—

is tuition too.”

Banshee looked up.

“This is—

far too much.”

“Are you buying—

a building?”

“Trying to become—

faculty?”

“Use it—

for students—

who cannot pay.”

Dolores—

froze.

Banshee—

stared.

“You mean—

donate it—

to the scholarship foundation?”

“Yes.”

“With one condition.”

Banshee's expression—

became—

cynical.

Of course.

Large donations—

often bought—

tax deductions.

reputation.

political influence.

student blocs.

Vikir said—

“Recipients—

must complete—

a set amount—

of volunteer service—

at orphanages.”

Banshee frowned.

“That's it?”

“Yes.”

For poor students—

a remarkably favorable trade.

Tuition—

for volunteer hours.

Enough money—

to support—

years—

perhaps more than a decade—

of awards.

Banshee studied—

Vikir.

*What does he gain?*

Nothing obvious.

“Fine.”

“We can establish—

an award—

through the foundation.”

“What name?”

Vikir—

remained quiet.

Banshee waved a hand.

“Then—

the Vikir Award.”

“They'll know—

who supported them.”

“You'll become—

famous.”

Vikir raised—

one hand.

“No.”

“Donor identity—

confidential.”

“Use another name.”

Banshee adjusted—

his glasses.

Huge donation.

No tax advantage.

No public credit.

No influence—

attached to the donor.

His belief—

that everyone—

acted only—

for self-interest—

took a direct hit.

Vikir's voice—

lowered.

“Call it—

the **Peri Award**.”

Banshee—

did not recognize—

the name.

Dolores—

standing beside him—

narrowed—

her eyes.
