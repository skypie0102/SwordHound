**<Basement Floor 11 — River of Flow>**

This was truly a strange space.

For creatures moving quickly—

time seemed to pass correspondingly quickly.

For creatures moving slowly—

time seemed to pass more slowly.

The principle existed in the ordinary world too.

Here—

inside the tower—

it was grotesquely exaggerated.

Vikir moved at high speed.

And each time—

the Scale of Forced Equality continued working.

Ding!

[Agility +1…]

[Stamina +1…]

[Physical Resistance +1…]

[Magic Resistance +1…]

[Reflexes +1…]

The harder and faster Vikir moved—

the faster his lower stats climbed.

Crack.

He tilted his head.

An arrow passed through empty air.

No wasted movement.

Barely any motion at all.

His Reflexes stat had awakened—

and now it was rising continuously.

That made things possible that once would have depended on rare inborn talent.

Natural gifts.

Abilities only a tiny number of people throughout human history possessed from birth.

Vikir—

a person who had never been blessed that way—

was obtaining those things one by one inside the tower.

His field of view seemed to expand.

Then contract.

Then expand again.

The passage of time itself felt distorted.

As though he and everyone else were living on different timelines.

*Time is moving several times faster—*

*and yet everything looks slower.*

*Is this what innate talent feels like?*

Arrows that once would have demanded perfect focus—

now looked almost stationary.

Avoiding them became no harder than walking around a tree.

And every moment—

the process became easier.

Because Reflexes kept rising.

*I originally intended to unlock all six stats first—*

*then slowly raise the three special stats.*

*I never expected to find a floor where time itself would accelerate the process.*

The Hell Tree’s floors were random.

Locations.

Rules.

Terrain.

Everything varied.

Finding a place like this—

was an extraordinary stroke of luck.

And once a tower stat increased—

it did not fall again.

Inside or outside—

the gain remained.

Only the visible status window disappeared after leaving the tower.

*If I get out of here…*

*I may genuinely be able to reach the Eighth Fang without Dolores’s help.*

A realm beyond any officially recognized Sword Master stage.

Even Hugo—

Head of House Baskerville—

had reached only the Seventh Fang after a lifetime.

*But that comes later.*

First—

Vikir needed to escape this tower.

Crunch!

He caught the arm of a charging warrior.

Twisted.

Crack.

The Ballak warrior dropped into the mud.

Splash.

Occasionally—

high-level opponents dropped golden candies when defeated.

Vikir picked one up.

*Maybe I should exchange these golden candies for ordinary candy.*

*If I did—*

*I could raise the current ceiling above 798.*

He rejected the idea immediately.

Golden candy raised **Level**—

and with it, stats by percentage.

Its value increased the stronger the user already was.

So the best time to consume it—

was later.

At a critical moment.

For now—

saving golden candy was more efficient.

Vikir rolled his neck.

Crack.

Crack.

His eyesight had improved enough that he could see his own reflection in dew hanging from distant leaves.

He had grown taller again.

Arms longer.

Legs longer.

Because he was moving more intensely than anyone else—

his body was aging more quickly too.

*At this rate—*

*I may eventually return to my pre-regression apparent age.*

That did not bother him.

The older body felt more familiar.

And it would probably draw less unnecessary attention.

Slash!

Aura twisted like a serpent.

Incoming arrows split apart.

The same slash continued into the obstacles behind them.

Stone.

Wood.

Nothing stopped it.

The Ballak warriors recoiled.

“Is he one of the high-level ones too?!”

“That makes no sense!”

“How can a newcomer be this strong?!”

“We can’t beat him!”

“This is outside the rules!”

“What are the fairies doing?!”

“We need the hunting chief!”

“If she doesn’t come—”

Their morale broke.

Vikir caught one fleeing warrior by the neck.

Slammed him down.

At the last instant—

the warrior twisted his body to reduce the impact.

Interesting.

Enough control to avoid unconsciousness.

Vikir looked closer.

The man seemed older than the others.

Gray in his hair.

Then another detail caught Vikir’s attention.

*That movement.*

*Baskerville grappling?*

Long ago—

Vikir had taught Ballak warriors some of the Baskerville family’s basic close-combat techniques.

The adults had not cared.

Ballak’s own martial methods were better suited to them.

But several children—

had followed Vikir around and copied everything.

*Could this be…*

Vikir frowned.

He still bent the captured warrior’s wrist and elbow until—

Crack!

The arm gave way.

“If you continue resisting—

you’ll end up like him.”

The strongest and oldest-looking warrior had been subdued instantly.

The others fell silent.

Then voices came from behind Vikir.

“Serves you right, barbarians!”

Tudor.

“I can understand why they’re shocked.”

Sancho.

“Even I’m shocked.”

Pigi.

“But seriously—

how did you get this strong?”

And Bianca—

“Ah.”

“So you **were** the one who smelled like rum on the Academy roof.”

“I knew it.”

All four joined Vikir.

Tudor looked toward the recovered food bags.

“They’re full!”

“Vikir, don’t stop me.”

“I’m going to—”

Sancho interrupted.

“We got everything back.”

“The crops.”

“The hunted meat.”

Pigi looked ready to cry.

“My tomatoes.”

“My radishes.”

“My cabbage…”

Bianca found another bundle.

“And the salted pork.”

“We almost died collecting salt near the Daylily colony for that.”

Vikir raised one arm.

Stopped all four.

Because something else had caught his eye.

Flash.

An earring.

On the captive Ballak warrior.

A glass bead.

Modern workmanship.

And with Vikir’s enhanced vision—

he could read the faded engraving.

**Made in Cindywendy Studio**

Vikir knew the item.

It belonged to one of Cindywendy’s earliest trade batches for Ballak.

Cheap decorative beads—

specifically selected for Ballak children.

*Why is an old warrior wearing one?*

PING!

An arrow came from the front.

This one—

was different.

Powerful enough that Vikir could not ignore it.

“…!”

He deflected it with the flat of his sword.

Pain ran through his wrist.

Vikir released the captive.

Stepped back.

A woman entered the clearing.

Long hair.

Apparently in her early thirties.

But her presence—

felt decades older.

Hardened.

Experienced.

Fierce.

She reminded Vikir of Akwilla.

*This one is dangerous.*

For the first time on this floor—

cold sweat touched him.

Even with all six stats awakened—

Vikir could not guarantee victory against the woman before him.

She was not as overwhelming as the Dragon Majin from the previous floor.

But she was clearly more dangerous than Cerberus.

More dangerous than the Daylily of Blood Tree.

More dangerous than the Shadowless King of the Black Sea.

Vikir tightened his grip.

Then—

thud.

The woman walked forward.

And knelt on one knee.

“Greetings.”

“Hunting Chief of Ballak.”

Silence.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

The other Ballak warriors.

Even Vikir.

All stared.

Vikir studied the woman’s face.

A mature warrior.

A presence close to Akwilla’s.

If someone like this had existed in Ballak back then—

Vikir should have remembered.

“…?”

He searched his memory.

Then—

“…!”

The years peeled away.

The hardened expression.

The accumulated dignity.

The decades of survival.

Beneath all of it—

Vikir saw the face of a child.

A little girl.

Holding out something wriggling in her hands.

*…Can I trade this caterpillar for a glass bead?*

Vikir finally remembered.
