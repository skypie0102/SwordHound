WHOOSH—

Dolores raised the hammer.

Then brought it down.

She remembered training with Pope Nabokov I after the Hell Tree.

At the time—

Dolores had doubted herself.

Doubted her healing.

Doubted her strength.

Nabokov gave her a simple lesson.

**A third-rate healer treats an ally after the ally is injured.**

**A second-rate healer treats the wound immediately.**

**But a first-rate healer—**

Dolores completed the teaching herself.

“REMOVES THE THREAT BEFORE IT CAN INJURE AN ALLY!”

WHAM!

The hammer struck Pedro’s face.

His body flew backward.

Bianca stared.

“…How were you carrying that?”

Dolores answered seriously.

“The proper method is apparently to hold it between your back muscles.”

“But I don’t have enough muscle or strength.”

“So I tied it to my back with rope.”

“And hid the hammer under my skirt.”

Bianca stared harder.

Dolores lifted the weapon again.

“Anyway.”

“Back to prevention.”

WHAM!

Pedro—

still dazed on the ground—

took another hit.

“GAAAH!?”

The impact was so violent his eyes bulged outward.

He could no longer tell whether his vision was burning from holy power—

or blunt trauma.

“CAVALRY!”

“WHAT ARE YOU DOING?!”

No cavalry reached him.

Sinclaire’s golden barriers.

Bianca’s collapsing terrain.

Dolores’s holy field.

All slowed the charge.

And as Pedro’s mental control weakened—

some riders began waking.

Pedro finally understood his mistake.

He had become too greedy.

Too determined to capture Tudor and Bianca personally.

And he had chosen steep mountain terrain—

the worst possible ground for cavalry.

Tudor saw the same opening.

“We only need him.”

Behind them—

Cindywendy’s mercenaries and reinforcements sent by allied Houses moved down to contain the cavalry.

Dolores.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Sinclaire.

All converged on Pedro.

“You insolent brats!”

Pedro released the aura of a peak Graduator.

Demonic energy erupted.

His body began changing.

Demonization.

FLASH!

Dolores’s stronger holy field crushed down.

Sinclaire’s golden hands pinned the transforming body.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Attacked from every side.

Arms.

Legs.

Mobility.

All destroyed.

Cindywendy’s mercenaries fired into the gaps.

Even then—

Pedro writhed.

It took **twenty-four sanctified wooden stakes** driven into his body before he finally stopped.

The effect on the cavalry was immediate.

Men blinked.

Looked around.

Recovered awareness.

“Where…?”

“Why are we here?”

“Master Tudor!?”

Tudor stared at them.

Only a small portion of the Invincible Cavalry was present.

Most had been deployed to distant civil-war fronts.

His expression darkened.

“La Mancha knights.”

“Brainwashed by a demon.”

“Sent to die for nothing.”

His hatred shifted toward Pedro.

“You should have died when you helped send my friend to prison.”

“Now accept the death you earned.”

[WAIT!]

[SPARE ME!]

[I’LL TALK!]

[I’LL TELL YOU EVERYTHING!]

Pedro had survived once before by trading information for life.

Everyone except Tudor wanted to interrogate him first.

Tudor eventually agreed.

“Then answer.”

“Everything.”

“Truthfully.”

“If you lie—

I cannot guarantee your life.”

Pedro trembled.

[Yes.]

“But if you answer honestly—

I will spare you.”

“On the honor of House Donquixote.”

The awakened Donquixote knights glared at Pedro.

He began talking.

The Night Walkers learned several important things.

The demon wearing Passamonte’s skin—

was the **Fourth Corpse**.

Its true name—

**Chimeries**.

Its power could push humans toward excessive courage.

Recklessness.

Blind boldness.

The Invincible Cavalry had mostly been sent to civil-war fronts.

The **Invincible Armada** was stranded far from the family estate—

because drought had lowered the sea level.

And Chimeries—

inside Passamonte—

was prioritizing the capture of Tudor alive.

Bianca frowned.

“Why Tudor and me alive?”

“If your goal is simply to remove enemies—

our heads would be easier.”

Pedro shook his head.

[I DON’T KNOW.]

[HE NEVER TOLD ME.]

Tudor asked the question that mattered most to him.

“My father?”

“Is he alive?”

Pedro hesitated.

[…Yes.]

“His health is poor.”

“But he is alive.”

Tudor’s face changed.

“Really?”

[YES!]

[I may be a demonized man now—

but I was once a Donquixote knight!]

[I’m not lying!]

Tudor closed his eyes.

“Thank God.”

“For protecting my father.”

Pedro looked around.

His mana was gone.

His body—

destroyed.

[I told you everything.]

[Release me.]

[A knight of La Mancha would never break a promise made on honor.]

Tudor’s eyes narrowed.

“Yes.”

“I swore on honor.”

“As a knight of House Donquixote.”

Pedro almost smiled.

[There is nothing more sacred than La Mancha chivalry.]

[I’m not worthy even to speak of it, but—]

Tudor shook his head.

“You’re right.”

“You are not worthy of House Donquixote’s honor.”

“But neither am I.”

[…What?]

Tudor raised the broken spearhead.

“My name is **Tudor**.”

Cold metal flashed.

“No surname.”

SLASH!

Pedro’s head fell.

Black blood sprayed across the ground.

Tudor looked down at him.

“So House Donquixote’s honor has nothing to do with me.”

◆◆◆

Deep inside Tochka—

the Night Walkers gathered again.

“Retreat.”

Tudor pointed at the map.

Not a true retreat.

A deception.

“We use the cavalry as cover.”

“Make it look like they returned after failing.”

“Enter Donquixote territory without a battle.”

“Then infiltrate the castle.”

“And kill Passamonte.”

Everyone agreed.

Even Cindywendy.

“Donquixote forces are already choking our supply routes.”

“If we cannot take control of that territory—

we cannot keep feeding the refugees.”

“And Vikir’s plan fails.”

Tochka had been discovered.

They could no longer wait.

Strike first.

If battle was inevitable—

choose its time.

Tudor’s blood moved at the thought of rescuing Cervantes—

and reclaiming what had been stolen.

Dolores warned them.

“Only Sinclaire and I have directly experienced a true Ten Corpse.”

Dolores had faced Dantalian.

Belial.

Amdusias.

Sinclaire had faced Belial.

Pedro—

even demonized—

was nowhere near that level.

“And years have passed.”

“They will be stronger now.”

“We prepare for everything.”

Vikir himself had never approached a Demon King casually.

He prepared.

Calculated.

Then risked his life anyway.

Everyone listened as Tudor sketched Donquixote’s internal defenses.

Guard positions.

Traps.

Secret passages.

Hidden routes.

Pigi looked at the map.

Then quietly said:

“…I wish Vikir were here.”

Silence.

Pigi immediately waved his hands.

“Sorry!”

“I shouldn’t have said that.”

Dolores put a hand on his shoulder.

“It’s fine.”

“We all think it.”

Tudor nodded.

“I think it too.”

“I wish he would appear in front of us like a miracle.”

“But he’s in Nouvelle Vague.”

“We cannot wait for imagination to save us.”

“We finish what he entrusted to us.”

“Then we go get him.”

His words settled over the room.

“Vikir is alive.”

“We’ll meet him again.”

“Until then—

we become people he can be proud of.”

Dolores nodded.

“We have to grow too.”

“So Vikir doesn’t have to carry everything alone.”

“So he can depend on us.”

Sancho.

Bianca.

Sinclaire.

Pigi.

All shared the same thought.

Only Cindywendy stayed silent.

Under the table—

her fingers touched a letter received from Nouvelle Vague.

An official notice.

**<Night Hound — Vikir Van Baskerville>**

**[DECEASED]**

The report claimed Vikir died during a failed escape attempt.

Cindywendy crushed the paper in her hand.

*They don’t need to know.*

For the Night Walkers—

Vikir was more than a leader.

He was faith.

Breaking that faith—

could destroy them.

Cindywendy—

who trusted neither luck nor miracles—

closed her eyes.

*Still…*

*Couldn’t there be one miracle?*

She said nothing.
