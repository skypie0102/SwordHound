# Chapter 25: Camus Morgue (6)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/025.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 25 is an aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English forms for Adolf Morgue, Sixth Circle, Camus Morgue, Hugo Le Baskerville, and established sword/magic terminology follow current project/Fandom evidence.

“You said it wasn't meant for me to hear, so I stopped listening after that.”

Hugo immediately raised one hand to the corner of his mouth.

The rest of his face remained perfectly composed.

It fooled no one.

He was hiding a smile.

Camus stared at Vikir with shining eyes.

“Heh… hehehe…”

Only Adolf looked ready to burst a blood vessel.

After a moment, he straightened and turned to Hugo.

“Clan Head. Would you allow me to speak with this child a little more seriously?”

For a senior Morgue statesman to take this much interest in an eight-year-old was highly unusual.

Hugo found Adolf's sensitivity amusing.

“What is this? Is one of the empire's great mages trying to bully an eight-year-old boy?”

He returned Adolf's earlier accusation almost word for word.

Adolf did not back down.

“Haha. This child may someday become my niece's husband. I'm not asking as the representative of the Morgue delegation. I'm asking as her uncle.”

“Uncle! It isn't like that!”

“Hush, Camus. Let your uncle handle this. A matter like this should be checked at least once.”

He spoke firmly to Camus, then sent Hugo a meaningful glance.

Give me this much face.

“…”

Hugo considered the fact that it had been years since he had spent this much time dealing directly with Morgue.

Then he waved one hand.

Do as you please.

◆◆◆

Vikir accepted Adolf's challenge readily.

Even before his regression, Adolf Morgue had been an absolute powerhouse who was difficult to approach carelessly.

By forty, he already represented his family at major official events and held genuine political power.

A chance to sample even a fraction of his strength was valuable.

Hugo had probably reached the same conclusion.

That was why he allowed the test.

After the annual competition ended, the training ground emptied.

Vikir and Adolf stood facing one another across the broad field.

Hugo and Camus watched from a distance.

Adolf addressed Vikir.

“Child of Baskerville. I will test your qualities. Show me everything you have.”

Vikir nodded.

His thoughts were less obedient.

‘If I showed you everything, you'd probably faint.’

He considered Adolf as he remembered him from the first life.

At Vikir's pre-regression peak, an ambush or assassination attempt against Adolf might have succeeded roughly half the time.

A direct confrontation?

Perhaps one chance in ten.

Current Vikir had not yet recovered all of that old strength.

There was also no reason to expose what he did have.

He only needed to satisfy Adolf's curiosity by a reasonable amount.

‘Let's see how strong one of Morgue's top mages really is at this point.’

People said true experts could learn much about one another from a single handshake.

Vikir intended to use this exchange the same way.

Adolf raised one hand.

“Child. I'll give you a handicap.”

He swept that hand toward the ground.

Rumble—

Earth rose and spun itself into the shape of a pot.

Whoosh!

Fire poured from Adolf's palm and baked the clay hard.

Gurgle…

Water gathered inside the newly made vessel.

Three attributes—earth, fire, and water—combined casually in a matter of moments.

Snap.

Adolf flicked his fingers.

The water-filled pot floated into the air and settled neatly above his head.

Balancing it there with magic, Adolf looked down at Vikir.

“I'll fight you while carrying this full pot. If you can make even a single drop spill from it, you win.”

In other words, Vikir was being invited to attack freely.

Vikir drew his shortsword and took his stance.

Adolf simply crossed his arms.

Tap!

Vikir moved first.

He charged straight ahead.

Camus shouted in alarm.

“Ah! You can't just charge Uncle head-on!”

Adolf looked over at her with an expression of profound disappointment.

“Camus. What am I supposed to do when you tell him that?”

A transparent barrier appeared in front of Adolf.

BANG!

Vikir struck it and was thrown backward.

“When fighting a swordsman, shield magic is one of the best answers. Even an aura-coated sword has difficulty penetrating a proper barrier.”

Adolf was not boasting.

Shield magic was one of his specialties.

He could vary the size, thickness, shape, and position of his barriers instantly depending on the situation.

The shield that had just repelled Vikir was no larger than necessary to intercept the child's body.

Minimum mana.

Maximum efficiency.

Vikir let the recoil carry him backward, landed lightly, then changed direction.

Tap! Tap!

His foot kicked several pebbles from the ground in the opposite direction.

They flew toward the water pot.

“Aiming at the target directly is too obvious.”

Adolf did not even turn his head.

A small shield appeared and intercepted the stones.

A second shield formed on his other side just in time to catch Vikir's renewed charge.

BANG!

Vikir bounced away again.

Adolf unfolded one arm.

“My turn to attack.”

Flaming spears materialized around him.

Whoosh! Whoosh! Whoosh!

They shot through the air fast enough to gouge deep holes in the packed earth wherever they landed.

Camus cried out.

“Uncle! That's too much! You never used magic like that when you trained with me!”

Adolf's expression became even more wounded.

“Camus. Whose side are you on?”

Vikir kept moving through the fire.

‘So this is his current level.’

The Adolf Morgue Vikir remembered had not changed in any fundamental way.

The rhythm of his defense.

The timing of his offense.

The way he layered small barriers rather than wasting mana on one enormous wall.

Familiar.

‘I know how to deal with him.’

Vikir possessed decades of combat experience from his first life.

Now he also possessed the harmless face and small body of an eight-year-old.

Used properly, the contrast could make even an expert lower his guard.

‘If I really wanted to, I might even be able to kill him.’

The thought passed through his mind coldly.

There was no reason to act on it.

Hugo was watching.

This needed to remain a test.

“Hah!”

Vikir deliberately let his movements become a little more frantic, as though Adolf's fire spears were forcing him into desperation.

Then he rushed the barrier again.

CLANG!

His blade struck.

“Impossible.”

Adolf's shield held.

CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!

Vikir attacked again and again.

Every strike hit the barrier.

Adolf began to smile.

“No matter how many times you try, the answer will be the same. Your sword cannot break my shield.”

Vikir continued.

Hum—

The blade moved.

CLANG!

Same angle.

Same point.

Again.

CLANG!

Again.

CLANG!

The attacks became almost mechanical.

Not merely similar.

Identical.

The sword struck one precise point on Adolf's barrier every single time.

Adolf finally noticed.

“…Oh?”

The barrier itself was beginning to wear at that spot.

Repeated impacts were accumulating magical fatigue in one tiny area.

Adolf laughed.

“Hahahaha! You're trying to bore through my shield by striking one point over and over? Bold. And I have to praise the concentration and sword control required to hit the exact same place without deviation.”

Then his eyes cooled.

“But an egg does not break a boulder merely because it hits the same point repeatedly. Did you compare the durability of my shield with the durability of your sword before choosing this plan?”

He was right.

CLANG!

Vikir's sword struck the barrier again.

Crack!

The blade shattered.

Fragments spun away through the air.

Vikir stepped back holding only the broken remainder.

Hugo watched in silence, one hand resting against his chin.

Camus looked disappointed.

Adolf began his lecture.

“Persistence is admirable, but stubbornness has to be matched to the opponent. Your talent is impressive, but it is still far from enough to make you suitable for my niece—”

He stopped.

Something cold touched the top of his head.

‘Hm?’

Drip.

Another drop.

Then another.

Water ran through Adolf's hair and down the side of his face.

“…?”

He slowly looked upward.

The pot above his head was leaking.

A fragment of Vikir's shattered shortsword was embedded in its side.

Water streamed around the metal shard.

The repeated attacks on the shield had not been meant to pierce Adolf's defense at all.

Vikir had broken his own sword on purpose.

He had controlled the angle so that one fragment would fly past Adolf's barrier and strike the real target.

The pot.

Adolf stood there with water running from his hair to his chin and down his neck.

His expression went completely blank.

Step.

Step.

Step.

Vikir walked toward him.

He looked at the leaking pot and spoke calmly.

“It really did spill.”

He was talking about the water, of course.
