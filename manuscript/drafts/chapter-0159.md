# Chapter 159: Familiar Face (2)

Quovadis, Pedo, Hebe, Geronto, Beelzebub, Old Testament Faction, and New Testament Faction. Sssssss—

Ephebo's exposed face began dissolving almost immediately.

Skin.

Flesh.

Features.

All of it melted into the water flooding the corridor.

Greasy black residue spread across the surface.

The stink of rotten meat thickened.

But before the face vanished—

Vikir and Dolores had both seen it.

A face familiar to them.

“…Mozgus?”

Vikir stared down at the dissolving corpse.

Bald.

No eyebrows.

Sharp, severe eyes.

Tightly set lips.

The resemblance was unmistakable.

**Mozgus Quovadis.**

A Quovadis paladin.

A devout inquisitor who carried a massive scripture and stood at the front of heresy trials.

In Vikir's previous life—

Mozgus had later fought demons on the front lines.

A human.

A hero.

A man who ultimately died taking an enormous number of demons with him.

*That Mozgus was definitely human.*

*So how…?*

Dolores answered before Vikir could finish the thought.

“That isn't Lord Mozgus.”

She examined what remained of Ephebo.

Then shook her head.

“Lord Mozgus has a twin brother.”

“An older twin.”

“Unlike us—”

“he belonged to the Old Testament Faction.”

The twin was also a paladin.

A man with an iron-wall body and powerful holy strength.

Years ago—

he had supposedly withdrawn deep into an Old Testament monastery for closed-door training.

After that—

no one saw him.

That—

at least—

was the public story.

Vikir looked down at the corpse.

“So he didn't disappear because he entered seclusion.”

Dolores's face tightened.

“His body was taken.”

She swallowed.

“There are quite a few people inside Quovadis who are currently in closed-door training…”

“Surely…”

Vikir gave her no reassurance.

“Nothing can be ruled out.”

And Quovadis was not the only concern.

Vikir remembered **Seth Le Baskerville**.

The second son of Baskerville—

another powerful figure once said to have withdrawn into seclusion.

If the same pattern existed inside both Baskerville and Quovadis—

then every famous warrior or mage who had vanished for long periods—

especially under the excuse of closed training—

had to be considered at risk.

Vikir looked down at Ephebo's headless body.

A thought settled into place.

*The Ninth Corpse.*

One of the Ten Corpses.

The demon behind this network clearly possessed the ability to take people—

corrupt them—

turn them into demonic undead—

control them—

and appropriate faces.

Even **Quilt's** current face—

the face resembling an actor who had retired long ago—

might originally have belonged to someone else.

*That would explain the actor's disappearance too.*

Then another face surfaced in Vikir's memory.

A red-haired girl.

“When are you entering the Academy?”

“Let's coordinate it.”

“I might enroll one or two years early.”

“If we become first-years together—”

“wouldn't that be fun?”

A connection reaching back to when Vikir was eight.

**Camus Morgue.**

An uneasy feeling stirred.

After Vikir disappeared into the Red and Black Mountains—

Camus had reportedly continued searching for him.

Meanwhile—

Vikir spent roughly two years training in Ballak territory.

Then Camus abruptly left the Morgue Light Faction.

Transferred into the Dark Faction—

the branch famous for its black magic.

More recently—

she too had entered closed-door training.

Even Vikir could not completely explain why.

*…Worry about it later.*

*Hunt first.*

He clicked his tongue behind the plague-doctor mask.

Most problems became much easier—

once the demon responsible was dead.

Vikir picked up the black leather covering that had hidden Ephebo's face.

The artifact emitted demonic power similar in quality to Beelzebub's.

Far weaker in concentration—

roughly a quarter as dense.

But there were three more.

Hebe.

Pedo.

Geronto.

*If all four are the same—*

*then together, they may be another matter.*

He stored the black covering inside his cloak.

Then—

“Um…”

“Night Hound.”

Dolores called hesitantly.

Vikir turned.

Her expression held too many things at once.

Sadness.

Confusion.

Suspicion.

Trust.

“If all of this is happening inside Quovadis…”

She looked down.

“Then what am I supposed to believe?”

Dolores had grown up believing one thing absolutely.

Quovadis represented good.

Yes—

the Old Testament and New Testament factions fought.

But she had believed the indulgence dispute was temporary.

A doctrinal conflict inside the same faith.

Eventually—

both sides would reconcile beneath the teachings of Rune.

Now—

that belief was collapsing.

There were demons behind the Old Testament faction.

Not merely corruption.

Not merely greed.

Actual demons—

living among humans.

Dolores felt as though the foundation beneath her entire life had cracked.

Quovadis was not absolute good.

It could be corrupted.

Manipulated.

Used.

Just like any other human institution.

And if demons had already entered society this deeply—

then the implication was worse.

*War.*

*A war with the Demon World.*

Dolores was intelligent enough to sense the scale of the storm.

And—

perhaps for the first time—

she understood how long the masked figure in front of her had been fighting it.

“I don't know anymore.”

Her voice weakened.

“I don't know what I'm supposed to believe.”

“What I see isn't necessarily the truth…”

Dolores lowered her head.

The sorrow and uncertainty on her face made for a beautiful, melancholy sight—one that would make any man who saw it feel a pang of heartache.

Vikir did not comfort her.

He simply turned forward.

“What you believe—”

“you decide for yourself.”

Then he kept walking.

As always.

◆◆◆

Vikir moved from the fourth ring into the third.

Dolores hesitated only briefly—

then followed.

Dormitory Three housed children aged thirteen to sixteen.

Dormitory Two—

ages eight to twelve.

The two structures were linked like twins—

forming an H-shaped complex.

Another notice stood inside.

The same night-worker safety rules.

The lettering here—

however—

was slightly faded.

And the deeper they moved—

the stronger the demonic presence became.

Wet rot.

Decaying meat.

The suffocating pressure of a narrow grave filled with damp soil.

*There's more inside.*

The Night Hound advanced by scent.

Dolores—

meanwhile—

had reached a conclusion of her own.

The Night Hound had not been randomly attacking Quovadis branches.

There was a pattern.

A reason.

A rule.

From that point onward—

she stopped merely following him.

She began helping.

“I'll report this to the senior leadership.”

“Not the Old Testament side.”

“The New Testament Faction.”

“Especially the groups I know personally.”

“I believe they can be trusted.”

Vikir did not look back.

“…Do what you want.”

Dolores took the answer as permission.

She quickly wrote a letter.

Ephebo.

The corrupted Quovadis twin.

The evidence inside Indulgentia.

A request for immediate scrutiny of House Indulgentia.

Then—

Dolores produced a small white pigeon.

She tied the message to its leg.

The recipient:

**Cardinal Martin Luther.**

Leader of the New Testament Faction.

Under normal circumstances—

Dolores might have sent something this serious through her father.

**Humbert L. Quovadis.**

But Humbert led the Old Testament side.

After what she had seen tonight—

even family ties were not enough to override caution.

“Please fly quickly.”

Dolores stroked the bird.

“Sorry for making you work at night.”

The pigeon spread its wings.

Flutter—

white feathers crossed the darkness.

It rose toward the sky.

Then—

slash!

A crimson arc cut through the night.

The pigeon split apart.

The letter—

caught in the same force—

burned into fragments.

“…”

Vikir narrowed his eyes behind the mask.

That attack was fast.

Very fast.

Ssssss—

the odor of rotting flesh thickened.

Mixed with the damp smell of grave soil.

Two tall shadows appeared in the corridor connecting Dormitory Three and Dormitory Two.

Dolores immediately raised her guard.

“Who's there!?”

Two men stepped into view.

Tall.

Lean.

Neatly dressed.

Black coverings over their faces.

**Pedo.**

**Hebe.**

Two more of Quilt's masked hounds.

Like Ephebo—

both radiated the pressure of powerful high-ranking undead.

But that was no longer Dolores's greatest concern.

She stared at the black masks.

Then asked—

quietly—

“…Were they people we knew too?”

Someone personally selected—

corrupted—

and controlled by a Demon King-class being—

was unlikely to have been ordinary in life.

Their hidden faces probably belonged to people with names.

Reputations.

Families.

Vikir looked at Pedo and Hebe.

Then farther into the darkness beyond them.

“…I hope not.”
