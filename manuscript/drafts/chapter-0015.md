# Chapter 15: Solitary (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/015.txt`. Canonical English forms for the 10,000 Book Library, Baskerville Fangs, and the scripture title *Lurking Embedded Teeth* are normalized from current indexed Fandom evidence. Recovered English Chapter 15 is an aligned secondary reference only.

The 10,000 Book Library.

Buried deep within the Baskerville Clan’s main castle, it was an enormous library even by the standards of the empire.

“Come to think of it, this really is my first time seeing it for myself.”

Vikir passed through an entrance guarded by several knights and entered the restricted area.

Once he presented the pass personally signed by Hugo, no one attempted to stop him.

Some of the knights even smiled faintly as they saluted.

Before long, Vikir stood at the passage leading into the deeper reaches of the library.

The structure had been carved into black stone and stretched inward like the throat of some colossal beast.

Rows upon rows of books loomed over him like enormous teeth.

“I need to move quickly.”

Vikir had spent so much of his life alone that talking to himself had become a habit.

He kept the remaining time in mind and began his search.

His thoughts were urgent.

His footsteps and gaze were not.

He moved slowly through the shelves.

Old paper.

Ink.

Dust.

The scents normally associated with a warm, quiet library carried no such comfort here.

These shelves contained methods for handling cold steel.

Calculations for finding the shortest route to an enemy’s death.

Efficient, ruthlessly logical theories of killing.

Across generations of Baskerville history, countless dead swordsmen had left their arguments behind in written form.

The library felt less like a place of learning than a prison corridor lined with silent, merciless killers.

Sword manuals stared down at him like rows of exposed teeth.

Vikir was used to that kind of gaze.

Step. Step. Step.

His footsteps disappeared into the depth of the corridor.

The farther he went, the older and more valuable the books became.

*Severing Incisor Swordsmanship.*

*Crushing Molar Swordsmanship.*

*Stabbing Canine Swordsmanship.*

*Double Impaling Snaggletooth Swordsmanship.*

*Lying Wisdom Tooth Swordsmanship.*

There were manuals Vikir recognized.

Manuals he had desperately wanted to learn in his previous life.

And manuals so advanced that he had never dared imagine being permitted to touch them.

All were arranged in neat rows like teeth.

Heavy locks prevented theft and unauthorized reading.

A collateral relative or illegitimate child could possess enormous mana and decades of practical combat experience and still struggle to gain access to swordsmanship beyond the Fourth Fang.

Members of the direct line could generally learn at least the Fifth Fang.

Elite direct descendants could reach the Sixth.

The Clan Head and Vice Clan Head had access to the Seventh.

The present Hugo Le Baskerville had mastered seven Fangs.

Those seven Fangs had helped make him one of the continent’s foremost swordsmen—a Sword Master.

The head of the Morgue Clan, renowned as a great mage, was counted among his rivals. Their martial and magical standing were therefore considered comparable in broad terms.

Before long, Vikir stopped.

In a library vast enough to resemble a sea of books, locating any single volume without guidance would have been difficult.

But Hugo had told him exactly where to look.

A valuable collection sat behind several layers of locks.

Vikir tried the key Hugo had given him.

The case opened.

Inside rested the manual Hugo had recommended.

**Baskerville Sixth Fang.**

Swordsmanship of a level Vikir had never been allowed to dream of in his previous life.

He took out the book and began reading carefully.

Its diagrams traced fang-shaped sword paths and explained methods of cutting, thrusting, defending, crushing, redirecting, and striking again.

Above all, it explained how to manifest six Fangs.

Hugo had surely believed it impossible for an eight-year-old to understand and memorize such material in a single day.

That was probably why he had offered the book with a mixture of genuine favor and quiet condescension.

But Vikir was not truly eight years old.

He was a veteran swordsman who had survived forty years of life, decades of war, and the Age of Destruction itself.

A Baskerville hound among Baskerville hounds.

He had long since mastered the theory behind the First through Fourth Fangs.

He had even inferred much of the Fifth Fang on his own despite never having been formally taught it.

For nearly twenty years in his first life, he had refined the Fourth Fang to its limit while remaining unable to cross the wall beyond it.

What he had lacked was the formula for advancement.

“… So that’s it.”

Vikir’s eyes sharpened.

“After the fourth Fang, the direction of the mana flow has to change. That’s why I could never draw the fifth.”

The obstruction in his understanding disappeared all at once.

He absorbed the manual like dry earth taking in water.

It took him roughly three hours to understand and memorize the theory of the Sixth Fang.

That speed was possible only because of the enormous experience he had accumulated while trapped at the Fourth Fang in his previous life.

Snap.

Once he had committed the contents to memory, Vikir closed the book.

Learning the road from the Fourth Fang to the Fifth and Sixth was valuable.

But it was not why he had come here today.

Vikir returned toward the entrance of the 10,000 Book Library.

This shallower area was accessible even to the knights guarding the library and the servants responsible for cleaning it.

“Oh? Young Master, why are you back already?”

“Did you forget something? We can fetch it for you.”

“Are you hungry? Shall we bring you something to eat?”

The servants addressed him warmly.

Vikir could see a mixture of respect and fear in their eyes.

News of the practical examination had apparently spread quickly.

He shook his head.

“I’m finished with what I needed to see.”

The servants stared.

So did the knights.

“Already?”

“You should stay longer.”

“There is still plenty of time.”

None of them said what they were really thinking, but their disappointment was obvious.

To abandon the priceless sword manuals preserved in the deep restricted sections after only a few hours seemed incomprehensible.

There were people who would have risked their lives for the chance to read even one of those books.

Vikir himself would once have felt the same way.

Now he remained perfectly calm.

“Before I leave, I want to look through some other books. You don’t need to worry about me. Go back to your work.”

He walked toward the shallowest part of the library, where miscellaneous volumes had been dumped together.

Several knights watching from a distance whispered among themselves.

“If it were me, I’d stay in the restricted shelves and memorize every sword manual I could.”

“He’s still too young to understand what they’re worth. He’ll regret wasting this chance when he grows older.”

“We should tell one of the elders. Someone ought to stop him from spending his time on useless books.”

If they had seen Vikir’s expression, they would not have spoken so casually.

He searched the neglected shelves with absolute focus.

Dust lay thick over books no one cared about, shoved together without order.

Vikir checked them slowly and carefully.

Hours passed.

At last, he found what he had come for.

It sat in a corner of a shelf that everyone could see and everyone had learned to ignore.

An old book.

Dust covered its surface so heavily that no preservation magic could have been protecting it.

“…”

Vikir stood there for a long time, staring at the title.

**Lurking Embedded Teeth.**

A basic swordsmanship book so unimpressive that even Baskerville children ignored it.

An old manual classified among the library’s worthless miscellaneous texts.

But who in the present world understood its true value?

Only Vikir.

Only someone who had survived the Age of Destruction knew that this book was one of the greatest treasures in the entire Baskerville Clan.

Creak—

He pulled at it.

The volume had apparently remained untouched for so long that it resisted coming free from the shelf.

Vikir shifted several books blocking it and finally dragged it out into the open.

“… Lurking Embedded Teeth.”

He carefully brushed away the dust.

Then he opened the book.

Rustle—

Pages flicked past under his fingers.

The reason it had been discarded among miscellaneous texts soon became obvious.

Several pages had been torn out.

The surviving sections were uneven, like a jaw with missing teeth.

The damage struck precisely where the most important explanations should have been.

The text jumped from one incomplete idea to another.

No ordinary reader could reconstruct its meaning.

That was why the book had been dismissed as useless.

But Vikir knew.

‘… I know what was written there.’

He remembered the missing pages.

Before his regression, the operation to retrieve them had been burned into his memory under two names.

**Operation Recover the Torn Pages.**

**Operation Restore the Missing Fangs.**

The hound who had crossed one deadly obstacle after another to collect those scattered pages one by one had been Vikir Van Baskerville himself.
