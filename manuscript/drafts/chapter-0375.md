RIIIP—

The sound of something tough being torn.

One day—

the sky split open.

Scarlet fire burned around the wound.

Massive serpents of flame raised their heads toward the ground.

RUMBLE—

RUMBLE—

RUMBLE—

Red droplets covered the world.

Fire rain fell everywhere.

Grass and trees on the mountains burned away.

Forests became deserts of ash.

Oceans and lakes boiled dry into wastelands.

Living things either burned to death—

or dried away.

Even skeletons seemed to scream after death.

Those who remained alive suffered simply by breathing.

An age came in which nothing could be promised.

Nothing guaranteed.

Words like **protect** and **preserve** became obscene jokes.

Yesterday.

Tomorrow.

Those ideas vanished.

The rain of fire became a flood of flame.

It raged across the continent for one hundred fifty days.

Five months.

Without pause.

The whole world drowned in fire.

Burned corpses piled into mountains.

Across every horizon—

scarlet flames licked the sky.

**The Long Rain of Terror.**

Endless rain of fire.

Wind of fire.

Thunder of fire.

Floods of fire.

A season that announced the extinction of mankind.

The demons’ ultimate weapon.

The disaster that erased ninety-eight percent of surviving humanity—

and inflicted the greatest damage on the Human Alliance.

◆◆◆

Three-thirty in the morning.

“Aaaah!”

“Hot!”

“Hot!”

“Help me!”

Screams suddenly filled the silent stone cell.

The desperate voices of people burning alive.

“…!”

Vikir opened his eyes.

Cold sweat covered his body.

A nightmare.

For the first time in a long while—

he had dreamed about **that age**.

“…Fire rain.”

“It’s been a long time.”

Someone had once said that trauma from fire never truly disappeared.

The image remained branded into the old hound’s eyes.

Several minutes after waking—

his hair still stood upright.

Gooseflesh still covered his arms.

Drip.

Drip.

Splash.

Vikir washed his face with salt water falling from the ceiling.

The icy water ran through his hair.

Only then—

did the dream release him.

The Long Rain of Terror.

The season that had hurt humanity more than any other disaster during the Age of Destruction.

The period when countless fireballs fell from the sky and burned the world.

Most faces Vikir had known—

met—

or remembered—

had turned black and vanished under that rain.

*I wonder if Cindywendy completed everything I asked.*

Vikir sat heavily against the wall.

He had warned her long ago.

*A great flood is coming.*

*Prepare an ark.*

Now—

he thought about the future.

It had been nearly two years since he entered Nouvelle Vague.

*It feels like I was in solitary only a few days ago.*

*Time moves strangely fast.*

Much had changed.

Level Ten construction had advanced enormously.

The already-deep crater had become deeper still.

Now—

people called it the **Great Crater**.

Prisoners died.

New prisoners arrived.

Those who had controlled the prison population two years earlier had weakened under brutal labor and starvation.

Some died.

Some lost influence.

Their places were taken by younger inmates—

or by prisoners who had served less time.

A handful of new guards had also arrived.

Most did not last.

Prisoner resistance.

The territorial behavior of veteran guards.

The brutal environment.

Unknown endemic illnesses.

Many died on duty.

But Vikir—

for the most part—

had gotten what he wanted.

Level Ten progressed.

He caused no incidents.

He worked harder than anyone.

And both prisoners and guards had come to trust him.

There was only one thing he could not obtain.

**News.**

More precisely—

news from the surface.

Nearly two years inside Nouvelle Vague.

What had happened outside?

Vikir tried repeatedly to find out.

Nothing.

Most guards knew no more than prisoners.

Only a tiny group of senior officers received real information.

Once—

Vikir caught a small clue.

An unreliable one.

“Damn it.”

“Why did this magazine stop coming?”

“I liked this one.”

Bastille had complained while throwing away a third-rate pornographic magazine.

It was infamous.

Crude.

Sexually controversial.

Very popular among guards.

Then—

suddenly—

no new issues arrived.

A bestseller could simply end.

That was possible.

But Vikir considered other possibilities.

*Supply problem?*

*No paper?*

*The publisher destroyed?*

*Or does nobody on the surface have enough peace left to buy trash like this?*

He rested his chin on one hand.

It might be ridiculous—

building a theory from one missing magazine.

But paranoia had kept him alive before.

Preparation never hurt.

*The demons are probably preparing too.*

Maybe they had begun—

the very day Vikir entered Nouvelle Vague.

“…I need results soon.”

Impatience.

A rare feeling.

Then—

BWOOOOO—

Four o’clock.

The wake-up horn cut through the air like a blade.

As always—

Vikir had already been awake for half an hour.

He was the first to leave his cell.

The BDISSEM barrier softened strangely—

almost like leaves folding aside—

creating an opening.

An absurd material.

“Labor begins, trash!”

Bastille’s shout drove everyone toward the work site.

Miserable food.

Almost no rest.

Bodies growing thinner every day.

Across two years—

Vikir had watched countless faces appear and disappear.

Most prisoners crawled toward the labor zone looking as though they would die at any moment.

Every day resembled yesterday.

Tomorrow would resemble today.

Scorching air.

Exploding steam.

Sulfur.

Flame.

Burning ground.

Heavy loads.

No sleep.

Sweat.

Hunger.

A daily race toward death.

The prisoners had no past.

No present.

No future.

Their thoughts were always the same.

Before labor—

*Maybe I should just die.*

During labor—

*Please let me live.*

After labor—

*I survived today.*

The same cycle.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Vikir was different.

*Today.*

*Today it has to happen.*

Every day was a new attempt.

Every moment—

another exploration.

Every task—

a race toward a fixed destination.

Unlike the others—

Vikir had entered Nouvelle Vague willingly.

He knew exactly what he wanted.

For two years—

not one day passed without him breaking stone—

digging earth—

forcing the crater deeper.

And today—

at last—

something changed.

BOOM!

An unfamiliar explosion shook the center of Level Nine.

Explosions were common here.

Like hunger.

Like death.

This one was different.

Heavier.

Louder.

And followed by an unusually long earthquake.

“RUN!”

“Lava wave!”

“What the hell was that?!”

“Everyone out!”

Guards and prisoners retreated.

Falling rock.

Rising lava.

Every sign warned of another collapse.

But one person moved forward.

Vikir.

While everyone else panicked—

he stared through the broken strata.

At something blue.

Something burning—

yet not red.

A sphere.

A blue mass of incandescent lava.

A being that roared—

and shook the earth.

Vikir whispered:

“…Found you.”

“Poseidon.”

Humanity’s hope.

The counterattack of the Human Alliance.

After nearly two years inside Nouvelle Vague—

Vikir had finally found what he came for.
