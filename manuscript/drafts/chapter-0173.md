Vikir stared at the morning paper.

*They actually published it.*

He had selected some of the most absurd condemnations he had heard—

written them down—

and submitted the result.

Now it had thousands of views.

Hundreds of comments.

*Fear of the Night Hound is spreading.*

Parents in the Imperial Capital had apparently begun warning crying children—

*Stop crying or the Night Hound will come take you.*

Vikir had become a proper urban monster.

A villain large enough to frighten an Empire.

*I'll need to be more careful while hunting demons outside the Academy.*

Running into Imperial Knights—

would be annoying.

While Vikir considered future operations—

a cold voice cut through the room.

“Vikir.”

He looked up.

**Professor Banshee Morgue** was staring directly at him.

“Eyes closed again?”

“Thinking?”

“Or simply not paying attention?”

“Yes.”

“That's what I was doing.”

Banshee's eye twitched.

“Still shameless.”

“Let's see whether you have the knowledge to justify it.”

As usual—

Banshee disliked Vikir's attitude.

So he attacked with questions.

Deliberately twisted ones.

Problems beyond undergraduate level.

“The Imperial Army suffers every rainy season from outbreaks of giant parasitic worms on the Western Front.”

“Give separate responses for specimens under one meter and those above ten meters.”

“Travelers are increasingly attacked by goblins.”

“They are small and individually weak, but dangerous in groups to people without mana.”

“Give a realistic measure that could protect travelers departing through the Imperial Capital gates.”

Then—

without pause—

“The greatest threat to the knights and mages defending the highland fortress is not the wyvern, but it also cannot be said that it isn't the wyvern.”

“Can the opposite therefore be regarded as true?”

“Answer quickly.”

Vikir did.

“For the smaller parasites under one meter—”

“salt, sugar, or carbonic acid can inflict fatal damage.”

“For the larger specimens—”

“copper arrows repel them efficiently.”

Next.

“Goblins have highly sensitive noses.”

“They hate strong foul odors.”

“When ginkgo trees drop fruit—”

“don't discard it.”

“Collect it.”

“Give a bag to travelers leaving the capital.”

“If goblins attack, throw the fruit.”

“Most will lose the will to fight and retreat from the smell.”

Banshee narrowed his eyes.

“And the third question?”

“Yes.”

Silence.

Vikir remained calm.

There were two reasons he almost never failed these attacks.

First—

Banshee's course centered on **Practical Monster Strategy**.

That had been Vikir's profession for decades.

Battlefield survival.

Monster weaknesses.

Efficient killing.

He could answer from experience what many researchers knew only from old papers.

Sometimes—

his solutions could supplement—

or completely replace—

research that was already decades out of date.

Second—

Banshee punished badly answered questions in a particularly irritating way.

Not only the individual.

The entire department could lose attitude points.

Vikir preferred to avoid attention.

But once Banshee chose him as the target—

failing deliberately would make every Cold Department student remember who cost them points.

People held grudges far longer than gratitude.

So—

answering correctly—

and occasionally earning points for everyone—

was actually the quieter option.

Banshee stared at him.

“…”

He wanted to be irritated.

The answers made that difficult.

They were answers a veteran might give—

someone who had spent decades surviving on battlefields.

How could an eighteen-year-old freshman know them?

Worse—

some contained methods not yet present in accepted scholarship.

Banshee remembered another incident.

*Venomphion.*

The huge desert scorpion.

Vikir had claimed it possessed a hidden second stinger.

Banshee had sent a specimen to the **Royal Monster Institute**.

The claim had been confirmed.

Interview requests followed.

Banshee had waved them away.

*It wasn't my discovery.*

*It was my student's.*

Since then—

his attention toward Vikir had sharpened.

Finally—

Banshee had to admit what irritated him most.

The boy was extremely knowledgeable.

“Impressive.”

“Very impressive.”

“Theoretically—”

“you know enough to stand here and teach your classmates.”

“Your seniors.”

“Possibly…”

His mouth twisted.

“…even me.”

“That's an exaggeration.”

Vikir's short answer made Banshee look even more offended.

Then the professor muttered—

“I hope you attend graduate school someday.”

“And if you do—”

“you should come under my supervision.”

For the first time—

Vikir felt a chill.

Colosseo graduate school was infamous.

So brutal—

there were jokes that defeated foreign prisoners sold into slavery felt pity for Academy graduate students.

The first-years did not know that.

They stared at Vikir in awe.

Recognition from Professor Banshee.

A graduate-school invitation.

Nearly impossible honors.

At the front—

Sinclaire no longer even pretended not to stare.

Her eyes were full of curiosity.

◆◆◆

Class ended.

Students packed their things and moved toward the next lecture—

or their dormitories.

Vikir joined them.

Then Banshee stepped into his path.

“Vikir.”

His voice carried faint displeasure.

“Your column.”

“The one in the newspaper.”

Banshee was Ryukeion's faculty advisor.

He had been the person who chose to publish Vikir's anti–Night Hound commentary.

Now—

he seemed genuinely curious.

“Did you really see the Night Hound during volunteer service?”

“Exactly as I wrote.”

“I happened to be walking a corridor at night.”

“I caught one brief glimpse.”

“So you truly know nothing about his face?”

“Or voice?”

“No.”

“I see.”

Banshee frowned.

Then muttered—

“Good.”

“Those flies keep sticking to me.”

He looked back at Vikir.

“If outside reporters harass you for interviews—”

“tell me.”

“I have a duty to protect students working for the Academy newspaper.”

“Yes.”

Banshee nodded.

Then—

“…Wait.”

He turned back.

“If you were on volunteer service—”

“that hour was after lights-out.”

“And during curfew.”

“Why were you wandering a corridor?”

“It wasn't even in the direction of the toilets or water dispensers.”

“Sorry.”

“…Sorry?”

“That's all?”

“Yes.”

Banshee pinched the bridge of his nose.

“I know perfectly well that boys and girls traditionally gather to drink on the last night of volunteer service.”

“Do they?”

“Yes.”

“That doesn't mean I must officially tolerate it.”

“Understood.”

Banshee opened the ledger.

One point.

Deducted from Vikir's attitude score.

“Don't wander around at night again.”

The Night Hound—

was being told not to wander at night.

Vikir nodded.

Banshee gave a satisfied snort and left.

Only then did the friends watching from a distance approach.

Pigi arrived first.

“Vikir!”

“Did you just lose an attitude point?”

“Why?”

“Breaking curfew on the last night of volunteering.”

Pigi looked horrified.

“You told him!?”

“Lie!”

“If you lose more points—”

“you'll be doing service again!”

“Though this time it would only be inside the Academy…”

Because of the current instability—

the Academy had suspended off-campus student service for the moment.

Future penalties would be worked off on campus—

possibly during the precious festival period after midterms.

That—

was unpleasant enough.

Tudor and Sancho joined them.

“What did Professor Banshee want?”

“Probably another lecture.”

Both worked with Vikir in Ryukeion.

Both knew he had written the published column.

Tudor leaned closer.

“Seriously.”

“You actually saw the Night Hound that night?”

“Yes.”

“Why didn't you tell us?”

“You looked so serious on the way back.”

Tudor put a hand on his shoulder.

“You must've been terrified.”

“I thought you kept falling behind because you were still embarrassed about wetting yourself.”

“If something like that happens again—”

“talk to us.”

Sancho nodded solemnly.

Then came the questions.

What did the Night Hound look like?

How frightening was he?

Vikir supplied a deliberately useless answer.

“Very tall.”

“Broadly built.”

“Seemed older.”

“Face covered by a mask.”

“Black cloak over the whole body.”

“Hoarse voice.”

“I was too far away to hear clearly.”

Tudor rubbed goosebumps from his arm.

“You really saw him.”

“That must've been terrifying.”

Sancho nodded.

“I might not have been able to move.”

“You have strong nerves.”

Pigi swallowed.

“They say the Night Hound is at least Graduator-level.”

“How strong is that?”

“Stronger than professors?”

“Surely not…”

The same puppies who had once bragged that they would catch the Night Hound—

were now fluffing their fur in fear.

Vikir found it—

slightly endearing.

Then—

“What's this?”

“A group of idiots from the Cold Department?”

Another puppy stepped into their path.

Tall.

Dark-haired.

Handsome.

Cold-faced.

A small snake badge at his chest.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

All stiffened.

“…Granola.”

**Granola de Leviathan.**

Third son of one of the Seven Great Houses.

A first-year elite of the Hot Department.

Its vice class representative—

and an exceptionally arrogant one.
