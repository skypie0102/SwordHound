# Chapter 110: The Great Banquet (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/110.txt` — `b02e3225fee0f4da4fa440e01751c5c6c2915a6c`. Recovered English Chapter 109 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Hugo Le Baskerville.

Patriarch of the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan.

One of the seven pillars supporting the great Empire of Rock.

Back in the age when rival powers divided the continent, Hugo had made enormous contributions to the wars that unified them beneath one empire.

For those achievements, he had earned the name **Sword Star** and the title of Marquis.

He had been forty years old.

Roughly ten years had passed since then.

Hugo was a man of iron blood and a furnace heart.

He had driven violent tribes far beyond the frontier and eradicated hundreds of species of monsters.

His strength and achievements had even given him great influence in the empire's central politics.

Countless Baskerville hounds had died along the way.

Hugo had never cared.

Once he chose a direction, he never looked back.

His resolve was as blunt and absolute as an axe falling.

…And yet—

that iron-blooded man was shaking now.

Hugo stared at the little girl in front of him with an expression John Barrymore had not seen in decades of serving him.

Pomeranian.

The child who had burst from the adjoining room had run straight into Vikir's arms.

“Cold! Thunder! Boom, boom! Scary!”

Vikir could only hold her.

Hugo's reaction was too strange for him to do anything else.

He glanced sideways.

Barrymore looked even more stunned.

That was understandable.

The invincible Hugo Le Baskerville did not make faces like this.

Shock.

Fear.

A silence so awkward it seemed to freeze the office.

Then Hugo took one step forward as though possessed.

“Y-you… that face… could it be…?”

His hand trembled.

So did his voice.

Vikir had never realized until now that either of them could.

Hugo's unsteady gaze fixed on the side of Pomeranian's face.

Black hair half covered her blood-red eye.

Pomeranian peeked out from Vikir's arms.

Then recoiled.

“Waaah! Scary!”

She immediately buried herself deeper against Vikir.

A bearded middle-aged man approaching with a trembling hand would have frightened most children.

“Mustache man! Nose hair! Gross! Don't like!”

Hugo froze.

The words struck him harder than any blade.

“M-my… mustache?”

He raised a shaking hand beneath his nose.

Vikir stepped in.

“Father. Please calm down.”

The coldness in his voice seemed to pull Hugo back to himself.

Once Hugo regained some of his usual composure, Vikir continued.

“I found this child on the road. I only brought her along to run small errands. Why are you so concerned about her?”

“A child you… found on the road?”

Hugo answered Vikir, but his eyes never left Pomeranian.

She was already pressed too tightly into Vikir's arms for him to see her face properly.

When Hugo leaned to one side for another look, Barrymore intervened.

“My lord. Homeless children are as common as grains of sand. There is nothing remarkable about Young Master Vikir taking in one as an errand girl.”

Hugo's trembling finally stopped.

“…A mistake, then. Yes. The butler is right.”

His familiar expression returned.

Cold.

Controlled.

Unmoving.

And yet his fingers still drifted toward his mustache.

Barrymore watched them.

“Does your mustache feel uncomfortable, my lord?”

“…What are you talking about, Barrymore?”

“Ah. Nothing. I spoke without thinking.”

Hugo shot him a severe look.

Then he sat back down and exhaled.

“I apologize, son. For a moment, I mistook something. You had to witness an embarrassing lapse.”

Hugo had apologized.

That surprised Vikir almost as much as everything before it.

He called for Chihuahua.

“Director. The thunder frightened her. Give her a quiet room tonight.”

“Of course. I'll prepare some hot cocoa as well.”

Chihuahua took Pomeranian with practiced ease.

The familiarity between them helped reinforce Vikir's story.

If Chihuahua had clearly known the girl for some time, Hugo could more easily dismiss his own reaction as a momentary delusion.

Chihuahua was quick-witted.

He sensed Vikir's predicament immediately.

After giving Hugo and Barrymore a polite nod, he shifted naturally between Hugo and Pomeranian, blocking the child's face from view.

Then he carried her from the office.

Hugo covered part of his face with one hand and coughed dryly.

Vikir watched him.

A Sword Master who had reached the extreme was not merely strong in body.

Such a person possessed a soul tempered through brutal discipline.

Strength of mind and body alike was necessary to reach that realm.

For a man like Hugo to lose control so completely—

*There is definitely something here.*

Vikir decided he needed to think much more carefully about Pomeranian.

Then Hugo spoke.

“…Son.”

“Yes, Father.”

Vikir bowed briefly.

The next words were exactly what he had expected.

“Attend the Great Banquet tomorrow.”

The Great Banquet.

A small family dinner open only to a select handful among Baskerville's direct bloodline—those who had already distinguished themselves enough to stand among the family's elite.

For most lower-ranking Baskervilles, attending even once was a lifelong dream.

Vikir had once felt the same.

An ordinary seventeen-year-old Baskerville, especially one bearing the middle name *Van*, would have leaped with joy at Hugo's invitation.

Vikir simply nodded.

“I'll see you tomorrow evening, Father.”

Hugo looked at him for a moment, then nodded.

Only Barrymore raised a handkerchief to the corners of his reddening eyes.

*He's become so composed.*

The old butler's thoughts were almost painfully obvious.

◆◆◆

That night, Vikir returned to the residential room assigned to him at City Hall.

He bathed in hot water and lay down on the soft bed.

It felt wrong.

For the last two years, he had slept on straw, leaves, earth, and stone.

The comfort beneath him was more unsettling than the hard ground had ever been.

Eventually, he climbed out of bed and lay on the floor instead.

Before doing so, he slipped a pillow beneath the blankets, shaping the bedding as though someone still slept there.

An occupational habit born from years of assassinations.

“…”

Bare floor beneath his back.

No blanket.

Only then did his body relax.

*Come to think of it, I used to choose hard stone whenever I camped during hunts too.*

Two years among Ballak's hunters had left marks that would not disappear overnight.

Vikir folded his arms beneath his head.

His thoughts returned to the afternoon.

*Hugo's reaction to Pomeranian was far beyond anything I expected.*

Until now, Vikir had hesitated over whether to tell Hugo about Pomeranian and the portrait pendant.

After seeing him today, the shape of an answer had begun to emerge.

*I'll test him after tomorrow's banquet.*

Then he would choose the right moment to reveal the card in his hand.

Perhaps it could strike Hugo harder than any blade.

But one condition came first.

Pomeranian could not be harmed.

Vikir closed his eyes and began arranging possibilities.

Then—

“…!”

One eye opened.

Hiss—

A breath of wind so faint an ordinary person would never have noticed.

Cold night air touched his skin.

The window had opened without a sound.

A black shadow slipped inside.

It approached the bed.

A short, sharp dagger appeared in its hand, carrying a faint trace of mana.

Soon the intruder stood beside the place where Vikir was supposed to be sleeping.

But instead of stabbing down—

the shadow carefully lifted one edge of the blanket.

Then slipped beneath it.

Vikir watched the entire thing from the floor.

“You've been quick to gather information. I haven't even been back a full day.”

He rose with a dry smile.

The physical ability of a Peak Sword Graduator erupted in an instant.

Clang!

Beelzebub flashed from his hand and knocked the dagger aside.

Thunk!

The short blade spun across the room and buried itself deep in the wall.

At the same time, Vikir's other hand shot forward.

His fingers closed around the intruder's throat.

An arm was twisted behind the shadow's back.

A heartbeat later, the intruder was pinned against the bed with Vikir above them.

He leaned closer.

“Next time, request a meeting properly.”

His voice was quiet.

“If you sneak in like this again, I might kill you by accident.”

“…I'll keep that in mind.”

The answer remained remarkably calm despite the choking pressure.

The black cloth covering the intruder's face slipped away.

A familiar face appeared beneath it.

Someone Vikir had not seen in a long time.
