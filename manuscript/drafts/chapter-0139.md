# Chapter 139: Men are Power (1)

“If you avoid Venomphion's first stinger on the tail—

and the second stinger hidden in its abdomen—

it's closer to a B+ monster than an A.”

Professor Banshee frowned.

“…Second stinger?”

He looked at the preserved scorpion.

“What second stinger?”

Venomphion was known as a massive desert scorpion.

Everyone knew about the obvious weapon near its tail.

One strike from that venomous barb could kill a traveler before they managed more than a few steps.

That was why every surviving guide warned the same thing.

Watch the tail.

Avoid the stinger.

But—

two stingers?

Even Banshee had never heard that.

The Imperial danger rating was **A**, not B+.

Vikir finally realized the problem.

*Right.*

*This hasn't been discovered yet.*

Knowledge that would become common among adventurers during the Age of Destruction—

was still unknown in this era.

Venomphion research had barely begun.

Years from now—

a researcher would accidentally uncover the truth.

The monster possessed another venomous barb hidden beneath the armored plates of its abdomen.

The discovery would happen almost by accident.

A specimen's heavy exoskeleton would be broken down for research.

A dissolving solution would spill across the abdominal armor.

And beneath it—

a smaller concealed stinger would appear.

That one discovery would explain many previously confusing attack patterns.

It would also help establish safer hunting methods.

Eventually—

Venomphion's practical danger assessment would fall.

But none of that had happened yet.

Vikir stopped talking.

“…I may have used incorrect research material.”

Banshee's expression hardened.

“Incorrect material?”

His voice became colder.

“In my class?”

He looked toward the Cold Department.

“A student careless enough to make an error like that has no business here.”

“Ten attitude points deducted from the entire Cold Department.”

Groans filled the classroom.

Several students glared at Vikir.

Vikir sighed internally.

*That creates unnecessary enemies.*

He could take a personal penalty.

Dragging the entire department down with him—

was different.

So he spoke again.

“Before deducting group points—”

Banshee looked over.

“please examine the abdominal armor of your specimen.”

The professor's eyes narrowed.

“You're claiming your report is correct?”

“If it's wrong—”

Vikir answered—

“reduce my personal attitude score to zero.”

“But don't penalize everyone else.”

Banshee's mouth twitched.

A mocking smile.

“Very well.”

“Since you're willing to sacrifice yourself so nobly—”

“we'll test it.”

He turned over the preserved Venomphion.

Its curled legs exposed a broad armored plate beneath the abdomen.

A part of the specimen no one normally disturbed.

Banshee raised a scalpel.

Mana gathered along the blade.

He cut into the plate.

The front row leaned closer.

Then—

snap!

Something shot upward.

Students recoiled.

Banshee fell backward in surprise.

A small venomous barb had sprung from beneath the abdominal armor.

It passed close enough to sever a lock of hair from his forehead.

The room went dead silent.

Vikir looked at the stinger.

*That's why almost no one discovered it.*

Even after death—

Venomphion could launch one final attack.

Most people who found the hidden stinger—

never lived long enough to report it.

Banshee pushed himself back to his feet.

His eyes locked on Vikir.

“How did you know?”

“I guessed.”

Banshee stared.

“…Based on what?”

Vikir chose his explanation carefully.

“There are very few reliable eyewitness accounts of Venomphion.”

“Because most people who encounter one die.”

“I wondered whether the obvious tail stinger alone could explain that mortality.”

“So I looked for another mechanism.”

He pointed to the abdominal plates.

“The largest and most structurally complex armor was the most suspicious place.”

Banshee looked from Vikir—

to the hidden barb—

back to Vikir.

A hypothesis that simple—

leading to a major discovery?

Ridiculous.

Except the evidence was sitting on the table.

Banshee swallowed.

Then his academic mind took over.

“…I see.”

He looked more closely at the specimen.

“This also explains the irregular attack reports.”

Students leaned in.

“Venomphion sometimes abandons its pincers and attacks awkwardly with its middle or rear legs.”

Banshee pointed toward the concealed barb.

“If this abdominal stinger is single-use and takes a long time to regenerate—”

“then those seemingly erratic movements may simply be compensation after it has already discharged the hidden weapon.”

A murmur spread through the classroom.

“Oh…”

Understanding.

Excitement.

Everyone turned toward Vikir.

Almost everyone.

Sinclaire—

seated near the front—

looked down.

Her own report had divided Venomphion into three behavioral types.

Carefully researched.

Original.

Plausible.

And now—

one discovery from Vikir explained those apparently different behaviors more directly.

Around her—

students whispered.

“How did he figure that out?”

“That's incredible.”

“I thought being athletic was impressive.”

“But being that good at theory is something else.”

“Didn't he do well in Naphtali too?”

“That was without mana.”

“Practical exams will use mana.”

“He's a commoner, right?”

“No family training.”

“So the midterms could expose him.”

Another student shook her head.

“Still.”

“He offered to take the entire penalty himself.”

“That was decent.”

The general opinion of Vikir was improving.

Vikir disliked that fact regardless of whether the attention was positive or negative.

Nearby—

several first-year girls whispered among themselves.

“I think I figured out my type.”

“What?”

“Maybe I like the quiet, studious kind.”

“The nerd type.”

Another laughed.

“Only if the nerd is handsome.”

They glanced toward Vikir's bangs and glasses—

trying to imagine the face they had briefly seen after Naphtali.

One girl turned to Bianca.

“Bianca.”

“What do you think?”

“You have to admit he's not bad.”

Bianca Fo Usher looked up.

“Being handsome doesn't matter.”

“Being smart doesn't matter.”

Her friend blinked.

“Then what does?”

Bianca answered immediately.

“A man needs strength.”

No hesitation.

No embarrassment.

“I have no interest in someone weaker than me.”

Her standards were absolute.

“Vikir or anyone else.”

“If he can't even reach Expert level—”

“what's the point?”

“But he did well in Naphtali.”

“No mana.”

Bianca leaned back.

“Real strength is proved by aura.”

“Density.”

“Volume.”

“Control.”

She closed her eyes.

“When the practical midterms arrive—”

“we'll see.”

“To survive at this Academy, weak people eventually get exposed.”

Her friends sighed.

“That's harsh.”

“He's handsome and smart.”

“What more do you want?”

Bianca ignored them.

She glanced once toward Vikir.

*What's so interesting about him?*

A commoner.

Good at theory.

Physically unusual—

perhaps.

But no visible mana foundation.

*Boring.*

She yawned.

Then stopped paying attention.

◆◆◆

Far from the Academy—

in a neglected corner of the Imperial Capital—

stood a small, run-down orphanage.

Night had long since fallen.

“Please!”

A heavyset man crawled backward across the floor.

“Spare me!”

“I didn't do anything!”

The **Night Hound** stood over him.

Beelzebub's red aura glowed in the dark.

Vikir's brow furrowed.

The man had already mistaken him for a **Sword Master**.

Strictly speaking—

Vikir was still a **Peak Sword Graduator**.

But Beelzebub condensed his aura so densely—

so close to solid—

that the misunderstanding was understandable.

“…Doxeller.”

Vikir's voice was flat.

“Fifty-two.”

“District administrator.”

“Director of this orphanage.”

He stepped closer.

“You cooperated with demons.”

“Sold children.”

“And because the Imperial Capital has so many unacknowledged noble children—”

“you specialized in the ones you considered ‘high quality.’”

Doxeller shook his head frantically.

“No!”

“There's no such thing as a high-quality orphan!”

“Children are all—”

He stopped.

Too late.

Vikir's eyes narrowed.

The mask of benevolence had slipped.

A broker hiding behind the title of orphanage director.

Whether Doxeller fully understood who ultimately bought the children—

did not matter.

Vikir raised Beelzebub.

Then—

cold wind pressed against his back.

Not natural wind.

Something heavier.

More dangerous.

Vikir moved.

Boom!

A massive object struck where he had been standing.

Stone shattered.

Part of the structure collapsed.

Vikir turned.

A man in a neat black suit stood inside the dust.

His face was hidden beneath a stitched black hood.

A large hammer rested in his hands.

And from his body—

came an overwhelming stench of decay.

Demonic.

Vikir's attention sharpened.

*Ten Corpse?*

He tapped Beelzebub lightly.

The blade straightened from the impact it had just absorbed.

Red aura scattered from its tip.

The black-masked figure raised his hammer.

Boom!

He struck the ground.

Dust and broken stone surged upward—

blocking the scattered aura.

A low growl came from beneath the hood.

Vikir watched the black aura covering the hammer.

Dense.

Heavy.

Graduator-level.

Upper rank.

Strong—

but not strong enough to be one of the Ten Corpses themselves.

*A subordinate.*

Vikir's grip tightened.

“Good.”

“Then taking you should lead me closer to your master.”

The black-masked creature raised its hammer again.

Vikir's expression changed.

That stance.

That handling.

He knew it.

The creature was preparing a blunt-weapon technique.

A technique belonging to—

**Quovadis**.
