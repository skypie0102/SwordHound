# Chapter 472: Tochka Annihilation Battle (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/472.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 470 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established project terminology controls the six active Baskerville Counts, Baskerville Fourth/Sixth Forms, Marquis de Sade, D’Ordume, Souare, BDISSEM, Flubber J. Tarbond, Kirko Grimm, Sadi, Poison Humans, and Butler Barrymore.

Black shadows moved through the dust.

The hounds of House Baskerville—

running like wind through grain.

Waiting for them—

were the fighting dogs of earlier generations.

CLACK.

CLATTER.

CLACK.

Ancient Baskerville swordsmen approached.

Little remained but skin and bone.

Rusty metal.

Worn teeth.

Jawbones knocking together.

Then—

SHING.

The old famous blades bared their teeth.

And six current Counts drew theirs.

Boston Terrier.

Great Dane.

Isabella.

German.

Cu-Chulainn.

Metzgerhund.

Boston Terrier charged first.

Great Dane followed.

“Hahaha!”

“Toothless old dogs!”

“Who told you to imitate active-duty hounds?”

Great Dane glanced sideways.

“…Those are our ancestors.”

“Exactly!”

“If they’re dead—

they should stay buried and wait for memorial meals!”

The undead Baskervilles collided with the living.

Boston Terrier’s specialty—

broad slashes.

Wild.

Almost stormlike.

Imprecise—

but brutally destructive.

He gathered multiple undead into each attack and tore through them.

Great Dane—

the opposite.

Narrower trajectories.

Dense.

Heavy.

One gigantic undead hound rushed him.

Great Dane’s sword dropped.

The target split under sheer weight.

Then Isabella and German entered.

“Stop drifting into my area.”

“Go somewhere else.”

German bristled.

“Hmph.”

“You want all the credit?”

Isabella looked around the absurdly crowded battlefield.

“…With this many enemies?”

The two were often described as if their personalities had been exchanged.

German fought with fierce competitiveness—

almost persecution complex.

Isabella moved as though playing chess.

Minimal motion.

Minimal aura.

Maximum result.

One precise line—

and dozens fell.

German matched her with his own skill.

Undead fighters worth hundreds of ordinary soldiers collapsed like straw.

Meanwhile—

Metzgerhund and Cu-Chulainn remained near Vikir.

Almost like bodyguards.

Except their attention—

was on his sword.

“Sixth Form!”

“Show me your Sixth!”

“Fourth Form!”

“Let’s settle it with the Fourth!”

Both men were proud.

Almost arrogant.

But when someone had refined one technique to the edge of mastery—

that pride was earned.

Vikir felt their burning stares.

Then sighed.

Six fangs appeared.

BOOM!

The **Sixth Form** tore through the undead line.

Vikir had refined it over countless years.

Even by Baskerville standards—

the execution was master-class.

Then—

without delay—

four fangs.

The **Fourth Form**.

The technique he had spent his entire first life polishing.

This life had only sharpened it further.

The four fangs extended as naturally as breathing.

Undead Baskervilles exploded apart.

“…Oh.”

“…As expected.”

Metzgerhund and Cu-Chulainn stared.

Then became even more competitive.

BOOM!

Metzgerhund’s Sixth Form smashed one section of enemy line.

CRASH!

Cu-Chulainn’s Fourth Form crushed another.

The Counts advanced almost unopposed.

Baskerville knights followed behind them.

At the very front—

Vikir.

SLASH!

Every swing of Beelzebub sent more heads into the air.

Then—

whoosh.

A damp southeast wind passed over the battlefield.

Carried away some of the blood smell.

Through the red fog and dust—

Vikir saw something.

“…!”

Giants.

Poison Humans—

more than ten meters tall.

Their bodies had been stretched for height.

Bones and muscle had become fragile in exchange.

But the arms—

were absurdly long.

Long enough to reach over Tochka’s walls.

Red Death poured from every pore.

*Siege mutants.*

Specially made for climbing and breaking fortress walls.

If they reached Tochka—

the defense would become much harder.

Orca could probably respond.

But every little bit of pressure mattered.

Refugees remained behind those walls.

Fortunately—

the giants were slow.

Vikir decided immediately.

“Independent detachment!”

“Take down the tall mutants first!”

“Yes!”

The six Counts turned without hesitation.

Hundreds of knights shifted with them.

Vikir redirected the formation across the battlefield.

Then—

a laugh floated from nearby.

“Hehehehehe.”

“Where are you young pups running so desperately?”

Vikir looked over.

An old man stood on a high rock.

**Marquis de Sade.**

Behind him—

Nouvelle Vague’s strangest collection.

D’Ordume.

Souare.

BDISSEM.

Flubber.

Kirko.

Even demonized Sadi.

Vikir stared.

“…What kind of group is this?”

“An arrest squad?”

Sade laughed harder.

“Hehehe!”

“Don’t be stupid.”

“I borrowed them from that old bastard Orca.”

“Elite squads are better for counterattacks.”

The irony—

was impossible to miss.

Former guards—

now following the prisoner they once contained.

D’Ordume and Souare looked especially unhappy.

Souare—

who had once managed Sade’s solitary confinement—

looked almost offended by reality itself.

Sade pointed ahead.

“You’re heading to the wall?”

“Yes.”

“If the giants reach it—

we’ll have a problem.”

“Hehehe.”

“Fast judgment.”

“And correct.”

Sade—

a tactical monster—

had reached the same conclusion.

Then his gaze shifted toward the six Counts.

“Oh.”

“So that unfamiliar pressure was you.”

“The new Baskerville Counts?”

All six frowned at **new**.

The replies came immediately.

Boston Terrier smiled.

“Aren’t you too old for a battlefield?”

“You should be closer to those undead over there.”

Great Dane nodded solemnly.

“Senior.”

“That’s rude.”

“A man his age must have needed enormous courage just to leave the rear.”

Isabella’s eyes were colder.

“An Imperial special-class war criminal.”

“We’ll discuss returning you to prison after the civil war.”

German snorted.

“Watching criminals clean up their image is disgusting.”

“You got lucky, old man.”

Metzgerhund studied Sade.

“Legendary monster from the previous generation.”

“Good.”

“I need something to test my improved hunting instinct on.”

Cu-Chulainn said only:

“…Strong.”

“I want to fight you.”

Sade laughed.

“The younger the puppy—

the louder it barks.”

“This generation of Baskerville seems unimpressive.”

“Cane Corso was the only real one—”

BOOM!

An explosion near Tochka cut him off.

Vikir and Sade both turned.

The tall Poison Humans—

which had been reaching toward the walls—

began falling.

Black slashes cut their ankles from below.

One.

Then another.

Then another.

Sade’s eyes brightened.

“Oh?”

“There’s someone useful down there.”

“Who is it?”

The force guarding the base of the wall advanced toward them.

At its head—

a black storm.

Then a voice.

“I heard the red whistle.”

“But I was slightly late to assemble.”

“I dislike blaming age.”

“But years are inconvenient things.”

Vikir froze.

The man stepped through the blood.

Neat suit.

Perfect posture.

Elegant mustache.

Then—

a formal bow.

“…You truly have grown, Young Master.”

**Butler Barrymore**—

stood before him.
