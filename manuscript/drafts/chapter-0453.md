# Chapter 453: Declaration of War (4)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/453.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 451 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established project terminology controls Vikir, Sakkuth de Leviathan, Flubber, Dolores L. Quovadis, Red Death, Poison Humans, Baskerville Eighth Form, Cane Corso, Long Rain of Terror, Tochka, Seven Counts, and Morgue Light / Dark Factions. The local witness’s “Rainy Season of Fire” is normalized to the already accepted **Long Rain of Terror**.

CRACK.

A neck broke.

Smoke drifted across the battlefield.

Vikir looked down at what he had just killed.

At first glance—

human.

Except for the broken neck.

Then the details became obvious.

Blood-red eyes.

Red spots across the skin.

No sanity behind the expression.

A **Poison Human**.

Mindless.

Violent.

Drawn toward ordinary people the moment it saw them.

And worse—

contagious.

A bite.

Enough exposure to the red miasma leaking from the skin.

Either could turn another human into the same thing.

They resembled low-grade undead.

Zombies.

But that comparison understated the danger.

The bodies were too strong.

The poison too aggressive.

“Tsk.”

“These are worse than him.”

Vikir remembered someone from Nouvelle Vague.

**Sakkuth de Leviathan.**

The infamous Leviathan criminal who had taken responsibility for the **First Red Death Incident**—

and been permanently cast out of his family.

Sakkuth had once said something strange.

*How did you know?*

*There is no plague toxin in my blood.*

At the time—

Sakkuth was strong.

Savage.

Barely sane.

But still capable of thought.

And—

critically—

not contagious.

No outbreak followed him through Nouvelle Vague before Flubber devoured him.

These new Poison Humans were different.

Stronger.

More obedient.

Able to follow orders from some controlling force.

And the Red Death they emitted—

was more contagious than the old version.

*How are there so many?*

Poison should have a production limit.

Raw materials.

Time.

Facilities.

Yet these things appeared in numbers that defied that logic.

The mystery had existed even before Vikir’s regression.

House Leviathan’s mass production of Poison Humans—

had never been fully explained.

*At least Dolores is here now.*

A timely priestly blessing could save someone bitten or exposed.

And the space immediately around the Saintess—

filled with holy power—

could repel the Red Death itself.

Bringing Dolores to his side early—

and helping her grow stronger—

had been one of Vikir’s most important changes to history.

Vikir checked the battlefield.

Bodies everywhere.

He made sure none still moved.

Then looked at the ground.

Huge gouges.

Eight deep fang marks.

His own work.

*My Eighth Form has improved again.*

Even after Nouvelle Vague—

even after the Hell Tree—

there was still room to refine it.

That realization made him more cautious—

not less.

Cane Corso’s words returned.

[You may never reach the Ninth Form while alive.]

[The Ninth exists beyond the threshold of death.]

[To reach the Tenth—

you must die and awaken again.]

A legendary island visible only on stormy nights.

A rainbow one could see—

but never touch.

The Ninth Form existed.

Vikir knew that.

But the path remained closed.

*Is death truly the only way across?*

Even among Sword Masters—

depth existed.

A higher principle.

Something beyond mere accumulation of aura.

Vikir had been thinking about it ever since Nouvelle Vague.

Then—

[AAAAAAH—!]

A shriek broke his concentration.

Something moved beneath the bodies.

A girl crawled out.

Young.

Not even ten.

Her skin—

covered in red spots.

Red mist leaked from her body.

She lunged with teeth and nails.

Vikir went still for a fraction of a second.

The face reminded him of someone.

*Nymphet.*

A child he had once met—

briefly—

while volunteering at the orphanage.

Vikir reached out.

Caught the girl by the neck.

CRACK.

The body went limp.

The violent spasms stopped.

At last—

peace.

Vikir had been moving from battlefield to battlefield—

destroying Poison Humans one cluster at a time.

This girl—

was the final target in the current area.

He looked around.

Wheel ruts.

A half-destroyed wagon.

Broken water jars.

“…As expected.”

“They’re targeting Tochka’s supply line.”

Every road carrying drinking water toward the fortress—

had been seeded with Poison Humans.

Leviathan was applying pressure already.

And something else was wrong.

Poison Human units had been disappearing from other civil-war fronts.

Not defeated.

Withdrawn.

Gathered.

*The full attack is coming.*

To the rest of the Empire—

a battle at Tochka might look small beside the vast fronts of the civil war.

To the demons—

it was different.

Too many dangerous people had gathered there.

Night Walkers.

Refugees.

Survivors.

People the demons had failed to eliminate.

A forgotten border fortress—

was becoming the place where humanity’s future would be decided.

Vikir closed his eyes.

And remembered.

Before regression—

House Leviathan had once surrounded Tochka with an enormous Poison Human army.

They justified the invasion with noble language.

A great house—

they claimed—

had the duty and right to administer land whose local rulers had lost the ability to govern.

What followed was called a territorial war.

In reality—

its scale approached war between nations.

At first—

the human alliance won.

Baskerville swordsmen crushed the enemy line.

The Seven Counts.

Seven knight orders.

Even old monsters from the Senate—

men dismissed as relics—

entered battle.

Enemies vanished like candles in wind.

Morgue joined them.

Light Faction.

Dark Faction.

Open force and hidden force working together.

Even a Leviathan strengthened by Donquixote and Usher—

began to buckle.

Victory tilted toward humanity.

*Then it happened.*

The **Long Rain of Terror**.

One hundred fifty days.

Five months.

Fire falling from the sky without rest.

A flood made of flame.

The demons’ final weapon.

The disaster that destroyed ninety-eight percent of humanity.

Vikir remembered the horizon.

Burned corpses.

Red-black earth.

A sky that never stopped burning.

Cold sweat appeared.

RUMBLE.

He opened his eyes.

Far away—

the sky over House Leviathan had darkened.

Clouds carried a disturbing red tint.

Even the lightning looked wrong.

Beneath them—

the withdrawn Poison Human armies were gathering.

Vikir had circled Leviathan’s main estate.

Searched for an opening.

There was none.

The defense was too tight.

He had hoped to discover the mass-production secret.

Failed.

*Still.*

*Knowing the army is concentrating is enough.*

Time to return.

Prepare.

Fortify Tochka.

The Red Death itself could be held back—

at least temporarily—

with Dolores at the center.

The larger problem was raw combat strength.

They would have to trust Tochka.

A natural fortress designed to make one defender worth a hundred attackers.

Outside the walls—

the Red Death would dance.

Inside—

the Saintess’s protection.

Two worlds divided by stone.

Once Vikir returned—

the gates would close.

No unnecessary sorties.

No wandering outside.

Everyone would hold.

“….”

Before leaving—

Vikir looked back at the dead girl.

How many more?

How many innocent people would die before this ended?

If the Age of Destruction arrived—

counting the survivors would become easier than counting the dead.

Children.

Ordinary workers.

People who knew nothing.

People neither heroic nor evil.

Moderately selfish.

Moderately kind.

Moderately cautious.

Ordinary.

Almost all of them—

gone.

WHOOSH.

Vikir struck a match.

Dropped it among the Poison Human bodies.

Fire spread.

Across cloth.

Hair.

Dry grass.

This famine-struck—

drought-stricken land caught easily.

Soon—

the corpses burned.

Vikir waited until the contamination was reduced to ash.

Then turned away.

The Night Hound was going home.

To the fortress built for defense.

To the people waiting for him.

To **Tochka**.

And to the battlefield where the final war would begin.
