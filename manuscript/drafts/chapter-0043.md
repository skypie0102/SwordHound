# Chapter 43: The Fiancée (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/043.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 43 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Established terminology controls **Camus Morgue**, **Raspane Morgue**, **Adolf Morgue**, **Highsis / Middlesis / Lowsis Morgue**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Rokoko Clan**, and **Morgue Clan**. The Chinese source controls Camus's age, first-life recollection, dialogue, and relationship/reveal chronology; later Fandom marriage material is not imported.

The supernova of Morgue.

The only daughter of the clan head, Raspane Morgue.

Bloodline, talent, temperament, appearance—there was no area in which she fell short.

No one doubted that she would one day become the head of the Morgue Clan.

*And yet right now, she's still just a fifteen-year-old girl.*

Vikir looked up at the killing ground of fire and iron spikes.

Camus Morgue stood proudly above it, looking down on everyone below.

The three battered sisters sprawled on the ground trembled at the sight of her.

“C-Camus, your sisters...”

“W-We were only trying to stop the intruders...”

“They started it first!”

Highsis, Middlesis, and Lowsis were a year older than Camus.

Yet under the sheer pressure Camus radiated, they could barely breathe. All three remained pressed to the ground.

For a prestigious magical family like Morgue, where even a small difference in age normally translated into a meaningful difference in achievement, the sight was unusual.

Then Camus smiled sweetly.

“Camus? Did you just address me by name, dear sisters?”

“Ah! N-No, ma'am!”

“Move. Unless you want to end up like those.”

Camus gestured impatiently.

Along the border stood corpses impaled on iron spikes and burned black.

It was now painfully obvious who had created that brutal display.

“A-Aah!”

Terrified by their younger sister's warning, the three sisters fled.

Once they disappeared, an eerie silence settled over the battlefield.

“...”

“...”

“...”

Even Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro, who had been baring their fangs moments earlier, lost their fighting spirit in front of Camus.

Soon Camus rode toward the Baskervilles.

She stopped her horse directly in front of Vikir, looked him in the eye, and spoke.

“Welcome, partner.”

Naturally, she meant partner in the joint punitive operation against the monsters and tribes of the mountains.

◆◆◆

The little Morgue girl Vikir had last seen seven years ago had changed considerably.

The freckles were gone.

So were the protruding teeth.

There was still a little youthful softness in her cheeks, but it was already easy to tell how striking she would become.

Vikir remembered seeing her a few times from a distance before his regression.

*She was around thirteen then, wasn't she? She really was beautiful.*

Even Vikir, who paid very little attention to women's appearances, had found her beauty remarkable.

“Dazzling” was not too strong a word for it.

It was said that if Morgue gathered all the love letters and marriage proposals sent to Camus, the clan would have enough firewood to last an entire winter.

Camus herself had known exactly how to use that attention.

She had kept countless men wrapped around her finger and had been linked to scandal after scandal.

Of course, there had been strategy behind it.

Although she privately despised the men drawn in by her beauty, she manipulated their affections to stir rivalry and conflict among powerful families, then directed the benefits produced by those struggles back toward Morgue.

It had been an intensely political game.

She gave neither her heart nor her body to any of them, which only made those men desire her more.

She had been like an empress ruling over a host of captives.

Under her influence, Morgue prospered, and she later distinguished herself greatly in the war against the Demon Realm.

...But all of that belonged to Vikir's first life.

The Camus of this life was strangely quiet by comparison.

By fifteen, she should already have been manipulating important figures from the Imperial Family and the other six great clans.

Yet no such rumors existed now.

For some reason, despite having reached an age when marriage negotiations were already common among great families, there was not even a whisper of her seeing any man.

*I don't understand it.*

Vikir rode on with a faint sense of puzzlement.

Camus stayed close beside him.

“...So Baskerville's position is that the tribal damage has reached this level, and we've classified the incidents accordingly... Morgue therefore decided to cooperate with Baskerville in dealing with them...”

She kept talking.

Vikir gave only the occasional short response.

Camus finally asked him directly,

“Does Baskerville really know so little about the tribes of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain?”

“How could we not? We fight them constantly. Baskerville probably knows them better than Morgue does.”

“Then why send only these rookies? They all look like half-trained hounds.”

Camus had evidently gauged the visible strength of the Baskerville delegation already.

Vikir almost answered, then stopped himself.

Morgue already knew that Baskerville's true main force was hidden beyond the mountains.

“You're asking even though you already know?”

Camus gave a mischievous laugh.

“I wanted to see whether *you* knew.”

“Of course I know. It's an internal clan matter, and I'm the person leading this delegation.”

“You could have been a disposable puppet your family sent to take the blame. But now I know.”

Camus urged her horse slightly ahead, then glanced back with a small smile.

“You're trusted quite a bit inside Baskerville.”

Her questioning continued.

“Do you know which tribe is the most troublesome?”

Of course Vikir knew.

Before his regression, searching for and suppressing enemies throughout Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain had been one of his main duties.

“From Morgue's perspective, the worst would be the warrior tribe, **Ballak**. Then the shamanic **Rokoko Clan**.”

The Ballak Tribe appeared most frequently around the frontier.

Their population was small, but each individual possessed formidable combat ability, enough to make even Baskerville wary.

“Their archery is far more powerful than the Empire's. We still don't fully understand its principles.”

“Mm. Exactly. They're difficult people to read.”

Camus nodded, then looked at Vikir with bright eyes.

“You know a lot about the mountain tribes. Eight points.”

“Huh?”

“Out of one hundred. You failed.”

Camus stuck out her tongue.

Vikir frowned.

“I answered correctly. Why is my score so low?”

“Because you answered correctly.”

“...?”

Seeing that he still did not understand, Camus's lips curled into a sly smile.

“What good is a man who's too clever?”

“...?”

“I don't like clever men. A man should have a little stupidity in him.”

Apparently, the mindset that had once enabled her to toy with countless admirers had not disappeared.

Vikir decided the remark was not worth answering and urged his horse forward.

Camus followed without hesitation and continued scoring him.

“Horsemanship: six points. Is the horse too big for you because I'm shorter than you?”

“...”

“Fashion sense: four points. Your clothes are much too dark.”

“...”

“No answer? Manners: one point.”

“...”

“Hmm. Face: ninety-nine points! You grew up well. Minus one because you don't manage your expressions properly.”

After listening to this long enough, even Vikir's ears began to feel hot.

He cut her off.

“Stop with these pointless evaluations.”

“Why are they pointless?”

“What possible use do they have?”

“Obviously they're evaluations we'll need for our future.”

*Our future?*

At Vikir's incredulous look, Camus shrugged and lifted her chin.

“If you're a candidate to become my future husband, naturally I have to examine you carefully.”

“...”

“If you think that's unfair, you can evaluate me too.”

“...”

“Actually, I'd rather you did. I need to know what you think of me.”

Vikir stared at her.

“Why am I a candidate to become your husband?”

“Why? You passed my uncle's test last time.”

Vikir thought for a moment and remembered his duel with Adolf Morgue seven years ago.

Adolf had balanced a water jar on his head.

At the final moment of the contest, Vikir had broken Adolf's sword and used a flying fragment to shatter the jar.

That had been enough to pass Adolf's test.

*...But that was seven years ago.*

Camus spoke as though it had happened yesterday.

“How could Uncle use such a simple test to decide whether someone qualifies as my husband candidate!? I'm furious! Because of what he decided that day, my entire marriage path has been blocked! He made that promise in front of everyone, so now I'm practically committed! But what can I do? A promise is sacred law! Ugh, I hate it, but I suppose I have to accept my fate. I lost. I lost...!”

No one had accused her of anything, yet she worked herself into a fever all on her own.

Vikir watched her and thought,

*As expected of an outstanding fire mage.*

If one trained fire magic far enough, could one eventually make oneself spontaneously combust from embarrassment?

Vikir was mildly curious.

Still, one matter was one matter and diplomacy was another.

It would be troublesome to offend someone who might one day become the matriarch of Morgue.

So Vikir tried to be considerate.

“Forget what happened that day. Let's treat it as though it never happened.”

Camus went rigid.

Vikir watched her freeze and thought,

*Paralysis magic? That was instantaneous. Why did she cast it on herself?*

Mages occasionally did incomprehensible things.

He did not particularly care, but for diplomatic purposes he was about to ask what was wrong when—

“Hey! How can you pretend something never happened when it *did* happen!?”

Camus suddenly shouted.

For the first time since his regression, Vikir was genuinely caught off guard.

He opened his mouth, but Camus shouted again.

“I'm a genius! If I see something once, I never forget it! Remember that!”

Something flew at Vikir's face.

He caught it.

A piece of black cloth.

It was the right size for an eight-year-old child to wear as a cloak.

The Baskerville insignia was clearly visible on it.

It was the cloak Vikir had used seven years ago to cover Camus after her clothes had been destroyed.

Even after all that time, the fabric still carried the faint smell of sweat from that day.

Vikir held it up and frowned at Camus, who had already ridden farther ahead.

*If she isn't going to take it back, she can keep it.*
