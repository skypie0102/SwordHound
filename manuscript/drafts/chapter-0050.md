# Chapter 50: The Husband Hunt (5)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/050.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 50 is an aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English forms used here include **Ballak Tribe**, **Aiyen**, **Ahun**, **Adonai**, **Divine Archer**, **Hugo Le Baskerville**, **Adolf Morgue**, and **Sword Graduator**. The source's contemporary **Night Fox** remains deliberately unidentified: later Fandom material naming Akwilla and Aiyen's future succession is chronology-spoilered and is not imported. `境外三灾` is kept descriptively as one of the Empire's **Three Calamities Beyond the Border** pending stronger dedicated canonical evidence.

Vikir wrinkled his nose.

*...Passed?*

He had no idea what Aiyen meant by that.

There was no time to press her for an explanation.

Instead, Vikir searched his memories for anything that might tell him exactly who the Ballak girl was.

Fragments of information from before his regression surfaced one after another.

*Ballak Tribe. An isolated mountain people. Population roughly three to five hundred. Lives in the highlands of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain. Hunters who ride wolves and use bows. Extremely poor relations with the Empire. Both men and women fight. Every tribesperson possesses considerable combat ability...*

Once he had sorted through the fragments, Vikir had a clearer picture.

The Ballak were not widely known beyond the mountains, but a few names from their history had reached even the Empire.

The first was **Adonai**, the legendary warrior known as the **Divine Archer**.

He had been one of the greatest warriors in Ballak history and one of the Empire's so-called **Three Calamities Beyond the Border**.

Under him, Ballak had entered one of its greatest eras.

Adonai had once declared war on the Empire virtually by himself.

During a war lasting one hundred days, he killed **542 Sword Graduators**.

The number of ordinary soldiers who died was beyond counting.

The Empire suffered such astonishing losses in those hundred days that afterward it effectively classified Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain as lawless territory and avoided another full-scale war there.

Imperial histories naturally tried to diminish the humiliation.

Even stripped of exaggeration, however, the achievement remained absurd.

*...But this isn't Adonai's era.*

Vikir considered the timeline.

Adonai belonged to a generation long before his own.

Even Vikir had only learned of the Divine Archer through histories and old battlefield accounts.

Adonai had disappeared around the time the Baskerville Clan first moved west to develop the frontier.

Most historians assumed he died around then.

Hugo Le Baskerville had not even been born yet.

So who else from the Ballak had become famous enough to fit the woman in front of him?

*If I think of someone more recent... the Night Fox.*

The mysterious archer had fought Baskerville and Morgue for ten days before vanishing.

When Vikir was young in his first life, the two great clans had mounted a large joint punitive expedition into Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

One Ballak sniper had distinguished herself so brutally that the survivors gave her a title.

**Night Fox.**

Almost nothing else had been known about her except that she was a woman.

Over ten days, she killed sixteen Baskerville Sword Graduators, ten Morgue Fourth-Circle mages, and six Fifth-Circle mages.

Thirty-two elites in total.

Vikir had been too young to witness that disaster himself, but he remembered how many instructor-level swordsmen around him simply never returned.

*And it seems this life is following the same pattern.*

Not long ago, during the territorial dispute, someone had scarred Sword Master Hugo across the bridge of the nose and seriously wounded Sixth-Circle mage Adolf in the chest.

Vikir strongly suspected the Night Fox had been responsible for both.

*But Aiyen is too young.*

The Night Fox of the current era should be somewhere in her early to mid-thirties.

Even a genius archer would need years of training before she could wound a Sword Master and a Sixth-Circle mage.

Aiyen looked barely into her late teens.

She was extraordinarily skilled, but still nowhere near the level Vikir associated with the Night Fox.

*I've never seen her name in the histories either.*

Then what was she?

Perhaps a descendant of the Night Fox?

Very little reliable information about Ballak existed in imperial records. Even Baskervilles who had spent their lives fighting along this frontier knew surprisingly little about the tribe's internal structure.

If the Night Fox had children, Aiyen could certainly be one of them.

*At this point I'm becoming a Ballak researcher.*

All of those thoughts flashed through Vikir's mind in only moments.

Then—

FSSHT!

A whisper of displaced air came from somewhere in the dark.

Too faint to be an arrow.

But the instincts Vikir had forged in the Age of Destruction did not permit even a hair's breadth of carelessness.

FLAP!

He snapped his cloak across the projectile.

A cactus thorn fell away.

The needle was coated in paralytic poison—the same kind that had struck Camus earlier.

“...What!? How did you block that!?”

Ahun's startled voice came from beneath the roots of a tree.

A short blowpipe was still near his mouth.

Vikir considered counterattacking, then suppressed the impulse.

His priority was getting Camus to safety.

Doing so would also place Morgue even further in his debt.

“Hold on a little longer. I'll get you back to your uncle soon—?”

Vikir stopped.

Camus was staring blankly up at him from his arms.

Her face was bright red.

Her breathing was quick.

Her heartbeat was pounding far faster than it should have been.

*Was there something mixed with the paralytic?*

Vikir frowned.

“Were you poisoned with something else?”

“...Probably.”

That was bad.

Vikir immediately increased his pace.

Aiyen raised her bow in a flash, but even she could not simply stop him once he had committed to withdrawing.

WHIRR! WHIRR!

More nooses flew through the darkness.

Ballak's female warriors were remarkably persistent about throwing them toward Vikir.

But—

SHRAK! SHRAK!

Vikir released enough aura to cut every rope apart.

With so many witnesses around, he limited himself to the level of a **Low Sword Graduator**.

That was more than enough against ordinary Ballak warriors.

Aiyen was different.

TWANG!

Her arrows demanded Vikir's full attention even while he held back.

She aimed cleverly at his legs and coordinated her shots with the nooses coming from other directions.

Carrying Camus made the situation much more awkward.

Camus clutched his clothes and groaned.

“...Ugh. I never imagined I'd become the useless heroine who only drags the hero down.”

“What heroine? Don't flatter yourself.”

“I'll never let my guard down like that again. So understand what an honor this is. You'll never see me this helpless again in your life—ugh!?”

Vikir adjusted his grip and simply carried her more securely.

Then—

RUMBLE!

A tremendous explosion split the battlefield.

A wall of ice erupted between Vikir and the Ballak warriors.

The frozen barrier expanded until it surrounded the retreating hunters.

Adolf Morgue.

A senior figure of Morgue's Mage Council, leader of the clan's Light Faction, and one of the Empire's foremost mages.

His power had already passed the ordinary limits of the Sixth Circle and was reaching toward the Seventh.

“Step aside, nephew-in-law.”

Adolf traced formulae through the air.

Volume.

Density.

The latent heat of melting ice.

Remaining mana.

The strain on his magic tools.

Countless variables were calculated in an instant.

The result was overwhelming.

KRRRRRRR—!

The flooded ground began to freeze.

Hail and snow scattered through the air as if an ice age had descended on the jungle.

Ballak warriors and their wolves found their ankles frozen to the ground.

Their bodies were hardy enough that the sudden freezing did not simply shatter their legs.

It did not matter.

They could no longer move.

“Bite them all to death.”

Baskerville's hounds fell on the immobilized warriors.

Morgue's mages joined the assault, and screams erupted throughout the frozen jungle.

Adolf reached Vikir and took Camus from his arms.

“Camus! Are you all right!?”

He immediately cast detoxification magic.

FLASH!

**Cure Poison.**

It was an advanced spell beyond the reach of most mages.

Even Adolf could only remove part of the poison.

“This paralytic is vicious. Completely purging it won't be easy. You may need months of rest after this. Once we return, we'll visit the holy Quovadis family.”

The toxin was formidable if even Adolf could not cleanse it completely.

Once enough of the paralysis had lifted, Camus abruptly forced herself upright.

Then—

THUMP!

On legs that had not fully recovered, she stumbled straight toward Vikir and threw her arms around his neck.

“You saved me again!”

Adolf's face fell behind her.

“Camus. Your uncle is standing right here.”

“Uncle, why did you take so long!? I'm telling Mother!”

“...”

Adolf looked even more wounded.

Vikir pushed Camus back gently and addressed him.

“Lord Adolf. I think Camus may have been hit by a second poison. A moment ago she was—”

“What? There isn't any poison in her except the paralytic.”

“...?”

That made no sense.

Vikir had clearly seen her flushed face, rapid breathing, and racing pulse.

But if Adolf said there was no second toxin, then there was no second toxin.

Before Vikir could turn back toward Camus—

WHIRRRR!

Another rope trap came flying.

This one was different from the simple lassos used before.

It twisted unpredictably through the air like a living snake.

“...!”

Vikir knocked it aside with the back of his blade.

Aiyen stood at the other end of the rope.

Her eyes were fixed past Vikir.

On Camus.

“Surrender quietly. There isn't much time.”

Her Imperial was still stiff.

Vikir frowned.

*Not much time?*

What did she mean?

Was it merely another mistake caused by her poor Imperial?

At this moment, Morgue and Baskerville held the overwhelming advantage.

If anyone was running out of time, it should have been the Ballak.

Explosions continued across the jungle.

Magic flashed brightly.

High-pitched screams echoed among the trees.

RUSTLE... RUSTLE...

The flooded jungle responded to the commotion.

Leaves began to tremble.

Then—

“...It's here!”

Ahun's voice broke into a panicked scream.

“It came after all!”

Every Ballak warrior froze.

“...?”

Vikir tilted his head.

Even the instincts he had honed through the Age of Destruction had not yet detected anything definite.

But—

BUZZZZZZ—

Beelzebub, hidden in the artery of his wrist, began to vibrate violently in warning.

“It's here!”

“**Madam Eight-Legs!**”

“She's here!”

The Ballak warriors were people who could face brutal torture without changing expression.

Seeing them this frightened told Vikir enough.

Silence fell for a moment.

Then something peered at them from the darkness.

A darkness denser and heavier than the night itself spread across the flooded jungle.

Drawn by the light and noise of battle—

An unknown terror had arrived.
