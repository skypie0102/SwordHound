# Chapter 146: Volunteering for the Golden Week (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/146.txt` — `a9a66d0662958927f6daca53ca2d3ead674128b8`. Recovered English Chapter 145 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Quilt Rune Indulgentia, Ephebo, Quovadis, Dolores L. Quovadis, Old Testament Faction, New Testament Faction, Humbert L. Quovadis, and Martin Luther. The source-timed “Ninth of the Ten Corpses” recognition is retained here without importing Quilt's later demon name.

Quilt was a remarkably handsome middle-aged man.

Even from a distance—

his resemblance to a famous stage actor who had retired and disappeared several years earlier was obvious.

Vikir recognized him at once.

*The Ninth of the Ten Corpses.*

The face was different.

The thing inside—

was not.

The stench assaulting Vikir's nose was unmistakably demonic.

It was weaker than what he remembered before his regression.

Still—

it was more than dangerous enough.

And there was something else that hardened his certainty.

Four shadows moved around Quilt.

Shuffle.

Shuffle.

Shuffle.

Shuffle.

Directly behind him walked a huge man in a suit.

A black covering hid his face.

A golden plate on his chest bore a name.

**Ephebo.**

Two thinner men moved at Quilt's sides.

They wore the same suits.

The same black masks.

Their nameplates read:

**Pedo.**

**Hebe.**

And at the front—

walked a woman.

Her figure was striking.

Her face was hidden beneath the same black covering.

Her plate read:

**Geronto.**

Three men.

One woman.

Vikir's attention settled first on the massive figure behind Quilt.

*…Ephebo.*

*So it really was him.*

Vikir knew that creature.

They had fought during his previous assassination attempt.

Vikir had nearly captured him.

Then the underground structure self-destructed—

and Ephebo escaped in the chaos.

Vikir had bent an iron support and driven it straight through the creature's body.

Ephebo had torn himself free anyway.

*An undead at the upper end of the Graduator realm.*

*And he uses Quovadis blunt-weapon techniques.*

*Troublesome.*

Vikir stopped breathing.

He waited for Quilt and his escorts to pass the alcove.

Soon—

Quilt and the four shadows drew level with the place where Vikir and Dolores were hidden.

For an instant—

[…]

Ephebo's head tilted.

The movement was subtle.

Almost like an animal catching a scent.

Vikir pressed closer to the wall.

The recess sat between two sections of stonework.

Cabinets on either side cast enough shadow to hide them from a casual glance.

[…]

Ephebo turned his head farther.

Toward Vikir.

Then—

“Oh! What a pleasure.”

Quilt's voice sounded from ahead.

Ephebo's head returned to its former angle.

Quilt had stopped to greet a heavyset nobleman.

A friendly smile covered his face.

The noble returned it.

“You've become so busy lately that it's difficult even to arrange a meeting.”

Quilt laughed pleasantly.

“Well, so many nobles have been requesting audiences to confess their sins.”

“I'm one of them. Hahaha.”

The man's voice lowered.

“Which brings me to the reason I came.”

“I'd like to purchase some indulgences.”

“Indulgences?”

Quilt smiled.

“How much?”

“About one billion gold.”

“One billion.”

Quilt nodded thoughtfully.

“That can cover quite a lot of sin.”

“Have you killed anyone recently?”

“Hehehe. Nothing too serious.”

The noble waved a hand.

“I bought a concubine not long ago.”

“She may have come from one of the barbarian tribes.”

“Very disobedient.”

“I indulged her a little, and she ran away.”

“Oh?”

“A slave refused to obey?”

“Yes.”

The noble sighed as though discussing a troublesome purchase.

“Enforcement against illegal slaves has become stricter lately.”

“You don't even get proper guarantees when merchandise changes hands.”

“So I took my escort knights to retrieve her.”

“I disciplined her on the way back.”

“She didn't last long.”

“She died.”

Quilt raised an eyebrow.

“And the death of one slave girl is worth a billion gold in indulgences?”

“Oh, no.”

The noble chuckled.

“I was still angry afterward.”

“On the road home, I came across a village of heretics.”

“So I burned the entire place.”

“There were probably thirty children among the dead.”

Quilt laughed.

“In that case, five hundred million should be enough.”

“Hehehe.”

“Then consider the other five hundred million a tip to the god gracious enough to forgive me.”

The two men laughed together.

Quilt nodded approvingly.

“A wise choice.”

“Our Rune Religion is generous toward human error.”

“People make mistakes.”

“People commit sins.”

“As long as they pay the proper ransom, the debt can be settled.”

“Exactly.”

The noble smiled.

“No matter the crime, the appropriate payment wipes it away.”

“Indeed.”

Quilt's smile broadened.

“Even if someone forced a Saintess into an immoral act—”

“a sufficient offering would purchase absolution.”

“The moment a coin from this world drops into the offering box—”

“the soul rises from hell toward heaven.”

“This doctrine was recognized by **John Tetzel L. Quovadis**, former cardinal of the Old Testament Faction.”

Still conversing—

Quilt and the noble continued down the corridor.

Ephebo followed.

So did Pedo.

Hebe.

Geronto.

Soon—

all five disappeared from sight.

Vikir had heard every word.

The corpse-stench Quilt left behind was so thick that Vikir's sense of smell nearly went numb.

“…Suffocating.”

A cold voice answered from beneath his chin.

“…I'm not the one who's having trouble breathing.”

Only then did Vikir register the position they were still in.

He stepped back.

Dolores emerged from the narrow recess.

Her expression was hard.

“What exactly were you doing just now?”

Vikir closed his mouth.

He could not simply explain that Quilt and his entourage were demons.

But—

when Vikir remained silent—

Dolores seemed to reach an entirely different conclusion.

“Do you think I'm going to thank you for such pointless consideration?”

“…?”

Vikir looked at her.

Dolores's anger rose.

“You thought I'd be afraid to meet Quilt?”

“I'm not!”

“I don't need to hide from him!”

“How dare you treat me like a coward!?”

Now Vikir understood.

Dolores believed he had dragged her into the alcove because he wanted to spare her from encountering Quilt.

That he had been—

in his own awkward way—

considerate.

Why would Dolores dislike meeting Quilt?

The answer could be found in the newspapers Vikir had been reading.

The Quovadis family was divided into two major political-religious camps.

The **Old Testament Faction**—

led by Cardinal **Humbert L. Quovadis**.

And the **New Testament Faction**—

led by Cardinal **Martin Luther**.

The two groups disagreed on many things.

But the most visible dividing line was—

**indulgences**.

Certificates sold in exchange for remission of sin.

A person paid an amount corresponding to the weight of the wrongdoing—

and the religious institution declared that sin absolved.

The Old Testament Faction defended the system.

Selling indulgences brought enormous donations into the Rune Religion—

and built a formidable political and financial base.

Most of its senior members were older, high-ranking clergy.

The New Testament Faction condemned that culture.

Its members argued for reform.

Its support came largely from younger and lower-ranking clergy.

And one of its most visible representatives was—

**Saintess Dolores L. Quovadis.**

The so-called—

**Awakened Young Blood.**

She had chosen a path sharply different from her father—

Cardinal Humbert—

one of the dominant figures of the Old Testament Faction.

The strain between father and daughter was no secret.

Quilt stood on the opposite side.

He was a textbook Old Testament cleric.

House Indulgentia itself was one of that faction's important pillars.

It was therefore easy to imagine that Dolores and Quilt had a poor relationship.

And moments earlier—

Quilt had said something deliberately vicious.

*Even forcing a Saintess into an immoral act can be forgiven if you pay enough.*

Thinking back—

he may have known Dolores was hidden nearby.

No.

He almost certainly had.

Dolores's eyes shone with angry tears.

Her normally calm voice trembled.

“I don't need that kind of concern!”

“And my family's affairs are mine to handle!”

“What right do you have to—!”

She already disliked Vikir.

At the Academy—

she knew him as a lazy first-year.

A habitual rule-breaker.

A student who arrived late—

entered restricted areas—

and spoke carelessly about the Night Hound without knowing anything about him.

Now—

shame that an outsider had witnessed her family's internal ugliness—

anger at Quilt—

self-reproach for having wanted to hide from him even for an instant—

the complicated feelings she held toward the Night Hound—

and every other strain she had been carrying—

burst out together.

“You're the worst!”

Dolores tried to restrain herself.

But she was still young.

Still inexperienced.

She turned away from Vikir and disappeared down the corridor.

“…”

Vikir watched her leave.

*What does that have to do with me?*

Whether the Saintess was angry—

did not matter.

If anything—

Dolores had been useful.

Her presence had drawn attention away from Vikir.

Quilt and Ephebo had passed without recognizing him.

Overall—

that was fortunate.

Vikir immediately began following Quilt.

It was daytime—

so he could not move openly as the Night Hound.

But his eyes had already taken on a murderous red gleam.

The hound bared its fangs.
