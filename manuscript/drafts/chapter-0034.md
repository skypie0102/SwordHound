# Chapter 34: The Social Club (3)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/034.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 34 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. The chapter's violence, criminal charges, executions, and torture content are preserved without sanitization; obvious office/referent drift in the Chinese translation layer is resolved from immediate continuity and documented in QA.

The news spread quickly.

The seven spoiled heirs who had swaggered through Underdog City, squandered fortunes on luxury, and committed every vice imaginable had all been arrested at once.

Whenever three or more citizens gathered, the incident became the first topic of conversation.

“Those bastards tried the new Deputy Magistrate and finally got taught a lesson.”

“Serves them right. They treated ordinary people like insects, and now they've finally met someone they can't trample.”

“What do you think will happen to them? The new Deputy Magistrate doesn't seem like the type to let something like this slide.”

“They're still sons of the old local families. He'll humiliate them a little, then let them go.”

As they spoke, people naturally turned toward the north side of the central square.

The proclamations Vikir had posted there still fluttered in the wind.

<div class="info-window">
<p>Constitution of Baskerville Territory, Article 00, Section 0.</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Human Trafficking — Penalty: Death</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Gambling — Penalty: Amputation of the wrist</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Usury — Penalty: Removal of the eyes</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Entertainment Establishments — Penalty: Facial tattooing</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Lobbying — Penalty: Exile from the territory</p>
</div>

Nothing on the notices had changed since the day they were first posted.

The law contained no exception saying the wealthy should be treated gently.

There was no clause saying the powerful would be forgiven.

The crimes of the seven arrested heirs were beyond dispute.

They had frequented and operated illegal entertainment businesses.

They had engaged in illegal lobbying.

They had participated in illegal human trafficking.

They had consumed and distributed prohibited drugs and made illicit profits in the process.

Tax evasion, assault, and sexual violence were merely additional charges.

The resulting sentence was equally clear.

**Death.**

A crime for which no excuse or plea for leniency could erase the penalty.

The citizens whispered among themselves.

“Are they really going to execute the sons of all seven local houses?”

“Are you stupid? Haven't you figured out the new Deputy Magistrate yet? This is the man who paid out ten billion over a wooden stake!”

“Even so, killing all seven of those notorious bastards will have consequences.”

“Surely he'll just beat them, humiliate them, and leave it there.”

“Right. No matter what, he can't actually sentence them to death.”

Nearly everyone reached the same conclusion.

This was a political struggle between a newly appointed official and the entrenched local magnates.

If the local houses lowered their heads, apologized, and paid the proper price, the conflict would end as such conflicts always did.

Sure enough, the **Montblanc, Pierre, Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Ferragamo, Hermès, and Prada Families** sent apologies to the new Deputy Magistrate.

The old local powers bowed.

Late at night, citizens watched ornate carriages bearing each family's crest roll into City Hall loaded with lavish gifts.

They clicked their tongues knowingly.

*Of course.*

The seven young men in custody would be released.

Their fathers would give them a vicious scolding.

That would be the end of it.

Perhaps the local magnates would seek revenge for the humiliation.

Perhaps they would lower their heads from now on and watch the Deputy Magistrate's mood more carefully.

The seven wastrels themselves would behave for a while.

The new young Deputy Magistrate would have won an impressive but ultimately ordinary political victory over the local rich.

The citizens would feel moderately satisfied.

Moderately angry.

Then, in time, they would moderately forget.

That was how the story was supposed to end.

...

...No.

That was how everyone *knew* it would end.

Right up until dawn, when **seven severed heads** appeared hanging in the middle of the central square.

The heads had been packed in salt for display.

With their bodies gone, only their twisted faces remained.

Every expression suggested that the men had suffered terribly before dying.

Beneath them, another notice listed the punishments carried out before the executions.

<div class="info-window">
<p>Constitution of Baskerville Territory, Article 00, Section 0.</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Entertainment Establishments — Penalty: Facial tattooing</p>
<p>Execution note: The character <strong>色</strong> (“lust”) was tattooed across the entirety of each offender's face.</p>
</div>

<div class="info-window">
<p>Constitution of Baskerville Territory, Article 00, Section 0.</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Gambling — Penalty: Amputation of the wrist</p>
<p>Execution note: Both wrists were severed in accordance with the statute.</p>
<p>Because punishment was applied retroactively according to the number of violations, further cuts continued at fixed intervals after the wrists themselves were gone.</p>
<p>One offender received the wrist-cutting punishment seventy-two times.</p>
</div>

<div class="info-window">
<p>Constitution of Baskerville Territory, Article 00, Section 0.</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Usury — Penalty: Removal of the eyes</p>
<p>Execution note: Carried out without leniency in consideration of the victims' petitions.</p>
</div>

<div class="info-window">
<p>Constitution of Baskerville Territory, Article 00, Section 0.</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Human Trafficking — Penalty: Death</p>
<p>Execution note: Carried out according to law.</p>
</div>

<div class="info-window">
<p>Constitution of Baskerville Territory, Article 00, Section 0.</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Lobbying — Penalty: Exile from the territory</p>
<p>Execution note: Because the offenders were already dead, their bodies—excluding the displayed heads—were expelled beyond the city walls.</p>
</div>

The citizens could only gape.

The laws themselves had always existed.

No one could claim Vikir had suddenly invented these penalties merely to torture his enemies.

What shocked them was the identity of the people who had actually been subjected to them.

These were not powerless common criminals.

This was no longer an ordinary political contest.

It was a war of extermination that would end only when one side had been destroyed.

Everyone could smell the bloodstorm coming.

◆◆◆

Not long before the executions—

Vikir stood before the iron bars of an underground cell.

Inside were the seven young masters, beaten until their bodies were swollen, torn, and bloodied.

Their ruined mouths made some of their shouting nearly unintelligible.

“My father... when my father finds out...!”

“You won't get away with this...!”

“Please! Ignore them! Spare me!”

Chihuahua Baskerville stood beside Vikir, visibly restless.

“Deputy Magistrate... did you really have to beat them *this* badly?”

His words protested, but there was unmistakable satisfaction buried in his expression.

Vikir turned toward him.

“Do you think I went too far?”

“What? Of course! Of course you did! There's such a thing as moderation. Once you've beaten people into meat patties, how are you supposed to deal with the aftermath...?”

Vikir smiled faintly.

“Don't worry. I haven't started yet.”

“...What?”

Chihuahua stared at him.

Vikir offered no immediate explanation.

The local self-governance organizations had originally existed to check and supervise corrupt regional officials, educate local society, and coordinate relations with City Hall.

Over time, however, those organizations had rotted.

Their members grew corrupt themselves and eventually began coveting power that belonged to Baskerville.

“You insane bastard!”

“Just you wait!”

“Filthy Baskerville dog!”

The curses echoing from the cell only proved the point.

Vikir did not care about their hatred.

“When it gets dark, I'll execute all seven of them.”

...!

The prisoners fell silent.

Vikir continued.

“And while I'm at it, I'll wipe out every punk in the clubs and every devil prowling the streets.”

There was no bluff in his voice.

Everything he said would be carried out exactly as stated.

A full-scale **war on crime** had begun.

The seven heirs froze for a moment.

Then they rushed the bars, grabbed them with both hands, and shook them desperately.

“Please! Spare me!”

“Let me go! Please let me go!”

“I'll give you my entire fortune!”

“Don't kill me...!”

Men who had spent their lives regarding other people as insects now crawled like insects themselves.

Chihuahua felt a complicated mixture of emotions.

There was a savage satisfaction in seeing men like this brought low.

At the same time, he feared what the entrenched local families would do in retaliation.

Their reprisals could be crude, but they could also be deadly.

Businesses associated with Vikir might suddenly lose every sponsor.

Public events he organized might become empty.

Trade with neighboring cities could turn hostile.

Tax revenue could collapse.

Yet Vikir seemed almost unnaturally calm.

As if he had already accounted for all of it.

“There's no need to worry, General Secretary.”

“But...”

“Their families won't be able to protest.”

“What? Why not?”

For once, Vikir answered Chihuahua's question readily.

“They're connected to criminal organizations all across Underdog City.”

“What? H-How do you know that...?”

Vikir closed his mouth.

He could hardly explain that he had seen it all before his regression.

Chihuahua pressed the real problem instead.

“But evidence is what matters. Even if we find evidence, what happens when those families retaliate afterward?”

“Everything is under control. Don't worry.”

Vikir smiled.

Evidence could be found.

And if it could not be found, it could be **manufactured**.

What happened after this was not particularly important to him.

He did not intend to remain in Underdog City forever.

‘Whoever succeeds me is going to suffer.’

Vikir knew that he would not remain Deputy Magistrate for long.

The world would soon begin to change violently, and before long he would likely be sent to a real battlefield.

If he gained admission to the Academy, he would leave the city immediately.

That meant the long-term political consequences of tearing Underdog City's old order apart would eventually belong to someone else.

‘While I'm still Deputy Magistrate, I might as well restore discipline in one decisive stroke.’

Even if the medicine caused side effects, he intended to prescribe a powerful dose.

The achievement itself would become part of his reputation and strengthen his future career.

Whoever came after him could worry about the rest.

Vikir had never been fond of his fellow Baskervilles anyway.

But every long journey began with a single step.

First, the seven men in front of him had to reveal evidence tying their families to criminal organizations.

More importantly, they had to give up the locations of those organizations.

Vikir stepped closer to the bars.

“Let me list your crimes.”

“...”

“Kidnapping women and imprisoning them illegally. Sexual assault. Threats. Forcing victims to take illegal drugs. Human trafficking. Coercing women into prostitution. Bribing officials. Extortion. Intimidation. Hiring men to commit violence. Murder...”

The seven prisoners began struggling again.

“T-That's not true! If you have evidence, then punish us!”

“So you're saying you're guilty, but there's no evidence?”

“T-That's...”

They exchanged uncertain looks.

Then their expressions hardened.

‘Even if we die here, we can't drag our families down with us.’

If they confessed everything, there would be no hope of revenge.

Their houses might abandon them outright.

And considering what kind of madman Vikir was, even that might not be enough.

He could invoke collective punishment and tear their families out by the roots.

“We won't talk!”

“Never!”

For the sake of their houses, the seven wastrels resolved to keep their mouths shut.

But then—

“Oh. You're finally here.”

Vikir raised one hand toward the entrance to the underground corridor.

Apparently, he had been waiting in front of the cell for someone.

“...?”

The seven prisoners followed his gaze with growing unease.

A short, heavyset old man came down the corridor carrying a large basket.

“Your order is finished, my lord.”

He was a torture technician who worked beneath City Hall.

A moment later, Vikir overturned the basket onto a rack.

Clatter—!

More than a hundred blades and metal implements spilled out.

Every one of them looked grotesquely wrong.

Some were bent.

Some twisted.

Some warped into strange shapes.

Some were blunt.

Some were rough and serrated.

The old torturer bared yellow teeth in a grin.

“Well now. I've been doing this work for thirty years, and even *I've* never seen this many frightening tools. Where in the world did you come up with things like these?”

“I didn't invent them. Where I came from, these were ordinary tools.”

“Heh heh... Was the place you came from hell?”

“Close enough. You learn a few things there.”

“Ugh. No thank you. Just looking at these, I can guess how some of them are meant to be used. Even I'd vomit everything in my stomach if I had to watch.”

The implements were horrific enough to disgust a man who had spent thirty years professionally torturing prisoners.

Vikir picked them up and approached the cell again.

“...Hhk!”

The seven young masters sensed what was coming and instinctively crawled away from the bars.

Unfortunately for them—

**CLANG!**

Vikir forced open the iron door without hesitation and stepped inside.

Faces from his old life drifted through his memory.

‘Those were good times.’

Under the torture techniques his old comrades had used, even captured demons had eventually surrendered military secrets.

The methods developed during the **Age of Destruction** were enough to make creatures from hell lose control of their bowels and bladders in terror.

Vikir knew how far torture techniques would advance in the future.

Looking at the seven prisoners trying so desperately to appear resolved, he almost found them endearing.

‘Every one of them has that expression saying they'll never break.’

A faint smile touched Vikir's lips.

‘...Please don't.’

He meant it sincerely.

He wanted enough time to savor a little more of the nostalgia.
