# Chapter 367: The Underground Extension Construction (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/367.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 365 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical **Garam Nord** is freshly verified through the designated Fandom index. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Cindywendy, Isabella, Decarabia, Nouvelle Vague, Level Nine, Level Ten, and the surviving demon threat.

Construction of the new **Level Ten** began.

Digging through the inside of the enormous volcano—

was brutal.

Even Vikir—

who had crossed countless battlefields and survived more life-or-death struggles than most people could imagine—

considered it among the heaviest labor in human history.

CRRRK.

He pushed a massive boulder uphill with bare hands.

The rock was not merely heavy.

It was burning hot—

as though it had just been dragged out of a furnace.

And the boulder was hardly unique.

Excavated soil.

Bursting groundwater.

Sulfur gas.

Molten stone.

Steam.

Flame.

Everything in the volcanic worksite—

was hot.

Prisoners crossed scorching rock barefoot.

Carried enormous loads.

Worked until skin dried and cracked.

Blood.

Sweat.

Moisture.

Everything seemed to evaporate—

leaving shriveled flesh wrapped around bone and nerve.

Compared with this—

the admission ceremony felt almost like a child’s game.

And if **Vikir** thought the labor was severe—

ordinary prisoners had no chance.

People collapsed all across the construction site.

Whenever someone fell—

a guard’s whip cracked.

Even Level Six and Seven inmates—

monsters by ordinary standards—

dropped one after another beneath the endless labor and miserable food.

Every death frightened the prisoners working above them.

“You hear?”

“Someone died in Level Six.”

“What?”

“Isn’t that where the life-sentence monsters are?”

“Even they die?”

“I thought everyone down there was inhuman.”

“Monsters still have stronger and weaker monsters.”

“If that dead guy were on our floor—

he’d probably rule us like a king.”

The stronger the fallen prisoner—

the more terrifying the deeper worksite became.

So higher-level prisoners did everything possible—

to avoid reassignment downward.

Meanwhile—

those already working deeper developed a strange pride.

“Heard someone upstairs died.”

“Level Six?”

“Embarrassing.”

“Someone that weak should’ve been moved upward.”

“So who ate the corpse?”

“I’m hungry.”

“Everyone tore it apart.”

“Nothing left but bone and skin.”

A warped sense of superiority.

Pride in enduring worse suffering than someone else.

Bodies dried like mummies—

received no sympathy.

No mourning.

Only relief.

Mockery.

Fear.

Sometimes—

hunger.

That—

was the Level Ten construction site.

Then—

TOOOOT.

A horn sounded.

Lunch.

Ten minutes.

The one oasis of the day—

melting faster than ice cream dropped into lava.

Guards moved between exhausted prisoners.

Dropped food from baskets.

Vikir received his single daily ration.

THUD.

A lump of bread fell in front of him.

Black enough to resemble charcoal.

Already coarse bread—

burned harder during cooking.

On top—

a whole salted fish.

Not gutted.

Half-rotten from being transported through the heat.

The smell was terrible.

The prisoners—

ate anyway.

Vikir chewed the bread and fish slowly.

*Did the messages get through?*

Before being transported to Nouvelle Vague—

he had sent instructions through **Cindywendy**.

Then—

just before entering the Iron Maiden—

he had left one more message through **Isabella**.

He had no way to know what the demons remaining on the surface might do during his absence.

With several of their own already dead—

the survivors would certainly be nervous.

But Vikir had business inside Nouvelle Vague.

He could not remain aboveground.

So—

he had entrusted his future plans to people he considered reliable.

*For now—

I do what I can here.*

Vikir lifted his head.

The vast crater spread below.

Sulfur fumes.

Lava.

Steam.

A dormant volcano—

that looked anything but dormant.

*What I’m looking for is down there.*

He stared into the pit.

How much farther would they need to dig?

Decarabia—

quiet until now—

spoke from his chest.

[There isn’t much time.]

[That thing must be nearby.]

“I think so too.”

Vikir nodded.

The Level Ten project existed to expand Nouvelle Vague deeper into the volcano.

And—

by coincidence—

that served part of his own objective.

He was not here merely because the prison wanted Level Ten.

There was something buried below.

Something Vikir needed to find.

That was one reason he had voluntarily entered Nouvelle Vague in the first place.

Thousands of prisoners—

were effectively digging for him.

Then—

“…!”

Noise from a higher work section interrupted his thoughts.

“Hey!”

“**Stupid Garam**!”

“Not enough bread!”

“What kind of distribution is this?!”

“You trying to starve us?”

“Give us **your** bread!”

Prisoners—

were shouting abuse at a guard.

And the guard—

looked helpless.

Vikir recognized him immediately.

*Again.*

**Garam Nord.**

Cap pulled low.

Face scarred by burns.

Memorable at a glance—

yet difficult to read clearly.

“I-I’m sorry.”

“There was a distribution problem.”

“We’re short on bread.”

“Bullshit!”

“You let another team steal ours!”

The prisoners were furious.

Understandably.

That bread—

was their only meal.

Nouvelle Vague’s labor system had an unusual structure.

Each guard supervised a group of prisoners.

Assignments changed every month.

Performance was recorded for both prisoner and guard.

Good monthly results brought benefits.

Bad results—

penalties.

So guards pushed prisoners aggressively.

Whips.

Threats.

Solitary confinement.

But fear alone could not maximize labor.

So guards also competed to obtain—

better tools.

Extra bread.

More cooperative inmates.

Everything in Nouvelle Vague was scarce.

If one group gained something—

another group lost it.

Garam—

apparently—

lost those competitions often.

His prisoners received fewer tools.

Less bread.

Other guards took advantage of him.

So did inmates.

“Why’d I get stuck in Stupid Garam’s team?!”

“He’s the worst-performing new lieutenant!”

“Even his coworkers push him around.”

“Prisoners too!”

“Heard that burn scar came from an inmate.”

“I heard prisoners steal his meals.”

“That’s nothing.”

“High-level inmates make him serve them at night.”

Listening—

Vikir finally understood why Garam seemed to appear everywhere.

*He gets assigned every unpleasant job.*

Guiding a Level Nine prisoner—

was something most guards would avoid.

Too dangerous.

Too unpredictable.

Of course the least respected junior would be sent.

Meanwhile—

the ten-minute lunch break had ended.

Garam’s prisoners remained on the ground.

“We didn’t even get bread.”

“So we’re not working.”

“Beat me.”

“I’m done too.”

“This month’s quota is already ruined.”

“Just punish us.”

“Go ahead.”

“Take out that three-section staff and kill me.”

Garam panicked.

He really was—

in some ways—

a little hopeless.

Vikir watched.

Then thought.

*Other prisoners slack off constantly too.*

The stronger the prisoner—

the less cooperative they tended to become.

Especially in the deeper levels.

Guards had trouble controlling them.

If a dangerous inmate simply avoided causing a riot—

that alone counted as success.

*Which means—

if I work too hard by myself—

that becomes suspicious.*

Vikir was Level Nine.

Most Level Nine cells were empty at any given time.

Inmates frequently moved in and out of solitary—

for insubordination—

or because they were being evaluated for future Level Ten confinement.

Yet Vikir—

for personal reasons—

needed the excavation to progress.

If he openly worked harder than everyone else—

people would ask why.

*I need an excuse.*

*And there’s something else I want to test.*

Calculation complete.

Vikir lowered the boulder he had been carrying.

Then—

let go.

GRRRRRRRR.

The huge rock rolled down the slope.

Fast.

“What is that?!”

“Boulder!”

“Stop it—”

“No!”

“MOVE!”

Guards scattered.

BOOM!

The rock crashed into a lava pit.

Stone fragments and molten spray exploded outward.

Every head turned upward.

Toward the Level Nine work area.

Vikir stood there.

Expressionless.

“I’m not working.”

He wanted to see—

how the guards would respond.
