# Chapter 142: Identity of the Villain (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/142.txt` — `0ba20650ef1b656b0b421efa292ce158f92daaf9`. Recovered English Chapter 141 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Dolores L. Quovadis, Night Hound, Saint Meka, Red Death, Saintess's Tears, Ryukeion, Tudor Donquixote, Sinclaire, Sancho Barataria, Bianca Fo Usher, Pigi, Vikir, and Professor Banshee Morgue. Later demon identities remain spoiler-guarded.

Dolores was thinking seriously about what had happened the night before.

*…That mask I picked up yesterday.*

*I'm sure I've seen it before.*

She had.

The previous night, Dolores had received word that the Imperial Guards had mobilized.

As the Saintess of Quovadis—

and as head of the Academy newspaper club—

she had hurried to the scene herself.

What she found was devastating.

Many people were dead.

The buildings had collapsed as though a huge quantity of explosives had gone off at once.

Beneath the ruins of the orphanage—

the remains of boys and girls had been found.

Their blood had been drained away.

Their bodies had dried into mummified husks.

The second- and third-year student reporters who came with her had never seen anything like it.

Neither had the Imperial Guards.

And nearby—

there had been a half-burned mask.

Dolores quietly picked it up.

When footsteps approached behind her—

she hid it beneath her clothes without even thinking.

*That was definitely the Night Hound's.*

*Why was it there…?*

She remembered the first time she had met him.

It had been while the Academy was on break—

when she was staying at a residence away from campus.

Among the stream of greedy and shameless nobles who came calling—

he had stood out.

*…Just a lost lamb.*

He had not boasted about prestige.

He had not flaunted wealth.

He had simply lowered his head with unusual humility.

The impression had stayed with her.

Later—

the Night Hound had devoted himself to treating the people of Saint Meka who had been infected with the Red Death.

His clothes were suspicious.

His identity was suspicious.

His actions were suspicious.

But his care for people who were suffering had been real.

So had his willingness to work for them.

He had even demonstrated a faint trace of sacred runic power.

*The mask I found yesterday is definitely his.*

*It still has the marks and blood left by the Red Death patients he treated.*

Dolores was certain.

The half-burned plague-doctor mask hidden against her body—

was the Night Hound's.

Then why had it been at the scene of the previous night's massacre?

Had he really killed the orphanage director?

Had he harmed the children there?

*…But why would someone like him do that?*

She could not believe the Night Hound she had seen in Saint Meka had been acting.

He had treated people while covered in filth and blood.

There had been something genuinely noble in the way he worked.

Even when he stole the **Saintess's Tears** at the end—

Dolores had believed there must have been some reason tied to justice.

*Was the Night Hound really responsible?*

*No.*

*That can't be right.*

*I know what I saw.*

*He isn't evil.*

*If he really was the one who did it… then there must be a reason.*

Her head throbbed.

The mask was unquestionably evidence connected to the previous night's incident.

And she had hidden it from the investigators.

That meant—

whatever her intention—

she had interfered with the investigation.

Because of her personal feelings.

Almost like an accomplice.

Since the previous night, Dolores had been tormented by two things at once.

Her trust in the Night Hound.

And the guilt of having obstructed the Imperial Guards.

*Was it really right to hide the mask?*

*Did I do the right thing?*

She thought about it when she woke.

While bathing.

While eating.

During class.

Even now—

in the middle of the club meeting.

And there was something else.

*I wonder if the Night Hound is all right.*

*The explosion must have been enormous if half his mask burned away.*

*He's very strong, but if he wasn't prepared for the blast…*

*Where is he now?*

*What is he doing?*

*Could he be badly hurt somewhere?*

She was even worrying about his injuries.

Dolores did not understand why that thought bothered her so much.

That uncertainty only deepened her confusion.

Then—

“‘Night Hound’… that's a good name!”

A voice beside her snapped Dolores out of her thoughts.

“Huh?”

She looked up.

The second- and third-year members were already moving busily around the room.

Whirr—

clatter—

whirr—

The magical presses were running.

Today's paper was already being printed.

Dolores stared blankly.

Beside her, Pigi gave an enthusiastic thumbs-up.

“‘Night Hound’ is good. It's a little crude, but that makes him sound even more violent, doesn't it?”

“Mmm. I agree. There's something gloomy about it. It makes me uncomfortable.”

“And all the incidents happen at night.”

Tudor and Sancho nodded along.

So did Sinclaire and Bianca.

“I completely agree with the president!”

“Me too.”

Dolores blinked.

“…What are all of you talking about? What did I say?”

Then she saw the newspapers.

Stacks of them.

Already printed.

The front page read:

> **WHO IS THE ‘NIGHT HOUND,’ THE VILLAIN TERRORIZING THE IMPERIAL CAPITAL!?**
>
> **Views: 0**
>
> Late last night, near dawn, a mysterious figure appeared and reduced an orphanage on the outskirts of the Imperial Capital to ruins…
>
> Strangely, the attacker did not touch the valuables inside the vault…
>
> **Comments: 0**

Dolores nearly shouted.

“W-What is this!?”

Pigi answered cheerfully.

“You suggested the name ‘Night Hound,’ President. Everyone thought it was perfect, so we started printing immediately!”

“No, wait—when did I say that? Did I really say that?”

Everyone nodded.

Only a few minutes had passed—

and the decision had already become print.

Dolores looked at the growing mountain of newspapers and sighed deeply.

“Everyone, we still haven't established that this person is the killer. If we print a headline like this now, it will look like we're writing for attention instead of reporting facts.”

Tudor, Sancho, Sinclaire, and Bianca exchanged looks.

Then the objections came.

“The guards already gave testimony. They said the Night Hound destroyed the building and killed the director. There's physical evidence too.”

“The children looked as though they'd been dead for some time… but something like that happening inside an orphanage managed by Quovadis is hard to believe. It honestly sounds more plausible that the Night Hound did it.”

“Because you're from Quovadis, you're trying to stay neutral, right? That's admirable. But with this much evidence, couldn't we run it for now?”

“Night Hound or whatever—if he ever gets in my sights, I'll put an arrow through him.”

The freshmen were excited.

Everyone talked at once.

Dolores sighed again.

Because of one careless murmur—

she felt as if she had damaged the Night Hound's reputation in an instant.

Yet the others were not entirely unreasonable.

Was the Night Hound truly a villain?

If he was—

then what had she seen in Saint Meka?

What had become of that clear, self-sacrificing devotion?

If he was not—

then how should she explain the bodies found at a Quovadis-managed orphanage?

Or the enormous unexplained fortune hidden there?

Everything was tangled together.

Dolores knew more than anyone else in the room—

because she alone had hidden the mask.

That only made the uncertainty worse.

At last—

she made her decision.

“We're rewriting the article.”

The room quieted.

“Make it as objective as possible. Remove bias. And from the Imperial Guard interviews, cut any speculative statement that isn't supported by physical evidence.”

“What? But if we remove all of that, what will we even have left to write?”

“Don't jump to conclusions about things that haven't been established.”

Dolores's voice hardened.

“If we later learn what the Night Hound's real purpose is, we can write a critical article then.”

“And if he deserves condemnation at that point—”

“we'll condemn him properly.”

The rest of the newspaper club slowly nodded.

Bianca was the first among the freshmen to accept it.

“If that's what the president says, then let's do it that way.”

“…I agree.”

Sinclaire nodded too.

Sancho and Pigi hurried to stop the printing presses.

They began correcting the article.

Tudor—

with his particularly heroic temperament—

still looked dissatisfied.

Since the Naphtali match had brought him closer to Vikir, he leaned over and spoke quietly.

“Hey, friend. What do you think?”

“…About what?”

“The Night Hound.”

Tudor lowered his voice.

“He looks like a villain to anyone who sees this. What do you think?”

“I don't have an opinion.”

“Oh, come on. How can you not have one?”

Tudor persisted.

“Does he look like a villain?”

“Or do you think there's some story behind what he's doing?”

Vikir paused before answering.

*Am I a villain?*

Countless faces passed through his mind.

The ruined world.

The hell that would descend within the next decade.

The faces of those he had failed to protect.

Comrades.

Friends.

Brothers.

Sisters.

Everyone he had missed.

Everyone he had loved.

Their final words.

Their deaths.

He had carried their lives and sacrifices forward—

while surviving alone.

How could someone like that not consider himself a sinner?

So Vikir answered without hesitation.

“He's definitely a villain.”

Because Vikir had never believed himself to be a good man.

But—

the silence that followed was strange.

“?”

Vikir looked up.

Tudor's face had stiffened.

The other freshmen were staring past him.

Vikir turned.

Dolores stood there.

Her gaze was cold.

“You said the Night Hound is definitely a villain?”

She had apparently approached just in time to hear the conversation.

“Were you listening to anything I just said?”

“…”

Vikir did not answer.

Dolores continued.

“It's better to let ten thieves go free than to condemn one innocent person.”

“No matter how convincing the circumstances look—”

“you find the final piece of hard evidence first.”

“Then you report.”

“…”

“And if ‘the pen is mightier than the sword’—”

Dolores pointed toward the desks covered with newspapers.

“then the sins committed by the pen can be heavier than those committed by a sword.”

“From now on, remember that you're journalists.”

“Don't casually state things you're not certain of.”

The rebuke had been aimed at Vikir.

Yet the other first-years lowered their heads as well.

Vikir alone remained seated with the same expressionless face.

That only irritated Dolores further.

“You're Vikir, right?”

She narrowed her eyes.

“You haven't started arriving late again, have you?”

Since the beginning of the semester—

Dolores had repeatedly caught Vikir violating Academy rules and had docked his conduct score.

Vikir still did not react.

Dolores inhaled as if preparing another lecture.

“Honestly, freshmen these days… Back when we—”

At that moment—

a second-year member rushed into the clubroom.

“President!”

Dolores turned.

“Professor Banshee, the club advisor, wants to see you!”

“What? Why?”

“Because of this article. He says it needs to be revised so that it clearly identifies the Night Hound as a villain.”

Dolores rubbed her forehead.

“Haah… Professor Banshee is conservative. He likes having an outside enemy to point at.”

She stood.

“All right. I'll speak with him.”

That ended the conversation.

Dolores followed the second-year out to meet Professor Banshee Morgue.

Only after she left did the tension in the clubroom finally loosen.

Tudor exhaled.

Then patted Vikir on the shoulder.

“Our president really sounds like an old-timer sometimes.”

“…She did before too.”

Tudor blinked.

“Before? When?”

“There was a time.”

Vikir only smiled faintly.

In his mind—

another Dolores appeared.

Older.

In her thirties.

Standing at the front of the Paladins on the battlefields of the ruined future.

Her voice thundered across the lines.

*“Why aren't the Paladins pushing harder!? Young people these days have no faith!”*

*“If you have time to rest, you have time to kill one more demon!”*

*“Back in my day, when a demon appeared, we charged immediately!”*

*“You've gotten stronger! Now charge!”*

*“Heroes don't die!”*

The Dolores Vikir remembered from before his regression—

had been leading the Holy Knights at the very front of humanity's war for survival.
