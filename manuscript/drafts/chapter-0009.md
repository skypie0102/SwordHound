# Chapter 9: Hounds of Hell (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/009.txt`. Canonical English forms for Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, Guide Dogs, Brown Rat Norvegicus, Bloody Beans, Hellhound, and accepted Baskerville names/terms are normalized from current project/Fandom evidence. Practical examinations were held often in the Baskerville Clan, sometimes several times in a single day.

Most of them set siblings against one another to establish who was stronger.

And most began the same way.

Ding—

With a bell.

The young Baskerville hounds, tense in their unfamiliar surroundings, immediately snapped to attention at the familiar sound.

Training took over.

Their fighting instincts surfaced almost automatically.

One nine-year-old sprinted toward the far side of the wasteland.

He had done well the previous year by seizing advantageous terrain before anyone else.

He was clever enough to remember the location.

He was not clever enough to consider how many eyes would follow him when he rushed toward it.

Several sharp eight-year-olds immediately chased after him.

They had no idea what he was after.

That hardly mattered.

If he wanted it, they intended to take it first.

The smartest children went even further.

They pretended to know something valuable, drew competitors after them, then lured those competitors into dangerous pits or swamps and removed several nuisances at once.

Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro had once excelled at that sort of trick.

“Take that side first.”

“Don’t order me around.”

“We know what we’re doing. You handle your own part.”

The coordination that had once made the triplets terrifying had visibly deteriorated.

Even so, they were still talented nine-year-olds who had used similar tactics the previous year to eliminate children older than themselves.

Only a few hours after the bell, defeated examinees began appearing one after another.

Some were knocked unconscious.

Some rolled down steep slopes.

Some fell into swamps.

Others were stabbed, cut, or otherwise injured.

The details varied.

The underlying cause did not.

Other Baskerville children had done it to them.

Clang!

The sound of metal striking metal echoed throughout the forest.

The children had been issued blunt shortswords for the examination and had begun fighting almost immediately.

The edges were dulled to reduce lethal wounds.

That did not mean killing was forbidden.

It merely meant killing was discouraged.

Taking another child’s life carried a point penalty, and the deduction was severe enough that most examinees tried not to kill with their own hands if they could avoid it.

Of course, that restriction meant little to someone confident enough to absorb the penalty—or subtle enough to kill without a Guide Dog noticing.

Even though the guardian knights watched day and night, mysterious deaths and supposed accidents still happened regularly during these examinations.

For some children, the practical was also a convenient opportunity to remove a rival or someone they simply disliked.

… Yet Vikir was nowhere near that struggle.

From the beginning, he had shown no interest in competing for points.

The instant the bell rang, he slipped away from the protected center and moved toward a stretch of wasteland near the forbidden-zone boundary.

‘… Was it around here?’

He searched his memories from both sides of his first life—as a child examinee and later as an instructor.

If he remembered correctly, following this route would lead to a wasteland barely inside the boundary.

An old dead tree stood there, its deep roots rotten enough to soften the surrounding earth.

Before long, Vikir found the place.

The soil was noticeably redder than the ground around it.

He broke off a branch and began digging.

Far away, his siblings fought, stole badges, and gained or lost points.

Vikir simply dug.

The Guide Dogs observed him from hidden positions.

One day passed.

Then another.

Vikir kept digging.

‘So he really intends to hide in that hole and wait out the examination.’

‘Disappointing.’

‘There’s no point watching him anymore.’

Because Vikir had stood out since infancy, some of the guardian knights had expected great things from him.

Watching him dig what appeared to be a coward’s hiding place was a severe letdown.

Hiding underground and surviving on rations was exactly the kind of behavior associated with weak, low-ranking children.

Eventually, one by one, the Guide Dogs shifted their attention elsewhere.

… Which meant none of them noticed how deep the eight-year-old had dug.

Nor what he was building at the bottom.

◆◆◆

“This place still feels like a cradle.”

Vikir sat comfortably at the bottom of the pit.

Coming back here almost felt warm and familiar.

The guardian knights outside were providing security for him without realizing it, and none of his competitors had any reason to visit this barren edge of the examination zone.

When he became thirsty, he collected dew from broad leaves hanging along the wall.

When he became hungry, he caught snakes, worms, or other small creatures and roasted them.

It was far tastier than the haggis and preserved rations served at the nursery castle.

Even his bed was better.

Baskerville children slept on hard, rough beds made from monster hide, shells, or thornwood.

Here, Vikir had lined the bottom of his pit with dry grass, sawdust, and warm ash.

He closed his eyes for a moment while roasting a rat he had caught the night before.

Even information about monsters this insignificant remained perfectly clear in Vikir’s memory.

Before his regression, he had desperately memorized entire monster encyclopedias.

And he had not been alone.

In the Age of Destruction, everyone had been forced to become a hunter.

‘… But right now, I’m the only hunter here.’

Perhaps it would be more accurate to say he was still closer to a hunter’s hound.

In a way, that only made the fate of the hunter more pathetic.

“What could be more ironic than a hunter killed by the dog he raised?”

A conversation with Hugo from shortly before Vikir’s execution surfaced in his mind.

‘When you train dogs, you have to accept that they may die. Two out of three usually do. Do you think that is cruel?’

‘No.’

‘Of course not. The master has to train too—with the knowledge that one day his own hound may bite him. You never know when a blinded fang will turn on the hand that raised it.’

Hugo understood that truth himself.

Perhaps that was why he trusted no one in the world.

Not even himself.

“…”

Vikir shook off the memory.

Then he reached into the small pouch at his waist and took out a deep-red bean.

A Bloody Bean.

A cocoa bean with an absurdly concentrated flavor.

He tossed one into the fire beneath the rat.

The gamey stink faded as the faint aroma of cocoa soaked into the meat.

‘Nothing gets rid of bad meat smell like cocoa beans. Especially Bloody Beans.’

It was a field-cooking trick he had learned from an old veteran in his squad during the war against the demons.

“… Camping always makes the past come back.”

Vikir looked around.

The gazes that had been monitoring him were gone.

The Guide Dogs had apparently decided he was boring and moved elsewhere.

That meant it was time.

Vikir reached for the pile of wood he had gathered.

He drew the blunt shortsword from his waist and began sharpening stakes.

Wooden spears.

One after another, he drove them into the floor of the pit with their points facing upward.

The stakes rose densely from below like the teeth of some enormous beast waiting with its jaws open.

What those teeth would eventually bite, even Vikir had not fully decided yet.

Beyond the boundary of the examination zone lay the forbidden region of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

There lived monsters larger and stronger than people of this age could readily imagine.

To someone who had survived the Age of Destruction, however, most of them were familiar.

“Out of wood.”

There was nothing left to sharpen into stakes or feed the fire.

Vikir climbed out of the pit to gather more.

The guardian knights were no longer watching him.

So there was nothing stopping him from quietly crossing the boundary and cutting wood inside the forbidden zone.

Before long, Vikir slipped past the line.

◆◆◆

The forbidden forest was unnaturally dense.

Trunks and roots grew across one another in every direction until the entire woodland resembled a giant maze.

Dark tunnels wound between roots blackened by old forest fires.

Looking into them, one could almost believe they led directly into hell.

Any ordinary young Baskerville hound would instinctively avoid such an ominous place.

Vikir deliberately entered it.

‘Let’s see… Their habitat should be around here.’

He walked through the forest for a long time.

In his first life, he had crossed this region countless times as an instructor and scout.

He knew it almost as well as his own home.

Before long, he found something.

Sizzle… sizzle…

The sound of embers burning beneath ash.

Vikir followed the scorched smell.

Soon he reached what he had been searching for.

A pile of dung.

Black feces, still hot enough that tongues of flame flickered through it.

Vikir knew only one monster that produced waste like that.

He raised his head and followed the root tunnels farther into the forest.

Then he saw the owner.

A massive body.

Sharp teeth and claws.

Two yellow eyes glowing like lamps.

The monster encyclopedia had contained little more than that.

Vikir, however, had fought more than a few Hellhounds during the Age of Destruction.

“Finally found you.”

They were the reason he had come this far into the mountain.

Vikir immediately turned and ran.

The Hellhound saw him and lunged like a rabid dog.

Bark! Bark! Bark!

Its roar thundered behind him.

Vikir darted into a nearby root tunnel.

Whoosh—

Unable to stop its own momentum, the Hellhound staggered several steps past the turn.

“As stupid as ever. You only know how to charge straight ahead.”

Creatures that crawled up from hell often had brutally simple movement patterns.

Even elite monsters like Hellhounds struggled when their target abruptly changed direction.

Grrrrr…

The beast turned and charged again.

Vikir prepared the next countermeasure.

Splash—

He scattered water across the ground in a thin line.

The Hellhound stopped dead.

For reasons peculiar to its kind, the monster could not cross water.

Even an extremely shallow, narrow layer was enough.

A creature that knew only how to charge straight ahead had no choice but to detour around the wet line.

Every hunter who survived the Age of Destruction eventually learned that weakness.

In the current era, Hellhounds had not yet been studied well enough for the fact to be widely known.

‘Once the Demon Realm opens and Hellhounds spread across the continent, everyone will learn soon enough.’

Vikir faced the beast.

It came around the water and charged again, its speed reduced but its ferocity unchanged.

Even a knight at Mid Sword Expert or above could struggle to defeat one alone.

In Vikir’s previous life, he had not become capable of reliably killing a Hellhound by himself until around eighteen.

But Vikir had not come here unprepared.

“Canine monsters always open their mouths when they run.”

As a veteran hunter of the Age of Destruction, he had prepared the perfect weapon for that habit.

Click—click.

Beans struck one another in his palm.

Bloody Beans.

Chocolate.
