# Chapter 38: Sponsored (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/038.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 38 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Existing canonical/project terminology controls **Burning Suspension**, **Pit Bull Knights**, **Underdog City**, **Deputy Magistrate**, **Morgue**, and the seven local-house spellings. The final stray Korean loanword `티오` is treated by meaning—an available vacancy/space—and rendered contextually rather than as a person named “Tio.”

Burning Suspension was gone.

Not merely shut down.

Burned to the ground.

Vikir had ordered the entire hotel that housed the club put to the torch.

Roar—!

The enormous building became a bonfire visible across Underdog City.

The den where the local heirs had gathered to drown themselves in luxury and vice burned before the eyes of the citizens.

Though it was already night, the blaze lit the streets almost like day.

A few young men lingered near the ruins, staring with obvious reluctance.

“What? Miss it already, you little bastards?”

The Pit Bull Knights standing in formation before the flames turned cold eyes on them.

The youths immediately fled with their tails between their legs.

Vikir had not burned only the club.

Before the flames he had displayed the seven most notorious heirs who had frequented its VVIP rooms.

Only their severed heads remained.

Marks of torture were still visible on them.

Beneath each head was a detailed record of the crimes committed while its owner was alive.

At dawn the following morning, Vikir personally appeared in the square to state the administration’s position.

He used no spokesman.

“Status means nothing before the law. Everyone must obey it.”

His tone was flat.

The effect was anything but.

Public opinion of the new Deputy Magistrate now fell into two categories: favorable and extremely favorable.

Negative opinions were almost impossible to find.

Whenever three or more citizens gathered, somebody praised Vikir as a just administrator.

Naturally, the local families that had lost the heirs they had raised did not remain silent.

The Montblanc, Pierre, Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Ferragamo, Hermes, and Prada families each submitted protests to the administration.

Vikir’s answer did not change.

“They were involved in numerous crimes. More importantly, they directly participated in the illegal slave trade—one of the crimes the Imperial Family treats most severely. Death was the only possible sentence.”

The Emperor regarded illegal slave trading as a first-class felony.

Most illegally traded slaves were tribespeople taken from beyond the Empire’s borders.

That did not mean the throne had suddenly become concerned with the rights of foreigners.

The true concern was private warfare.

To seize people beyond the border, provincial nobles had to raise private armed forces.

An army built for slave hunting could just as easily become the foundation of rebellion or a coup.

Several major uprisings in the recent past had in fact grown from forces originally assembled for slave raids.

The Emperor’s principle was therefore simple.

“War may inevitably create slaves. But war may not be waged for the purpose of creating slaves.”

From then on, illegal slave trafficking had been treated almost on the same level as treason.

Vikir drove the point home.

“I have secured the promissory notes issued by the seven executed criminals, their ledgers, and their cash-transaction records. House Baskerville is formally investigating the matter, and the results will be reported to the Imperial Family.”

In plainer terms:

Object too loudly, and you were volunteering to be investigated for treason.

The protests stopped at once.

The seven families no longer had the luxury of grieving over their dead sons.

Their own survival was suddenly at stake.

Before long, the heads of those proud houses personally came to the administrative office and knelt.

They bowed to the man who had killed their heirs and begged him not to destroy their families.

The price of raising their children badly had become terrifyingly high.

Their foreheads struck the marble floor.

Vikir showed no mercy.

“We have completed a full review of the black ledgers recovered from Burning Suspension. Those found guilty will be summoned and punished according to their crimes.”

The complete results would have filled page after page.

The essentials could be reduced to three articles.

Or, more simply:

- You.
- Are.
- Finished.

For some time after the new Deputy Magistrate took office, the ground around the execution yard never had time to dry.

It was stained by the blood of the parasites that had fed on Underdog City for years.

“The flowerbeds should grow nicely.”

Vikir looked toward the flowers Chihuahua had carefully tended near the execution ground.

The white lilies had turned red.

In any case, the old conservative powers that had once obstructed every administrative decision now kept their mouths shut.

Local magnates who had swaggered through the city now trembled at the sight of a civil servant’s shadow.

The seven houses that had once tried to play politics with Vikir had effectively been uprooted.

Rumors also spread beyond the city that Vikir possessed a portion of House Baskerville’s military authority.

Who would casually provoke a man who could call down Baskerville military power?

Within Underdog City, no faction remained capable of blocking him.

Instead, a different kind of support began to gather around him.

“The seven local families crossed the line long ago.”

“If you want to condemn the new Deputy Magistrate for being ruthless, condemn me first.”

“Can rotten flesh be restored by persuasion and comfort? The infected part has to be cut away. His methods were appropriate.”

Honest officials who had suffered for refusing corruption, and nobles dismissed or exiled for being too upright, began publicly supporting Vikir.

They possessed little money or formal power.

But ordinary people respected them as intellectuals, teachers, and mentors.

Their backing gave Vikir’s reforms greater legitimacy.

Vikir then reopened old cases and reinterpreted past precedents.

Wrongfully convicted prisoners and prisoners of conscience were released.

Investigating old cases required large numbers of clerks, officials, and contractors.

Government hiring expanded sharply.

Unemployment in Underdog City fell with it.

The increased wage budget was covered by the surge in tax revenue and confiscated wealth produced by the destruction of the illegal underground economy.

So the citizens began holding enormous demonstrations in front of City Hall.

<div class="info-window">
<p>&lt;Deputy Magistrate Vikir for permanent office!&gt;</p>
<p>&lt;We strongly demand that Deputy Magistrate Vikir remain in Underdog City forever!&gt;</p>
<p>&lt;We love you, Vikir!&gt;</p>
</div>

They were protests born from affection.

◆◆◆

General Secretary Chihuahua had been smiling for days.

The distrust and contempt that had greeted Vikir’s arrival had completely disappeared.

“Deputy Magistrate, you’re in? I brought you coffee.”

“You are the General Secretary. Do your own work. You do not need to make coffee for me.”

“This is personal gratitude. You saved me when that clown nearly stabbed me.”

“If I saved your life, one cup of coffee seems rather cheap.”

“Cheap? I put in two spoonfuls of sugar, two spoonfuls of cream, and even a little water.”

The two had become close enough to joke with each other.

Vikir was still learning handwriting from Chihuahua.

More precisely, he was learning how to imitate different styles of script.

He used those skills while drafting new laws and revising old ones.

Ruby-mine development in cooperation with Morgue.

Stabilization of agricultural prices.

Rewards for contributions to the territory.

Pardons for slaves who earned merit.

Balanced development of neglected districts.

A system that paired punishment for breaking the law with guaranteed benefits for obeying it.

Higher taxes on outside merchants.

Higher taxes on the wealthy.

Lower taxes on poor farmers.

Campaigns against superstition.

Nationalization of land.

Vikir selected policies he knew would prove effective in the future, then altered them to fit the present social conditions.

The legal officials could only marvel at the fifteen-year-old’s knowledge and bow more deeply.

Underdog City was developing at remarkable speed.

“...Are you really only fifteen?” Chihuahua asked with genuine awe.

Vikir did not bother dignifying the question with much of a response.

Soon, he turned to one of the most important tasks of the day.

“General Secretary.”

“Yes.”

Chihuahua hurried to his side.

Vikir gave the next instruction in the same indifferent tone as always.

“Is there still an open cell in the underground prison?”
