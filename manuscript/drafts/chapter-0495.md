**Continental Calendar — BE 1,020.**

The rivers of blood had finally dried.

The decades-long war between humanity and demonkind—

was recorded in history as the terrifying **Age of Destruction**.

And on the day humanity’s victory was carved into stone—

a head hung above the gate of Tochka—

humanity’s last fortress.

**Baal.**

The **Mother of Destruction**.

The spiritual pillar of the seventy-two Demon Lords.

When that head fell—

the Age of Destruction finally ended.

There were heroes whose names became immortal.

**Camus Morgue**—

head of House Morgue.

**Osiris Le Baskerville**—

head of House Baskerville.

**Sinclaire Bourgeois**—

head of House Bourgeois.

**Dolores L. Quovadis**—

Pope of House Quovadis.

**Orca Montreuil-sur-Mer Javert**—

Warden of Nouvelle Vague.

And many more.

Countless heroes—

through whose efforts humanity barely preserved its bloodline.

…And yet—

there were also heroes who fought more brilliantly than anyone—

but whose names never properly entered the history books.

They appeared one day without warning.

Alongside an **eighth guiding star**.

They descended upon the battlefield—

as if messengers promising salvation—

and drove the Demon Lords away.

Age unknown.

Origin unknown.

Names unknown.

But one thing was certain.

The man leading the six—

used the swordsmanship of the Iron-Blooded Sword House Baskerville.

Rumor claimed—

while still alive—

he had reached the supposedly impossible **Ninth Form**.

No one ever proved it.

The other five were even stranger.

Some looked **exactly** like already-living heroes.

Camus Morgue.

Dolores L. Quovadis.

Sinclaire Bourgeois.

Same faces.

Same powers.

One woman apparently came from a barbarian tribe already extinct in that timeline.

Another resembled a former Nouvelle Vague guard whose exact rank could never be confirmed.

History—

unable to explain the contradictions—

failed to record them properly.

But the facts remained.

At their hands—

Baal fell.

And humanity finally entered a long peace.

◆◆◆

When the Age of Destruction ended—

the six received only a tiny fraction of the recognition their achievements deserved.

And a small territory.

Small—

at least compared to what they had actually done.

But they withdrew willingly from the ugly struggle over rewards.

**One may share hardship—**

**yet fail to share prosperity.**

Rather than join the scramble—

they accepted what little they were given.

Retired quietly.

And disappeared from public history.

Later speculation suggested—

they founded an enormous household.

Raised many children.

And lived quiet—

comfortable—

long lives.

…

Still—

as a faithful chronicler—

a historian driven by curiosity—

a writer loved by the public—

and one survivor of the Age of Destruction—

I cannot finish this record without asking:

**Where did they come from?**

**Where did they go?**

**Who were they?**

**What did they live for?**

No research—

no matter how persistent—

has produced an answer.

Perhaps that question—

belongs not only to this writer—

but to all humanity saved by them.

Even now—

the constellation that guided their descent—

continues shining silently above us.

Seven stars—

brighter than ever.

**—Nymphet, 『History of Magic』, Middle Volume, p. 3,021**

### Credit Cookie

WHOOOO—

Dry wind peeled another layer from the desert.

A black cloak snapped.

A long gray beard moved with it.

An old man crossed the salt flats.

Perhaps it was a side effect of crossing timelines.

When others aged ten years—

twenty—

his body seemed to gain only one.

Children grew.

Then grandchildren.

Great-grandchildren.

Then their children.

A very long time had passed.

Now—

the old man was leaving.

Casting off every restraint.

Every obligation.

Giving himself at last to an instinct suppressed for most of his life.

WHOOOO—

Wind sharp enough to turn stone into sand swept across crescent dunes.

He climbed them silently.

Then—

found what he was seeking.

A tower.

Dark red against a white horizon.

Black as night.

Red as blood.

**THE GRAVE OF SWORDS**

The old man looked at the crude letters.

Then nodded.

“…A true Baskerville is born in the Cradle of Swords.”

Silence.

Then—

“…A true Baskerville dies in the Grave of Swords.”

A deep laugh rolled from inside.

[This is the Grave of Swords.]

[The final destination of those who pursue the ultimate meaning of the sword.]

The top of the tower opened—

like a great hound’s mouth.

An iron throne waited inside.

Upon it—

a white-bearded old man in black armor.

**Cane Corso Le Baskerville.**

One of the old Seven Counts.

A man even the Age of Destruction had failed to erase.

He stroked his beard.

Smiled.

[We have never met.]

[And yet your face feels strangely familiar.]

[Perhaps the intuition of those who reach the extreme realm transcends time itself.]

The gray-bearded old man—

Vikir—

did not answer.

Beelzebub slid from the back of his hand.

Cane Corso smiled more widely.

Then—

the two old swordsmen moved.

Nine fangs.

Against nine fangs.

Only an instant.

The moment they collided—

Vikir froze.

As if struck by lightning.

Something blocked for a lifetime—

opened.

Space and time warped.

FLASH.

Nine fangs tangled violently.

And beside them—

something tiny appeared.

A speck.

A glimmer.

A **tenth fang**.

Almost invisible.

But real.

Then—

the storm ended.

Only one old man remained.

Vikir.

He looked up at the tower.

Stood there for a long moment.

Then slowly—

walked inside.

Cane Corso’s fading voice followed him.

[Your birth was the birth of the sword.]

[Your death will be the death of the sword.]

**The End.**

**Thank you.**

### The Real Credit Cookie

BOOOOM!

Flame erupted into the sky.

“WHO DECIDED IT WAS OVER!?”

A furious red-haired woman stamped the ground.

“Is this a side effect of time travel?”

“Or something else?”

“He makes me unable to age—

then leaves me alone!?”

“Running away from responsibility!”

“And he calls himself a Baskerville hound!?”

Camus’s hair looked ready to become a volcano.

Red fire.

Black iron stakes.

All around her.

Beside Camus stood a bronze-skinned woman.

Strong muscles.

Bow across her back.

Collar at her throat.

Aiyen crossed her arms.

“I want many children.”

“The tribe still needs rebuilding.”

“Human numbers are low anyway.”

“More births are encouraged.”

“At least three digits.”

Beside Aiyen—

Dolores calmly knitted in white robes.

“Everyone should calm down.”

“Getting too excited is unhealthy.”

“Although…”

“We’re approaching demigod territory now.”

“So perhaps it doesn’t matter.”

She paused.

Then panicked before anyone else.

“Wait.”

“Did he run because our bodies became too strong?”

“Was he scared because we ask for too much!?”

Sinclaire—

white hair now cut short—

sighed.

“Sisters.”

“Stop nagging.”

“He ran away again.”

“If people shouted around me all day—

I’d want to run too.”

“Trust is important.”

The final woman—

black hair.

Red eyes.

A cool expression—

spoke.

“Leave it to me.”

“Catching escaping criminals is my specialty.”

“I already know where he went.”

Five pairs of eyes sharpened.

“Where?”

“That desert again?”

“If he went after the tower—

that is disappointingly predictable.”

“This feels different.”

“Don’t worry.”

“I’ve already located the tower.”

“I surveyed the area twice.”

“I could find it with my eyes closed.”

The five women—

who argued constantly—

united instantly whenever it mattered.

“Just wait!”

“When we catch him—

fifty years confinement!”

“You always say that.”

“Then you cry and beg him to come home.”

“I miss him already…”

“What’s there to worry about?”

“We have time.”

“And when we catch him—

cut his allowance in half.”

“You cut it every time.”

“No wonder he runs.”

“Then halve the allowance—

and double his defensive-duty rotations.”

“That might scare him even more.”

“I’ll just watch.”

“Decades together and you still pretend to be aloof.”

“This time he might really have left.”

“Please.”

“It’s obviously the Grave of Swords.”

“I marked it on the map.”

“The Yuuni Salt Desert?”

“The place with that Basilisk?”

“I beat it so badly during my last survey it stopped appearing.”

“If you mapped the area—

it probably realized and moved.”

“It’s intelligent.”

Then—

someone pointed.

“There!”

“I see it!”

Five noisy women crossed the salt desert toward the tower.

And somewhere—

across the long arc of Vikir’s life—

an old prophecy finally reached its punchline.

*A life spent fleeing pursuit.*

*But those pursuing you will find you.*

*They will reach where you are.*

*You cannot escape them.*

*Forever.*

*I see their furious faces.*

*Your future—bound to them forever.*

And at last—

the final line.

*You will be divided among five.*

The demon’s ominous prophecy—

had come true.

Just—

not in the way the demon intended.
