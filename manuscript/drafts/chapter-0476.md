The severed head creaked.

[Didn’t I tell you from the beginning?]

[Everything you do is pointless.]

Vikir said nothing.

[You cannot kill me right now.]

[Give up, Demon Hunter.]

Flauros kept talking.

Truth or lie.

Knowingly walk into deception—

or ignore the possibility and force his way through.

Camus stood beside Vikir with her jaw clenched.

“Hey.”

“Is what that bastard said true?”

“If he dies, does this Gate of Destruction really open?”

Two answers came at once.

Seere trembled on Camus’s shoulder.

Decarabia opened its single eye against Vikir’s chest.

[Flauros possesses the authority of lies.]

[Even we cannot tell whether what he says is true or false.]

Then Decarabia added—

[…But the possibility is high.]

[That one is extremely cunning.]

Even demons of the same rank could not tell whether Flauros was lying.

At the crossroads—

Vikir hesitated.

Let Flauros escape.

Or kill him—

and risk opening the Gate of Destruction.

Even now—

Flauros was slowly turning to vapor.

His severed head beginning to disperse.

Cold sweat rolled down Vikir’s skin like molten lead.

He clenched his teeth hard enough to crack them.

Then—

“Do as you wish.”

A heavy voice came from behind him.

Hugo.

His shadow fell across Vikir’s back.

“Son.”

Vikir did not turn.

Hugo continued.

“Even if a child makes the wrong choice—”

“it is the father who bears responsibility.”

The words struck Vikir as strangely unfamiliar.

Hugo himself muttered under his breath.

“…Ridiculous.”

“Since when did I become a father?”

Then—

scrape.

A boot ground against the earth.

“Wait—!”

Vikir spun around.

Too late.

BOOM!

Hugo swung.

A devastating slash struck exactly where Flauros’s head was trying to escape.

[AAAAAAARGH!]

A tearing scream ripped through the air.

The blow severed the demon’s last thread of life.

Vikir stared at the shattered remains of Flauros’s head.

Demons had to die.

Given a little more time—

Vikir probably would have made the same decision.

But this choice had carried an enormous burden.

And Vikir—

for once—

had hesitated.

Hugo—

a man Vikir had never truly thought of as a father—

had taken that burden onto himself.

Almost like indulging a child.

“….”

“….”

Their eyes met.

Complicated thoughts passed between them.

No one nearby dared speak.

Then—

RUMBLE!

Thunder exploded across a clear sky.

Dark clouds gathered.

A pillar of red light appeared overhead.

A colossal crimson line traced itself across the heavens.

It curved—

then began forming a vast geometric pattern.

Vikir recognized it immediately.

“The Gate of Destruction.”

Flauros had told the truth.

At the cost of a burden almost equal to annihilation—

he had opened the Gate of Destruction.

Then bound his own life to its seal.

With his life now completely extinguished—

the seal broke.

The gate opened.

A portal summoned by a high-ranking demon at the cost of its own life.

Perhaps Flauros’s soul was now paying a price worse than annihilation.

It no longer mattered.

The Gate of Destruction was open.

Vikir fought to keep his entire body from shaking.

The Long Rain of Terror was coming.

The long season that announced the end of the world.

The demons’ ultimate weapon—

the disaster that had once killed ninety-eight percent of living humanity and inflicted the greatest losses of the entire war.

Endless rain of fire.

Fire wind.

Fire lightning.

Fire floods.

Red drops would fall across the world and burn everything.

Grass and forests.

Mountains.

Oceans.

Lakes.

Animals.

Humans.

For one hundred fifty days—

the fire would continue.

“…Was he telling the truth?”

“A spatial gate of this scale…”

“I’ve never felt magic this evil.”

“How can something like this even exist?”

“Compared with this, Nouvelle Vague really was a cradle.”

Camus.

Aiyen.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Kirko.

All five stared at the impossible gate.

Seeing it with their own eyes—

and still struggling to believe it.

Then—

RUMBLE!

The entire sky shook.

Vikir shouted immediately.

“The first eruption is coming!”

He remembered this moment.

Before regression—

the Gate of Destruction had opened exactly like this.

First—

the gate itself.

Then—

an enormous torrent of hellfire.

The initial blast alone could reduce tens of thousands of soldiers to ash.

And the gate above Tochka was large enough to do exactly that.

The portal opened.

Heat spilled out.

Then—

BOOOOM!

A pillar of flame erupted.

A supernatural disaster on the scale of Nouvelle Vague’s volcanic eruption.

Like a tongue extending from a mouth—

the Gate of Destruction spat its first mass of crimson fire.

It reached toward everything below.

Vikir stepped forward.

This—

was why he had prepared since the moment he opened his eyes in the Cradle of Swords.

He forced every remaining drop of mana into Beelzebub.

A black sun formed at the blade’s tip.

Eight violently spinning fangs curved around one another into a sphere.

**Black Sun.**

Baskerville Eighth Form.

Against the Gate of Destruction—

it looked tiny.

Fragile.

A moth flying into a furnace.

*It doesn’t matter.*

*I can’t give up.*

Vikir clenched his jaw.

Every ounce of strength—

from the day he strangled a poisonous snake in the cradle until now—

went into one strike.

Dolores’s blessing wrapped around him.

The Black Sun met the Gate’s first hellfire.

Sizzle—

Vikir closed his eyes.

He imagined his body burning away.

But—

nothing happened.

No impact.

No heat.

“…?”

Vikir opened his eyes.

The Gate of Destruction.

The hellfire.

Tochka below.

And between them—

black fangs.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Seven.

Eight.

They were holding back the flames.

“No.”

“Not eight…”

Vikir counted again.

There was one more.

Nine.

A ninth fang tore ferociously into the hellfire.

Nine fangs resisting destruction.

**Baskerville Ninth Form.**

Black aura pushed against the Gate of Destruction and kept the apocalypse from swallowing Tochka.

Vikir had seen this power before.

At the Grave of Swords.

The final realm shown by a noble swordsman who had crossed the threshold of death.

Death Knight.

Nine fangs.

A Baskerville who had crossed the border between life and death.

Dead once—

yet granted a delayed existence beyond death.

*Since when did I become a father?*

Vikir looked toward the source of the Ninth Form.

Hugo Le Baskerville.

Head of the Iron-Blooded Sword House.

He stood there.
