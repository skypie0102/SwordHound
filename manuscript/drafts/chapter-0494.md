# Chapter 494: The Day After the Apocalypse (1)

FLASH.

A star flickered in the sky—

then vanished.

It lasted so briefly that almost no one noticed.

Almost.

Perhaps it was coincidence.

Perhaps unusually sharp eyes.

Or perhaps one exhausted soldier—

too tired of the ruined world to look anywhere except the night sky—

simply happened to see it.

Major Janet.

First Battalion.

207th Regiment.

Human Alliance.

Her lips—

scarred by burns and blades—

twitched.

“The guiding stars look strange tonight.”

Beside her—

Miryamu was roasting insects and lizards over a weak flame.

“Battalion Commander?”

“Did you say something?”

“No.”

Janet frowned upward.

“It just looked like there was something odd about the guiding stars.”

“What?”

Miryamu looked up.

Several nearby soldiers did too.

Seven stars.

The familiar constellation.

All shining normally.

Janet narrowed her eyes.

“…That’s strange.”

“I could’ve sworn there were eight just now.”

“Hahaha.”

“There are only seven guiding stars.”

“Even a seven-year-old knows that.”

Miryamu paused.

“…Although finding a seven-year-old these days is harder than pulling a star from the sky.”

Janet smiled bitterly.

Then looked away.

The Age of Destruction.

The rivers of blood had finally dried.

The decades-long war between humanity and demonkind—

seemed to have ended.

Human survivors had celebrated.

Carved victory into stone.

Then—

the **real** apocalypse began.

Sixty-two demons.

Every one of them more powerful and more malevolent than the Ten Corpses.

The Long Rain of Terror.

Then a Great Flood lasting one hundred fifty days.

The human world—

already ruined—

became Hell all over again.

*…Things were almost better when I was only a soldier in the old war.*

Then—

“Battalion Commander!”

“Air raid!”

“Monsters incoming!”

Captain Saladin ran toward them.

Farther away—

officers shouted orders.

Lieutenant General Kirko.

Brigadier General Hayar.

Colonel Kuberin.

Veterans who had somehow survived everything—

gathering the last Human Alliance forces for one final stand.

“Commander!”

“Move!”

“If this line falls—

there’s nothing behind us but the final refugees!”

“The children!”

Janet put on her helmet.

Raised sword and shield.

“Understood.”

“Everyone to position.”

Miryamu stood behind her with a spear.

Her voice trembled.

“This really is my final battle.”

“…Probably everyone’s.”

They watched the monster tide stain the horizon dark red.

Their voices—

quiet.

Almost like sisters talking before sleep.

Then Miryamu asked:

“Commander.”

“How do you stay calm?”

Janet glanced back.

“You never panic.”

“You always look like something is protecting you.”

Protection.

Blessing.

Once—

priests had made such things real.

Now almost all of them were dead.

Janet smiled.

“Of course I have something.”

“How else do you live strongly in a world like this?”

Miryamu brightened.

“Really?”

“What faith is it?”

Janet laughed softly.

“A letter.”

“…A letter?”

“A letter someone once gave me.”

She had treated its words like a talisman for years.

Long ago—

when life had become unbearable and Janet joined the army—

she met a young junior officer.

“His surname was Baskerville, I think.”

“Baskerville?”

“You mean those lunatics on the front?”

“Yes.”

“But he was different.”

“Reliable.”

“Excellent.”

“A true hound.”

She stared at the approaching enemy.

“He was falsely accused and executed midway through the war.”

Janet could barely remember his face anymore.

Only fragments.

Miryamu blinked.

“So he was romantic enough to write letters?”

“No.”

“That’s the strange thing.”

“He wasn’t.”

“Then?”

“The letter came after he died.”

Miryamu shrugged.

“Lazy postal service?”

“Maybe.”

“But it arrived a very long time after his execution.”

Their conversation ended.

BOOOOM.

Something huge entered the front line.

Two arms.

Two legs.

Four pairs of wings.

A long tail.

Curved horns.

Eyes burning like suns.

A body packed with dense muscle.

Janet swallowed.

“…Dragon Demon.”

A cursed hybrid of dragon and demon.

An eternal exile.

**Diaspora.**

Rejected by dragonkind.

Rejected by demonkind.

So it poured hatred onto everything else alive.

[ROAAAAAR!]

It charged the human line.

A creature that could tear thousands apart by itself.

Fear spread instantly.

“Damn it.”

“Why is something that lives in the deepest abyss here?”

“Did it crawl out for the final battle?”

Janet gritted her teeth.

There was nowhere left to retreat.

Behind them—

the last humans.

“STOP IT!”

“If the line breaks—

the refugees die!”

Light erupted around Janet.

She attacked.

Against an enemy this powerful—

one chance.

One strike.

She aimed for the opening after its punch.

CLANG.

Her sword broke the instant it touched the scales.

“….”

Despair crossed Janet’s face.

Above her—

the Dragon Demon grinned.

Its enormous fist came down like a meteor.

A direct hit would kill Janet.

The shock alone would erase everything for dozens of meters behind her.

More people would die.

*…So this is it.*

Then—

“COMMANDER!”

A hand grabbed her waist.

Miryamu.

She threw herself forward—

and shoved Janet away.

BOOOOM!

Janet hit the ground.

Rolled.

Rose instantly.

“M-MIRYAMU!?”

Dust covered everything.

No answer.

Of course there was no answer.

“…No.”

Tears streamed from Janet’s eyes.

She had thought those eyes were dry forever.

Apparently—

they still held water.

Miryamu.

The coward who had followed her since private rank.

Across countless battles.

Promoted slowly—

despite senior officers dying fast and promotion becoming easy—

because fear made her run so often.

“…That coward saved me.”

Janet bit down.

She would repay it.

Somehow.

She gathered the last scraps of mana.

Gripped her broken sword.

“If I die—

I’m taking you with me.”

Dust began settling.

The Dragon Demon stood there.

Strangely—

not defending itself.

Not moving.

*Now!*

Janet attacked before conscious thought caught up.

“AAAAAAH!”

Everything into one thrust.

The heart.

CRACK.

Her broken sword—

broke again.

Too many old fractures.

Too much force.

The attack opened a deep wound—

but missed the heart.

“…Ah.”

Janet knew.

Now it really was over.

No mana.

No strength.

If the monster grabbed her head—

she could not resist.

*Sorry.*

She had failed to avenge Miryamu.

Failed to avenge the others.

Janet closed her eyes.

Waited.

At least let it be painless.

…

Nothing.

No impact.

No pain.

*Is it already over?*

Was death really this quiet?

Janet opened her eyes.

“…!”

The Dragon Demon still stood in front of her.

Chest forward.

Body upright.

But—

nothing rested on its shoulders.

SPURT!

Black-red blood erupted from the severed neck.

Its head—

was in someone’s hand.

And at that person’s feet—

Miryamu sat alive.

Dazed.

Janet could not understand.

One second.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Seven.

Eight.

As many seconds as the stars she had counted.

Then Janet finally looked at the face of the man who had killed the Dragon Demon.

He—

was—

“You’re all still alive.”

A calm voice.

Almost as though he had returned from a walk.

A long leave.

He looked across the battlefield—

like a soldier returning to his old barracks.

“Captain Kirko.”

“Lieutenant Hayar.”

“Sergeant Kuberin.”

“Sergeant Janet.”

“Corporal Saladin.”

“Private Miryamu…”

He paused.

“First Platoon.”

“Fourth Company.”

“207th Regiment.”

“Still here.”

The face was in Janet’s memory.

And yet—

not.

The same man—

but decades younger.

Burns.

Sword scars.

Gone.

Only his presence remained.

Stronger.

More certain.

More overwhelming.

“Well done.”

“You did well.”

Miryamu—

still sitting on the ground—

pointed at him.

“T-this man…”

“He fell from the sky.”

“From the guiding star.”

The man smiled faintly.

“I see.”

“Private Miryamu.”

“You were too young back then to remember my face.”

“P-private?”

“I’m a sergeant…”

Miryamu looked baffled.

Janet did not.

Her face broke.

“Company Commander!”

“You’re alive!”

“…You recognize me?”

“Of course!”

“I knew you had to be alive!”

“Where were you?”

“Why do you look so young—?”

Vikir tilted his head.

“You thought I was alive?”

“Didn’t you see me beheaded?”

“Well…”

“Yes.”

“But afterward—

you sent a letter.”

Janet reached inside her clothes.

A parchment—

yellowed by decades.

**I miss you, comrade.**

**Are you well over there?**

Vikir stared.

“…So that letter really was real.”

“I still don’t understand.”

Other soldiers approached.

Eyes widening.

“Company Commander?”

“Is that really you?”

“You’re alive?”

“Where have you been!?”

“Everyone waited for you!”

“We all said there was no way you could really be dead!”

Reunion—

however—

could not last.

BOOOOM!

A massive hoof crushed the Dragon Demon corpse like soft clay.

A giant beast-shaped demon stepped over it.

Long mane.

Enormous hooves.

[HAHAHAHAHA!]

[I AM **OROBAS**!]

[THE EQUESTRIAN PRINCE OF THE ETERNAL HELL!]

[RANKED FIFTY-FIFTH!]

[WHO STILL RESISTS USELESSLY!?]

Human faces collapsed into despair.

“Rank fifty-five…”

“Why is something like that here!?”

“It’s over.”

“A Demon Lord descended.”

No one had time to sink into despair.

BOOM!

BOOM!

BOOM!

BOOM!

BOOM!

Five pillars of power struck from the sky.

Burning iron stakes.

A vicious long-range shot.

A holy pillar of light.

A golden fist.

A razor-sharp slash.

Orobas—

who had introduced himself only seconds earlier—

exploded into pieces.

[AAAAAAAAGH—!]

Five women stepped out from the crater.

“Ow.”

“My back.”

“Dimensional travel really does make me nauseous.”

“Quite an entrance for our first appearance here.”

“So this is our debut in another world.”

“I thought the ticking second hand would drive me insane.”

“The day after the apocalypse isn’t that impressive.”

Camus.

Aiyen.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Kirko.

Humans.

Demons.

Everyone—

stopped fighting.

Every mouth—

open.

Then—

RUMBLE.

A black aura storm expanded across dozens of meters.

At its center—

a massive black hound appeared.

Nine fangs.

The battlefield itself seemed to recoil.

Everyone swallowed.

Then—

before every gaze—

Vikir spoke.

“Kill the demons.”
