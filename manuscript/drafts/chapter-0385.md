# Chapter 385: The Rotten Dog of Nouvelle Vague (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from the target-385 segment of shared raw `source/chinese/chapters/385.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 383 is the verified shifted secondary witness and supplies only the documented localized closing gap after the Chinese target385 truncation. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, D'Ordume D'Orcdile, Souaré, Lieutenant Bastille, BDISSEM, Flubber, Flubber J Tarbond, Gulper / Elevator Eel, Sakkuth de Leviathan, Garam Nord, and Nouvelle Vague. The designated Fandom index confirms Lieutenant Colonel Flubber J Tarbond as a distinct officer from the Flubber barrier substance.

Early the next morning—

Nouvelle Vague was in an uproar unlike anything it had seen before.

“WHAT THE HELL HAPPENED?!”

D'Ordume D'Orcdile’s roar shook Level Nine.

The colonel—

one of the strongest candidates to become the next warden—

turned on the officers gathered behind him.

Lieutenant Colonel Bastille and the others stood with their heads lowered.

Night Hound had disappeared during the night.

The moment D'Ordume learned that Vikir was missing—

he had started tearing into his subordinates.

But there was almost nothing to report.

Only that an unidentified doctor had slipped out of the prisoner zone under the pretext of quarantine work.

And D'Ordume could not rage quite as freely as he wanted.

The search perimeter had become loose in the first place because of his own orders.

The suspicious object found in Level Ten.

The forced construction work around it.

The guard redeployment he had ordered to keep the project moving.

All of it had helped create the gap.

“Nothing unusual happened last night?”

“Nothing?”

“And nobody noticed until now?!”

The guards remained silent.

Then—

“Colonel!”

A subordinate came running.

“You need to see Level Five!”

D'Ordume’s brows jumped.

He rushed upstairs.

Then stopped cold.

“…What is this?”

A huge breach scarred the wall near the Gulper Eel pools.

Dead eels floated everywhere.

Nobody could even estimate how many had been swept outside.

Fortunately—

the damage had not become a catastrophic flood.

The Flubber membrane had already sealed the breach again.

Lieutenant Colonel **Flubber J Tarbond** had also been mobilized to stabilize the damaged area.

But the loss of the eels alone was enormous.

Gulper Eels were notoriously difficult to raise.

Many died suddenly while maturing.

Others simply failed to survive for reasons nobody understood.

And they grew painfully slowly.

A single mature specimen could be worth nearly as much as a ship.

Now—

dozens lay dead.

D'Ordume barely looked at them.

His attention had gone somewhere else.

A small girl crouched at the edge of the pool.

Wrapped in a blanket someone had thrown over her shoulders.

Both hands around a cup of warm sea-cow milk.

**BDISSEM.**

D'Ordume stared.

“…What happened to you?”

BDISSEM sniffled.

“I tried to catch the escapee last night.”

“And he got away.”

“What?”

“Why were you even out here—”

D'Ordume covered his forehead.

“Haa…”

A voice came from the side.

“You’re late.”

“Painfully slow.”

Colonel Souaré watched him with an openly mocking smile.

“And you want to become warden?”

“Shut up, Souaré.”

“Last night happened to be my one day off for the year.”

“Oh?”

“So while I was dealing with flood damage, dead Gulper Eels, and BDISSEM’s protection—

you were completely unavailable?”

“….”

D'Ordume ground his teeth.

Souaré looked around.

“Lieutenant Colonel Flubber handled the damaged section quickly.”

“The remaining flood damage can be repaired with Level One labor.”

“The eels are a terrible loss.”

“But at least BDISSEM is alive.”

“If she had died—

we could have had an unprecedented disaster.”

D'Ordume’s gaze returned to BDISSEM.

“…Where is the escapee?”

Souaré shrugged.

“Why are you asking me?”

“Capturing escapees is your jurisdiction.”

“I deal with the ones you bring back.”

She was right.

D'Ordume had no choice but to question BDISSEM again.

“Where did he go?”

BDISSEM jumped.

“He’s dead!”

“…Dead?”

D'Ordume went still.

Nobody escaped Nouvelle Vague.

Prisoners lived with mana and strength suppressed.

Bad food.

Brutal labor.

Weakened bodies.

And above all—

ten thousand meters of ocean overhead.

Still—

the matter needed a corpse.

“Then where is the body?”

BDISSEM looked away.

“…He rode a Gulper Eel out into the deep sea.”

“That doesn’t mean he’s dead.”

“It might as well!”

This—

at least—

was not entirely nonsense.

Using a Gulper Eel did **not** automatically mean reaching the surface.

That detail was classified.

Prisoners did not know it.

The Level Five breeding facility connected to more than three thousand circulating deep-sea currents.

Only one led to the surface.

The release point mattered.

Without the exact route—

Nouvelle Vague’s own figures put the random chance of reaching the surface at only **0.00033101622%**.

Every other route became a trap.

The eel drifted through the deep sea—

eventually looping back toward its origin.

The full circuit could take a century.

By the time the eel returned—

a passenger inside would be nothing but bones.

“He couldn’t possibly know the surface current.”

BDISSEM spoke faster now.

“He’ll be trapped in one of the loops.”

“He’ll drift down there for the rest of his life.”

D'Ordume narrowed his eyes.

“…That sounds like a guess.”

“You didn’t actually see what happened.”

“I did!”

BDISSEM’s voice cracked.

“I saw him ride one through the hole!”

“I saw it myself!”

D'Ordume crossed his arms.

Said nothing.

For far too long.

“…You personally witnessed his end?”

“O-of course!”

“Who do you think I am?”

“And I outrank you, so stop looking at me like that!”

Sweat beaded across BDISSEM’s face.

She was lying.

*I can’t tell them I woke up and he was simply gone.*

If Orca returned and heard that—

BDISSEM might find herself confined like a prisoner.

*There was a hole.*

*He was trying to move the eels.*

*He must have ridden one out.*

*He’s probably drifting somewhere in the abyss right now.*

*Damn him.*

She convinced herself.

Then—

by chance—

Souaré supplied exactly the support she needed.

“Now that I think about it…”

“That lunatic from Level Eight said something strange before he went into solitary.”

D'Ordume looked over.

“The plague man?”

“Sakkuth?”

“Right.”

“He said some man named Vikir had warned him that Night Hound was about to escape.”

“I thought it was nonsense.”

“Apparently not.”

Sakkuth de Leviathan—

sent to solitary after the recent riot—

had unknowingly become useful testimony.

Suspicion moved away from BDISSEM.

“A-all right then.”

“I’m going back to my room.”

She pulled the blanket tighter.

Fear of Orca’s punishment—

and of humiliation in front of her subordinates—

made her very eager for the subject to end.

D'Ordume remained suspicious.

But there was nowhere obvious to push.

A breach in the wall.

Missing Gulper Eels.

BDISSEM’s firm testimony.

Sakkuth’s warning.

Eventually—

he nodded.

“Night Hound died during the escape attempt.”

“We’ll process it that way.”

Souaré gave a small shrug.

“I suppose so.”

“A pity.”

“He was rather interesting.”

D'Ordume opened the prisoner register.

Found the entry.

Then struck a line through it.

**<Night Hound — Vikir Van Baskerville>**

“Transmit it to the surface.”

“Have his death entered into the record.”

“Yes, Colonel.”

A junior guard bowed deeply and accepted the register.

Then turned away.

Souaré’s gaze happened to follow him for a moment.

Tattered guard cap.

Unruly hair.

A huge burn scar across the face.

A uniform that looked almost too clean and new.

And a freshly attached nameplate.

**Garam Nord.**

*Was he always one of D'Ordume’s men?*

Something about him felt slightly strange.

Souaré dismissed the thought.

She was a colonel.

A candidate for warden.

There was no reason she should remember the face of every low-ranking guard.

More important—

D'Ordume had just made a spectacular mistake.

And Souaré intended to make sure everyone noticed who had cleaned it up.

“Let’s finish here.”

“I’m checking the upper levels.”

D'Ordume answered only with cigar smoke.

And so—

what might have become the greatest escape incident in Nouvelle Vague’s history—

ended with remarkable anticlimax.

Night Hound was officially dead.

And Vikir—

was still inside the prison.
