Darkness.

A child stood alone.

“Get out.”

A middle-aged man's voice.

One of the earliest memories the child still possessed.

“Trash like this isn't of my blood.”

Cold.

Absolute.

Then—

his mother crying.

“How dare something so lowly…”

“Throw him out.”

“Deal with it.”

Whispers surrounded them.

His mother's sobbing—

underneath.

Then the world turned.

Running.

His mother carrying him.

Forest.

Mountains.

Pursuers.

A cliff.

A violent river.

Hungry wolves.

Then—

merchants.

Mercenaries.

Strangers staring.

Years passed.

Memories blurred.

His mother—

who almost never smiled—

always looking at him with sorrow.

Then a new father.

A stepfather.

Gentle.

Patient.

Someone who gave love to a son who was not his by blood.

A pillar for the family.

Slowly—

his mother smiled.

The home changed.

Small.

Dark.

Wet.

Became—

larger.

Brighter.

Warm.

Food improved.

Life—

improved.

The child became his mother's pride.

His father's hope.

And so—

for them—

he chose a challenge.

**Colosseo Academy.**

His own effort.

And the savings his father had built through a lifetime of work.

His mother worried about sending him among the Empire's greatest geniuses.

His father—

spent everything without hesitation.

Smiled every day.

The child wanted to succeed.

To show something to the mother who had survived so much—

and the father who had spent his life making her happy.

But Academy life—

did not go smoothly.

Outside Colosseo—

the child had been lively.

A leader among peers.

Good at sports.

Inside the Empire's greatest concentration of talent—

he became mediocre.

An excellent mind.

A weaker body.

Practical evaluations—

failure after failure.

Other students—

bullying him for his timidness.

And now—

death.

At the limit—

the child's oldest memory returned.

“Get out.”

“This trash is not my blood.”

His sense of self—

started to break.

Then—

another voice.

“It's all right now.”

Simple.

Quiet.

More effective than any magic.

Birth shame.

Guilt toward his parents.

Responsibility.

Academic pressure.

Loneliness.

Pain.

For one moment—

all of it became lighter.

A friend's reassurance.

“…Ah!”

**Pigi** opened his eyes.

Three faces looked down at him.

Tudor—

crying openly.

Sancho—

also crying.

Bianca—

tears and mucus streaking her face.

“Pigi!”

“Breathe!”

“Come on!”

“Don't die!”

Sancho shouted—

“His breathing is back!”

“Northern resuscitation works!”

Bianca snapped—

“Then keep pressing his chest!”

“You—rub his arms and legs!”

Pigi coughed.

Sat up.

Pain stabbed through his ribs.

Cracked.

Perhaps broken.

He still searched immediately.

“Vikir?”

“Vikir!?”

Tudor.

Sancho.

Bianca.

Their expressions changed.

Then all four looked toward the same place.

Twang!

Twang-twang-twang!

Vikir—

shooting Killer Ants as fast as the bow could move.

◆◆◆

*This is enough.*

Vikir put an arrow through a Soldier Ant's head.

*Expert-level strength is sufficient.*

He did not need to reveal his **Graduator aura**.

Because—

*there's a method to hunting Killer Ants.*

A method—

unknown in the present.

The swarm charged.

Vikir fired not at the skull—

but at the thoracic plate where head and chest connected.

Crack.

The struck ant—

stopped.

Then turned around.

Walked backward.

The ants behind it collided.

Formation broke.

Vikir nodded faintly.

The Academy's golems were extraordinarily accurate recreations.

*Killer Ants have a biological compass here.*

*Destroy it—*

*and they lose direction.*

Humanity would not discover this for another twenty years.

In Vikir's previous life—

researchers had obsessed over one question.

How did Killer Ants return to their nests over enormous distances?

Pheromones?

Terrain memory?

Neither explained everything.

Storms erased scent.

Earthquakes changed terrain.

Newly hatched ants—

with no learned map—

still found home.

The real answer—

was an internal mana-field compass.

A specialized organ able to detect—

position.

Direction.

Destination.

Even the polarized rotation of mana fields under sunlight.

Destroy it—

and the ant's navigation collapsed.

So Vikir did not need to kill every Worker Ant himself.

He broke their compasses.

The disoriented ants reversed.

Blocked the swarm.

Were trampled by their own colony.

Then—

Vikir raised his eyes.

*Soldier Ants.*

The giant armored forms.

Impossible to forget.

In the previous timeline—

the battle at the Great Colony Basin had cost countless lives.

Human forces had learned the ordinary ants' weaknesses.

Started winning.

Then Soldier Ants emerged—

and reversed the battle.

Deeper in the nest—

near the underground catacombs—

they appeared in large numbers.

Few people in the present even knew those deeper zones existed.

A Soldier Ant—

was dangerous enough to compare with a Hellhound.

Even an elite **Sword Expert Advanced** fighter could struggle one-on-one.

Yet—

it too had a weakness.

Like using chocolate against a Hellhound—

there was a trick.

*Find the exact point.*

Vikir concentrated.

He only needed aura in the **intermediate-to-advanced Sword Expert range**.

Not Graduator output.

The difference was control.

The same tool—

in a novice's hand—

and a veteran's—

could produce completely different results.

Dark-red aura gathered at the arrowhead.

Twang.

The arrow flew.

Thud!

The Soldier Ant collapsed.

One shot.

Vikir allowed himself a faint smile.

The monster's greatest strength—

was its skull.

Wide.

Heavy.

Built for impact.

Everyone knew that.

Almost no one knew—

the weakness was hidden inside the same structure.

Its skull protected the left and right brain cases separately.

A small cartilage bridge—

no larger than half a fingernail—

connected the systems beneath the center.

Strike there—

coordination between both halves collapsed.

Neural network.

Sensory network.

Movement.

All gone.

The body remained almost untouched.

The ant—

fell.

Vikir stepped onto the disabled Soldier Ant.

Fired again.

If a Worker Ant slipped through—

his thigh dagger opened its neck.

Veteran movements.

Battlefield efficiency.

Nothing flashy—

unless one understood what was happening.

Arrow.

Arrow.

Arrow.

More ants died beneath each other's feet than by direct hits.

Compass-damaged Worker Ants ran backward.

Disabled Soldier Ants became walls.

The wave—

collapsed on itself.

Baby Madam—

meanwhile—

continued swallowing the paralytic poison.

Vikir advanced.

When arrows ran low—

he retrieved shafts from the front.

Climbed another Soldier Ant corpse.

Shot again.

Behind him—

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

All stared.

“…This isn't a dream, right?”

Phase 2—

passed.

Then Phase 3.

A stage difficult even for third-years.

Still—

Vikir kept moving.

Finally—

the timer reached zero.
