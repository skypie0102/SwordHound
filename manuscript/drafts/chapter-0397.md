# Chapter 397: Dead Man Walking (2)

Vikir remembered something Garam had once said.

Back when Vikir first entered Nouvelle Vague—

a prisoner had looked at the admission ceremony and panicked.

*What the hell is this?!*

*This isn’t admission!*

*This is basically an execution!*

Garam had answered calmly.

*No.*

*Nouvelle Vague has a separate execution ceremony.*

*And it is much worse than this.*

Now—

Vikir understood.

RIP—

A mouth opened in the middle of the abyss.

Its enormous jaws warped empty space itself.

Inside—

layer upon layer of strange space folded over itself.

Squares.

Triangles.

Circles.

Malformed geometries.

No order.

No balance.

Clouded eyes bulged like those of a drowned corpse.

And they reacted to the falling prey above.

Vikir stared.

*Brigadier General Flubber.*

*The most mysterious of the Five Wardens.*

People claimed its species was some kind of beastfolk—

possibly related to mollusks or snails.

But that was only a guess.

No one could identify its true class.

Order.

Family.

Or even why anyone had ever thought to call such a thing beastfolk.

It did not resemble a human.

Age—

unknown.

Sex—

unknown.

Species—

unknown.

Origin—

unknown.

Destination—

unknown.

Even the title **Brigadier General** was merely a human social rank imposed on an ancient creature.

Flubber had lived here before humanity discovered Nouvelle Vague.

No one knew what it was.

Or how it had come here.

The oldest prison records said only one thing.

**It has existed since the beginning.**

*Whatever it is—*

*it and BDISSEM are two of the prison’s core security systems.*

BDISSEM suppressed prisoners’ mana and physical strength.

Flubber—

the creature—

spread the strange mucus also called **Flubber** across the prison.

That self-restoring membrane kept the deep sea outside.

OOOoooo—

The creature made a strange sound.

A long, thick tongue emerged from the darkness.

From beneath its base—

dark-green mucus poured continuously.

The very substance coating Nouvelle Vague’s windows and openings.

And Sakkuth fell straight toward it.

“AAAAAAAH!”

At last—

he understood something Vikir had wondered about before.

The first stage of Nouvelle Vague admission.

The Blade Bridge.

People who fell.

People who—

on paper—

were still listed as alive.

This was where they went.

Sakkuth plunged into the abyss.

SPLASH.

Or—

perhaps **sank** was more accurate.

Flubber’s body consisted of black substance streaked with deep green.

Not hot.

Yet viscous and heavy—

like molten lead.

An endless mass of slime.

No visible bottom.

Like phlegm caught in the throat of some giant from primordial myth.

Sakkuth screamed.

“AAAAAAAAAAAAAH!”

The sound filled the enormous hollow.

Even veteran Nouvelle Vague guards covered their ears.

Bit their lips.

But one person did not move.

Vikir.

He stood at the cliff edge.

Expressionless.

Looking down.

Sakkuth—

already covered in slime—

looked up as his body began to dissolve.

He found Vikir.

“…YOU!”

“…YOU!”

Understanding finally came.

The rescue was a lie.

The jailbreak—

a lie.

There were no messengers hidden ten thousand meters beneath the sea.

No invisible allies waiting in the darkness.

This was Nouvelle Vague.

A place without dreams.

Hope.

Future.

Anything.

“VIIIIK—!”

Flubber rose around his mouth.

Sakkuth cursed upward.

A final surge of hatred entered him.

More energy than he had possessed moments before.

Murder.

Revenge.

Malice.

All meaningless.

Those things mattered only while one remained alive.

Inside Flubber’s maw—

Sakkuth was merely a fresher-than-usual piece of meat.

GRRRR—

The folds swallowed more of him.

Dragged him downward.

Sakkuth de Leviathan.

Into a bottomless black mire.

The final light he saw in the world—

framed Vikir’s expressionless face.

Then Vikir’s lips moved.

Sakkuth focused with the impossible concentration of a dying man.

And read them.

**Know whether you are the predator or the prey if you want to survive.**

The same lesson Vikir had given him at their first meeting.

“…!”

Sakkuth screamed with everything left in him.

Perhaps rage.

Perhaps a curse.

Perhaps—

at the very end—

something like repentance.

It never reached anyone.

GRRRR—

The sound swallowed him.

Then he vanished beneath the black surface.

Whether Sakkuth found the peace people wished upon the dead—

nobody knew.

One thing was certain.

He would never escape the abyss.

Ever.

◆◆◆

An eerie silence remained.

Even Kirko—

usually fearless—

was sweating.

“…Brigadier General Flubber’s executions are always horrifying.”

Vikir returned from the cliff.

Nodded.

*Flubber.*

*It doesn’t seem intelligent.*

*But during a jailbreak—*

*I have no idea what kind of variable it could become.*

A bizarre life-form extending throughout Nouvelle Vague.

Maintaining.

Repairing.

Unofficially—

possibly the strongest monster inside the prison.

Vikir had partly used Sakkuth’s execution to see this unknown Warden for himself.

Now—

he needed to decide whether Flubber should be avoided.

Controlled.

Ignored.

Or somehow removed.

For the first time since entering Nouvelle Vague—

Vikir had encountered a variable he could not calculate.

Then—

“Captain Garam.”

Vikir turned.

Lieutenant Colonel Bastille stood behind him.

Several adjutants in uniform waited nearby.

“You called for me, Lieutenant Colonel.”

Vikir saluted.

Bastille smiled.

“Yes.”

“One troublesome prisoner is finally gone.”

Bastille had always been sensitive to riots.

Disturbances damaged his performance evaluations.

And Sakkuth—

volatile—

violent—

constantly causing incidents—

had become one of his worst headaches.

“…Executing prisoners is not simple.”

“The moment a death sentence becomes known—

they often resist.”

“Riot.”

“Attempt escape.”

“Plan attacks.”

“And the guards who conduct executions may suffer afterward as well.”

“I’m glad this one ended without trouble.”

“Exactly.”

“A Level Eight prisoner calmly accepted his execution.”

“And the execution officer appears to have suffered no meaningful aftereffects.”

“…Correct?”

“Yes.”

Bastille’s smile widened.

“You kept a Level Eight prisoner quiet right until the end.”

“You handled that execution alone.”

“You never faltered.”

“….”

“Everything about this was your achievement.”

“What a waste it would be—

not to reward someone like you.”

“You remind me of myself when I was young.”

Vikir wanted the speech to end.

So he said something he would rarely say.

“I will follow you for the rest of my life, Lieutenant Colonel.”

Bastille beamed.

A man talented at building factions—

he had apparently decided Garam belonged to his line.

“Good.”

“I am not like those incompetent rulers and commanders whose names became stains on history.”

“Loyal and capable subordinates deserve rewards.”

“…?”

Vikir looked mildly puzzled.

Bastille held out a black box.

“I hope you like it.”

“Anything bestowed by you would be an honor.”

“Hahaha!”

“Open it now.”

Bastille looked pleased.

As though the gift itself proved his generosity.

Under the eyes of Kirko—

and every nearby guard—

Vikir opened the box.

Click.

“…!”

Silver light rose from inside.

Everyone’s eyes widened.
