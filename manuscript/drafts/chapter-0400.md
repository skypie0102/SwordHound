“Hehehehehe.”

“What are you doing?”

The low voice echoed through the cavern.

Vikir studied the man ahead.

Every patch of skin visible beneath the uniform—

black.

Smooth.

Hairless.

The muscle arrangement was subtly wrong for a human body.

His face lacked eyelids.

Nose.

Lips.

Pale eyeballs sat exposed in raw-looking sockets.

Sharp teeth showed constantly.

BUBBLE.

BUBBLE.

The fibers making up his body moved independently—

like boiling tar.

**Lieutenant Colonel Black Tongue.**

Lowest formal rank among the Five Wardens.

Yet in seniority—

older than almost all of them.

Only Flubber predated him.

*He was already Lieutenant Colonel when D'Ordume and Souaré were Second Lieutenants.*

Vikir evaluated him.

Strength—

roughly estimable.

Intent—

not.

Cruelty—

not.

What Black Tongue truly wanted—

not.

So Vikir chose the safest response.

Ordinary fear.

“Forgive us.”

“We damaged one of the leeches by accident.”

“I was startled.”

“My heart is still racing.”

He even let a little sweat show.

A deliberate display of weakness.

Like an old hero hiding extraordinary ability by pretending to fear thunder.

It worked.

The oppressive aura around Black Tongue softened—

slightly.

“Hehehe.”

“So you’re Captain Garam—

no.”

“Major now.”

“You don’t look as fearless as the rumors say.”

“I’ve been overpraised.”

“That is stressful.”

“No.”

“The rumors make you sound like a monster.”

“It’s almost pleasant to see something human in you.”

“….”

“Sit.”

“Anywhere comfortable.”

“And don’t worry about the leeches.”

“They’re troublesome to raise.”

“I’ve basically thrown these away.”

“Soft, though.”

“I use them as cushions sometimes.”

Black Tongue turned.

The muscles across his back squirmed.

Vikir looked closer.

They were leeches.

Tiny.

Tough.

Packed over every part of Black Tongue’s body like individual muscle fibers.

A full-body suit of parasites.

*So this is why people say he’s human—

but nobody treats him like one.*

A person covered every hour of every day in leeches—

without a gap—

hardly looked human.

Black Tongue sat on one of the swollen parasites.

SQUISH.

Yellow-green fluid leaked from its mouth.

Something inside moaned.

“….”

“….”

Vikir and Kirko said nothing.

*There’s probably a person inside that cushion too.*

Kirko’s expression hardened.

Vikir glanced at the floor.

*Sit if you want.*

*Or stay standing.*

Kirko chose the parasite.

SQUISH.

It trembled beneath her.

Thick bloody liquid leaked out.

No human moan.

Kirko’s shoulders loosened by a fraction.

Black Tongue noticed.

“Hehehe.”

“Need to fart?”

“….”

“Joke.”

“Just a joke.”

“You’re too tense.”

“Trying to loosen you up.”

His facial muscles—

or the parasites replacing them—

shifted into something resembling a smile.

“All right.”

“Interrogation.”

“Simple questions.”

“Don’t stiffen up.”

“Poor blood flow is unhealthy.”

“And bad for flavor.”

“….”

“Hehehe.”

“Another joke.”

“Young people these days are so serious.”

Black Tongue leaned closer.

“I suppose I am frightening.”

“My appearance.”

“My personality.”

“But ordinary guards almost never meet me.”

“Some serve here for decades without seeing my face.”

“….”

“I mostly meet the same kind of people.”

“Those who steal supplies.”

“Break military discipline.”

“Leave posts without permission.”

“Insult superiors.”

“Bad people.”

“If you live honestly—

you have no reason to fear me.”

Then came a long lecture.

Do not judge by appearances.

Do not commit crimes.

Live uprightly.

Eventually—

Black Tongue opened Garam’s investigation file.

“Hm.”

“Commoner birth.”

“Ordinary poor family.”

“Career slightly below average.”

“And then…”

He narrowed his eyes.

“Sudden improvement.”

“When did it begin?”

A finger traced the file.

“After the Night Hound escape incident.”

“Interesting.”

“Something major happened?”

“Awakening?”

“Since then—

your performance shoots upward.”

“As though you became someone else.”

Vikir remained still.

“You suppress a Level Eight riot.”

“Receive exceptional promotions.”

“Lieutenant Colonel Bastille clearly favors you.”

“Distant relative?”

The black tongue slid across his teeth.

His gaze became predatory.

Waiting for a crack.

Vikir gave him one—

the harmless kind.

“Yes.”

“I’m from the surface.”

“I recently received a telegram.”

“My parents and siblings died.”

“The village too.”

Black Tongue tilted his head.

“Oh?”

“What happened?”

“They say demons appeared.”

“My hometown burned.”

“I received only a short message.”

“I don’t know the details.”

“Hm.”

“Communication with the surface is terrible down here.”

“That must be frustrating.”

Black Tongue looked back at the file.

“So that became the turning point?”

“Yes.”

That much—

was plausible.

Vikir had survived the Age of Destruction.

His ability to suppress mana—

strength—

presence—

had deceived monsters and Sword Masters.

Even Hugo had failed to see through him.

Black Tongue had no reason to succeed.

“Hm.”

“Yes.”

“If that happened to me—

maybe I’d change too.”

Black Tongue scribbled notes.

“The surface has apparently become chaos these last few months.”

“Demons.”

“Monsters.”

“Dungeons.”

“Gates.”

“That sort of thing.”

“That’s why Orca—

D'Ordume—

Souaré—

all went up.”

“More prisoners than ever.”

“I don’t know the details.”

“I’m stuck down here.”

He continued marking the file.

Then shut it.

“Done.”

“You’re clean.”

“Fresh.”

“No issue.”

“If the inspection is finished—

I’ll return to work.”

Vikir stood.

Black Tongue smiled.

“What?”

“You’re leaving before I dismiss you?”

“I’m a Lieutenant Colonel.”

“Bold.”

“I have a lot of work.”

“Forgive me.”

“Sit.”

Vikir stopped.

“….”

“Hehehe.”

“Why the frightening face?”

“Relax.”

Then Black Tongue waved.

Not at Vikir.

Past him.

“You may go.”

His eyes were fixed on Kirko.

“Hehehehehe.”

“Ah.”

“It has been a very long time—

hasn’t it?”
