# Chapter 35: Slave Auction (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/035.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 35 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. The Chinese heading is anomalous (`拍卖场之花 (1)`), but Chapters 36–37 and the recovered English sequence establish this as **Slave Auction (1)**. Proper nouns remain reveal-chronology scoped: the tribal girl and Baron Gambino's secretary are not named before the Chinese source names them.

Night.

The outskirts of Underdog City.

Luxury carriages crowded a remote clearing that normally saw no visitors.

A vast black tent covered the entire lot.

Masked nobles and wealthy merchants strode inside with their heads held high.

Tonight was the day of a slave auction.

Naturally, it was not one that had been reported to the Baskerville Clan.

Every slave displayed here was therefore unregistered merchandise.

Many were people who never should have been eligible to be bought or sold in the first place.

Tribespeople who had lost their homes.

Nobles carried off from distant lands.

Commoners who had simply disappeared one day.

Some were kept in chains.

Some had been broken with drugs and repeated abuse until the will to flee or report their captors had vanished.

And some were still in the process of being turned into that kind of merchandise.

“Welcome, welcome! A heartfelt welcome to tonight's ‘Freak Show’! Come in, come in!”

A clown dressed like a pierrot greeted the arriving guests.

Before long, a middle-aged man approached him.

He had a long goatee and a somewhat comical face, but at the moment he looked anything but relaxed. He kept glancing around nervously.

The clown inspected his identification.

“Hmm, hmm. Mr. Chihuahua Montblanc. Ah, you're here on behalf of the Montblanc Family. That splendid signature really is their trademark—no one else could possibly imitate it... Hm?”

The clown had been about to let him through, but stopped.

Chihuahua visibly stiffened.

The clown narrowed his eyes at the document.

“It says here that you didn't come to buy slaves... You came to sell one?”

“Ah, yes. That's right. I'm here to sell.”

“What are you selling?”

“Hmm? Oh, I have him tied up over there for now. There's only one. A young boy, so he doesn't take up much space.”

The clown smiled and bowed again.

“I see. The Montblanc gentlemen have always been excellent customers who purchase slaves in bulk. Since you've come to sell this time, I wondered whether one of your previous purchases had disappointed you.”

Chihuahua dabbed the cold sweat from his forehead with a handkerchief and shook his head.

“No. I've always been satisfied with the quality of the slaves purchased here.”

“Of course. We sell only carefully selected merchandise. People stripped of family and homeland, secretly kidnapped, then thoroughly broken until they obey absolutely and no longer think of running away or resisting. Quality guaranteed.”

The clown bowed deeply once more.

“Welcome to the Freak Show.”

◆◆◆

A peculiar tension filled the auction hall.

Masked men and women sat around a central stage beneath a haze of smoke.

Many of the wealthy people who drove Underdog City's underground economy were gathered here.

A noblewoman wearing a butterfly mask stared greedily at a naked tribesman displayed onstage.

A gentleman in a bat mask appraised a slave who had once been a mercenary.

There was no meaningful distinction between the things brought onto the auction platform.

Ancient jars.

Famous works of art.

Sharp swords.

Rare animals and monsters.

Human beings branded as slaves.

To the people gathered here, the categories hardly mattered.

They were accustomed to treating objects, beasts, and people as things of equal value.

The masked guests covered their mouths with fans as they chatted.

“I hear it's fashionable in the Imperial Capital these days to collect unusually shaped slaves and put them on display.”

“I don't care for that trend. A slave only needs to be beautiful.”

“Shouldn't a slave either work well or fight well?”

“Hahaha! Personally, I enjoy a tragic history. Fallen nobles, kidnapped aristocrats... Those are much more interesting.”

“Whatever the case, I hope there's something useful tonight.”

Most eyes remained fixed on the slaves.

The clown serving as auctioneer stepped onto the stage and raised his voice.

“Ladies and gentlemen! This sword and shield are clearly relics of an ancient civilization! Thirty million gold has been offered! Anyone higher? No? Sold! Congratulations to the gentleman in the snake mask! And now, feast your eyes on this crown set with magnificent jewels! Surely it once rested on the head of some king from a fallen kingdom—”

Then the audience's attention snapped elsewhere.

The next lot was not human.

Even so, it seized every eye in the room.

A black buffalo packed with muscle.

Immense horns.

A massive body.

And three crimson eyes.

It was an enormous monster native to the mountains.

Yet no one fled when it appeared onstage.

The reason was simple.

Murcielago was already dead.

The auctioneer shouted.

“Ladies and gentlemen! This precious A-rank monster was hunted by Baskerville knights and secretly diverted here! Hide, flesh, bones, organs—there isn't a single part that goes to waste!”

Nineteen wounds marked the Infernal Buffalo's body.

It appeared to have died only after all nineteen hearts had been destroyed.

The price of the carcass immediately surged.

“One hundred million gold!”

“One hundred twenty million!”

“One hundred forty million!”

“One hundred ninety million!”

“Two hundred million!”

“Two hundred fifty million!”

The entire hall boiled with excitement even though the item was not a human slave.

After selling the Infernal Buffalo for a handsome sum, the auctioneer rode the momentum into the main event.

“Ladies and gentlemen! From here on, we auction only humans and humanoid races!”

One person after another was brought onto the stage.

A tribesman whose empty eyes suggested he had already surrendered everything.

A kidnapped noblewoman sobbing and begging to be sent home.

A terrified commoner boy who still seemed unable to understand where he had been taken.

A fallen noble girl screaming that she would rather die than endure any more.

“One hundred million!”

“Two hundred million!”

“Two hundred fifty million!”

“Add another seventy million!”

“That woman is mine! Nobody touch her!”

“Don't make me laugh. Bring more money.”

The more the captives cried, begged, and despaired, the more frenzied the bidders became.

Then the auctioneer, who had been buoyed by the brisk sales, suddenly looked confused.

“...What? Huh? You're putting this one up too? Really? Fine. I'll try.”

He seemed to be arguing with the men backstage.

A moment later, he forced his expression back into a smile.

“Ladies and gentlemen! The next item is unlike the others! A very distinctive charm—and plenty of room left for training!”

In other words, even the slavers had failed to tame this one.

After doing his best to dress up the truth, the auctioneer had the slave brought to center stage.

Unlike the previous captives, this one arrived inside a sturdy iron cage.

“GRAAAAH!”

A continuous, feral roar erupted from within.

A young tribeswoman thrashed madly inside the cage.

She looked around seventeen.

Black hair streaked with silver.

Sharp, triangular ears.

Her face looked black at first glance, but only because it had been smeared with ash. Beneath it, her skin appeared light brown.

A spiked collar encircled her neck.

The animal hide draped over her body had been reduced to tatters.

What showed through the torn clothing was lean and powerful, but also covered in scars.

Combined with her savage snarling, the sight discouraged every bidder in the room.

She shouted something in a language no one around the stage understood.

Worse, the smell of wild animals and a body left unwashed for many days hung over the platform.

Several noblewomen frowned and turned their heads away.

“...Who is going to buy this?”

Even the auctioneer muttered under his breath.

Still, he had merchandise to move.

He tried again.

“Ladies and gentlemen! The traders say they found her deep in the jungle! Don't miss your chance! Imagine the fun of breaking in a slave this fierce! Use her in bed or throw her into a gladiator pit! Show us your skill at taming slaves! Opening bid—five million gold!”

Silence.

Not one paddle rose.

Normally someone would at least offer the minimum to preserve the auctioneer's dignity and keep the mood alive.

Not this time.

“Fine! Lowering the opening bid! Three million! Anyone for three million?”

Silence.

“Two million! Two million! I'm practically giving her away!”

Nothing.

“All right! One million! Take her for one million! Boil her for meat if that's all she's good for—I'm selling her at flesh value!”

Still no one bid.

At last, the auctioneer clicked his tongue and waved dismissively.

“Forget it. She won't sell. I told you only to bring guaranteed merchandise. Drag the cage away! Do better next time.”

The slavers came onto the stage and began hauling the iron cage backstage.

Even then, the girl fought.

She lunged through the bars and bit off one trader's finger.

The hall erupted into fresh chaos.

The organizers needed something dramatic to seize control of the room again.

The auctioneer decided it was time for the night's true centerpiece.

“Ladies and gentlemen! Attention! All eyes on the stage! What I consider tonight's finest lot is finally here!”

A boy walked onto the platform.

His wrists were shackled, and heavy chains hung from them.

His face looked barely fifteen.

Yet the weight of the restraints did nothing to disturb his stride.

The audience murmured at the quiet confidence in every step.

When he reached the center of the stage, torchlight poured down over his face.

His features came fully into view.

The entire hall fell silent.

“...Oh my.”

The noblewomen—and more than a few middle-aged men—stared at him in open fascination.
