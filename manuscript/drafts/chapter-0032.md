# Chapter 32: The Social Club (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/032.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 32 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. **Burning Suspension** follows current indexed Fandom evidence; local-house names and other chapter-specific terms remain source-controlled where no dedicated canonical entry is available.

A towering hotel stood in the heart of Underdog City.

On its sixty-ninth and seventieth floors was an enormous club.

**Burning Suspension.**

It was the most exclusive social club in Underdog City, a place where the city's richest and most powerful young people gathered to burn through their youth.

Bottles of top-shelf champagne worth one hundred million gold apiece were opened one after another while lively music thundered day and night.

Unknown varieties of hookah filled the rooms with thick smoke, and brilliant lights dyed the haze every color imaginable.

The atmosphere was so lavish and extravagant that it could have rivaled a ball at the imperial palace.

“Come on! Stack them!”

“Bring all of them over!”

“Let's raise the tower again today!”

Deep inside the club was a private room available only to VVIPs.

Six hours there cost ten million gold.

Seven young masters were drinking inside.

Bottle after bottle of champagne worth more than one hundred million gold was carried in on ice.

At the center of a vast table, champagne glasses were being stacked into a pyramid.

The bottom level held one hundred glasses in a ten-by-ten square.

Above them came eighty-one glasses in a nine-by-nine square.

Then sixty-four in an eight-by-eight square.

Above that came forty-nine.

Then thirty-six.

Twenty-five.

Sixteen.

Nine.

Four.

Finally, one last glass was placed at the very top.

The champagne tower was complete.

The seven young masters laughed as they poured an entire bottle of one-hundred-million-gold **Donquixote Perignon 666** into the highest glass.

Glug, glug, glug...

Once the top glass filled, champagne overflowed into those beneath it.

Whenever a bottle emptied, they tossed it aside without hesitation and demanded another.

The liquor cascaded downward level by level until all **385 glasses** in the tower had been filled.

One of the young masters laughed.

“Let the servants drink whatever spilled onto the table.”

The waiters standing near the door rushed forward at once.

“Thank you, young masters!”

“Thanks to you, even people like us get to taste liquor this expensive.”

“We'll serve you with everything we've got today!”

Smiling ingratiatingly, the waiters bent over and licked the champagne dripping across the tabletop.

The young masters laughed and scattered gold coins at their faces.

“Now *that's* the trickle-down effect.”

Champagne poured from the highest glass to the lowest, filled every cup beneath it, and soaked the table besides.

The seven men roared with laughter at the sight.

They were the core members of the local **Youth Self-Governance Committee**.

Officially, it was merely a civilian association made up of second- and third-generation heirs from old local landowning and merchant families.

In practice, their influence over Underdog society could not be ignored.

Their families possessed enough wealth and local power to perform lower-level public functions, and their noble status placed them far above ordinary citizens.

Because those houses had lived in the region for generations, they knew the surrounding territory intimately.

The Baskerville Clan therefore delegated some minor authority to them and allowed them to handle local matters on their own.

In return, they supplied Baskerville with information about the region and paid tribute and taxes, receiving a degree of autonomy in exchange.

Historically, however, the young masters gathered here were descendants of houses that had lost struggles for power in the imperial center and been driven out to the frontier.

The Baskerville Clan was different. It had come west under a special imperial mandate to expand the border.

Compared with Baskerville, the local houses were nothing extraordinary.

The young men themselves did not see it that way.

“Better to be the head of a snake than the tail of a dragon.”

“It's more fun out here where the imperial family can't stick its nose into everything.”

“Exactly. I went to a club in the Imperial Capital last time, and this place was even more extravagant.”

“Besides, we've got *those Baskervilles* protecting us. How much safer can you get?”

“Heh heh heh. At this point, aren't the Baskervilles basically our hunting dogs?”

They were absurdly sheltered young men who understood nothing of the wider world.

While Baskerville focused on expanding the frontier, these people had rotted from within.

But where did the money funding this extravagance actually come from?

The legitimate income of the local houses was not especially high.

At most, they collected reservoir-water charges from irrigated fields, road-use fees, tolls, and proceeds from livestock sales.

Their ordinary expenses were similarly modest: wages for stewards, servants, and serfs, along with the routine cost of maintaining their estates.

Neither income nor expenditure should have supported anything like Burning Suspension.

They had another source of revenue.

**Illegal slave auctions.**

People were kidnapped or imprisoned without legal sanction, then sold as slaves.

Baskerville's recent frontier expansion had displaced more and more tribal peoples from their homes.

The local houses exploited that chaos by deceiving or forcibly abducting those survivors and trafficking them in secret.

In simple terms, they rode on Baskerville's labor and scavenged the scraps.

There were no taxes to pay, and the supply of victims remained steady.

The black money accumulated rapidly.

The young masters burned through those illicit profits as quickly as they could.

They could not safely deposit the money in legitimate banks anyway.

“The champagne tower's full! Bring the girls in! Pick the obedient ones!”

They sent the waiters out for a moment.

Then the seven began whispering among themselves.

“By the way, it's great that the uptight **Messinadnaro Family** is gone. We can finally enjoy ourselves without anyone nagging us.”

“That's how it should be. We're spending our own money.”

“Framing them and driving them out was the right call, wasn't it?”

“Let's never let people like that into our circle again.”

They lounged back with bubbling hookahs between their lips.

One of them suddenly raised his head.

“Speaking of which, have you heard about the new Deputy Magistrate?”

“Yeah. We already submitted a request for an audience. His middle name is **Van**.”

“Hm. Van? Then he's collateral blood or an illegitimate son, right?”

“He should consider it an honor that *we* invited him.”

“Heh heh. He'll come running in a panic like all the others.”

Whenever a new Deputy Magistrate arrived, the group performed what they liked to call **training the rookie official**.

There was nothing complicated about it.

They simply threw a party so dazzling and extravagant that the new official lost himself in it.

The message was obvious.

*If you want to keep enjoying places like this with us, you'll do what we say.*

It was no different from slipping a collar around a dog's neck.

The young masters snickered.

“His name was Vikir, wasn't it? Anyone heard of him?”

“No. First time I've heard the name.”

“He's fifteen. Apparently this is his first time being sent away from the main family.”

“What? He's completely a kid.”

“Definitely a kid. I heard he threw a huge drinking party as soon as he got to City Hall. Sounds like he's pretty debauched already.”

“Heh heh heh. Then he'll fit right in with us.”

One of the young masters suddenly grinned as an idea occurred to him.

“Why don't we haze him while we're at it?”

He raised one finger and explained.

“We throw a huge party and invite him. Bring in the best liquor and the best women. We all have a great time.”

“And then?”

“After the party ends, we stick him with the bill.”

The room exploded with laughter.

“Good. That's hilarious.”

“I want to see his face when he realizes how much it costs.”

“He'll learn what it means when people spend this kind of money entertaining him. Otherwise he'll start taking it for granted.”

“A fifteen-year-old official won't have that kind of money. And if he asks his family for it, he'll get chewed out.”

“Then after he panics, we tell him it was a joke and cover the bill ourselves.”

They continued discussing how best to embarrass and discipline the newly appointed Deputy Magistrate.

Just then—

“Young masters! The women are here!”

A waiter opened the door with a broad grin.

A line of women soon entered the room, staring around in surprise at the luxury.

Some were familiar faces.

The young masters waved cheerfully.

“Hey, you're back again?”

“You came back after everything that happened last time?”

“Throw her out. We don't like her.”

“Why? I think she's pretty.”

“Hey! Sit over here this time!”

The women entered one by one.

And then—

Something strange slipped in behind them.

A boy who looked barely into adolescence.

He walked naturally behind the line of women and into the room.

His manner was so casual that none of the waiters thought to stop him.

The seven young masters were busy examining the women and making crude jokes when they finally noticed him.

“Who's this kid? Did someone order a male attendant? He's pretty cute, at least.”

“What? Does one of you know him?”

“No.”

The young masters, the waiters, and the women all looked equally confused.

How had this boy simply followed them inside?

One of the waiters smacked his forehead.

“He walked in so confidently that nobody even questioned it. You little brat! How dare you come in here—!”

The waiter reached for the boy's hair.

He never touched it.

**Crack.**

Something thick and solid snapped.

The waiter looked down and discovered that his wrist was now bent in a direction it was never meant to face.

“AAAAAGH!?”

The boy had broken a grown man's arm with nothing but grip strength.

“What the hell is this kid!?”

Several other waiters rushed him together.

Less than a second later, every one of them was on the floor.

The seven young masters stiffened.

“Who are you? You come in here looking for trouble? Do you even know who we are?”

“I know.”

The boy's voice contained no emotion.

“You're the idiots from the Youth Committee.”

No respect.

No fear.

No servility.

Not even contempt.

Nothing at all.

The young masters stared at him.

A moment later, they burst into laughter.

“That's right. We're the idiots from the Youth Committee.”

“So he knows who we are.”

“Then all that's left is for us to learn who *he* is.”

“Kid. Who are you?”

They seemed entertained by the unexpected interruption.

Their amusement vanished with the boy's answer.

“Vikir.”

The newly appointed Deputy Magistrate.

**Vikir Van Baskerville** had come personally.

The young masters quietly lowered the feet they had been resting on the table.

Then one of them asked hesitantly,

“D-Deputy Magistrate? Why are you here...?”

“You called for me.”

The seven stared blankly again.

Then—

“Hahahaha! Deputy Magistrate, you're refreshingly direct! We didn't expect you so quickly!”

The atmosphere was anything but friendly.

The young masters had just been insulted in front of their servants and guests, and their pride was already wounded.

*Should we start training the rookie now?*

*Fine. Let him help pay for the alcohol.*

*Sit him down, entertain him properly, and when the party's over, make him cover the bill...*

Their plan never got that far.

**Tap.**

Vikir placed one hand on the table.

Then—

Sssssssss...

The dark crimson aura unique to Baskerville slowly rose around him.

Mana flowed from Vikir's body, through his hand, and into the table.

A vicious resonance spread through the wood.

A violent vibration followed.

Grrrrrrrrrrrr...

The champagne on the table began to boil.

Then the enormous tower changed.

**Crack!**

The single glass at the very top shattered as though it had exploded from within.

Glass fragments and foaming champagne flashed through the air.

**Clink!**

**Clink! Clink!**

**Clink! Clink! Clink!**

The four glasses on the level beneath it burst.

Then the nine below them.

Sixteen.

Twenty-five.

Thirty-six.

Forty-nine.

Sixty-four.

Eighty-one.

One hundred.

The champagne tower collapsed.

Not from the bottom upward.

From the top down, one level at a time.

Crash—!

Crash—!

Crash—!

Countless shards of glass and champagne foam poured across the VVIP room like a torrential rainstorm.

The seven young masters stood drenched beneath it.

They managed only one response.

“...We'll pay for the drinks.”
