The sky was beginning to pale.

Following the order Hugo had given at the Great Banquet the night before, Vikir returned to the main residence first thing in the morning.

“Enroll in the Academy.”

Hugo remained as blunt as ever.

An official document slid across the desk toward Vikir.

It was a family notice announcing recruitment for the Academy's new semester.

> **Colosseo Academy — Recruitment Notice for the 20th Class**
>
> **Application period:** Imperial Year 19, November 4–November 26  
> **Program:** 8 semesters (4 years)  
> **Entrance date:** Imperial Year 20, January 1

Colosseo Academy.

A vast collective educational institution established by the Rock Empire.

More than seven thousand students studied there at any given time, and when graduates still attached to the Academy were included, the number exceeded twenty thousand.

Its faculty and staff alone numbered roughly four thousand.

It was one of the highest institutions of learning in human history.

Every year, it admitted young people from the Seven Great Families that supported the Empire, along with heirs and talents from countless other houses.

For the young nobles of the Empire, admission itself was considered a lifelong honor.

And with reason.

Academy graduates filled elite positions throughout the Imperial Family and every major field of society.

Emperors of the Rock Empire, heads of the Seven Great Families, billionaires, leaders of enormous guilds, famous scholars, politicians, jurists, merchants, cultural figures, journalists, academics, athletes—

the list went on.

Its alumni network had no equal within the Empire.

Among all social organizations, few possessed comparable influence.

“…”

Vikir quietly studied the document.

During their four years at the Academy, students were officially judged without regard to family background.

Admission and graduation were decided by ability.

There were exceptions, but most students entered around twenty and graduated around twenty-four.

After graduation, they could remain in the Imperial Capital and serve the Empire or return to their original families.

Hugo wanted Vikir to return to Baskerville after graduating.

His eldest son, Osiris, had taken the same path.

Vikir intended to do so as well.

“I'll go.”

He accepted without hesitation.

Before regression, Vikir had also been to Colosseo Academy.

*…Of course, not as a freshman.*

He had gone as a hound attached to incoming students—guarding them, running errands, and handling whatever work was assigned to him.

The freshmen he had followed at the time were none other than the triplets.

Highbro.

Middlebro.

Lowbro.

Hugo nodded.

“Are there any brothers you want to take with you?”

Colosseo reserved a fixed number of places for Baskerville.

It was effectively a guaranteed family quota.

Given Baskerville's name and influence, that was hardly surprising.

Vikir answered immediately.

“I'd like to go with Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro.”

He had once given Hugo the same answer before regression.

Hugo looked mildly surprised and stroked his chin.

Then he nodded.

“Among your brothers, they're useful enough.”

But usefulness was not the only reason Vikir wanted to take the triplets.

His thoughts naturally returned to what had happened after the banquet the night before.

*…That was unexpected.*

On the way to his room, the triplets had come to see him.

Shing—

With strange determination on their faces, all three drew their swords and blocked Vikir's path.

For a moment, Vikir had considered settling everything then and there.

He could erase the troublesome witnesses—

and bury what they had seen in the Depths two years ago.

But then—

Thud.

All three brothers dropped to one knee before him.

Their heads bowed.

Each sword was turned inward, its tip resting against the top of its owner's right foot while the hilt faced Vikir.

If Vikir reached out and pressed down, the blade would pierce straight through the knight's own foot.

It was an act of placing one's life in another person's hands.

For a hound, it was the equivalent of rolling onto its back and exposing its belly.

“What are you doing?”

Vikir had asked, genuinely puzzled.

Why were they making the oath used by Imperial knights when they met the master they had chosen for life?

The triplets answered.

“We want to repay you for saving our lives.”

“We do.”

“We do.”

Even now, the answer remained unexpected.

The triplets had feared Vikir ever since he beat them when they were nine.

That fear became acknowledgment after he hunted Cerberus.

It became respect after he killed a troll alone.

And then came Madam Eight-Legs.

When Vikir released the aura of a Sword Graduator before a colossal monster that seemed like death itself—

their feelings changed again.

Awe.

Reverence.

Two sides of the same emotion felt by people who encountered a genius so far beyond them that they could never hope to catch up.

The triplets had been carried away on the backs of their guardian knights that day.

They had seen their own helplessness.

And they had seen Vikir's power blazing like the sun.

On the day they barely escaped with their lives, the three gathered and made a vow.

“From this day forward, we are Vikir's Trident.”

They would repay the life-debt they owed him.

From that point on, the triplets never revealed anything about Vikir.

They kept silent about the impossible strength he had shown two years earlier.

And when Vikir returned after two years, they never pointed out that the strength he publicly displayed seemed not to have advanced at all.

They knew better.

They knew Vikir had already reached the Intermediate Sword Graduator level two years ago.

And they knew he had grown far stronger since.

Even so, they protected their chosen master's secret.

“…”

Vikir silently rubbed his chin.

The three brothers had been born as hounds.

Hounds needed a master to obey.

Vikir already knew their ability and loyalty from his previous life.

Before regression, these same three had been among those who pressured and drove him relentlessly.

Now they had chosen to kneel before him.

Vikir ended the recollection and looked back at Hugo.

“How many attendants can I bring?”

“As many as you like.”

Hugo nodded readily.

Vikir nodded in return.

Before regression, he had lived inside the Academy as an attendant to incoming students.

Baskerville attendants rotated through Colosseo by semester, supporting whichever members of the family had enrolled.

Their “support” amounted to guard duty and errands.

A typical knight-squire's role.

Vikir had entered the Academy as an attendant to Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro.

Being able to listen in on lessons had been useful.

Almost everything else had been dull, exhausting work.

Laundry.

Preparing materials for practical evaluations.

Memorizing schedules and meal plans.

Guard duty.

Settling disputes.

Delivering love letters.

*I took plenty of abuse back then.*

Whenever Vikir returned to the main house, he often came back from some brutal assignment with fresh scars across his face or walking with a limp.

The pampered sons and daughters of noble families at the Academy had looked down on the battered attendant whenever he appeared.

*It doesn't matter.*

*I'm not going to the Academy to learn.*

Vikir had two reasons for entering Colosseo.

First—

to escape Hugo's constant observation and increase his own power.

Second—

to deal with the names on the “blacklist” he had prepared before regression.

It might finally be time to put on the long-sealed mask of the Night Hound again.

Then Hugo spoke.

“That's all. You may leave.”

Having said everything he intended to say, Hugo dismissed him.

But Vikir still had one matter to settle.

A transaction.

Instead of leaving, he raised his head.

“Father. There's something I'd like to ask.”

“…?”

Vikir rarely asked Hugo for anything.

That alone caught Hugo's interest.

Vikir continued.

“I understand that my disappearance two years ago brought the main family enormous diplomatic and trade gains in its dealings with Morgue.”

Hugo nodded.

“And you said I would be rewarded for it.”

“Yes. I said you would receive an appropriate reward. We're still deciding what that should be.”

Vikir's contribution had been so large that an ordinary reward could not properly match it.

Hugo had still been considering the matter.

Vikir had simply brought it up first.

“I want that reward now.”

“Oh?”

Hugo's eyes sharpened with interest.

“Is there something in particular you want?”

It was rare for Vikir to volunteer a desire.

Hugo watched him closely.

Vikir spoke.

“What I want is for you to answer one question honestly.”

“…?”

Hugo's brow tightened.

Depending on the question, that could be an unpleasant request.

“What is it?”

“Then I'll take that as your agreement.”

Vikir met Hugo's eyes.

His next words were even more direct than Hugo's usual manner.

“What was your relationship with your late first wife, Lady Roxana?”

Hugo's entire body went rigid.

It was the reaction of a dragon whose most forbidden scale had been touched.

Shock.

Agitation.

Vikir had never seen Hugo react that way before.

“…What are you trying to do?”

Hugo's voice became glacial.

A terrifying chill spread from him.

Vikir remained calm.

All the goodwill and familiarity he had painstakingly built with Hugo seemed to be collapsing by the second.

He did not flinch.

“Get out.”

“…”

“Forget the Academy. Your admission is canceled.”

Hugo waved him away.

In all his life, he had almost never reversed a decision because of emotion.

Now he had done exactly that.

Vikir kept the same steady pace.

“Then I'll consider my Academy admission canceled.”

“…”

“But the gains the family received from my disappearance two years ago cannot be canceled.”

The meaning was obvious.

*Answer the question.*

Hugo finally lost his temper.

“What answer are you trying to hear?!”

“The literal one.”

Vikir's tone did not change.

“What did you think of Lady Roxana?”

Pomeranian's bright, smiling face passed through his mind.

What would Hugo do if he learned he had a granddaughter?

Would he fly into a rage?

It seemed entirely possible.

*He's a cold-blooded man without mercy.*

His first wife had entered his life through marriage.

Their eldest daughter had been kidnapped by the tribes of the Depths.

And now that daughter had left behind a granddaughter of mixed tribal blood.

Vikir had already imagined more than 365 possible reactions Hugo might have to Pomeranian's existence.

For every one of them, he had prepared measures intended to protect both himself and Pomeranian.

And yet—

Hugo gave an answer that appeared in none of those scenarios.

“She was the only woman I ever loved in my entire life.”
