# Chapter 22: Camus Morgue (3)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/022.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 22 is an aligned secondary witness only. **Camus Morgue** and her source-revealed future title **Ironblood Empress** are normalized from current Fandom evidence; no later wiki-only biography is imported.

“Does the Morgue Clan not teach its children that they shouldn't covet what belongs to someone else?”

Vikir's first words upon entering the chamber reversed the atmosphere once again.

Adolf's faintly amused expression stiffened.

Camus glared at Vikir.

Hugo, on the other hand, suddenly wore the subtle look Adolf had been enjoying until now.

“…”

“…”

Two prodigies with enormous pride faced one another in the middle of the room.

The once-in-a-century talents of two great clans.

Vikir studied Camus Morgue carefully.

Fiery red hair.

Ruby-colored eyes.

A straight little nose, round cheeks, a scattering of freckles, and the unmistakable softness of an eight-year-old face.

‘So this is the Camus Morgue I knew.’

For a moment, the child before him overlapped with the woman from his memories.

Remove the baby fat.

Let the childishness fade.

Add years of experience.

Then the familiar face emerged.

**The Ironblood Empress of the Morgue Clan.**

A prodigy from a magical family that had produced countless great mages.

In adulthood, Camus would possess not only exceptional magical ability but also beauty and political skill that made her a formidable presence in high society.

She would enter several political marriages, each one strengthening the Morgue Clan's position.

At the height of her power, she would personally campaign through Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, hunting monsters and barbarians with spear and fire and establishing a frontier marked by blood and ash.

In her later years, she would be remembered by an enormous imperial-style epithet.

But all of that belonged to a distant future.

Right now, she was simply an angry eight-year-old girl.

“Hey! What did you just say?”

Camus's voice was sharp.

“Why is the ruby mine yours? It's ours!”

Adolf smiled.

Hugo frowned.

Vikir answered with one sentence.

“You came a long way just to throw a tantrum.”

This time Hugo smiled.

Adolf frowned.

Camus ground her teeth.

The childish approach clearly was not going to work on him.

So her expression changed.

The innocent irritation disappeared.

Her tone became cold and precise.

“If you dislike emotional arguments, then let's use logic. I'll explain why the ruby mine belongs to Morgue.”

She unfolded a map and began laying out the terrain.

“The only surface entrance from which the rubies can actually be mined lies in our territory. Baskerville cannot reach the vein independently from the surface. Therefore it would be more practical for Baskerville to grant Morgue the right to mine the section beneath Baskerville land and receive compensation for use of the territory.”

Camus had grown up as the treasured direct-line prodigy of a great magical clan.

Few adults could easily dismiss her reasoning.

No child her own age ever had.

That confidence lasted until Vikir answered.

“Rubies are useful as magical materials. Baskerville has little reason to mine them directly.”

Camus's eyes brightened in triumph.

Vikir continued.

“But opening Baskerville territory for a little money would be a far greater loss than leaving the rubies underground.”

Her expression went blank.

For the first time in her life, someone her age had simply refused to be carried along by her logic.

And he had done it without hesitation.

Most boys her age were supposed to be dull and easy to overwhelm.

Why was this one different?

The argument unfolding in front of Hugo and Adolf no longer sounded like an ordinary quarrel between eight-year-olds.

Both children understood the central conflict between the two clans well enough that the adults found themselves listening seriously.

Camus narrowed her eyes.

“How strange. I've never met anyone like you. There isn't anyone my age in my clan who can even hold a conversation with me.”

“You should go outside more.”

“…Excuse me? I've inspected our territory plenty of times. What gives you the right to say anything about how I live?”

“To be honest, I don't care how you live. We're talking about the rubies. If you want to change the subject, do it by yourself.”

The complete lack of interest in Vikir's voice made Camus even more competitive.

She had spent her entire life surrounded by people who praised, indulged, or deferred to her.

Being dismissed so casually by someone her own age was completely new.

And she absolutely refused to lose an argument this important.

“The mine entrance is in Morgue territory! The mining rights are ours!”

“The vein continues under Baskerville territory. Once you cross the boundary, the rights are Baskerville's.”

“Baskerville doesn't even care about rubies!”

“Exactly. Baskerville cares about territory. So don't cross into it. Is that difficult to understand?”

Morgue wanted the rubies.

Baskerville wanted control of its land.

Their priorities were fundamentally different.

The adults behind them were divided along exactly the same line.

The argument went nowhere.

Then Camus's ruby-red eyes flashed.

“Fine. I'll show you exactly who owns the ore.”

She took a brush and ink and drew on the floor.

One large circle around Vikir.

Another around herself.

The two circles touched at their edges.

Then she had two servants hold up a broad sheet of paper along the boundary between them.

From the other side of the makeshift wall, Camus spoke.

“That side is your land. This side is mine.”

“…”

Vikir said nothing.

Hugo and Adolf watched with growing interest.

Rip!

Camus tore a hole through the paper wall and pushed one arm into Vikir's circle.

A small pale forearm crossed the boundary.

“Now. Whose arm is this?”

Adolf's expression brightened immediately.

Hugo's darkened.

The analogy was obvious.

The arm had crossed the border.

That did not make it belong to the owner of the land on the other side.

By the same logic, Camus argued, the ruby vein remained Morgue's even where it extended beneath Baskerville territory.

It was an old kind of property argument—the same logic people used when the fruit of a tree extended over a neighbor's fence.

Vikir looked at the arm.

“…Whose arm?”

He was not the kind of person who surrendered merely because an analogy was clever.

Snap!

His hand closed firmly around Camus's forearm.

Before she could even yelp, Vikir answered.

“Mine now.”

A heavy pressure rolled off him.

Not mana.

Not aura.

It was the force of presence belonging to someone who had survived the Age of Destruction and spent decades hunting and being hunted.

For an instant, even Hugo and Adolf reacted.

For Camus, who was only eight, it was overwhelming.

“W-what?”

She tried to pull her arm back.

It did not move.

Riiip—

The paper wall tore farther as Vikir pulled her closer.

The two children ended up nearly face-to-face.

“…!”

Camus's eyes wavered.

She had rarely spent time outside her own clan, and she had never been this close to a boy from another family.

Black hair.

Black eyes.

Pale skin.

A face unlike the familiar faces she saw every day at home.

For one confused instant, the novelty of it overwhelmed the political argument.

‘He's… kind of cute.’

The thought appeared before she could stop it.

Her cheeks reddened.

She looked down.

“I-I don't belong to you! What are you talking about?”

Hugo and Adolf both watched with complicated expressions.

“Ahem.”

“Cough.”

An important territorial dispute had somehow tangled itself with the embarrassment of two children, creating an atmosphere too awkward for either adult to acknowledge directly.

Vikir shattered it.

“Who said *you* were mine?”

His grip tightened just enough to pull her arm farther across the boundary.

Camus stumbled closer again.

Before she could protest, Vikir's other hand moved.

“I said your arm is mine.”

Something flashed out from his waist.

A short blade.

Sharp-looking.

Fast.

Camus froze.

Adolf's eyes widened.

Even Hugo, normally impossible to surprise, reacted.
