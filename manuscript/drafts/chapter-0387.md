“They expect us to eat this garbage?!”

“Are they insane?!”

“How can people work in a place like this?!”

“At least give us some time to rest!”

“When do we get to see our families?!”

“Prisoners are human too!”

“We have rights!”

Level One had erupted.

The prisoners gathered in a mass.

Most were recent arrivals.

They had not yet adapted to Nouvelle Vague.

Still newborn puppies—

ignorant of the kennel they had entered.

“Did you hear?”

“Someone escaped yesterday.”

“So Nouvelle Vague isn’t that impressive after all.”

“The guards are running everywhere.”

“Looks full of holes.”

“Hehehe.”

“If security is this loose—

maybe I’ll try escaping too.”

They had been overlooked while the guards dealt with greater chaos.

For a brief moment—

the lowest-level prisoners had landed inside a blind spot.

Then—

THUD.

THUD.

THUD.

Heavy footsteps approached.

Level One prisoners did not require elite wardens.

Junior officers were enough.

Second lieutenants.

Lieutenants.

The guards formed a line.

Batons.

Blades.

Whips.

Axes.

The prisoners recoiled.

“…What?”

“They’re bringing real weapons?”

“Are they planning to kill us?”

“We’re already restrained!”

“These people are insane!”

They were inexperienced.

And it showed.

A major stood before the suppression unit.

“Listen carefully.”

“In Nouvelle Vague—

the floors with the most riots are Level One and Level Two.”

The prisoners stared.

The guards did not.

The more one knew—

the more one saw.

High-level prisoners understood Nouvelle Vague.

They knew what resistance cost.

They knew what waited above them.

That knowledge made them cautious.

Low-level prisoners—

especially new ones—

still believed ordinary rules applied.

“The Level One prisoners cause trouble constantly.”

The major continued.

“They’re fresh.”

“They still talk about human rights.”

“Organize protests.”

“Complain.”

“Start riots.”

Then he smiled.

“And we usually allow it.”

Several prisoners went quiet.

“Sometimes we even encourage it.”

“We occasionally place informants among them and manufacture the conditions ourselves.”

“Today’s riot appears spontaneous.”

“But the purpose is the same.”

“Practical experience for you.”

The junior guards answered together.

“Yes!”

The prisoners began looking at one another.

An informant?

A planted agitator?

Was someone beside them working for the prison?

The moment suspicion appeared—

their unity cracked.

The major pressed the advantage.

“They are still Level One prisoners.”

“But remember what that means.”

“Before Nouvelle Vague—

many were famous criminals on the surface.”

“They survived the transfer process.”

“They survived intake.”

“They are strong.”

“BDISSEM restraints do not make them harmless.”

“Do not underestimate them.”

“Yes!”

Then—

the suppression began.

◆◆◆

Blood and torn flesh scattered across the floor.

Guards beat prisoners with iron clubs and three-section batons.

Even junior guards held an enormous advantage over Level One prisoners wearing BDISSEM restraints.

“Th-this is a violation of human rights!”

A military boot pressed a screaming prisoner’s face into the ground.

“Human rights?”

A cold voice answered.

“Why are you looking for them ten thousand meters underground?”

Kirko Grimm looked down.

Blood covered part of her face.

Her eyes moved across the prisoners.

“You cannot be reformed.”

“You cannot be educated.”

“You preyed on systems ordinary people worked to build.”

“You endangered people who had done nothing to you.”

“And now you ask for rights?”

These were Kirko’s words.

The prisoners under her gaze shrank instinctively.

Then—

CLANG.

A chain struck her from behind.

Kirko stumbled.

A Level One prisoner wearing BDISSEM cuffs stared at her with bloodshot eyes.

A cross-shaped scar marked his forehead.

“Hehehe.”

“You know who I am?”

Kirko touched her aching back.

“No.”

“Not at all.”

The man’s face twisted.

“What?”

“You don’t recognize me?”

He bared his teeth.

“I was Number Eight—”

“No.”

He corrected himself.

“I’m **Pal Euspear**.”

“The Monster of the Crossroads!”

Pal Euspear.

A recent Level One inmate.

And—

unlike many prisoners around him—

a former student from a prestigious institution.

Colosseo Academy.

At some point—

he had abandoned the student path and fallen fully into crime.

“Damn it.”

“I picked fights with high-ranking noble brats in the Hell Tree.”

“Then they got me outside.”

“Who would’ve guessed that place would disappear so suddenly?”

The Hell Tree had temporarily rewritten every hierarchy inside it.

Old authority had disappeared.

Stats.

Items.

Luck.

Those mattered more.

Pal had benefited early.

And then used what he gained to torment students who had once been untouchable.

Especially Dolores.

He had targeted her repeatedly.

Harmed many classmates.

Built a reputation inside the Hell Tree.

Then the Hell Tree vanished.

Everyone returned to the real world.

And the people he had abused—

remembered.

“Damn.”

“I thought that place would last forever…”

Political retaliation followed.

Expulsion.

Crime.

Eventually—

Nouvelle Vague.

But the stats Pal had accumulated inside the Hell Tree had not become completely meaningless.

Even in BDISSEM restraints—

he retained more fighting ability than an ordinary Level One prisoner.

“Die, bitch!”

Pal wrapped the chain between his cuffs around Kirko’s neck.

“Ghk!”

Kirko dropped her sword.

She had not expected strength like this from Level One.

A prisoner this capable belonged on Level Two.

Possibly Level Three.

He had likely hidden his actual strength during classification.

Kirko twisted.

Tried to break free.

Pal committed his whole body to restraining her.

The melee around them swallowed the pair.

No guard was close enough to help.

Pal looked down at her.

Licked his lips.

“Hehehe.”

“There’s a woman like you in Nouvelle Vague?”

“Good.”

“I’ve had plenty of urges building up.”

Kirko shut her eyes tightly.

She had always known something like this could happen.

In bed.

On a quiet watch.

In the shower.

At meals.

Every Nouvelle Vague guard lived with the possibility carved into them.

*So today is the day.*

One mistake.

One bad moment.

That was enough.

Kirko prepared herself to endure whatever came next.

Then—

THUD.

CRACK.

CRUNCH.

Something hot splashed across her face.

“…?”

Kirko opened her eyes.

Red.

Blood.

Pal’s blood.

He stared upward in panic.

A thick, hard three-section baton had been rammed into his mouth—

past broken teeth—

deep into his throat.

“Ghk!”

“Ghh—!”

His lips were torn.

Front teeth missing.

The tissues inside his mouth and throat mangled.

He tried desperately to spit out the weapon.

Impossible.

The hand driving it forward—

was too strong.

“…Are you all right?”

Emotionless voice.

Familiar face.

**Garam Nord.**

Twenty-one.

Second lieutenant.

Junior Nouvelle Vague guard.

Someone Kirko had known for years.

Garam looked down at her without expression.

WHOOSH.

He pulled the baton free.

Pal vomited.

Blood.

Saliva.

Tears.

Then—

THUD.

A boot smashed into the back of his head.

He went limp.

“…You?”

Kirko stammered.

She had never imagined needing rescue from the man everyone called Stupid Garam.

Rotten Dog.

Then—

a memory.

*No.*

*This might be the second time.*

During Sakkuth’s riot—

Garam had helped her too.

That time had felt different.

Then Kirko jolted.

This was not the moment for reflection.

The riot was still raging.

“Garam!”

“What are you doing here?!”

“What about the prisoners in your assigned area?!”

“You should have suppressed them before coming to help me—”

“There aren’t any.”

Kirko stopped.

“…What?”

For one instant—

she wondered if she had misheard.

Then she looked behind him.

The bent three-section baton dripped blood.

Beyond it—

dozens of prisoners lay collapsed.

Broken.

Unmoving.

Garam spoke briefly.

“There are still more prisoners to suppress.”

Kirko stared.

So did the other guards who had just arrived.

Nobody knew what to say.
