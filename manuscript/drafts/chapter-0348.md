# Chapter 348: Key man (1)

Darkness.

At the center of an endless void—

Pigi stood alone.

*Get out of here.*

A middle-aged man’s voice.

The moment Pigi heard it—

he knew.

*That dream again.*

The same vision he had seen during midterms—

when a pile of gargoyle statues nearly crushed him to death.

The nightmare he escaped only because Vikir helped him.

*Trash like this cannot be of my blood.*

The voice was brutally cold.

Then—

his mother’s quiet sobbing.

*You lowly thing.*

*You don’t even know your master.*

*Get out of here immediately.*

*…Dispose of him.*

Whispers gathered around the sound of his mother crying.

Then the familiar sequence began.

His mother running away.

Forests.

Mountains.

Pursuers.

A steep cliff.

A raging river.

Hungry wolves.

The shocked faces of passing merchants and mercenaries.

His mother slowly smiling again.

A kind stepfather.

Years of hard preparation for the Academy entrance exam.

Dormitory life—

difficult—

but survivable because of friends.

The hallucination flowed onward—

like time itself.

Then Pigi realized something.

Sticky fibers had wrapped around him.

“…Huh!?”

The empty darkness was gone.

Fleshy walls surrounded him.

The nauseating sensation of being trapped inside the throat of an enormous monster.

Pigi struggled forward.

Muscle-like strands snapped around his arms and legs.

Then—

a gate appeared.

Huge.

Circular.

Surrounded by roaring flame.

**<Entrance to the Gate of Destruction — Serpent’s Tongue>**

The tops of the fire pillars split into forked shapes—

like a snake’s tongue.

Countless ominous columns of flame rotated around the gate.

Then—

an overwhelming desire seized Pigi.

Go through.

Enter.

A primitive impulse so strong that resisting it felt impossible.

*Why do I want to go in?*

A normal human would never willingly approach a place like this.

Yet Pigi kept walking.

Then—

“Hey, Pigi.”

Tudor’s voice behind him.

“You can’t go there.”

“We’re human.”

Pigi stopped.

Sancho called too.

“That gate is for demons.”

“We can’t go through.”

Bianca shouted.

“Are you insane?!”

“Come back!”

More voices joined them.

“Come back, Pigi.”

“It’s safe here.”

“Don’t go there.”

“Over here!”

“Turn around!”

“Look at us!”

His mother.

His stepfather.

Friends from home.

Everyone he had missed.

Everyone he loved.

Pigi’s feet stopped.

His eyes blurred.

His head began to turn.

Then—

*Never look back.*

A voice struck directly into his heart.

His stalled resolve restarted.

His cloudy mind cleared.

“…!”

The voices behind him continued.

“Pigi!”

“Please look at us!”

“Behind you!”

“Turn around!”

“Run!”

“Come this way!”

Each time Pigi almost obeyed—

another sound echoed inside his chest.

Hot.

Heavy.

Like a beast breathing.

*Never look back.*

*Keep walking forward.*

An old man’s rasp.

Pigi closed his eyes.

Took one step.

“Pigi!”

“Are you crazy?!”

“Why are you ignoring us?!”

“Come back!”

“Look here!”

“It’s Mom!”

“You ungrateful brat!”

“If you don’t turn around right now—”

Pigi’s face twisted.

People he had desperately wanted to see—

calling him like this.

How could he keep walking?

“Ugh…”

He stopped again.

Closed his eyes.

Started to turn.

Then—

THUMP!

Two rough hands seized his face.

“Ah!?”

Pigi’s eyes flew open.

Before he could turn fully—

the hands forced his face forward again.

He looked down.

Wrinkled.

Scarred.

Covered in thick blood.

The owner of those hands stood close beside him.

*Walk forward.*

That same hoarse voice.

Pigi trembled—

but obeyed.

At the edge of his vision—

a tall figure moved beside him.

A middle-aged man.

Burns and blade scars covered his hands and face.

He limped.

Still—

he half-supported Pigi while walking.

One hand around the neck.

Another at the shoulder.

Then the jaw.

Every grip preventing Pigi from turning around.

Soon—

they reached the gate.

Flame.

Heavy steel.

Terrifying to look at.

For some reason—

Pigi was not afraid of the gate itself.

Only the voices behind him bothered him now.

Without the mysterious man walking beside him—

he knew he would never have come this far.

A strange sense of accomplishment filled him.

He did not know why.

Only that he **had** to cross.

Pigi opened his mouth to thank the man.

The grip disappeared.

*Never look back.*

*From here on—*

*you walk alone.*

The figure vanished.

So did the voice.

Pigi knew—

without needing to see—

that this person should never have been here.

And now—

he had gone somewhere impossibly far away.

For good.

“…He was a good man.”

Pigi pushed open the gate.

Then threw himself into the darkness beyond it.

Creeeeak—

The gate closed behind him.

◆◆◆

“Ah!?”

Pigi bolted upright—

sweat-soaked.

The first thing he saw—

was his own face reflected in a puddle.

“I’m back?”

He touched his skin.

Young again.

A teenager.

All the years spent inside the River of Flow—

physically reset.

Then Pigi looked upward.

“…!”

Something even more shocking waited above.

The ceiling—

dark as the night sky—

was covered in enormous roots.

Deep taproots extending from the main body of the Hell Tree.

And from those main roots—

countless thinner roots hung downward.

Fruit-like cocoons dangled beneath them.

Human bodies slept inside.

Only their faces were exposed.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Bianca.

The Ballak warriors.

All wrapped tightly.

All unconscious.

Pigi instinctively understood.

These enormous roots—

were part of the Hell Tree’s sustaining core.

Inside the main root—

vein-like channels drew power upward from the waters and fires of Hell.

Seeds.

Aquatic life.

Creatures resembling those found on lower floors.

The Daylilies of Blood.

The Shadowless King of the Black Sea.

Everything had traveled through this system.

“…Our emotions.”

Pigi’s voice shook.

“Hatred.”

“Killing intent.”

“Fear.”

“Anxiety.”

“The desire to live.”

“They’re using all of it as fuel—

to connect the human world and Hell.”

Pigi could not destroy the enormous root himself.

But—

there was something he **could** do.

Shing.

He drew his sword.

His body had returned to its original age.

His Level and stats—

had not.

The years of combat experience remained in his hands.

“Haa!”

Pigi swung.

Aura extended from the blade.

One cocoon fell.

Then another.

Then another.

Pigi ran first to Tudor.

Tore apart the fruit-shell around him.

Then slapped his face.

“Tudor!”

“Wake up!”

“Sancho!”

“Bianca!”

Pigi slapped with everything he had.

One by one—

they woke.

“Huh?”

“I thought Father was calling me to turn around…”

“Wait.”

“Sis?”

“She called me and then disappeared…”

“Where are we?”

“Ugh.”

“My head.”

“My face is young again?”

The order mattered.

The first to die—

woke first.

Pigi cut down the remaining cocoons.

Ballak’s people began waking too.

Everyone who had died—

everyone Vikir had killed—

was alive.

Tudor stared.

“Wait.”

“Is that really the Ballak hunting chief?”

Bianca blinked.

“She’s a child.”

Sancho looked around.

“Not just her.”

“They’re almost all children.”

The young girl before them—

was Aheul.

The terrifying veteran warrior whose strength had overwhelmed them—

was physically a child again.

The same was true for many Ballak warriors.

Their bodies had reset.

Their accumulated experience—

Levels—

stats—

remained.

They were children who had already lived for decades inside the River of Flow.

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca understood.

They themselves had lived seven years there.

They looked again at the Hell Tree roots.

“…So that’s it.”

“The emotions we released—

hatred, killing intent, fear, desperation—

fed the Hell Tree.”

“A conduit between Hell and the human world.”

“The tower pulls Hell’s fire and water through these roots.”

“And the monsters from the lower floors traveled up the same route.”

Then—

“There!”

Aheul shouted in awkward Imperial speech.

Everyone followed her finger.

A much larger fruit hung above them.

Heavy.

Dense.

Inside—

Vikir.

“We’ll get him!”

Pigi raised his sword carefully.

Aura formed at the tip.

A crescent flew upward.

Slash!

The cocoon fell.

Everyone rushed over.

“Vikir!”

“Wake up!”

“He’s breathing.”

“He’s just sleeping.”

They slapped him.

Shook him.

Nothing.

Vikir had been the final survivor.

The last person to “die.”

That meant—

the last person to wake.

No one doubted him now.

“Vikir knew.”

“The first to die would leave the hallucination first.”

“So dying early was actually the advantage.”

“Everyone who reached this floor was strong.”

“They’re used to surviving until the end.”

“That means they’d resist death longer—

and stay trapped in the illusion longer.”

Now the mission made sense.

**<Mission — Kill or Be Killed!>**

It did not mean only **kill**.

It required **being killed** too.

Everyone had to experience death.

And the last survivor—

became the final loser.

The mission forced people to betray others.

To abandon them.

To hurt them.

Then reversed the positions.

Those betrayed first woke first.

And because **everyone** had to clear—

the awakened victims held the fate of the remaining sleepers.

A cruel mechanism.

Then—

“…!”

Vikir opened his eyes.

Everyone crowded around.

“Vikir!”

“Are you okay?!”

“How many fingers?”

“What did you do in the River of Flow after we died?”

Vikir blinked.

Looked at his hands.

“…The wrinkles are gone.”

“Age spots too.”

“So physical age resets after waking.”

Everyone stared.

Wrinkles.

Age spots.

How long had Vikir remained alone there?

He did not answer.

Instead—

his status window told the story.

**<Vikir>**

**LV:** 1

**Stats:**  
Strength: 798  
Agility: 798  
Stamina: 798  
Physical Resistance: 798  
Magic Resistance: 798  
Reflexes: 798

Six stats.

All 798.

Total—

4,788.

Every special stat awakened.

Every stat equalized.

After a moment—

Vikir looked toward Pigi.

Then smiled faintly.

“…Good work.”

His voice was rough.

Dry.

For just a moment—

it sounded like an old man.

Pigi froze.

“T-that voice!”

Everyone looked at him.

Pigi grabbed Vikir’s hand.

“That was you.”

“In the hallucination.”

“The voice that kept me from turning around.”

“The scars.”

“The moles.”

“Your nails.”

“The hand was exactly the same.”

*Never look back.*

*From now on—walk alone.*

“Vikir.”

“If you hadn’t helped me—

I would’ve turned around.”

“I never would’ve woken up.”

“And I couldn’t have saved everyone else.”

Vikir stared.

*What?*

*I don’t remember that.*

Then he remembered Lost Paradise.

The impossible presence of people from the old timeline.

Perhaps—

something similar had happened.

Maybe an image of his pre-regression self—

or a remnant formed from memory—

had manifested and helped Pigi.

It was only a hypothesis.

Ding!

**<Mission Complete — Kill or Be Killed!>**

**The deathmatch is finally over!**

**How did dying once feel?**

**When the latecomers complete the mission, the predecessors become eligible for reverse summoning outside the tower.**

**Only those who completed the mission may be reverse summoned.**

Golden candy rained down.

A deeper gate appeared.

Then—

the prompt Vikir had feared.

**<Reverse summoning of predecessors will begin.>**

**<Do you accept reverse summoning?>**

Aheul refused.

Vikir nodded.

“Good choice.”

He patted her head.

He still did not know what had truly happened to the Beetlemen who accepted reverse summoning.

Remaining inside—

for now—

was safer.

“I’ll clear the tower.”

“When it’s over—

go back to the jungle.”

“Rebuild Ballak.”

Aheul and the other Ballak children nodded firmly.

After decades of tower experience—

they would return as extraordinary warriors.

Then Tudor spoke.

“Vikir.”

Sancho.

Bianca.

Pigi.

All looked at him.

“Why was Pigi the only one who woke up first?”

It was a reasonable question.

Pigi was not the strongest.

Not the toughest.

Not the most disciplined.

Yet he had broken free first—

and saved everyone.

Vikir looked at the small wound on Pigi’s arm.

The black blood dried there.

Then he spoke.

“Listen carefully, Pigi.”

After decades of thinking in the River of Flow—

Vikir had reached one possibility.

“You may be the key—

to destroying Amdusias’s abyss.”
