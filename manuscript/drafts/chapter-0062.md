# Chapter 62: The Protagonist of Hunting (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/062.txt` — `68c0110eec864429aebd42142991ba3694d8130f`. Recovered English Chapter 61 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Madam Eight-Legs**, **Aiyen**, **Ahun**, **Akwilla**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Oxbear**, and **Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain**. Marriage/reproductive pressure remains source-level social context and is not romanticized.

The harvest celebration ended successfully.

That night, about one hundred kilograms of the very best Oxbear meat was selected before the festival finally concluded.

Ballak hunters carried those premium cuts far from the village and hung them from a tall tree.

Vikir looked puzzled.

Aiyen explained.

“An offering to Madam Eight-Legs.”

Vikir nodded.

He had already met that monster in the darkness of the jungle.

An overwhelming creature.

Something no ordinary person could encounter and survive.

Ballak apparently offered portions of prey—and sometimes the corpses of prisoners of war—by hanging them high in trees.

What surprised Vikir was that nothing touched the offering.

Hyenas.

Vultures.

Even insects.

All avoided it.

As though the jungle itself knew who would come to claim the meat.

*Were the Baskerville and Morgue warriors killed here in the past offered to Madam Eight-Legs too?*

Ballak did not practice cannibalism.

It seemed plausible.

◆◆◆

Vikir rose early the next morning.

Drunken Ballak warriors slept in the dew along the paths.

He carefully stepped around them and headed toward the edge of the village.

His body was recovering rapidly.

But healing consumed energy.

The Oxbear meat, blood, and organs he had received were not enough.

He needed more food.

Despite technically still being a slave, the guards let him leave without much argument.

Ahun happened to be serving as sentry captain.

“You killed one half-dead bear and suddenly think you can wander wherever you like?”

He scowled.

Then waved Vikir away.

“Fine. Go get yourself killed.”

Vikir entered the jungle alone.

Before long he reached the tall tree where the offering had been hung.

“……!”

The one-hundred-kilogram mass of meat was gone.

No ordinary animal had approached it.

The only conclusion was that its owner had come during the night.

Dried white slime clung to the area.

Nearby vegetation had withered.

“Disgusting monster.”

Vikir left and walked toward a muddy stream.

Water and land blurred together beneath vines and thorn-covered plants.

He climbed onto a high branch and prepared a fishing line.

The longest, strongest Oxbear hair became cord.

A sharpened piece of Oxbear bone became the hook.

Vikir bit his fingertip and let a few drops of blood fall into the river.

BUBBLE. BUBBLE. BUBBLE.

The muddy surface reacted immediately.

He skewered a frog onto the hook and cast it into the bubbles.

Something struck almost at once.

A fish covered in sawlike teeth came thrashing out of the water.

> **Natteri Piranha**  
> **Danger Rating — Individual:** D  
> **Danger Rating — School:** A  
> **Size:** 30 cm  
> **Found at:** Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, 6th Ridge  
> Usually moves in schools numbering at least several thousand. Timid alone, but in a group will attack even enormous enemies. Extremely sensitive to blood and known to swarm from deep muddy water until only bones remain.

The frog was shredded.

The Oxbear-hair line and bone hook survived.

The fish had a blue-green body and red belly.

Vikir threaded it onto a sharpened branch.

Then he used a piece of its flesh as the next bait.

Cannibalistic bait drove the Natteri into an even greater frenzy.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

More kept coming.

Some individuals were more than seventy centimeters long.

Others had black or pale bodies and red eyes.

“These are supposed to be difficult to catch.”

Vikir looked at the growing line of fish.

Natteri reacted strongly to human blood.

Blood containing mana attracted them even more intensely.

Ballak hunters apparently did not know that detail.

Vikir saw no reason to advertise it.

He built a small fire from leaves and dry wood.

Some fish he roasted immediately.

Others he smoked for storage.

He also caught freshwater crabs and turtles from the banks.

By sunrise, his leaf basket was full.

◆◆◆

When Vikir returned to the village, he found a strange sight.

A long line had formed in front of Akwilla's tent.

At least thirty people.

All young women.

*Some kind of ritual?*

He had to pass them anyway.

They had not noticed him approaching from behind.

“I've been here since dawn. Good thing I'm near the front.”

“I slept here.”

“I should've come earlier.”

Vikir slowed.

Their next comments were stranger.

“After yesterday's hunting festival, they'll probably free him, right?”

“Then he'll need a bride from the village.”

“I'm asking the chieftain for first place.”

“He looked so capable processing the meat yesterday.”

“He helped bring down an Oxbear. Of course he's strong.”

“And he's good-looking.”

Vikir began backing away.

His danger instincts had started reacting before he fully understood why.

Then—

“You people!”

A sharp shout scattered the line.

Aiyen appeared carrying three deer.

She threw them down.

“I fed him the soup yesterday and now you're all trying to steal the result at the last moment?”

One woman shouted back.

“Then use him first and pass him on!”

“Shut up!”

“Keeping him all to yourself is selfish!”

“If you think it's unfair, go outside and catch your own!”

“I've tried! I've never found one like that!”

“How far did you even go?”

“To the Imperial border!”

“Then go all the way to Hell!”

Aiyen pulled a short blade from her thigh.

The line retreated.

Even bold Ballak women did not casually pick fights with the youngest hunting captain.

Aiyen snorted.

Then she muttered to herself while turning away.

“At this rate someone will snatch him before I recover properly. Can't trust any of these shameless women…”

She stopped.

Her eyes met Vikir's.

He had ended up standing half-hidden behind a tent.

For some reason, he felt as though he had been caught eavesdropping.

Aiyen looked even more alarmed.

“……How much did you hear?”

“……”

“No. Nothing.”

“……”

“……Ahem.”

An awkward silence followed.

Eventually Aiyen scratched her neck and walked over.

She was almost a hand taller than Vikir.

“My mother held a council of elders last night.”

“……”

“You came up.”

Aiyen had already told him that the hunter who brought back the greatest prey traditionally gained freedom from slavery.

Apparently the reward did not end there.

“As the honored hunter from yesterday's festival, you can ask my mother for one thing.”

Then Aiyen added,

“She may assign you a mate.”

A useful outsider who had just been freed was valuable.

Giving such a person a spouse encouraged them to stay.

*So that's why they were lining up.*

Vikir sighed.

Aiyen narrowed her eyes.

“Is there any woman in the tribe you especially like?”

No.

Vikir shook his head immediately.

His body was still damaged.

He was stranded in hostile territory.

Dangerous monsters surrounded him.

Romance was not a priority.

Aiyen's expression shifted through several emotions too quickly to read.

Then she put both hands on his shoulders.

Her approach was forceful.

Her actual touch was surprisingly light.

“When my mother calls you later, I'm deciding what you ask for.”

Aiyen leaned closer and lowered her voice.

“I already know what you should request.”

Her eyes shone like she was planning something.
