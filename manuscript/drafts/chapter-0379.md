“Kururururu…”

Sakkuth’s eyes had no focus.

His face twisted strangely.

Foam and saliva ran from his mouth.

“I don’t need anything anymore.”

“After I kill you—

I’ll reach Level Nine.”

“Then Boss will trust me again.”

His crazed gaze locked onto Kirko and Garam.

Shing.

Kirko leveled her sword.

Ready.

Garam—

still trembled.

His face looked miserable.

“Kirko…”

“Wouldn’t it be better to run?”

“If we do—

he’ll reach the other zones.”

“Other prisoners are one thing.”

“This one can spread plague over a huge area.”

“But…”

“If you’re scared—

go.”

“Idiot.”

“I’m fighting.”

Kirko gave Garam one disappointed glance.

Then fixed her attention on Sakkuth.

Aura gathered at her blade.

Gaseous.

Dense.

Almost liquid.

Sakkuth laughed.

“Kurururu…”

“Not bad.”

“For a woman born and raised inside Nouvelle Vague.”

Kirko’s expression changed.

Only slightly.

But enough.

The tension remained.

The coldness remained.

Still—

a crack had opened.

Sakkuth saw it.

“About twenty years ago—

there was that incident.”

“A prisoner assaulted a guard.”

“That’s why Nouvelle Vague guards became so sensitive to insubordination from below.”

“….”

“Kurururu…”

“And I heard a child was born from that disgraceful event.”

Sakkuth had found the buried wound.

Kirko’s anger ignited.

“Prisoners should not speak about things they do not understand.”

Her sword shot forward.

Aura spread wide.

Cutting.

Piercing.

Covering broad space.

A practical method against a huge target like Sakkuth.

But the attack was designed more to punish—

to create fear through shallow wounds and blood—

than to kill a prisoner who had already decided to die.

“Kururururu!”

Sakkuth advanced through the storm of cuts.

“You’re a frog from the bottom of a well.”

“You’ve never even seen the outside world.”

“And why should someone born from rape ever walk on the surface?”

“You’re useless.”

“So stay down here in the abyss.”

Sakkuth grabbed Kirko by the collar.

She tried to retreat.

Failed.

His teeth moved toward her.

Kirko closed her eyes.

Then—

“Take it back!”

A three-section baton struck from the side.

CRACK!

Sakkuth’s teeth shattered.

“What?!”

He stumbled.

Hands over his mouth.

Kirko—

on the ground—

looked up.

Garam stood between them.

For once—

he had moved when it mattered.

His hands and legs were shaking terribly.

His voice—

was not.

“Kirko is admirable.”

“She deserves respect.”

“Trash like you has no right to insult her!”

“What—”

Kirko’s cheeks flushed instantly.

Garam did not notice.

He was too focused on Sakkuth.

“Kurururu…”

Sakkuth spat out broken teeth.

Smiled through blood.

“Stupid Garam.”

“You’re brave today.”

“Because I insulted your crush?”

“N-no!”

“A coward guard who buys prisoners lunch…”

“So pure.”

“Does your crush even know?”

“Does she know what a weak, useless guard you are?”

“No!”

“That’s not—”

“Kurururu…”

“No?”

“You and her mother have something in common.”

“You both know how to give things to prisoners.”

“Bread.”

“Bodies.”

“Kururururu!”

“You bastard!”

Garam poured mana into his baton.

Too hot.

Unstable.

Anger made it shake.

Then—

“Stop.”

A hand touched Garam’s shoulder.

Kirko.

Standing beside him again.

Calm.

“Do not fall for psychological warfare.”

“He can’t use mana.”

“He is trying to disrupt our concentration.”

“Oh.”

“That’s what he’s doing?”

“Yes.”

“We handle him together.”

Garam’s face brightened.

“Right!”

“Of course.”

“You really are amazing, Kirko!”

“Ugh.”

“Idiot…”

Despite the complaint—

a rare smile touched Kirko’s mouth.

She did not look unhappy.

…But this was not a heroic novel.

Not a youthful battle story where courage automatically produced victory.

THWACK!

Sakkuth swung his arm.

The BDISSEM chain snapped outward like a whip.

Kirko’s faint smile vanished—

as her head whipped sideways.

BOOM!

She flew.

Her neck twisted badly.

Her head struck stone.

She collapsed without even screaming.

Sssss—

Blood sprayed from her head.

Then thickened against the hot ground.

“…What?”

Garam stared.

As though his mind had not yet processed what happened.

Sakkuth walked toward Kirko.

Giggling.

“Oh?”

“She’s alive.”

“Impressive.”

Kirko twitched.

Sakkuth clicked his tongue.

“What talent.”

“If she survives—

she might become quite a hero when she gets older.”

Then—

something dirty entered his eyes.

“Maybe it would be better to crush that kind of future now.”

“Good face too.”

“Good body—”

CLANG!

Sakkuth stopped.

Garam had moved between them.

Baton raised.

“Do not touch Kirko.”

“Kurururu…”

“I will protect her with my life.”

“Because I admire her.”

“And respect her—”

Sakkuth punched him away before he finished.

CRASH.

Garam hit stone.

Rolled—

like a puppet with cut strings.

Sakkuth laughed.

“You talk too much for someone who hasn’t even made lieutenant.”

“I’m the one who risked my life.”

“I have to reach Level Nine and regain Boss’s trust.”

He walked toward Kirko again.

Then—

tap.

A weak blow struck his shoulder from behind.

Garam.

Somehow—

still moving.

“…?”

Sakkuth turned.

Garam’s voice came out broken.

“Don’t…”

“touch…”

“Kirko…”

A vein bulged on Sakkuth’s forehead.

“You annoying insect.”

His elbow slammed down.

CRACK.

Garam’s jaw shattered.

Teeth flew.

Blood sprayed.

Still—

Garam held Sakkuth’s ankle.

“Kir…”

“ko…”

“Ah.”

“So persistent.”

“I finally thought I could enjoy myself with a woman.”

Sakkuth looked down.

Garam still radiated killing intent.

Weak.

Shaking.

Desperate.

Sakkuth laughed.

“Brat.”

“If you want someone to feel killing intent—

focus.”

“Think about killing them.”

“Only killing them.”

“Push it into one point.”

“Then—

maybe—

their skin will tingle.”

“What you’re doing?”

“Nothing.”

He shook Garam loose.

Turned back toward Kirko.

Reached.

Then—

ROAR.

The killing intent behind him exploded.

Sakkuth froze.

One hand went to his neck.

The pressure was unreal.

It did not merely threaten his throat.

Or heart.

It felt as though something had already pierced his whole body—

dozens—

hundreds—

of times.

Sweat covered him.

Slowly—

he turned.

Garam was still on the ground.

But another prisoner now stood there.

A similar-sized man.

Calm face.

Red eyes.

“Is that how you project killing intent?”

Vikir.

The Night Hound.

He had watched everything.
