# Chapter 129: Admission to the Academy (4)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/129.txt` — `59717ac8c05eadf387b9a817a7e88da66facbf02`. Recovered English Chapter 128 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir Van Baskerville, Night Hound, Sword Graduator, Ten Corpses, Cindywendy, Quovadis, Bianca Fo Usher, Colosseo Academy, and the 20th class. The chapter's blacklist testimony distinguishes crimes Bartison has already committed from betrayals Vikir remembers him committing in the previous timeline.

Night.

Several mercenaries stood smoking outside a large, run-down warehouse.

They chatted lazily.

Without the usual noise coming from inside—

they were in an unusually good mood.

“It's nice not hearing those brats complain.”

“The warehouse was packed with children for days.”

“So where did they all go?”

“All gone in one day.”

“A wagon came this morning and took them.”

One mercenary shrugged.

“I didn't ask where.”

“That's how you live longer.”

Not long ago—

the warehouse had held many orphans with no families or connections.

Now it was empty.

In a few days—

another group would arrive from somewhere.

Then—

sooner or later—

they too would disappear.

“I wonder where the children come from.”

Another mercenary exhaled smoke.

“And where they go.”

“Don't ask useless questions.”

“That kind of curiosity shortens your life.”

“I heard they end up at some orphanage in the center of the Imperial Capital.”

“Right.”

“A facility run by Quovadis.”

The men stretched and yawned.

Then—

all of them fell silent.

A huge man had appeared beside them.

Nearly two meters tall.

**Ron Bartison.**

Captain of the guard detail.

A brutal mercenary from the north.

Nicknames followed him everywhere.

**Flesh Grinder.**

**Bone-Skinning Hammer.**

And several others just as unpleasant.

“Shut up and stand watch.”

Bartison looked down on the men.

“If you want to trade those worthless lives for a few coins.”

“…”

“Trash.”

He clicked his tongue and walked past.

Inside—

he sneered.

What could low-level mercenaries without mana say to him?

Bartison had reached the **Sword Graduator** realm.

They had not.

Then—

he stopped.

“…?”

The guards had not merely become quiet.

Thud.

One collapsed.

Then another.

And another.

“What the hell?”

Bartison reached immediately for the hammer at his waist.

A long shadow stretched across the ground in the firelight.

“What…?”

He looked up.

A figure stood on the warehouse roof.

Black cloak.

Black hat.

A long, ominous beaked mask hiding the face beneath it.

“…Are you Ron Bartison?”

The voice was low and cold.

Bartison swallowed.

Sssss—

Aura gathered around his hammer.

Liquid aura.

The unmistakable sign of a Graduator.

“What do you want?!”

He swung—

or tried to.

Both wrists had already been severed.

The hammer flew from his grip and crashed behind him, its aura dispersing.

Bartison stumbled backward in shock.

No blood sprayed from the cuts.

The severed wrist stumps had been cauterized instantly by intense heat.

His mouth opened to scream—

but the Night Hound had already closed the distance.

“…Ron Hubert Bartison.”

Vikir's disguised voice remained mechanical.

“Four years ago, you abused and murdered a thirteen-year-old neighbor as part of the sacrifice used to make your first pact with a demon.”

Bartison's face drained of color.

“After that, you continued stealing information from the Northern Guild Alliance and passing it to demonic agents.”

Vikir continued.

“And eventually—”

“You betrayed the First Platoon, Fourth Company, 207th Regiment, 75th Division, Fifth Corps of the Human Alliance.”

“While on watch, you used a signal mirror to reveal your platoon's sleeping position to the enemy.”

“The entire unit was destroyed.”

Vikir tilted his head.

“Correct?”

Bartison stared.

“The first part…”

His voice shook.

“The thing from four years ago—yes.”

“But everything after that?”

“I never did any of that!”

“Yes.”

Vikir answered immediately.

“Not yet.”

Bartison froze.

“What?”

He tried to retreat.

He could not.

Both ankles had been severed.

A strangled sound escaped him.

Vikir did not explain.

The blood-red blade in his right hand drove through Bartison's throat.

Bartison struggled a few times.

Then died beside the warehouse.

Vikir looked down at the body.

Then watched.

The corpse began to decay with unnatural speed.

Just as Seth Le Baskerville's had.

“The smell is the same.”

Vikir frowned.

People who had made pacts with demons carried a rotten odor in the soul itself.

The deeper their corruption—

the stronger the stench.

Veteran fighters who had survived the Age of Destruction could recognize it almost instinctively.

People of the present age could not.

For now—

Vikir alone knew what to look for.

Sssssss—

Bartison's face decayed faster than the rest of the body.

Within moments—

bone showed beneath.

Vikir narrowed his eyes.

*Is that connected to the ability of whichever Corpse made the pact with him?*

Another detail for investigation.

He took a small owl from inside his cloak.

Then wrote a letter describing what he had observed.

The message was for **Cindywendy**—

whose network of agents and informants made her the best person to investigate patterns like this.

Once the owl departed—

Vikir turned back toward the campfire.

Several bottles of liquor had been left nearby.

He picked up the strongest rum.

Then poured it over the bloodstains on his cloak.

The harsh alcohol washed the fabric clean.

Vikir opened the blacklist.

Found **Ron Bartison**.

Tore out the page.

Then tossed it into the fire.

The paper blackened.

Curled.

Burned to ash.

A short while later—

“Ugh…”

“My neck…”

“What happened?”

The mercenaries outside the warehouse began waking one by one.

“Huh?”

They looked around.

The fire was almost dead.

Beside it lay a faceless corpse.

◆◆◆

Dong—

dong—

dong—

The great clock tower at the center of Colosseo rang twelve times.

Just after midnight.

The night's work had ended earlier than Vikir expected.

He had crossed the Imperial Capital from end to end.

Mansions in wealthy districts.

Warehouses on the outskirts.

Gambling dens in the slums.

Underground clubs.

Isolated houses near the lake.

Brothels.

By the end of the night—

twenty-nine names had been removed from the blacklist.

Every target shared the same traits.

Evidence of a pact with a demon.

A history—or future history—of betrayal.

And after death—

the same unnatural decay of the face.

Unfortunately—

Vikir had gained almost no information about the identities behind the pacts.

That meant the investigation would be long.

*I didn't expect a harvest on the first day.*

This was only the beginning.

There were still many pages left.

Vikir returned to Colosseo.

Tap.

He crossed the Academy's outer wall—

high and thick as a fortress wall.

From above—

the scale of the campus was even more impressive.

The dormitories sat quiet in the darkness.

Elsewhere—

light poured from classroom windows.

Laughter echoed through the night.

*Orientation is still going?*

Vikir hesitated.

Return to the dormitory and sleep—

or show his face at the welcome gathering?

The answer came quickly.

*I'll appear for a little while.*

Not for networking.

Not to impress classmates.

He wanted an alibi.

And—

though he would not say so aloud—

he was a little concerned about Pigi being there alone.

Vikir removed the mask.

Stored it beneath his cloak.

Then landed on the roof of the academic building.

He was about to descend the stairs when—

Whoosh!

Boom!

A firework shot into the sky.

For an instant—

the burst of light illuminated two people near the rooftop railing.

A young man.

And a young woman.

Vikir's eyes narrowed.

He recognized the woman immediately.

**Bianca Fo Usher.**

A tall, striking freshman of the 20th class.

One of the two students tied for first place in the Cold Department practical assessment.

The young man beside her—

Vikir did not remember particularly well.

Their atmosphere was obvious enough.

The young man squeezed his eyes shut.

“I…”

He swallowed.

“I fell in love with you the moment I saw you!”

“Please go out with me!”

“I'll treat you well!”

A second-year student—

apparently.

Bianca sighed.

Deeply.

She brushed back her bangs.

“Senior.”

Her expression remained firm.

“You called me all the way up here just to say that?”

“Uh…”

“Yes.”

“I mean—yes.”

Bianca exhaled.

“Then let me apologize first.”

“I don't have time for dating.”

“I only entered the Academy today.”

The senior panicked.

“But going out with me wouldn't hurt you!”

“I'm in the student council!”

“And I'm part of one of the leading clubs—”

“I'm sorry.”

Bianca turned away.

The matter was finished.

Behind a stone pillar—

Vikir thought quietly.

*Peaceful times.*

He almost smiled.

*Nothing to do with me.*

Watching students more than twenty years younger than his accumulated memories stumble through their first romances felt strangely distant.

Vikir stepped away from the pillar.

At that exact moment—

“…!”

Bianca's head snapped around.

“Who's there?”

She moved immediately.

Fast.

Straight toward the pillar where Vikir had been standing.

*As expected from Usher.*

Vikir shifted once.

Then vaulted over the wall.

No need for the stairs.

Railing to railing.

Pillar to pillar.

He disappeared toward the academic building.

Seconds later—

Bianca reached the spot.

Her eyes widened.

“…What?”

She looked over the stone.

The railings.

The stairs.

“I definitely sensed someone.”

Nothing.

Whoever had been there—

was gone.

“Did I imagine it?”

Bianca frowned.

The archers of Usher possessed extraordinary sight and hearing.

They were famous not only for archery—

but for physical agility and pursuit.

And Bianca was the eldest daughter of that house.

For someone to slip away after she noticed them—

inside the Academy itself—

made no sense.

“I must have been too sensitive.”

She started to turn away.

Then—

stopped.

A faint scent crossed her nose.

Strong rum.

There was no reason for that smell to linger on an empty rooftop staircase exposed only to wind and moonlight.

Bianca's expression hardened.

*Someone was here.*

That part was certain.

Someone had been standing there.

Then vanished.

And had escaped her pursuit.

*Impossible.*

When Bianca first sensed the presence—

the person had still been nearby.

If nothing remained now—

then whoever it was had moved faster than she had.

*Who?*

*A senior?*

*A professor?*

Neither explanation satisfied her.

Why would either run from her?

And in pure speed—

Bianca had enough confidence to believe even many upperclassmen and professors would struggle to lose her.

Her placement assessment had proven it.

“…Who was it?”

Bianca stood in the night breeze—

following the fading scent of rum.

Then—

“Bianca!”

A voice called from a window below.

“Hurry!”

“The freshman talent show is about to start!”

“…”

Bianca looked back once more.

Then reluctantly turned away.

The smell of strong rum remained behind her—

fading into the night.
