# Chapter 188: Aftermath (3)

Midterms—

half finished.

A quiet afternoon.

Sunlight on a café terrace.

“Busy lately?”

“Yes.”

Dolores sat across from Vikir.

Officially—

this was a club-leader check-in with a first-year member.

Unofficially—

Dolores had things she needed to say.

“About the accident…”

She looked uncomfortable.

“I'm sorry.”

“I didn't correct the misunderstanding quickly enough.”

“It grew into something ridiculous.”

“And…”

“Thank you for stepping in.”

Vikir nodded without hesitation.

“People make mistakes.”

“That's what makes us human.”

Dolores froze.

“…!”

A voice—

from another night—

echoed in her memory.

*People make mistakes.*

*That's what makes us human.*

The Night Hound.

The same comfort.

Different person.

*Coincidence.*

Obviously.

There were only so many ways to reassure someone.

Dolores almost laughed at herself.

Then returned to the practical reason for meeting.

The **Attack test**.

Vikir bowed his head slightly.

“The study notes you sent helped with the written exam.”

“Thank you.”

Killer Ant questions—

appeared often enough that choosing them as a free-research subject had been efficient.

Research once.

Use it in written preparation.

Use it again in Defense.

Reduce duplicated study.

Dolores smiled.

“Those study-note compilations aren't rare.”

“Most third-years have one.”

“Mine is just organized better than average.”

“What matters is what you did with it.”

“You turned ordinary notes into a legendary research result.”

Vikir nodded.

“Yes.”

Dolores blinked.

Then laughed.

“…You really do accept praise directly.”

What she had once interpreted as arrogance—

now seemed closer to simple confidence.

“Anyway.”

“I wanted to prepare you for the Attack test.”

“You handled the Defense exam well enough that you probably don't need advice.”

“But still.”

She raised a finger.

“Midterm weighting.”

“Written—ten percent.”

“Defense—fifty.”

“Attack—forty.”

Written and Defense—

done.

Only Attack remained.

And unlike Defense—

Attack was individual.

No party.

No tank.

No healer.

No one to cover mistakes.

The structure—

simple.

**Professor versus student.**

Not literally.

The professor stood at a distance—

controlling a humanoid stone golem.

Head.

Torso.

Two arms.

Two legs.

A basic human frame.

The golem mirrored the professor.

Movement.

Speed.

Combat style.

Students could attack it with full force.

No need to hold back.

The professor—

remained physically safe.

That was the purpose.

Measure the student's actual combat ability—

without forcing faculty to absorb lethal techniques.

Dolores raised her index finger.

“Problem one.”

“You're an archer.”

“In a one-on-one Attack test—”

“that's a disadvantage.”

During Defense—

archers benefited from front-line teammates.

Stand far away.

Finish weakened monsters.

Interrupt leaks.

Accumulate points.

Here—

there was no front line.

If the golem closed the distance—

the fight could end immediately.

And because the professor felt none of the golem's pain—

shots intended to hurt—

intimidate—

or disable nerves—

did nothing.

An archer had to keep moving.

Maintain distance.

Land high-power shots.

All while matching—

or exceeding—

a professor's combat tempo.

“That balances the Defense advantage.”

Dolores lifted a second finger.

“Problem two.”

“The professor assigned to your section is…”

Her expression darkened.

“**Professor Sadi.**”

Vikir narrowed his eyes.

Sadi.

Full name—

**Donatien Alphonse François Sadi de Sade.**

“The professor who disappeared with barrier Key B.”

“And was found dead drunk?”

Dolores sighed.

“…Yes.”

Sadi—

strictly speaking—

was a patronage appointment.

The **Sade family** had once been noble.

A marquis house.

Then scandal—

decline—

near extinction.

Sadi was its last surviving bloodline.

Rather than allow the descendant of an old high noble house to collapse completely—

the Academy had given her a professorship through the old networks of aristocratic courtesy.

Dolores rubbed her forehead.

“Her personality is strange.”

“Her work ethic is worse.”

“Students avoid her more than almost any professor.”

“Most courses assigned to her don't get enough enrollment to run.”

“I tried attending several.”

“Nine times out of ten—”

“she canceled without warning.”

“Usually the first class of the morning.”

Vikir looked at her.

“And the tenth?”

“She announced the cancellation in advance.”

“So…”

“She effectively taught none of them.”

Even Dolores—

usually gentle—

sounded exhausted.

Vikir had heard Banshee and other faculty complain about Sadi too.

Still—

Dolores gave the one point in her favor.

“She is strong.”

“Very strong.”

“Besides being an Academy professor—”

“she advises the Imperial Guard on arrest and interrogation.”

“And lately—”

“the military has used her in tracking criminals.”

“Which criminal?”

Dolores looked at him.

“Who else?”

Night Hound.

*So that's one of the trackers after me.*

Vikir considered it.

Then dismissed the thought.

Sadi's reputation—

lazy.

drunk.

resentful.

Why would someone like that suddenly become enthusiastic about hunting the Night Hound?

No answer yet.

Dolores—

still worried—

continued.

“She has another reputation.”

“When she sees a talented student—”

“she tries to ruin them.”

“How?”

“Excessive violence during sparring.”

“Serious injuries.”

“Deliberately leaving students traumatized.”

Dolores hesitated.

“There are also rumors of sexual humiliation or trauma.”

“Toward both men and women.”

Vikir's eyes narrowed.

“Why?”

“No one knows.”

“Some say she resents talented heirs from families that still have power—”

“because she is the last member of a fallen marquis house.”

“But that's only rumor.”

What was certain—

was the pattern.

Talented students.

High-status heirs.

Beautiful students.

Model students.

People Sadi liked breaking.

Vikir—

after the Defense test and written research—

had become an obvious target.

That was why Dolores was worried.

Then—

“Good afternoon!”

A bright voice interrupted.

“Your parfait and espresso.”

Clink.

Cold glass.

Hot cup.

The server smiled.

**Sinclaire.**

Dolores blinked.

“Sinclaire?”

“You work here?”

“Yes!”

“Weekend mornings normally.”

“I covered last night and this afternoon too.”

Beside her—

Bianca wore the café uniform with a much less enthusiastic face.

Dolores looked between them.

“Why?”

Sinclaire answered first.

“Tuition deadline is coming.”

“My first-year scholarship doesn't cover everything.”

“And I wanted to try working in a café while I'm a student.”

Still cheerful.

Still poor.

Bianca—

had a simpler reason.

“The uniform is pretty.”

Vikir tilted his head.

Dolores—

somehow—

understood immediately.

Sinclaire added cookies and pie.

Not ordered.

“Service.”

“My authority.”

She looked at Vikir.

“Do you like sweets?”

“No.”

“Then I'll remember that next time.”

Nothing dented her energy.

Then she asked—

“Have you paid tuition yet?”

“No.”

“When will you?”

Vikir paused.

He genuinely had not thought about it much.

Too busy.

He had refused Baskerville support.

Cindywendy—

currently difficult to contact directly—

was not someone he wanted to use for small personal transfers anyway.

His scholarship covered most of the expense.

The remainder—

not enormous.

Vikir looked toward the café.

“I have a question.”

That alone—

made all three women look at him.

Vikir rarely volunteered questions.

Then—

“Do you hire men?”
