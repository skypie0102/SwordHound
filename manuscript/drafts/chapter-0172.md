Vikir took the article Pigi held out.

> **[Exclusive] The Night Hound—An Unprecedented Villain Sweeping the Imperial Capital!?**
>
> The Night Hound has appeared again.
>
> An unprecedented series of terrorist attacks now hangs over the Imperial Capital, yet the true nature of the threat remains unknown.
>
> Is the Night Hound one person?
>
> An organization?
>
> What does he want?
>
> No one knows.
>
> Not even the Imperial House or any of the Seven Great Houses has uncovered his identity.
>
> Attention is now focused on one question:
>
> **Who will expose this vicious criminal first—and bring him to justice?**
>
> The Seven Great Houses—Baskerville, Morgue, Donquixote, Usher, Leviathan, Bourgeois, and Quovadis—have joined the pursuit.
>
> A massive bounty has been placed on the Night Hound in the Imperial Capital…

Several magically animated photographs sat beneath the article.

The newspaper—

a major publication outside the Academy—

showed branches of Quovadis that the Night Hound had attacked.

Most prominently—

Old Testament-affiliated sites.

At the bottom—

a photograph of the ruins at Indulgentia Orphanage.

Vikir appeared in one corner.

So did Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Sinclaire.

Nothing suspicious.

Just a cluster of Academy volunteers at a disaster site.

Pigi leaned over the page.

“Look!”

“We're in the newspaper!”

“I never thought I'd appear in mass media in my life!”

He was delighted by his tiny role as background scenery.

Then—

his excitement died.

“…Wait.”

“This is a photograph of an awful disaster.”

“And I'm happy to be in it.”

“I'm horrible.”

“Calm down.”

Vikir patted his shoulder.

“The person who caused the disaster is responsible.”

“Not you.”

Then he looked back at the paper.

“…No article about the Saintess declaring holy war.”

Pigi nodded.

“Yeah.”

“That's strange.”

“They left out the attacks on the Old Testament branches too.”

“And the history of selling indulgences to powerful people.”

“And all the crimes those indulgences supposedly absolved.”

“Why didn't any of that make the paper?”

Vikir smiled dryly.

That—

was what the press often became.

A trumpet for the powerful.

A dog barking for the better piece of meat.

In the wild—

you bit only what you could survive biting.

*Cardinal Humbert's influence reaches farther than I thought.*

*Or someone above him intervened.*

No matter how Vikir searched—

he found no serious condemnation of the Old Testament scandal.

Instead—

every page seemed determined to make the Night Hound the public enemy.

Then Pigi remembered something.

“Oh.”

“Vikir!”

“Did you hear this week's club assignment?”

“Professor Banshee wants everyone to write a column condemning the Night Hound's terrorism.”

“Just a short current-events commentary.”

“If it's good enough, even a first-year's piece might be published in the Academy paper.”

Apparently—

Colosseo had decided to join the hunt too.

Vikir nodded.

*Better to move with the current.*

*Less suspicion that way.*

No one could condemn the Night Hound more convincingly than Vikir.

No one knew his “crimes” better.

*A column…*

*So I just write down the most ridiculous things I've heard people say about me?*

He took out a pen.

Then began—

a difficult battle with himself.

◆◆◆

“Why was my column rejected?”

Dolores stood inside Ryukeion's club room.

Across from her—

**Professor Banshee Morgue**.

Faculty advisor.

His dark eyes looked even harsher beneath black hair.

A monocle rested near the end of his hooked nose.

Banshee tossed Dolores's manuscript onto the desk.

> **Where Does the Night Hound's True Opposition Lie?**
>
> More than six Quovadis branches have been attacked by the Night Hound.
>
> Every one belongs to the faction commonly called the Old Testament.
>
> Materials released from the New Testament side indicate secret dealings between some Old Testament figures and prominent members of society.
>
> At the same time, no conclusive evidence that the Night Hound himself committed murder has yet been found at these scenes…

“The Academy is politically neutral.”

Banshee's voice was flat.

“A partisan column like this cannot be published.”

“Partisan?”

Dolores stiffened.

“I reported facts.”

“Facts?”

“Cardinal Humbert denies them.”

“So do the prominent people named in the indulgence records.”

“They're lying!”

“Perhaps that is *your* truth.”

Banshee folded his arms.

“You once insisted that we should not create ten innocent people merely to catch one criminal.”

“Wasn't that your principle?”

“…”

Dolores bit her lip so hard a vein stood out in her neck.

Banshee continued.

“You are the student council president.”

“A leading figure in the newspaper club.”

“And everyone knows you're the Saintess of Quovadis.”

“Everyone also knows Saintesses historically align more often with the New Testament side.”

He tapped the paper.

“Do you really think *you* personally writing this creates no appearance of bias?”

Dolores's forehead tightened.

Then she answered.

“Academy rules state that all enrolled students are equal.”

“And that status outside Colosseo does not determine status within it.”

“I am a Quovadis outside the Academy.”

“I belong to the New Testament Faction outside the Academy.”

“But while I'm here—”

“I am also simply Dolores.”

“A student.”

“So I have the same right as any other student to express an opinion about Quovadis.”

This time—

Banshee fell silent.

Their eyes held.

Then he sighed.

“…Fine.”

“I'll concede that point.”

Dolores blinked.

“Then why—”

“Quovadis sent the Academy an official document.”

“…What document?”

“Reporting guidelines.”

Dolores went still.

Guidelines—

instructions defining in advance how an event should be covered.

Banshee shook his head.

“The requested direction is clear.”

“Focus coverage on the Night Hound's terrorist acts.”

“Interview victims.”

“And wherever possible—”

“minimize or remove the words *Quovadis*, *Old Testament*, and *New Testament*.”

Dolores stared.

“That is an attempt to control the Academy's neutrality!”

“Nevertheless.”

“The dean is in a difficult position.”

“Cardinal Humbert has that much influence?”

“If it were merely Humbert—”

Banshee's expression hardened.

“I would ignore him.”

Dolores's face changed.

Inside Quovadis—

only one authority stood clearly above Humbert.

The **Pope**.

*No.*

*Surely not.*

Dolores had always believed the Pope—

oldest and most powerful among the classical holy figures—

would remain untouched by corruption.

*He's become very old lately.*

*His judgment has reportedly weakened.*

*Could Father be controlling what reaches his eyes and ears?*

It was only a suspicion.

But a new and unpleasant one.

Banshee tapped her rejected column again.

“And even without the external pressure—”

“this isn't suitable for the Academy paper.”

“It reads as though you're quietly defending the Night Hound.”

Dolores shot back—

“Then what will you fill the space with?”

“A theater-club advertisement?”

“A sports-club highlight reel?”

“Another dramatic behind-the-scenes piece about the Academy magazine cover model?”

“Don't be sarcastic.”

Banshee's eyes narrowed.

“I am the professor.”

“You are the student.”

He opened a drawer.

Removed another page.

A column.

Dolores read.

> **Evil Beyond the Line — The Night Hound**
>
> The Night Hound has crossed the line.
>
> His insults against the Imperial House and Seven Great Houses have become intolerable.
>
> The voices of innocent victims demand justice.
>
> Spears, swords, arrows, and magic will soon become the hammer that brings him before judgment.
>
> As one who personally saw the wounded Night Hound on the night of the Indulgentia Orphanage disaster, this writer can say only this:
>
> **Surrender now. It may be your last chance for a peaceful end.**

“I intend to publish this instead.”

Dolores stared at him.

“No evidence.”

“No objectivity.”

“No credibility.”

“Just emotional incitement.”

“Vague language.”

“Excessive rhetoric.”

For Dolores—

that was almost vicious profanity.

Banshee gave a thin smile.

“I agree that it isn't well written.”

“But it condemns.”

“It appeals.”

“It agitates.”

“In the present climate—”

“that makes it useful.”

“And the writer claims eyewitness status.”

“So it has at least some apparent credibility.”

Dolores said nothing.

“Midterms are near.”

Banshee rose.

“Study.”

“A student's first duty is still academics.”

He passed Dolores—

and left.

She remained behind—

jaw tight.

The Indulgentia disaster.

That night—

she had stood beside the Night Hound.

*The Night Hound isn't evil.*

*The demons hiding inside Quovadis were.*

How many more were there?

How many battles had he already fought?

How many still waited?

*And instead of helping him—*

*we're obstructing him.*

There was no easy way to reveal every demon—

every accomplice—

every person being manipulated without realizing it.

Even if such a method existed—

it would take time.

The Night Hound had already told her—

time was short.

Now—

after speaking with Banshee—

Dolores understood something more clearly.

Why the Night Hound had become an assassin.

Why he risked his life killing targets one by one.

“No.”

“I have to help him.”

A newspaper defense would change little.

If the Academy had already imposed reporting restrictions—

the pen was not enough.

She needed something more direct.

*I'll find the Night Hound myself.*

*See his real face.*

*Learn his name.*

*Then I'll be able to help properly.*

First—

she needed information.

Anything that might identify him.

Dolores's gaze returned to the replacement column.

A sentence stood out.

> **As one who personally saw the wounded Night Hound on the night of the Indulgentia Orphanage disaster…**

“Personally saw?”

The timing implied the writer had seen the Night Hound after the battle was nearly over.

Closer than most.

Closer—

perhaps—

than Dolores.

*Maybe the writer saw beneath the mask.*

Her pulse quickened.

Once the article appeared—

outside reporters would swarm whoever wrote it.

Fortunately—

Banshee had anonymized the byline.

Dolores—

as a club leader—

could still see the unpublished original.

*Abuse of authority.*

*But necessary for justice.*

She turned the page.

Found the author field.

Then froze.

A familiar name.

> **Cold Department, Class B — Vikir**
