# Chapter 270: Rich Friends (1)

“…They were people I could never forgive.”

Dolores sat atop the water tank of an abandoned building.

No one else was around.

The mass death of so many heretics and cultists had been frightening to witness.

But those same groups had committed atrocities far from the Church’s reach.

They preyed on the sick.

On families desperate for cures.

They frightened people.

Threatened them.

Accumulated wealth through exploitation—

then used that money to lobby central authorities and expand their influence.

To uproot organizations like that completely—

a simple and radical method had been necessary.

That was why Dolores had cooperated with Night Hound.

And the result—

was now in Vikir’s hands.

The force behind the network had finally surfaced.

An organization that cultivated provincial cults and heresies.

Used them to launder and move money.

Then slowly corroded nearly half of Quovadis from within.

At the end of the long chain—

was one name.

**Bourgeois.**

Vikir handed Dolores the ledger recovered from the scene of Ms. Ouroboros’s massacre.

“If you can handle it—

read it.”

His voice was flat.

But beneath it—

a cold warning.

Almost as though he were drawing a boundary.

Dolores had already made her choice.

Whatever thorn-covered path Night Hound entered—

she intended to follow.

She opened the ledger.

Rustle.

“…This is—!”

She did not need to study it for long.

**Bourgeois** appeared on almost every page.

Again.

Again.

Again.

“…How could this…”

Dolores’s voice trembled.

The objective seemed obvious.

Fund cults and heresies.

Use them to weaken Quovadis.

Encourage the Old and New Testament factions to split farther apart.

Then swallow both sides.

Control the Faithful Holy House from behind.

It was a silent war—

between the Empire’s great houses.

Bourgeois against Quovadis.

No armies.

No battlefield.

No blood spilled openly.

Yet the cruelty of the conflict was extraordinary.

*Morgue and Baskerville’s feud looks childish compared with this.*

Vikir remembered what Decarabia had told him.

“…This may have been something the Ninth and Sixth Corpses designed together.”

Dantalian—

the Ninth Corpse—

had been close to the Sixth.

Perhaps the Sixth was now completing a picture the two had drawn together.

Dolores looked back at the ledger.

“Bourgeois funds cults and heresies.”

“Those groups use that money to buy enormous quantities of indulgences.”

“Because their organizations and accounting are secretive by nature—

they’re perfect intermediaries.”

“The dirty money becomes clean through indulgence purchases.”

“And then enters the Old Testament Faction.”

Vikir nodded.

“Exactly.”

“Indulgence sales are tax-exempt by Imperial recognition.”

“Quovadis had no reason to treat the incoming money as suspicious.”

Dolores’s expression tightened.

“We accepted free sweets without questioning them.”

“And became fat, lazy pigs.”

“That’s what Quovadis has become.”

Vikir answered with one sentence.

“That reminds me of a Bourgeois family motto.”

Dolores looked up.

“…What is it?”

“There is no free lunch.”

A brutal summary of the world they were dealing with.

Dolores swallowed.

Why would Bourgeois target Quovadis?

The answer was simple.

Faith.

Piety.

Loyalty.

Things supposedly beyond the reach of money.

But if money could buy even those—

then wealth truly became absolute power.

“The sword of Baskerville.”

“The spear of Donquixote.”

“The bow of Usher.”

“The poison of Leviathan.”

“The magic of Morgue.”

Dolores lowered her eyes.

“I knew all of those could be bought.”

“But I thought at least faith itself could not.”

Vikir did not comfort her.

The investigation was not finished.

“Now that we know the Bourgeois family’s objective—

we find the real enemy hidden inside it.”

An individual within the group.

The one who had to be found—

and removed.

*Most likely the Sixth Corpse.*

Dolores hesitated.

“When we posted the Ninety-Five Refutations against the Old Testament—

they barely reacted.”

“And now we’re talking about confronting the Bourgeois family behind them.”

“Can we really do that?”

“Yes.”

Vikir answered without hesitation.

Dolores stared.

Vikir’s voice stayed firm.

“If we have power.”

Money.

Law.

Institutions.

Status.

The Bourgeois family was protected by all of them.

So how did one attack an enemy armored by society itself?

Force.

Quiet.

Hidden.

Overwhelming.

Violence.

*When Dolores exposed Indulgentia corruption before, even that failed to reach Bourgeois itself.*

*A frontal fight won’t work.*

Dolores watched Vikir’s back.

Her expression stiffened.

“…Are you planning to assassinate the core members of House Bourgeois?”

“Yes.”

For the first time—

Vikir openly shared an assassination plan with another person.

The memory of the Dantalian battle mattered.

Dolores’s temporary awakening had forced his strength up an entire realm.

*In a crisis—

that kind of help can matter.*

She still could not activate it intentionally.

But unpredictable things happened in real battles.

Dolores frowned.

“I’ve heard Bourgeois spends absurd amounts of money preventing assassination.”

“This could be difficult.”

Vikir nodded.

People with much to lose—

naturally feared losing it.

Even before regression—

Bourgeois elites had poured fortunes into security.

Private soldiers.

Mercenaries.

Guarded residences.

Guarded exchanges.

Guarded stores.

Constantly changing travel routes.

Multiple decoy carriages.

Secret movements.

And the elites themselves were not helpless.

From childhood—

they spent enormous sums on private tutors.

Martial training.

Magic.

Weapons.

They were individually capable too.

“Most Bourgeois elites live in heavily guarded estates.”

“Private forces.”

“Mercenaries.”

“Layer after layer of security.”

Dolores shook her head.

“How do we sneak in?”

“We don’t.”

Vikir answered.

“If they’re hard to find—

we make them come to us.”

“…How?”

Vikir rubbed his chin.

“An assassin needs many skills.”

“What qualities do you think one needs?”

Dolores—

who had never lived as an assassin—

tried anyway.

“Skill at killing?”

“And?”

“Stamina.”

“Agility.”

“Caution.”

“Patience.”

“Courage.”

“Nerve.”

“Judgment in emergencies.”

“And the discipline not to reveal information if captured?”

“One more.”

Dolores tilted her head.

Vikir supplied the answer.

“You need to know how to make money.”

“…?”

Dolores stared.

Vikir continued calmly.

“Sometimes money and reputation are required to get close to a target.”

“If an organization funds you, that helps.”

“But if the funding disappears—

or becomes unusable—

you need to raise operating money locally.”

Real assassinations were rarely like fiction.

Funds were not always waiting.

Especially when the target was wealthy and socially protected.

“The reason we need money here is simple.”

“Opportunities to eat lunch or dinner with senior Bourgeois figures are often auctioned.”

Dolores blinked.

“You pay just to eat lunch with them?”

“A lot.”

“More than an ordinary person earns in a lifetime.”

The buyer received more than a meal.

Investment information.

Connections.

Access.

Hints about the future.

“For example—

a lunch with Damien Bourgeois, the family’s second son—

costs around one hundred kilograms of gold.”

Dolores sucked in a breath.

That much money could build several temples in the slums.

For a New Testament Saintess perpetually short on funds—

the number was absurd.

Vikir spoke as if reassuring her.

“Don’t worry.”

“I’m rich.”

“Rich enough that Bourgeois elites will approach me themselves.”

Dolores looked surprised.

Then—

without thinking—

she inspected Night Hound from head to toe.

“…But you look poor.”

She had seen him during the Red Death outbreak.

Giving away every coin in his pockets to poor people.

Vikir paused.

Then corrected himself.

“I…”

Silence.

Then—

“I have a rich friend.”
