# Chapter 131: The Freshman Talent Show (2)

The old professors had tears in their eyes.

Age changed the way people heard the song.

The younger students had mostly treated Vikir's performance as strange.

Too serious.

Too old-fashioned.

But several veteran professors sat in complete silence.

Then one spoke.

“…I've never heard a military song hit like that.”

His voice shook.

“It felt like someone reached inside and read my memories.”

Another old professor stared down into his cup.

“It reminded me of the final fronts before the Empire was unified.”

His eyes unfocused.

“Fifty years ago…”

“So many died far from home.”

Names followed.

Old comrades.

Officers.

Enlisted men.

People who had not returned.

Another professor lowered his head.

“To everyone I left behind…”

His hands trembled.

“I'm sorry I'm the one who lived.”

The room quieted further.

These men had spent half their lives on battlefields.

Younger professors and teaching assistants who had experienced smaller wars also understood enough to be affected.

Some drank in silence.

Some wiped their eyes.

One veteran could hear another veteran inside the crude song.

That was the difference.

To the freshmen—

the performance was simply unusual.

To people who had buried comrades—

it carried a weight the young students could not yet understand.

And none of them knew the truth.

A person who had crossed from another point in time had just performed a memorial—

for men who had once been his seniors, superiors, and comrades.

Men who were alive again now.

Elsewhere in the room—

Tudor Donquixote scratched his head.

Beside him sat Sancho Barataria.

The two had been assigned the same room.

Tudor glanced toward Vikir.

“What was that?”

“He sings military songs like an old man.”

Then he nudged Sancho.

“You came from the mercenary guilds.”

“Have you ever heard that one?”

Sancho shook his head.

“Not in the north.”

He looked thoughtful.

“But it was good.”

“I'd like to learn it.”

Tudor leaned back.

“Maybe he has a veteran in the family?”

“He looks our age.”

Then his eyes narrowed slightly.

“Wait.”

“Wasn't he the written-exam first place?”

Sancho grunted.

“For a bookworm, he has more spirit than I expected.”

He looked amused.

“Singing a war song at a talent show takes nerve.”

The young prodigies still could not truly understand the emotion behind it.

Then—

someone interrupted.

“Excuse me.”

A girl with short white hair had approached from the Hot Department gathering.

**Sinclaire.**

She looked toward Tudor and Sancho.

“You're both in the Cold Department, right?”

“Yes.”

“Do you know the person who just sang?”

Tudor shook his head.

“Different class.”

Sancho did the same.

“We don't know him.”

Tudor glanced at her.

“Why?”

Sinclaire watched Vikir and Pigi moving away.

She had been paying attention earlier.

She had seen Pigi freeze when his name was called.

Seen the panic.

Seen Vikir place a hand on his shoulder and step forward without hesitation.

Sinclaire smiled faintly.

“No big reason.”

Then—

“He seems kind.”

Her eyes followed Vikir.

“I thought I'd like to know him.”

◆◆◆

Vikir returned toward the tables.

Before he could sit—

several elderly professors came over.

“Was your father a soldier?”

“I've never heard that song before.”

“Is it from another country?”

Another professor leaned in.

“What?”

“You composed it yourself?”

He looked genuinely shocked.

“A song like that?”

“Our Royal Academy sponsors military-song competitions.”

“You should enter.”

A third professor laughed.

“Forget that.”

“Take my composition elective.”

Vikir found the attention awkward.

But he did not refuse the drinks they offered.

How could he?

Some of these men—

in his previous life—

had been commanders.

Others had shielded younger officers in battle.

Some had entered enemy territory to save subordinates and died there.

Now—

they were younger.

Healthy.

Alive.

Holding cups toward him with no idea who he was.

Colosseo gathered heroes of the past, present, and future in one place.

It was inevitable that Vikir would encounter people tied to memories of his first life.

He accepted each drink.

Then returned toward his seat.

And stopped.

Bianca Fo Usher stood directly in his path.

Vikir gave no reaction.

He stepped around her.

Then—

“The rooftop.”

Vikir kept walking.

Bianca turned.

“…Rum.”

Still no reaction.

Vikir considered stopping.

He decided against it.

Step.

Step.

Step.

He continued as though he had no idea she was speaking to him.

Bianca's eyes narrowed.

The smell.

That same harsh rum.

The person she had chased on the rooftop earlier had carried it too.

No matter how hard Bianca had run—

she had failed to close the distance.

That had never happened to her so completely.

She had lost races before.

But not like that.

Not to someone she could not even identify.

“Wait.”

Bianca moved after him.

“You were on the rooftop earlier—”

Then—

“Cheers!”

“Drink!”

“Drink! Drink! Drink!”

A group of drunken upperclassmen surged between them.

Glasses collided.

Alcohol splashed.

Rum.

Beer.

Wine.

Cheap spirits.

The air filled with competing smells.

“Move!”

Bianca pushed through.

But once the cluster broke apart—

Vikir was gone.

“…Where did he go?”

She looked left.

Right.

Nothing.

Then she stopped.

There was rum everywhere now.

Strong enough to overwhelm the trace she had noticed.

*Was I mistaken?*

Bianca frowned.

The sharp smell she had caught on Vikir—

gone.

Buried under the alcohol filling the room.

Maybe she had imagined the connection.

Maybe even the rooftop smell itself had been misleading.

*…Probably.*

She looked away.

The Vikir she had just seen looked tired.

Plain.

Unremarkable.

Nothing about him suggested the speed or strength necessary to escape her pursuit.

And the strange military song had meant nothing to her personally.

There was little reason to keep watching him.

Across the room—

Vikir stood where Bianca was no longer looking.

*Sharp senses.*

She had picked up the rum left on his cloak after his work outside the Academy.

Fortunately—

the gathering was full of drunk students.

Their alcohol had swallowed his scent.

Vikir looked toward Bianca.

She searched a little longer.

Then gave up.

*I shouldn't underestimate them just because they're young.*

He mentally reviewed the Seven Great Families.

**Baskerville**—

swordsmanship.

**Morgue**—

magic.

**Quovadis**—

divinity.

**Donquixote**—

spearmanship.

**Usher**—

archery.

**Leviathan**—

poison.

**Bourgeois**—

wealth.

Even freshmen from those houses were still descendants of extraordinary lineages.

Carelessness would be a mistake.

Vikir exhaled quietly.

Then—

felt another gaze.

He turned slightly.

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

Student council president.

Head of the newspaper club.

She was looking directly at him.

Her expression was thoughtful.

*What is it?*

Dolores tilted her head.

*Why does he feel familiar?*

Vikir adjusted his bangs.

Pushed his glasses into place.

Then closed himself off as completely as possible—

careful not to let the saintess read anything deeper from him.

Harmless.

Ordinary.

Forgettable.

Fortunately—

Dolores's attention lasted only a few seconds.

Several senior professors called for her.

The department chair wanted to speak with her.

Her gaze finally moved away.

Vikir slipped farther from her line of sight.

*Enough.*

*Time to return to the dormitory.*

The gathering was winding down.

Some students were already too drunk to walk and were being helped out.

Vikir intended to disappear with the general movement.

Then—

voices stopped him.

“Hey.”

A second-year laughed.

“That face you made during the talent show was hilarious.”

Another voice followed.

“Must be nice to ride on your roommate's coattails.”

“Are you a parasite?”

“Come on.”

“Sing something by yourself.”

“Your seniors are asking.”

“Why aren't you doing it?”

A group of second-years had surrounded Pigi.

Pigi stood in the middle—

shrinking under their attention.

His face was red from alcohol.

“S-seniors…”

He hiccuped.

“I don't really have any talents.”

“And I'm not funny…”

The second-years laughed.

“No talent?”

“What kind of Academy life is that?”

“Dance.”

“You did it earlier.”

“All you did was wave your fists and stomp around.”

“Do it again.”

Another shoved a drink toward him.

“If you can't perform—drink.”

“Finish this and we'll leave you alone.”

“One shot.”

“Be a man.”

The group laughed as if they had found a toy.

Something easy to push around.

Someone unlikely to resist.

Vikir watched from a short distance away.

The second-years were smiling.

He was not.
