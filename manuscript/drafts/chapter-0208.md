Vikir—

still dressed as a witch—

stir-fried vegetables and meat.

Ingredients—

limited.

Quality—

mediocre.

Possibilities—

not.

*Memories.*

*We ate things like this at the front.*

He softened hard rations in milk.

The Age of Destruction—

had taught soldiers to cook.

Not because they enjoyed it.

Because they had to survive.

Supply wagons—

burned by demons.

Food that survived—

taken by higher command.

What reached the front—

wilted.

rotted.

spoiled.

So soldiers learned.

Which poisonous parts—

to remove.

How to rescue old meat.

How to tame bitter vegetables.

How to blunt overpowering spices.

Boil.

Stew.

Roast.

Steam.

Fry.

Make something edible—

out of almost nothing.

Vikir—

decades of battlefield life behind him—

had learned from everyone.

Mercenaries.

Soldiers.

Cooks who were not really cooks.

*For simple tavern food—*

*I can probably outperform the students here.*

He picked up—

a Morgue potato.

Earthy.

Dry.

Texture—

like sand.

He sliced it thin.

Salt.

Vinegar.

Mandrake leaves.

Sabac fruit.

High heat.

*That removes the earthiness.*

*Improves sweetness.*

*Softens the texture.*

He had done this before.

Long ago.

At a Morgue fortress.

A girl's voice—

returned.

“Your house doesn't have potatoes like these, right?”

Then—

the memory shifted.

**Camus Morgue.**

Eyes bright—

after tasting the dish.

“Hey.”

“Why don't you come to my house and cook?”

“….”

“I'll keep you.”

“Speaking of which—

when are you entering the Academy?”

“Let's coordinate.”

“I'll probably enroll a year or two early.”

“If we became first-years together—

that would be fun…”

A future—

she had drawn casually.

As if both of them—

would obviously be there.

Vikir's hand paused.

*Where is Camus now?*

*What is she doing?*

Then—

another thought.

*I still have room to wonder about someone else.*

That—

surprised him.

*So there is still feeling left in me.*

The conclusion—

fit too neatly—

with what he had been considering since the bath.

Emotion.

Seventh Fang.

Sword Master.

Vikir shook his head.

Returned to the pan.

*I'll probably see her soon enough.*

When thoughts became noisy—

hands could work.

Chicken.

Potatoes.

Red seasoning.

The same nameless dish—

front-line soldiers once ate while sitting on demon corpses—

now became—

a festival bestseller.

Vikir looked outside.

People—

eating.

Smiling.

Good food—

made people happy.

*It was like this then too.*

His future comrades—

dead in the history he remembered—

had eaten his cooking.

Laughed.

Complained.

Asked for more.

Now—

those same people existed again.

Young.

Alive.

Unbroken.

Vikir tightened his grip on the spatula.

*For them—*

*I have to live properly now.*

Kill more demons.

Cross more battlefields.

Become stronger.

But also—

live this day.

This moment.

One task—

at a time.

Across the room—

Dolores stared.

“…Wow.”

She tried—

not to.

Her eyes—

returned anyway.

Dolores had always believed—

she valued character over appearance.

Which was true.

But apparently—

that did not mean appearance had no effect.

Perhaps—

she had simply never seen a face that genuinely affected her before.

She looked away.

“Why does he have to be a witch…?”

Saintess.

Witch.

Opposites.

That—

was the explanation she chose.

People behind her—

were less restrained.

“He's unbelievably handsome.”

Dolores flinched.

For half a second—

she thought she had spoken aloud.

She had not.

Outside the fence—

a crowd.

Mostly—

students and visitors in zombie costumes.

They pressed against the barrier—

trying to see the kitchen.

“Vikir!”

“Look over here!”

“One picture!”

“How is that fair!?”

The comments grew—

increasingly ridiculous.

Someone claimed Vikir should win every beauty award in the Empire.

Someone else—

that his face could unify nations.

Another—

that every other attractive person should simply retire.

Tudor and Sinclaire—

heard only one thing.

“Sales are exploding!”

“More number tickets!”

Prices—

increased.

Customers—

still paid.

Many were nobles.

Money—

not the problem.

Then—

the rumor spread.

The witch—

was actually male.

Women flooded in.

The male customers—

did not leave.

If anything—

more arrived.

“Pretty is pretty.”

“Does it matter?”

“I heard the food is incredible too.”

Even Banshee—

had claimed a table.

“Vikir.”

“Sorry to interrupt.”

“Can I add toppings?”

“And bring me the new dish.”

Bianca returned from advertising.

Saw Vikir—

pan in one hand.

ladle in the other.

“What are you doing?”

“Why are you hiding in the kitchen!?”

“Go outside!”

“You are wasting your face!”

Vikir nodded.

Put the ladle down.

Immediately—

Tudor appeared.

“No!”

“The kitchen dies without him!”

Kitchen faction.

Advertising faction.

Two camps—

formed instantly.

Tudor pointed.

“Vikir is essential!”

“People are coming for the food!”

Bianca shouted back.

“He is wearing the perfect witch costume!”

“Every time he steps outside—

sales double!”

Behind Tudor—

cooks.

Behind Bianca—

hawkers.

“Don't disrespect the kitchen!”

“The festival lives or dies by attracting customers!”

Then—

a voice.

“Don't fight.”

Someone reached in—

and pulled Vikir away.

Vikir looked up.

“…?”

Familiar.

Very familiar.

And—

supposed to be far away—

on the western frontier.
