# Chapter 30: Special Laws of Vikir (3)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/030.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 30 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Legal amounts, sequence, Judy's gender, and punishment semantics follow the Chinese source rather than MTL drift.

<div class="info-window">
<p>Vikir's Special Law, Article 1, Section 1</p>
<p>Whoever pulls this wooden stake (hereinafter “the stake”) from the ground shall immediately receive 100,000,000 gold from Deputy Magistrate Vikir Van Baskerville.</p>
</div>

This was the first law the fifteen-year-old Deputy Magistrate enacted after taking office.

Not a single person in the legislative department objected.

They had no idea what the law was supposed to accomplish—or why Vikir had created it in the first place.

The citizens looked bewildered.

“What? A hundred million?”

“You have to be joking. A hundred million gold is four years' wages for an ordinary person.”

“All we have to do is pull out one wooden stake?”

“What kind of show is this?”

Whispers ran through the crowd.

No one understood Vikir's intentions.

The same was true of the officials from the enforcement division, including Chihuahua Baskerville.

The murmuring grew louder, but still no one stepped forward.

Vikir spoke again.

“No one?”

There was no answer.

So Vikir picked up a pen and added several more lines to the notice.

<div class="info-window">
<p>Vikir's Special Law, Article 1, Section 2</p>
<p>Whoever pulls this stake from the ground shall receive an additional 900,000,000 gold from Deputy Magistrate Vikir Van Baskerville.</p>
</div>

One hundred million, plus nine hundred million more.

“Whoever pulls out this stake will receive one billion gold in cash.”

One billion gold.

It was more money than an ordinary person could hope to touch in an entire lifetime.

The crowd began to laugh.

“This is ridiculous.”

“Is he making fun of us? Who's going to step out there?”

“Hey, kid! Stop fooling around and go home to bed!”

“Now that I look at it, this is actually pretty funny. Is this supposed to be comedy?”

The absurdity finally broke the crowd's restraint.

Some openly mocked Vikir as a clown.

He remained perfectly calm.

“No one?”

“No one~!”

“Not me~!”

Laughing answers came from every direction.

The officials behind Vikir, Chihuahua included, turned bright red.

‘Why are we the ones being humiliated?’

They fidgeted under the citizens' ridicule.

Vikir merely raised his pen again.

<div class="info-window">
<p>Vikir's Special Law, Article 1, Section 3</p>
<p>Whoever pulls this stake from the ground shall receive an additional 9,000,000,000 gold from Deputy Magistrate Vikir Van Baskerville.</p>
</div>

Ten billion in total.

The instant that number appeared, the square fell silent.

Utterly silent.

“Whoever pulls out this stake will receive ten billion gold in cash.”

Vikir's third clause had been proclaimed.

The central square, which moments before had been filled with mockery and jeering, suddenly felt devoid of life.

Countless people stood packed together without making a sound.

Even the enforcement officials found the vast silence unnerving.

Vikir remained unruffled.

“No one?”

This time, no one could answer.

The citizens no longer dared laugh.

They only glanced at one another.

“W-What is this? What's going on?”

“Is he really going to pay ten billion? Should I try?”

“Are you insane? Who knows what'll happen if you step forward?”

“Look at the sword on his waist. What if he decides anyone who accepts the challenge is insulting him and executes them on the spot?”

Nothing made sense.

Why would a newly appointed Deputy Magistrate do something like this?

Why would he throw away ten billion gold over a wooden stake anyone could pull from the ground?

No one in the square could see the answer.

“Maybe the stake is unbelievably heavy. Maybe an ordinary person can't pull it out.”

“No. We all watched the officials carry it over here without any trouble.”

“Don't go out there. There has to be some other trap.”

“Better not to make yourself stand out at a time like this.”

Only quiet whispers disturbed the square.

“...”

Vikir lifted his pen once more.

Just as he was about to add a fourth clause—

“I... I'll try!”

A small hand rose from the crowd.

A girl in worn-out clothes stepped forward with a basket of wilted flowers.

She walked with a limp, and the signs of malnutrition were plain on her face.

The moment she raised her hand, a kind-looking old man nearby hurried over to stop her.

He appeared to be a neighbor.

“Child! Judy! That's you, isn't it? Do you understand where you are? You can't just step forward here!”

“Grandpa, I'll be fine. My mother's sick. I have to do something. Lately the thugs have been causing trouble, and I can't even sell my flowers...”

“Oh, you foolish child! You're too innocent! If you stand out at a time like this, they could cut your head off! How can you be so naive about the world?”

“Hehe. If something happens to me, please look after my mother.”

Judy slipped past the old man and walked into the square.

Vikir looked down at her with a cold expression.

“Pull it out.”

Judy bowed her head as low as she could.

The citizens watched the thin girl anxiously.

Some pitied her.

Some mocked her.

Some were simply eager to see what would happen next.

Judy herself was sweating from fear.

‘He wouldn't really kill me... would he?’

She had made herself the center of a public spectacle.

Maybe, at the very least, he would give her a little money for being made a fool of.

‘Enough to cover the price of one basket of flowers would be fine.’

Then she could buy a bowl of hot meat porridge for her sick mother.

Under Vikir's icy gaze, Judy reached out.

Her thin, trembling fingers closed around the stake.

Then—

Pop!

The stake came free.

It had been exactly as light as it looked.

Even a starving girl could pull it from the ground.

“...”

Judy stared at the stake in surprise.

The crowd and the enforcement officials were equally stunned.

“She pulled it out.”

“She really did.”

“It's out.”

“Someone finally pulled it out.”

The whispers grew louder and louder.

Now everyone wanted to know what would happen to the girl.

The huge crowd held its breath.

Then—

“...”

Vikir raised one hand.

He reached toward his waist, where his sword hung.

Judy squeezed her eyes shut.

‘He's going to execute me.’

She did not know what charge he might invent.

Insulting a noble?

Insolence?

Greed?

Something like that.

In that brief instant, Judy thought of the people precious to her.

If she died here, what would happen to her mother?

“No!”

A desperate scream burst from the crowd.

A painfully thin woman pushed her way forward.

Her clothes were little more than rags, and her body was reduced to skin and bone. She looked half-dead already, yet somehow she found the strength to throw her arms around Judy and kneel before Vikir.

“My lord! Please! Have mercy!”

Judy's mother knelt with her daughter at Vikir's feet.

“We lowly people dared to presume we understood the intentions of someone above us. Please forgive her just this once! She's a good child... a truly good child! Ever since her father died, she's cared for her sick mother and sold flowers on the street every day...”

The woman broke into sobs.

“Please show mercy... People as lowly as us aren't even worthy of staining your blade with our blood. Please, just this once, spare us...”

She pressed her forehead against the ground and begged for her daughter's life.

Her cries stirred the crowd.

“...She's right! What did Judy do wrong?”

“You won't find a harder-working or kinder child in this market!”

“What crime did that mother and daughter commit?”

“You think having power means you can do anything? What gives you the right to torment Judy?”

The atmosphere in the square turned dangerous.

A crowd that had been deathly silent moments earlier began to boil.

Chihuahua and the other officials looked ready to panic as the mood edged toward a riot.

And then—

Vikir reached toward Judy.

His hand passed over the hilt of his sword.

Behind it hung a leather pouch.

Grab—

Vikir lifted the heavy pouch and held it out to her.

Judy looked blankly from the bag to Vikir's face.

Then she accepted it with trembling hands.

When she loosened the cord—

Shine!

A dazzling golden light spilled out.

Gold coins.

More than she could count.

The entire square fell silent again.

Judy.

Her mother.

The countless spectators surrounding them.

No one could speak.

Vikir's voice cut through the silence.

“Take the ten billion gold.”

The words sounded almost like an oracle.

Judy and her mother remained kneeling before a fortune they had never seen, heard of, or imagined possessing.

Everyone else in the square felt the same way.

Vikir looked out across the crowd.

“...This is the law.”

Then he declared to every citizen present—

“It will be obeyed.”
