# Chapter 140: Men are Power (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/140.txt` — `cdd9a996413b8b25bbdd467eba1d00c8a83f313f`. Recovered English Chapter 139 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Night Hound, Beelzebub, Ephebo, Quovadis, Peak Sword Graduator, Seven Fangs, Cindywendy, Dolores L. Quovadis, and Ten Corpses. Later identification of Ephebo's master remains spoiler-guarded.

Boom!

Red and black aura crossed.

Vikir landed and felt a sharp ache run through his wrist.

*Heavy.*

*Definitely Graduator-class strength.*

The black-masked creature had been driven through the outer wall of a nearby structure.

It emerged from the broken masonry carrying the same massive hammer.

Sticky black aura coated the weapon's broad head.

Thick.

Viscous.

Like boiling tar.

Vikir narrowed his eyes.

*Upper Graduator.*

Then his attention returned to the technique itself.

*That was unmistakably Quovadis.*

A blunt-weapon art associated with the faithful house.

Quovadis's divine traditions existed in direct opposition to demons.

Yet a creature reeking of demonic corruption—

was using one of their martial techniques.

*So is there a connection?*

Vikir did not jump to a conclusion.

Every great house had rotten branches.

But the clue mattered.

The collaborators he had hunted throughout the Imperial Capital shared a pattern.

Demonic contracts.

Rapid facial decay after death.

And now—

this creature had hidden its face beneath a black hood.

Maybe it belonged to the same network.

Maybe it could lead him to one of the remaining Ten Corpses.

Vikir raised Beelzebub.

“First I'll cut off your hands and feet. I only need your mouth for the interrogation.”

The magic sword's aura expanded.

Dense enough to appear almost solid.

A **Peak Sword Graduator** at full output.

The red arc crossed the room.

A marble pillar split diagonally.

The black-masked creature raised its hammer to block—

then burst through the falling rubble behind it.

When the dust cleared—

its torn suit exposed a heavily built torso.

Letters had been carved into the chest.

**EPHEBO.**

Vikir read them.

“Ephebo.”

“Your name?”

No answer.

Only—

whoosh!

Black aura surged harder.

Vikir recognized the level immediately.

Strong.

But not beyond him.

Roughly the kind of strength he himself had possessed around the early period of his stay with the Ballak.

A powerful subordinate.

Not a Demon King.

Vikir's mouth curved faintly.

“Good.”

“I was getting tired of chasing weak collaborators.”

He raised his own aura.

Then—

the **Seven Fangs of Baskerville** unfolded.

The first Fang bit into Ephebo's body.

The second widened the first wound and cut through it.

The third drove the second attack forward and linked it into the fourth.

The fourth crushed through Ephebo's body hard enough to deny regeneration or recovery.

The fifth gathered the recoil from the first four attacks and drove it back into him.

The sixth sealed every route of escape while supporting the seventh.

And the seventh—

smallest of them all—

waited inside what looked like the safest gap.

Ephebo found that opening.

Moved toward it.

Then realized too late—

it was bait.

Anyone who tried to escape through that apparent gap would be caught by the hidden line of the sixth Fang and reduced to something like a rag.

Ephebo was no exception.

The black aura collapsed.

In an instant, his body was left rag-like beneath the completed sequence.

Vikir stood over him.

“Small or not—”

“the Seventh Fang is still the Seventh Fang.”

Only Vikir and Hugo Le Baskerville fully understood what that incomplete Fang could become.

Right now—

it was still developing.

But when the Seventh Fang reached its complete form—

Vikir would step into the realm of **Sword Master**.

*And at that point—*

*even Hugo won't frighten me.*

His feelings toward Hugo had changed.

Learning that Seth's conspiracy had played a greater role in Vikir's previous-life execution had stripped away some of the hatred.

But not all of it.

Hugo had still been the one who carried out the execution.

Hugo had still discarded countless sons and hounds as expendable resources.

Vikir had not forgotten.

He looked down at Ephebo.

“Now.”

“Let's find out who made you.”

Vikir kicked the badly wounded Ephebo backward.

The creature slammed onto a protruding iron support.

Vikir seized the thick metal and bent it into a hook.

Ephebo strained—

but the hook-like support had pierced through his body and pinned him in place.

Vikir crouched nearby.

“Who is your master?”

“Who turned you into this?”

[Grrrr…]

No answer.

Perhaps refusal.

Perhaps Ephebo could not speak normally at all.

Either way—

Vikir did not need words alone.

A body could reveal information.

Bone structure.

Old injuries.

Mana distribution.

Blood condition.

Signs of training.

Evidence of social status and prior occupation.

He could learn plenty by examining what Ephebo had become.

Vikir intended to dissect the body piece by piece and read the evidence directly.

Then—

a strange laugh came from behind him.

“Hee…”

“Hehehe…”

Vikir turned.

Doxeller was still alive.

The orphanage director's face had gone slack.

Drool ran from one corner of his mouth.

His eyes no longer focused properly.

“He's coming…”

Doxeller muttered.

“He'll come…”

A laugh.

“My face…”

“He'll take my face…”

“Can't fail…”

Vikir watched carefully.

The man appeared to have lost his grip on reality.

That was hardly surprising.

A normal person trapped beside a battle between high-level Graduators—

surrounded by mana shockwaves, collapsing stone, and demonic pressure—

could break under the strain.

Doxeller staggered through the debris.

“I have to hide…”

“Hide…”

He began digging with his hands.

Stone.

Dust.

Broken timber.

He clawed through the rubble until his fingers were bloody.

At last—

he uncovered a large safe.

Vikir's eyes sharpened.

*Interesting.*

During questioning—

Doxeller had revealed nothing useful.

Now—

with his conscious defenses gone—

he was leading Vikir directly to something hidden.

Doxeller turned the dial.

Click.

Click-click.

Then—

stopped.

“Gh…”

His body stiffened.

Vikir sensed a violent surge of mana.

He moved instantly.

Boom!

Doxeller exploded.

A demonic failsafe.

Some restriction had been placed on him—

triggered by a specific action.

Vikir escaped the center of the blast.

Even so—

the shockwave tore through his clothing.

Minor wounds closed quickly under the Bog Salamander's regeneration.

His clothes did not.

Cloak.

Shirt.

Mask.

All partly burned.

Vikir removed the damaged plague-doctor mask.

Dropped it.

Then looked back.

“…Gone.”

Ephebo had escaped.

Flesh and fragments of internal organs remained on the rough iron where he had torn himself free.

Whatever the creature was—

its body could endure forms of escape no ordinary human could survive.

Vikir clicked his tongue.

Still—

the night had produced something useful.

The safe had been partly destroyed by the explosion.

Most of its contents survived.

Gold bars.

Jewelry.

Property records.

Large amounts of suspicious wealth.

Perhaps they had sold children with nowhere else to go to demons as food and taken this dirty money in return.

Vikir ignored all of it.

Behind the valuables—

he found stacks of papers.

Ledgers.

Money transfers.

Where children had been taken from.

Where they had been sent.

Names.

Routes.

Dates.

Vikir gathered them.

“This matters.”

The documents would go straight to **Cindywendy**.

He could already imagine her reaction.

*Please stop giving me more work!*

Vikir almost heard the complaint.

Then he left.

◆◆◆

After midnight—

the city guard arrived.

They stared at the wrecked orphanage.

“What happened here?”

“Looks like a giant monster tore through the place.”

Another guard frowned.

“Wasn't this orphanage affiliated with Quovadis?”

“Who would attack a religious institution?”

They searched the ruins.

At first—

they found little beyond destruction.

Then—

the safe.

A fortune of uncertain origin.

Gold.

Documents.

Jewels.

Far too much money for a supposedly frugal Quovadis-affiliated orphanage.

The investigation widened.

And eventually—

the guards searched beneath the building.

There—

they found the bodies of children.

Dozens.

Many estimated to have been around ten to thirteen years old.

The bodies buried beneath the orphanage had been reduced to skin and bone.

The discovery horrified even experienced guards.

Then another group arrived.

“…!”

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

Direct descendant of Quovadis.

Colosseo student council president.

Head of the Academy newspaper club.

The moment she heard that a Quovadis-affiliated organization had been attacked—

she had rushed to the scene.

Several student journalists came with her.

The newspaper club followed strict reporting rules.

First-years—

inside-Academy stories only.

Second- and third-years—

permitted to report outside the campus.

New students needed time to adapt—

and off-campus reporting could be dangerous.

Dolores, as a third-year, had no such restriction.

She stared at the ruined orphanage.

The hidden wealth.

The recovered remains.

“…What happened here?”

The guards answered the student reporters.

“It was already over when we arrived.”

“Judging by the blood and flesh splattered around the ruins—”

“the attacker was extremely strong.”

“Probably one person.”

Another guard looked troubled.

“Similar incidents have been happening across the city.”

“Some kind of terrifying vigilante or criminal is moving around at night.”

Pens moved quickly.

Tomorrow morning—

those statements would become the basis of an Academy newspaper article.

Dolores listened.

Her expression remained heavy.

Then—

something caught her eye.

“…?”

She waited until no one was watching.

Stepped back beneath part of the rubble.

Bent down.

Picked something up.

Half burned.

Blackened around the edges.

A long beak.

The remains of a plague doctor's mask.

Dolores stared at it.

“…This is…”
