D’Ordume’s spinning axe-blades carved through Poison Humans like a storm.

Souare struck the ground.

Stone spears erupted from below—

impaling anything that reached the wall.

The two old pillars of Nouvelle Vague were just as formidable on land as they had been in the deep sea.

Only two more fighters had joined the line.

Yet—

the pressure eased.

Orca felt his breathing stabilize.

He looked toward them.

“How did you know we were here?”

D’Ordume crushed a Poison Human’s skull.

“We’ll explain after the emergency.”

They had clearly gone through a great deal to reach Tochka.

Orca nodded.

The immediate priority—

was the wall.

And little by little—

the situation improved.

SPLASH.

GURGLE.

HISSSS—

Green liquid poured down Tochka’s sloped battlements.

The walls had been designed to deflect artillery.

Now—

that incline helped the living slime spread.

**Flubber.**

The mysterious organism from Nouvelle Vague.

It flowed across enormous sections of stone.

Then swallowed every Poison Human it touched.

The mutants struggled inside the slime.

The harder they fought—

the deeper they sank.

SSSSSS—

Flubber devoured them without hesitation.

Even the most grotesque mutants disappeared into its body.

Orca clicked his tongue.

“How did that thing get here?”

Souare answered.

“Probably followed us after it lost its home.”

“It was attached to Nouvelle Vague.”

Orca looked skeptical.

“Followed voluntarily?”

“That thing has loyalty?”

“No.”

Souare shook his head.

“It probably smelled food.”

“It ate a Poison Human named Sakkuth before.”

“Must’ve liked the taste.”

That—

Orca could believe.

Flubber was eating Leviathan’s Poison Humans with obvious enthusiasm.

Red Death—

apparently—

appealed strongly to it.

And the newer mutants were even more concentrated with poison than Sakkuth had been.

“…If it likes the flavor—

good.”

Orca meant every word.

The mutants that had terrorized Tochka—

looked like giant pieces of candy to Flubber.

But more reinforcements were coming.

“Warden Orca!”

“Give us orders!”

“I’m not taking commands from those other wardens!”

“Hahaha!”

“You’re the only one who can boss me around!”

Behind D’Ordume, Souare, BDISSEM, and Flubber—

figures appeared.

Dozens.

Then more.

All wearing Nouvelle Vague guard uniforms.

The wardens.

An elite force shaped by years of service ten thousand meters beneath the sea.

Whatever their personalities—

their combat ability was unquestionable.

A smile appeared on Orca’s normally rigid face.

He exhaled smoke.

“Good.”

“Suppression formation!”

“Remind those lunatics where they belong!”

Prisoners belonged in cells.

The dead—

in graves.

The wardens laughed.

Then took over the front.

“Hey!”

“If you can’t use aura or magic—

fall back!”

“What?”

“You brought your son into this battle?”

“Are you insane?”

“Get behind us!”

“This is work for people with nothing left to lose!”

They had spent their lives suppressing riots.

The Poison Human attack line began collapsing almost immediately.

With his old subordinates back—

Orca’s talent for fortress defense finally had the manpower to express itself fully.

“All unit leaders above sector-command level—”

“replace them with Nouvelle Vague personnel!”

“Reorganize the whole defensive structure in real time!”

“Pull the fire crews off the parapet!”

“Double the interval between drum signals!”

“Roll every stone stockpile off the battlements!”

A familiar officer translated Orca’s intent before anyone could ask.

“The fire crews are going for oil and powder!”

“The longer drum interval means the wardens can hold longer than the militia!”

“And rolling off the stones means we’re entering full close combat!”

“Drop shields and general-issue spears!”

“Use your real weapons!”

The Nouvelle Vague wardens understood Orca without explanation.

Others charged straight to the front.

BDISSEM disabled nearby mutants—

sealing strength and mana.

The helpless creatures were finished by militia spears.

Flubber kept growing as it ate.

D’Ordume and Souare tore through the largest mutants at a frightening pace.

Then Orca glanced at BDISSEM.

“Was Flubber always this small?”

“I remember it being much bigger.”

“This one is split off.”

“The main body is somewhere else.”

“…Somewhere else?”

BDISSEM started to explain.

A mutant screamed overhead—

drowning out the answer.

Orca frowned.

Then another thought occurred to him.

“If all of you made it here…”

“Does that mean **he** survived too?”

BDISSEM understood immediately.

“If you mean Black Tongue—

he didn’t escape Nouvelle Vague.”

“He died there.”

“…I see.”

Orca clicked his tongue.

“Fine.”

“A traitor like that would only divide us anyway.”

BDISSEM tilted her head.

“Well…”

“He didn’t disappear completely.”

“…?”

Orca stared.

Dead?

Not gone?

What did that mean?

Before he could force a clearer answer—

BOOM!

A section of wall collapsed.

A monster rose through the rubble.

Larger than every mutant they had seen before.

As if multiple mutated Poison Humans had been fused into one body.

Even Flubber could not swallow it in one bite.

[OOOOOOOOOO—]

The creature opened its enormous mouth.

Orca raised his club.

Then—

CRACK!

A black whip came down from above.

The mutant’s skull caved inward.

Its entire body toppled from the wall.

BOOM!

Dust rose outside.

Orca’s expression darkened before the cloud even settled.

Because he heard laughter.

“Hehehehehe.”

An old man stood on the opposite section of battlement.

Ghostlike.

Delighted.

**Marquis de Sade.**

He looked at Orca—

still wearing that same infuriating grin.

Orca growled.

“I thought you’d run off.”

“Where were you hiding?”

“Run?”

Sade looked offended.

“Why would I leave?”

“This is only now becoming interesting.”

Orca frowned.

Then shadows appeared behind Sade.

Many.

The wardens—

were not the only people who had escaped Nouvelle Vague.

“Hehehe.”

“Following Marquis de Sade is always entertaining.”

“To fight under him again—

what an honor.”

“I’ve been dying to rampage.”

“Ahh.”

“The old days.”

Former prisoners.

Escapees.

Many had once followed Sade during the **Forty-Seven Riot**.

Some were descendants of defeated powers.

Some had avoided family extermination.

Some simply worshiped Sade’s strength—

or his notorious life—

and had chosen to follow him.

Monsters of men.

Level-Nine prisoners.

Warriors who had spent years swallowing rage.

Sade chuckled.

“To be honest—

what kind of war were you planning to fight with Academy chicks and refugee militia?”

“This.”

“This is finally enough people to command properly.”

Orca spat out his cigar.

Lit another.

“…I hate that I agree with you.”

He looked away.

But Sade understood the message.

Orca—

defend the fortress.

Sade—

counterattack.

Sade raised his whip.

“ALL RIGHT!”

“COUNTERATTACK!”

“Wipe out every helpless bastard in front of us!”

“And don’t stop there!”

“Drive them all the way back to the water source!”

The Poison Humans caught in BDISSEM restraints staggered helplessly.

No one understood those restraints better than the gathered wardens—

and the prisoners who had once suffered under them.

Every eye sharpened.

Every weapon came up.

Then Sade poured oil on the moment.

“Aren’t you all thirsty!?”

“Then let’s go drink!”

Wardens.

Prisoners.

Refugees.

All of them were parched.

Now—

the fire in their eyes burned as fiercely as their dry throats.

The time for defense—

was ending.

The counterattack was beginning.
