Ahun spat on the ground and folded his arms with confidence.

A huge wild boar lay before him.

It weighed well over three hundred kilograms.

The Ballak warriors gathered around it praised the catch.

“That's enough meat to feed everyone!”

“I only got three rabbits.”

“Ahun's boar is definitely the best catch so far.”

The large communal hunt was also a competition.

Outside the chieftain, hunters who brought back the largest and most dangerous prey commanded enormous prestige in the village.

Ahun puffed out his chest.

“Even Captain Aiyen won't bring back something bigger than this.”

“So you'll lead the next hunting party?”

“By the rules, yes.”

There was only one problem.

The person he most wanted to boast to had not returned.

Aiyen, captain of Ballak's younger hunters, was still somewhere in the jungle.

Ahun waved dismissively.

“Even the captain has limits hunting alone.”

“She didn't go alone. She brought that slave. Vikir, wasn't it?”

“That cripple can barely walk! What help could he possibly be?”

Ahun's face reddened as he remembered the humiliation before the hunt.

“Just wait. They won't even bring back a rabbit. That slave will drag her down.”

The younger warriors nodded.

Whether Ahun was right mattered less than the fact that, for the moment, he had brought back the largest animal.

◆◆◆

Elsewhere, a waterfall crashed through a break in the rocks.

Sharp stone filled the channel beneath it.

Farther down stretched a broad white sandbar.

The water was so clear it was almost invisible except for the foam and noise.

Fat carp fought their way upstream.

Many failed.

The current hurled them onto the pointed rocks, where they were impaled.

Aiyen and Vikir watched from the brush.

“It's coming.”

The enormous female Oxbear approached the waterfall.

Scars covered its horns.

Dense muscle shifted beneath its heavy coat.

This was clearly an old, exceptional individual.

It did not need to hunt.

It simply picked up the fish that had already died on the rocks and swallowed them.

The river was brackish where fresh water met the sea.

Salt accumulated along the pale sand, and the Oxbear seemed to prefer getting moisture from its prey rather than drinking directly.

“That explains the huge crayfish downstream,” Vikir murmured.

They were not freshwater animals at all.

The carp continued their futile climb.

“My grandmother said that a carp that makes it all the way upstream eventually becomes a great beast,” Aiyen said.

Vikir had not asked, but nodded anyway.

Most of the fish failed.

A lucky few fell back into water.

Many landed on stone and died.

The old female ate those.

Other Oxbears began arriving farther downstream.

They watched the dominant female, then chose less favorable positions where they had to catch their own fish.

The giant female regarded them with obvious superiority.

Small cubs received more tolerance.

Several puppy-sized Oxbears darted in, grabbed fish heads, and escaped.

Vikir watched with interest.

“First time seeing cubs?” Aiyen asked.

“Yes. They're smaller than I expected.”

“Newborns are even smaller. About the size of a rat.”

Because Oxbears gave birth during hibernation, the mother could not support enormous newborns despite carrying them for roughly a year.

Aiyen added that hunters avoided pregnant females and mothers with cubs.

Get within a hundred meters of the young and the mother would attack.

Oxbears might cannibalize their own kind when starving.

Their protection of their young was another matter.

Then a large male approached the dominant female.

He was old and well built.

He came slowly, following her scent.

The female's eyesight appeared poor, but her ears compensated.

ROAR!

She drove him away immediately.

Vikir frowned.

“She keeps rejecting the males?”

She had already chased away several strong ones.

“What kind of male is she waiting for?”

Aiyen laughed softly.

“You've misunderstood.”

“……?”

“She isn't looking for the strongest male. The opposite.”

Eventually a younger, smaller male approached.

Compared with the eight-meter, roughly two-ton female, he was perhaps four meters and only a little over a ton.

He sniffed her coat and rubbed against her side.

This time she allowed it.

“Why the weakest one?” Vikir asked.

“Because she wants a mate she can completely control.”

Among Oxbears, an older, stronger mate could become dangerous during or after breeding.

A smaller, younger partner reduced that risk.

The choice still required a reproductively healthy animal.

Size and age mattered less than fertility and the ability to produce healthy offspring.

The female even yielded the best fishing spot to the smaller male.

Aiyen smiled.

“Ballak mate selection isn't entirely different.”

Vikir said nothing.

“Do you know what kind of outsider is considered ideal?”

He did not particularly want to know.

Aiyen told him anyway.

“Young. Healthy. Good reproductive potential. Even better if injuries have left them too weak to run or fight back.”

That was one reason Ballak husband hunts often ended with captives being beaten nearly senseless before being taken home.

The goal was to prevent escape and resistance without destroying reproductive health.

Vikir fell silent.

He decided the glint in Aiyen's eye was probably his imagination.

Then the female Oxbear made a strange sound.

Both hunters turned.

The smaller male abandoned his fish and hurried to her.

She caught him by the back of the neck.

Soon the pair left the salt-covered riverbank and disappeared into the deeper jungle.

Aiyen grabbed Vikir.

“We're moving.”

Her attention had shifted completely from the giant female to the smaller male.

“That male is going to be exhausted soon. When that happens…”

She lifted her bow.

Vikir nodded.

The real hunt was approaching.
