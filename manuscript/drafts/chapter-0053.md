# Chapter 53: Slaves of the Savage Tribe (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/053.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 53 is an aligned secondary witness only. This chapter source-reveals **Akwilla** as Ballak chieftain, Aiyen's mother, and the current **Night Fox**; the earlier spoiler guard therefore lifts from this point forward only. Established terminology controls **Aiyen**, **Ahun**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Madam Eight-Legs**, **Hugo Le Baskerville**, **Adolf Morgue**, **Sword Graduator**, and **Cerberus**. Recovered-English `Gradient`, gender flips, and kinship corruption are rejected.

Aiyen—the Ballak warrior and daughter of the chieftain—looked at Vikir.

“Get down.”

Was she trying to establish who stood above whom?

Apparently not.

PFFFT—

Vikir felt something cut through the air behind him.

At the same instant—

TUG!

Aiyen jerked the leash around his neck.

Vikir ducked.

An arrow passed over the back of his head and buried itself in the ground.

Aiyen shouted toward the bushes.

“You brats! Can't you practice somewhere else?”

Leaves rustled.

A group of children carrying bows and arrows shrieked and ran away.

“……”

Vikir straightened and looked at Aiyen again.

Water still ran from her silver-and-black hair.

This was undeniably the same captive he had freed from the slave auction.

“We've met before, haven't we?”

Her Imperial was rough.

Vikir judged that his Ballak was better than her Imperial, so he answered in her language.

“……We meet again.”

Aiyen's eyes widened.

“You speak Ballak?”

“A little.”

She approached with open curiosity.

Her sparse deerskin clothing clearly did not embarrass her; Ballak customs were different from imperial ones.

“That time, I was grateful,” Aiyen said. “We repay kindness twice over and hatred tenfold.”

“……”

“You saved me once. So I saved you twice.”

“……”

“I didn't expect you to challenge *her*. I was already escaping when I happened to look back.”

Without Aiyen's noose catching Vikir in time, Madam Eight-Legs would have killed him.

But that explained only one rescue.

Vikir gave her a questioning look.

Aiyen's eyes curved like a fox's.

“Didn't you see it on the way here?”

She pointed toward the village.

A thick column of smoke rose beyond the brush.

Vikir remembered the prisoners he had passed.

He could easily have been among them.

Apparently Aiyen was also the reason he had not been sent to the Thorn-Tree Punishment.

That raised the more important question.

“Why save me?”

Simply repaying a debt did not explain why she had thrown a lasso around his neck in the first place.

Nor had Aiyen been the only one trying to capture him.

Every Ballak woman in that raiding party had aimed a noose at him.

Vikir asked directly.

“Was that what you call a husband hunt?”

“Oh. So you've heard about it.”

It was not limited to Ballak.

Many mountain tribes kidnapped spouses from outside their own people.

The purpose was straightforward: avoid close inbreeding and introduce new blood.

And because Ballak was a warrior tribe, physical strength mattered greatly in choosing outsiders.

Aiyen walked once around Vikir, inspecting him like an appraiser examining merchandise.

Vikir, meanwhile, considered a different problem.

What if Ballak reported that he had survived?

What if they tried to use him in hostage negotiations with Baskerville?

That would destroy the plan he had just risked his life to create.

His chance to disappear from Hugo's surveillance and quietly rebuild his strength would vanish.

Preventing that would require either escaping the village or killing everyone who knew.

In his current physical condition, neither was realistic.

But he had worried for nothing.

Aiyen looked directly at him.

“Don't misunderstand. I'm not like the other women. I don't need a husband.”

“……”

“I caught you to use as a slave.”

Vikir had questions about that explanation, but kept them to himself.

Aiyen flicked the collar.

“In that condition, you're hardly fit for a husband's role anyway. Get used to being a slave.”

Ballak women normally regarded strong men taken from outside the tribe as potential breeding partners.

A captive considered unsuitable for that role might simply be killed.

Fortunately for Vikir, Aiyen appeared to have a different use for him.

She picked up the clothes left beside the river and tossed them over.

The moment Vikir caught them, the leash pulled taut.

Aiyen had already started walking back toward the village.

“You'll live in front of my home from now on and serve me.”

Every step made Vikir's body scream.

He endured it without complaint.

At least Tough Life was repairing him quickly.

Aiyen gave him his first proper order.

“First, let's settle how you address me.”

She stopped at the village entrance and looked back.

Her eyes narrowed as she stared down at him.

“Call me Master.”

◆◆◆

Vikir entered the Ballak village again.

Inside the settlement, people lived with a striking degree of freedom.

The idea of private property was weak.

If someone had nowhere to sleep, they could use a friend's tent.

If someone was hungry, a neighbor with extra food would share it.

Firewood, meat, and liquor moved freely among households.

Friends gathered in the largest tents to talk and play until they fell asleep.

Even someone brought in from outside could eventually move freely if the tribe accepted them as one of its own.

Within the village, the atmosphere was lively and generous.

The skeletons hanging from thorn trees along the border told outsiders a very different story.

Ballak was merciless toward those beyond the tribe.

Toward recognized insiders, it could be startlingly warm.

Even so, there were sacred places where ordinary members did not wander casually.

Aiyen led Vikir toward one of them.

A huge tent stood at the center of the settlement.

It could have held two dozen people.

Even playing children kept their distance.

Passing warriors bowed their heads toward the entrance.

People roasting meat redirected the smoke away from it, while those cleaning animal organs made sure the smell of blood did not drift nearby.

*The chieftain's tent.*

Aiyen entered without hesitation, holding Vikir's leash.

“Mother.”

Vikir saw the woman she had come to meet.

A great chair decorated with an eagle's head stood inside.

A woman sat there with one leg crossed, leaning back with casual authority.

Her face was hard.

Scars covered her body.

A cloak of eagle feathers hung over her shoulders, and an enormous bow rested behind the seat.

Most striking was the heavy scar over one eye.

The current Ballak chieftain.

**Akwilla.**

Aiyen brought Vikir forward.

He quietly knelt.

The moment he felt the woman's presence, a conclusion he had only speculated about before became certain.

Akwilla was the **Night Fox**.

The woman who had scarred the bridge of Hugo's nose.

The archer who had wounded Adolf badly enough to put him in bed.

One of the central reasons Baskerville and Morgue had never been able simply to push across Ballak territory.

She had killed sixteen Baskerville Sword Graduators, ten Morgue Fourth-Circle mages, and six Fifth-Circle mages.

And she had emerged without a wound.

Vikir even suspected the old arrow scars he had once seen on a Cerberus in Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain had been left by her.

*What if I fought her with the power I had before the regression?*

Could he have won at his peak?

*Head-on: zero percent.*

*Assassination: zero percent.*

*Escape… perhaps twenty percent.*

And that twenty percent meant survival, not victory.

He could not properly measure Akwilla's strength.

She belonged in the same class of predator as Hugo or Adolf.

Right now, Vikir had no means of opposing her.

Akwilla studied him in return.

“Daughter. Is this the Imperial man you kept talking about?”

“I-I didn't talk about him *that* much… but yes.”

“Hm. He's young. Younger than you.” Her eyes narrowed. “And something about him feels familiar.”

Her gaze pressed down like a predator examining prey.

Vikir lowered his head and avoided meeting it directly.

Fortunately, Akwilla did not recognize Hugo in him.

Hugo's face was harsh and angular.

Vikir had inherited more of his appearance from his mother.

Akwilla did, however, immediately understand Vikir's physical condition.

“Hm. What use is dragging home a wreck?”

“He'll be useful once he's healed. He challenged Madam Eight-Legs.”

“Hmph. Did he? Recklessness beyond your ability only buys suffering.”

Then Akwilla added casually,

“Still, a broken body isn't necessarily a problem. As long as his bloodline is sound—”

“Mother! That's not what this is!” Aiyen burst out. “I'm using him as a slave!”

“What? But I thought the reason you kept talking about him was—”

“That was the purpose from the beginning! A slave! I need a slave to assist with hunting!”

Aiyen cut her off in obvious embarrassment.

Akwilla watched her daughter's face for a moment and nodded.

“Fine. Whatever purpose you have for your slave is your business.”

Aiyen immediately bowed, then tugged Vikir's collar and hurried outside.

Once they were away from her mother, her expression stiffened.

The fluster from moments earlier disappeared.

“Now I'm giving you a task.”

Aiyen looked down at Vikir.

Her voice became deliberately imposing.

“It will be difficult.”

Apparently his first real assignment as a Ballak slave would not be a small one.
