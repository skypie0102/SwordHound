Hugo Le Baskerville looked down into the opened cavern.

“…Son.”

His expression did not change.

“What are you doing down there?”

The tone was almost absurdly ordinary.

A father who had happened to meet his son on the road.

Nothing more.

For an instant—

that familiarity made Andromalius fall back into the habits he had worn for decades.

“Ah—Father!”

His demonic face tried to form Seth's smile.

“Why are you here…?!”

Then Andromalius remembered what he looked like.

Red eyes.

Black flesh.

Ramlike horns.

A serpent growing from the stump of Seth's arm.

He was no longer wearing a convincing human disguise.

Anyone could see it.

A demon—

inside Seth Le Baskerville.

He had been discovered.

Caught in the act.

There was no point pretending anymore.

And yet Hugo's gaze did not change.

Cold.

Flat.

Dismissive.

Trash.

It seemed not to matter whether the thing before him was Seth—

Andromalius—

Andromalius wearing Seth's flesh—

or some mixture of Seth's remaining soul and the demon consuming it.

Once Hugo decided something was beneath his notice—

he looked at it as though it barely existed.

That was the gaze now resting on Andromalius.

“What a disgraceful sight.”

Hugo's voice stayed indifferent.

“Son.”

Something snapped.

[Do not call me that!]

Andromalius bared his teeth.

[Son? Son?!]

Demonic power surged.

[How dare a mere human mock me!]

The serpent replacing his left arm shot toward Hugo.

Then—

stopped.

[…?!]

Andromalius stared.

The arm had gone rigid.

*What?*

He tried to launch himself out of the cavern.

His feet would not move either.

Numbness.

Trembling.

The moment he faced Hugo—

Seth's body had frozen.

*Why?*

Andromalius forced power through the vessel.

Nothing.

*Why won't it move?*

He was a Demon King.

One of the Ten Corpses sent into the human realm to open the Age of Destruction.

Why should a human make his body lock in place?

*Fear?*

His pride rejected the thought.

*Impossible.*

*Even if he is a Sword Master, he is still human.*

Andromalius himself was not afraid of Hugo.

That much was true.

The problem—

was the body.

**Seth Le Baskerville.**

Seth's body was afraid.

Andromalius's eyes widened.

A hound could know that answering its master's call meant death—

and still go when summoned.

That was the nature drilled into Baskerville's dogs.

*But Seth is already dead.*

The demon strained against the paralysis.

It did not matter.

Seth's fear of Hugo had been carved so deeply into his body—

so deeply into what remained of him—

that even death had not erased it.

Now that terror was affecting the next being to use the vessel.

Andromalius.

*No.*

His expression finally cracked.

*This wasn't part of the plan.*

His feet shook.

*Move.*

He turned his eyes toward the distant river.

If he could reach the embankment—

break it—

flood the cave—

he still had a chance.

But Seth's legs would not obey.

They trembled so badly that merely standing had become difficult.

Then—

“…I'm sorry.”

A voice Andromalius had not expected.

He looked up.

Osiris stood beside Hugo.

His head was lowered.

His expression—

one Andromalius had never seen.

“I'm sorry, little brother.”

Osiris's voice was quiet.

“I didn't know you had fallen into something like this.”

Andromalius's panic vanished.

An opening.

He immediately reshaped Seth's face.

Pain.

Grief.

Resentment.

He cried out.

[This happened because of you!]

Osiris stiffened.

[If you had acknowledged me—]

Andromalius leaned into Seth's memories.

[If you had guided me onto the right path—]

His voice broke convincingly.

[I wouldn't have become this!]

[I wouldn't have sold my soul to a demon!]

“…”

Osiris lowered his head further.

His face grew heavier.

Andromalius watched him.

*There.*

The direction Osiris occupied was the only viable escape route.

If he could move the body little by little—

if he could use the hesitation—

Puck!

A blade erupted through Andromalius's chest from behind.

Black blood sprayed from his mouth.

[You again?!]

Vikir had climbed the cavern wall without a sound.

Beelzebub was buried through Andromalius's spine.

Vikir's expression remained blank.

“I'm used to climbing.”

He had spent days scaling sheer terrain on the way to Madam Eight-Legs' nest.

A vertical cavern was hardly unfamiliar.

Vikir looked up toward Osiris.

Once—

he had believed his eldest brother possessed no blood or tears at all.

Now he understood better.

“Don't listen to him, brother.”

Osiris raised his head.

“…!”

Vikir pushed the sword deeper.

“Seth died when he was still young.”

He looked at the demon.

“This thing has only been hiding inside his skin and acting.”

Half a lie.

Vikir knew that.

But there was no evidence available here that could precisely establish when Seth's original self had ended and Andromalius's control had begun.

And now was not the moment for a complicated explanation.

The words were meant for Osiris.

And they worked.

Vikir stabbed Andromalius several more times.

Puck.

Puck.

Puck.

When the serpent arm finally counterattacked—

Vikir withdrew.

He kicked against the rock wall and leapt upward.

His eyes met Hugo's.

Click.

Hugo gave a single nod.

Hugo was the patriarch of Baskerville.

Ordinarily—

every hound answered to him.

Not now.

For **half a day**—

Vikir was their master.

That was the reward Hugo had promised after Pomeranian was brought home.

Vikir glanced toward the sky.

The moon behind the clouds was slowly descending.

Until moonlight vanished and dawn arrived—

the military power of the Ironblood Sword House belonged to him.

So Vikir gave his first command.

“Bite him to death.”

No target needed to be named.

Boston Terrier grinned and drew his sword.

“Hehehe…”

His eyes shone.

“To think I'd get to cut down a demon like this in my lifetime.”

Great Dane drew his weapon beside him.

“A Demon King-class enemy.”

He rolled one shoulder.

“This will be valuable experience.”

The other Counts drew as well.

All seven had gathered at the estate to see Vikir.

Now—

their killing intent descended into the cavern at once.

Shing—

Osiris Le Baskerville unsheathed his sword.

The younger patriarch had always been awkward with emotion.

Brotherly affection—

if he possessed it—

had rarely reached his face.

Now some trace of it reached his blade instead.

A pale blood-red aura spread across the steel.

Flutter—

Black cloaks snapped in the wind.

Then—

seven hundred Sword Graduators raised their swords.

Seven knight orders.

Seven Counts.

Seven hundred hounds.

Puck-puck-puck-puck-puck!

Andromalius's body began to explode.

Slash.

Stab.

Slash.

Stab.

Fangs descended from every direction.

Madam Eight-Legs' venom had already crippled him.

Seth's body's terror of Hugo kept him from fleeing.

[Aaaaaaaagh!]

Andromalius released his authority with everything he had.

Bloodsucking flies filled the air.

Graveyard insects.

Ancient, monstrous swarms.

They surged upward.

But the cavern walls were wet with poisoned groundwater.

The air carried Vikir's venom.

The swarm could not climb high enough to escape it.

Then—

Shhk!

Osiris's sword fell.

It cut through Seth's physical body—

and Andromalius's spiritual body—

at the same time.

[…Gh?!]

Seth's head flew.

Andromalius's demonic head separated with it.

A strangely absurd end—

for one of the beings that had once helped drive the continent to the edge of destruction.

Silence followed.

Then the knights descended into the cave.

With the barrier gone, anyone could enter.

What they found inside shocked even Baskerville's veterans.

Demonic miasma.

Poison.

Mountains of dead flies.

And—

children's bones.

Far too many.

Boston Terrier came to Vikir first.

“You've done it again.”

Great Dane followed.

“My nephew really is extraordinary.”

He reached out and ruffled Vikir's hair.

“I'm proud.”

The other Counts were not far behind.

Men who had spent the banquet competing to recruit Vikir now found excuses to approach him—

to praise him—

to establish familiarity.

“…”

Hugo and Osiris did not join them.

They stood in silence—

looking deeper into the cave.

Bones everywhere.

Corroded implements.

Rot.

Filth.

A hidden hell inside Baskerville itself.

“…This existed inside my family.”

Hugo's voice seemed to rise from deep in his chest.

He revealed little.

But the shock was obvious.

Was it because of his second son's corruption and death?

Because a Demon King had hidden so close to him?

Both?

Perhaps even Hugo could not have separated the answers.

Osiris was easier to understand.

“…Seth.”

He looked into the pit where his brother had lived.

Cold.

Narrow.

Isolated.

Had Seth spent year after year here—

training his sword—

feeding his resentment—

living alone with poison in his heart?

Osiris thought back on the long periods when Seth had withdrawn to train.

*Where did it begin to go wrong?*

Vikir stepped beside him.

“Brother.”

Osiris turned.

“You don't need to blame yourself.”

Vikir looked at the ruined cave.

“This was a demon's doing.”

Osiris shook his head.

“No.”

His voice was firm.

“Giving a demon an opening is still a failure.”

He lowered his gaze.

“And that failure is mine.”

Vikir's eyes shifted slightly.

Osiris continued.

“Seth listened to me when we were young.”

That confession was unexpected.

Vikir did not show it.

Osiris's face carried faint grief.

“Regretting the fall and death of my own blood is one thing.”

He looked at the children's remains.

“But before that—”

his hands tightened.

“the people of Baskerville entrusted their lives to this family.”

“These children were part of its future.”

His voice became quieter.

“So many were sacrificed.”

“And I knew nothing.”

Vikir could not disagree.

Before regression—

these crimes had happened too.

No one had stopped them.

Then—

“Enemy!”

A shout erupted from farther inside.

Staffordshire Baskerville.

The veteran Pit Bull knight who had once served under Vikir drew his sword and pointed.

“The enemy is still alive!”

Every head turned.

“He's escaping!”

More than seven hundred pairs of eyes snapped toward the same point.

They had made one mistake.

They had underestimated the vitality of a Demon King.

[Hahahahaha!]

A severed head shot through the air.

Andromalius.

Only the head remained.

From the torn neck extended a long serpent tail—

winged—

thrashing through the air at terrifying speed.

He flew toward the river.

[You stupid hounds!]

His laughter became shrill.

[Die here!]

He raced for the embankment.

[The Gate may be delayed now—]

A grin.

[but that doesn't matter!]

[My other brothers will complete the task!]

“Hahahahahahaha—!”

He nearly reached the dam.

Nearly.

Shing—

Hugo drew his longsword.

Then—

BOOM!

A sound like an explosion.

A slash faster than lightning.

For one brief instant—

every Baskerville swordsman saw it.

Seven immense blood-red bolts.

Seven lines of power—

splitting the sky into seven sections.

The attack vaporized the heavy cloud cover.

Then reached Andromalius beyond it.

[Hahaha—ha?]

The laughter stopped.

There was no second attempt.

No regeneration.

No escape.

Andromalius disintegrated.

Not a scrap of flesh remained.

His spiritual body shattered with it.

Erased.

“…”

Silence spread through Baskerville's hounds.

The Seven Counts stared at Hugo's back.

Osiris did the same.

Even among monsters like them—

Hugo's power remained shocking.

Hugo looked up.

Above him—

the sky was still divided by the remnants of seven red cuts.

He touched his mustache.

“…Maybe I should shave it.”

His voice had returned to normal.

But his thoughts—

were elsewhere.

Roxana.

Penelope.

And the living trace of them who had returned to him—

Pomeranian.
