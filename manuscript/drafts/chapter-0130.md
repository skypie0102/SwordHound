# Chapter 130: The Freshman Talent Show (1)

Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Pigi, Bianca Fo Usher, Dolores L. Quovadis, Tudor Donquixote, Cold Department, Colosseo Academy, and the 20th class. The military-song wording is paraphrased rather than copied line-for-line while preserving the source's memorial function and emotional effect.

Vikir found **Cold Department, Class B** and stepped inside.

A crowd had gathered around long tables.

Drinks were being passed from hand to hand.

In the open space at the center of the room—

freshmen were taking turns showing off whatever talents they had prepared.

The moment Vikir entered—

a delighted voice called from beside the door.

“Vikir!”

Pigi waved both hands.

“You came after all!”

Then he glanced at the clock.

“Why are you so late?”

He rummaged through a box on the table and pulled out a name tag.

> **20th Class — Vikir**

“Everyone gets one when they come in.”

Pigi held it out.

“Here.”

Vikir took the tag.

Then looked at the box.

“…Why are you handing these out?”

That should have been a second-year student's job.

Pigi was a freshman too.

Why was he doing work meant for the seniors?

Pigi gave an awkward laugh.

“Oh.”

“The seniors said checking every name tag was a pain, so they asked me to help.”

Vikir raised his head.

Several second-years were scattered through the crowd—

doing nothing except enjoying the show.

“Tudor! That was amazing!”

“Encore!”

“Cold Department, let's go!”

“Tudor, you're the best!”

They had time to cheer promising freshmen.

Apparently—

they did not have time to hand out name tags.

Instead—

they had pushed the dull work onto Pigi, a newcomer with no visible family background or practical reputation.

Efficient.

And shameless.

Meanwhile—

freshmen who had already performed were seated along one side of a long table.

Professors, teaching assistants, and upperclassmen sat opposite them.

Most fourth-years were absent.

They were already focused on employment and graduation.

There were only a handful of third-years as well.

One of them—

however—

drew more attention than anyone else in the room.

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

Student council president.

Saintess.

She had been making rounds through the various classrooms and had paused here to help supervise the remaining upperclassmen.

“The president is here!”

“Saintess! Please accept a drink from me!”

“Senior! You're writing an article about the Cold Department orientation, right?”

“Please make us look good!”

“President, I love you!”

Dolores received every greeting with practiced composure.

She was the center of attention without trying.

Then—

the second-year student acting as host lifted a mana-amplified megaphone.

“All right!”

“Next talent!”

He grinned broadly.

“The super rookie everyone has been waiting for!”

“Please welcome **Bianca Fo Usher** of the 20th class!”

Applause thundered through the room.

Bianca stepped forward.

Her expression was calm.

Only the slight unevenness in her breathing hinted that she had come down from the rooftop in a hurry.

She bowed toward the professors and upperclassmen.

“As a junior still lacking in many ways—”

Bianca reached for a bottle.

“Allow me to offer everyone a drink.”

She opened it.

Then—

flicked her wrist.

Pop.

Pop.

Pop.

Small streams of liquor shot through the narrow neck.

Each arc passed cleanly over the people seated in front—

and dropped into glasses several meters away.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Every cup filled without spilling.

The upperclassmen erupted.

Bianca bowed once more.

A short performance.

Simple—

but precise.

And spectacular enough to keep the room roaring.

As she returned to her seat—

one thought crossed her mind.

*Whoever follows me is going to have a hard time.*

The pouring trick itself had not been extraordinary enough to justify that level of excitement.

But Bianca was Bianca.

Her family name.

Her placement score.

Her appearance.

Her reputation.

All of it amplified the reaction.

To follow her successfully—

the next freshman would need either comparable prestige or a genuinely unforgettable performance.

*Not my problem.*

Bianca sat down.

The host raised the megaphone again.

“Wow!”

“What a performance from Bianca!”

“This atmosphere is on fire!”

He looked toward the next name.

“We can't let it cool down now!”

“So let's go straight to the next freshman!”

His finger landed on the list.

“Next!”

“Pigi of the 20th class!”

At the back of the room—

Pigi froze.

“…Huh?”

His hands stopped over the name-tag box.

“I thought…”

He swallowed.

“I thought you said I'd be excused from the talent show if I handled these?”

The second-years who had given him the job—

looked away.

Smiling.

Pretending not to hear.

Pigi's face went pale.

The host kept waving.

“Come on!”

“We can't lose the momentum!”

“Pigi!”

“Hurry up!”

The room seemed to narrow around him.

His thoughts emptied.

His vision blurred.

His hands trembled.

Cold sweat spread across his face.

All those eyes—

fixed on him.

“Pigi?”

The host called again.

“Where are you?”

The applause began to weaken.

People looked around.

Confused.

Pigi's fear deepened.

He was shy even in ordinary conversation.

Looking someone in the eye could be difficult.

Standing in front of a packed classroom and entertaining them—

was something else entirely.

His hobbies were quiet ones.

Reading.

Music.

Drawing.

Caring for animals.

None of them naturally translated into an improvised performance before a noisy crowd.

If he had known in advance—

perhaps he could have prepared.

But he had relaxed only because he had been promised an exemption.

Now—

his panic was worse.

*What do I do?*

His legs felt rooted to the floor.

The room grew quieter.

Even the host began to look uncomfortable.

Then—

a hand settled on Pigi's shoulder.

“Let's do it together.”

Pigi turned.

Vikir stood behind him.

“We're roommates.”

The host immediately brightened.

“Oh!”

“You're Vikir, right?”

“The late one!”

He laughed.

“Normally we don't allow paired performances like this—”

“but since you're roommates, we'll make an exception!”

“All right!”

“Let's see some teamwork!”

Every eye in the room turned toward them.

“Vikir…”

Pigi looked up with wet eyes.

Vikir spoke calmly.

“I'll sing.”

Pigi blinked.

“…What?”

“You do the motions.”

Pigi's anxiety returned instantly.

“I-I'm clumsy.”

“And I can't keep pitch either…”

“It doesn't matter.”

Vikir demonstrated.

One fist moved down.

Then up.

Down.

Up.

When the fist dropped—

the right foot stamped once.

That was all.

Pigi copied him hesitantly.

“Like this?”

“Enough.”

Vikir nodded.

“Do it strongly.”

“With spirit.”

He guided Pigi toward the center of the room.

Then—

under the gaze of everyone present—

the performance began.

The host's smile faded.

So did several others.

Not because it was bad.

Because no one understood what they were watching.

Vikir was not singing a popular song.

Not a drinking song.

Not a comic tune.

A military song.

The kind once sung on the front lines.

A song about cold mountains.

Long marches.

Comrades lost far from home.

Old trees standing where young soldiers had died.

A song asking whether fallen comrades could still hear the anger of those who remained—

whether they could still see the eyes of the survivors who remembered them.

Pigi repeated the simple motion.

Fist down.

Fist up.

One heavy step.

Again.

Again.

Again.

It looked almost ridiculous with only one person doing it.

Vikir knew that.

Because the song had never been meant for one person.

In the Age of Destruction—

thousands had sung it together.

Then tens of thousands.

More.

Endless rows of exhausted soldiers—

stamping in unison.

Shouting the same words.

Turning grief into rhythm.

Turning fear into memory.

Vikir had no talent for entertainment.

He had spent too much of his life on battlefields to learn popular songs.

But there was one thing he knew.

A song that could make exhausted soldiers stand straighter.

A song that could make the grieving remember why they had survived.

So he sang.

And while he did—

he looked around the classroom.

Young faces.

Fresh faces.

People who had not yet become the men and women he remembered.

Some—

he had once fought beside.

Some had guarded his back on distant fronts.

Some had mourned him when he was falsely condemned.

Some had died long before he did.

Now—

they were here.

Alive.

Laughing.

Unscarred.

Vikir's voice deepened.

This was not really a talent show anymore.

It was a memorial.

A reunion no one else understood.

The final line faded.

Vikir stood straight.

Then gave a formal salute.

No flourish.

No smile.

He turned and stepped away from the center of the room.

Silence followed.

The lively atmosphere had vanished.

But not in a bad way.

Something heavier had replaced it.

A strange pressure remained in the room—

a sadness no one could quite explain.

“What…”

A student whispered.

“What kind of mood is this?”

“Why did he suddenly sing a military song?”

“That's way too serious for a talent show…”

Most of the younger students simply looked confused.

Then—

from the professors' side of the room—

came a quiet sound.

“…Ah.”

Several of the oldest professors had begun to cry.
