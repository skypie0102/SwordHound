# Chapter 262: The Inverted Pentagram (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/262.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 260 is the verified shifted secondary witness.

Rip—

the barrier split wider.

Vikir stepped through.

“Sorry.”

“I’m late.”

He looked completely unbothered.

Everyone else—

did not.

The entire crowd stood frozen.

Mouths open.

No one said a word.

Vikir raised the object in his hand.

“Should I register the artifact here?”

It was an ominous pendant shaped like an inverted pentagram.

At its center—

a closed eye.

“The artifact’s name and function are—”

“Vikir!”

Someone slammed into him.

Sinclaire.

Her face was covered in tears.

And snot.

“…?”

Vikir tilted his head.

Then—

“You bastard!”

Tudor tackled him from the side and locked both arms around him.

Sancho and Pigi rushed over too.

Bianca stood behind them with her arms crossed.

“What happened?!”

“We thought you weren’t coming out!”

“I told you this guy always causes trouble.”

Farther back—

Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro shifted anxiously.

Too many eyes were on them for the brothers to openly approach.

Then Dolores walked up.

“…”

“…”

They looked at each other.

Dolores said only one thing.

“Don’t make people worry.”

Short.

Controlled.

Exactly what a student council president would say.

Then Professor Banshee stepped forward.

The commotion stopped.

“Vikir.”

“Yes.”

“…How did you get out?”

Her expression was cold again.

“As everyone here knows, that gate requires all four keys.”

“It opens once per year.”

“And only for twenty-four hours.”

“There is one known way to tear its barrier from the inside.”

She paused.

“A Sword Master.”

“Or a mage of the seventh circle or higher.”

“Using full power.”

“…”

“So I have to ask.”

“How did you tear it open?”

Basilios, Hippolyte, and Whitebeard Whale moved closer too.

All three wanted the answer.

“I’ve served as Varangian’s vice director for years, and I have never seen that barrier torn.”

“Could I do it if I were inside and used everything I had?”

“Hehehe. Perhaps.”

“But a student certainly shouldn’t be able to.”

Students from all four academies crowded around.

Vikir answered calmly.

“I got lucky.”

“This artifact happens to break barriers.”

He lifted the pendant around his neck.

The red inverted pentagram remained still.

Banshee squinted at it.

“That…”

“Wasn’t that in the back corner of the vault?”

Hippolyte blinked.

“That was a display rack.”

Whitebeard Whale nodded.

“A rack for holding artifacts.”

“Why is it pendant-sized now?”

Vikir had apparently returned not with one of the artifacts—

but with the shelf they had been resting on.

Banshee swallowed.

“I see.”

“If it sat inside an artifact vault for all these years, perhaps the rack itself was an artifact.”

“You noticed what everyone else missed.”

“Thank you.”

“And its ability is barrier destruction?”

“Yes.”

“Show us.”

“It isn’t registered in the inventory.”

“I can’t.”

Vikir shook his head.

“I already used it once.”

“I don’t know when it can activate again.”

“Perhaps never.”

That was plausible.

Many ancient artifacts were single-use.

Others required absurdly long intervals before functioning again.

The representatives examined the object anyway.

By sight—

it was just hard mineral.

By touch—

the same.

Mana produced no obvious reaction.

Eventually—

they had nothing to add.

The unknown artifact entered the records based on Vikir’s testimony.

A one-use barrier-breaking relic.

◆◆◆

Twenty-four hours earlier.

Vikir had entered the vault with the other nine students.

Each of them quickly followed the pull of an artifact.

They scattered through the enormous storage chamber.

Vikir remained still.

Stone pillars divided the space into sections.

Artifacts lay everywhere.

Rings.

Necklaces.

Staves.

Swords.

Shields.

Helmets.

Shoes.

Every object carried mana.

Farther inside—

several especially powerful relics rested on a large rack.

A red sword.

Black armor.

Rings and necklaces glowing blue and green.

The strongest items had clearly been separated from the rest.

Most students followed the artifacts that called to them.

Eventually—

everyone left.

Only Vikir remained.

*Good.*

He turned.

Countless artifacts were shining toward him.

Shoes that increased speed.

Glasses that saw enormous distances.

A flute that could put everything nearby to sleep.

A bead that extinguished flame.

An ember that evaporated water.

A mirror that showed one’s future partner.

A ring that killed both its wearer and its target.

Useful.

Rare.

Powerful.

Vikir ignored all of them.

He had only been pretending to hesitate.

He had been waiting for the vault to empty.

Step.

Step.

Step.

He approached the display rack holding the highest-grade artifacts.

Even Dolores.

Bakilaga.

Hohenheim.

Lovegood.

None of them had been chosen by these particular treasures.

Now—

those treasures were all shining at Vikir.

He still ignored them.

Instead—

crash.

He swept every artifact off the rack.

Swords and rings clattered across the stone floor.

Vikir stared at the empty structure beneath them.

“Found you.”

“Seventh Corpse.”

“Decarabia.”

The rack itself.

The unnoticed red inverted pentagram.

That was what Vikir had come for.

In his future memories—

the vault had existed because of this object.

The other artifacts were camouflage.

Vikir gripped the rack.

Then poured mana into it.

Nothing happened.

At first.

Then—

his mana began vanishing.

Fast.

Like a tide being sucked away.

The rack drank it like parched earth.

“So that’s why.”

Vikir glanced around.

Every artifact that had been glowing moments before went dark.

The high-grade relics rolled silently on the floor.

No light.

No vibration.

No reaction.

Fear.

The artifacts were afraid.

“…I’m not.”

Vikir looked down at the inverted pentagram.

He had already killed three Demon Kings of this class.

There was no reason to fear another.

Boom!

He forced even more mana into it.

Power surged from every part of his body.

The mana he had accumulated after reaching Sword Master—

denser and deeper than before—

rushed into the inverted pentagram.

The rack began to grow.

First—

a large shelf.

Then a table.

Then something the size of a massive bookcase.

Then—

it filled the entire vault.

“…!”

Sweat appeared on Vikir’s forehead.

This had become a contest of mana.

He clenched his teeth until he tasted blood.

Every reserve opened.

He poured everything out.

Then—

BOOM!

The flow stopped.

Crack.

A fracture appeared at the center of the inverted pentagram.

It widened.

Then opened—

like an eye.

A bloodshot eye stared down.

Black air currents gathered around it.

A dreadful voice boomed through the vault.

[HAHAHAHAHA!]

[AT LAST—ENOUGH MANA!]

[THE TIME HAS COME TO OPEN THE GATE OF THE ABYSS AND ANNIHILATE HUMANITY!]

[DESPAIR, WEAKLINGS!]

[I—DECARABIA—HAVE DESCENDED!]

The Seventh Corpse.

Decarabia.

Its single crimson eye shone with malignant light.

Power erupted from the five points of its body.

Then—

“….”

Vikir and Decarabia looked directly at each other.

Decarabia froze.

Its eye shifted left.

[…?]

Then right.

[…?]

Nothing.

No mountains of corpses.

No rivers of blood.

No flames reaching the sky.

No land covered in ash.

No open gate to the Demon World.

Only—

an empty, dark vault.

At last—

Decarabia looked down at Vikir again.

Its voice became noticeably less confident.

[…Excuse me.]

[Who are you?]
