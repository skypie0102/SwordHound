The drinking water was gone.

Not low.

Gone.

A suffocating tension filled Tochka.

Cindywendy stood over a map.

Her expression—

grim.

“The water we managed to pull from the source bought us about three days.”

“That’s over.”

“No more extensions.”

Refugee arrivals had slowed after the water-source battle.

But the fortress still held an enormous population.

Every mouth—

needed food.

And water.

Cindywendy was responsible for both.

That burden showed.

Osiris sat beside her.

For once—

equally serious.

“Leviathan is committed.”

“The rocky basin below is packed with one-eyed Poison Humans.”

“More than triple our numbers.”

Tochka’s defensive structure helped when a small force fought another small force.

Against mass armies on both sides—

fortification alone could not solve everything.

Cindywendy exhaled slowly.

“We’re out of water.”

“Tomorrow is the absolute limit.”

“We need a decisive result before then.”

“Total war?”

Osiris nodded.

“There’s no other option.”

“With this many forces—

small battles will break out everywhere outside Tochka.”

“At least we have plenty of capable commanders.”

Orca.

Sade.

The great houses.

Veterans.

Monsters.

Even in this desperate situation—

a frontal breakthrough was not impossible.

Osiris looked toward Vikir.

“What do you think, little brother?”

“….”

Vikir did not answer.

He stood by the window—

counting stars.

Cindywendy frowned.

“This isn’t the time to admire the night sky.”

“You’re the one who insisted we hold Tochka.”

“Think of something.”

Normally—

Osiris might have calmed her down.

Tonight—

he was just as tense.

Then—

whoosh.

Night wind entered through the open window.

Cindywendy lifted a hand.

Osiris narrowed his eyes.

“…Wind?”

Naturally—

an open window could let in wind.

But Vikir’s expression changed.

He wet one finger.

Raised it.

“…Southeast.”

He crossed to the other windows.

Checked again.

Then returned to the southeastern side.

Looked toward the night sky.

“One.”

“Two.”

“Three.”

“Four.”

“Five.”

“Six.”

“Seven.”

The seven navigation stars.

A constellation known even to local shepherd children.

A reliable guide.

Whoosh.

Again—

southeast wind.

Cindywendy and Osiris slowly exchanged glances.

*Has wind ever come through that window before?*

Something about the room suddenly felt different.

Vikir counted again.

“One.”

“Two.”

“Three.”

“Four.”

“Five.”

“Six.”

“Seven.”

“One.”

“Two.”

“Three.”

“Four.”

“Five.”

“Six.”

“…Eight.”

a new blue light shone.

An eighth star.

Bright enough to burn itself into Vikir’s vision.

He nodded.

“Good.”

“One more.”

“It’s settled properly.”

“…?”

Cindywendy and Osiris understood nothing.

Then—

after ignoring everyone’s pressure for so long—

Vikir moved.

“Tomorrow.”

“At noon.”

“The final battle begins.”

The declaration sounded sudden.

But both Cindywendy and Osiris felt it.

This—

was not improvisation.

The next day’s battle was a stage Vikir had planned for a long time.

A genuine final act.

Noon.

The sun beat down brutally.

The Poison Human army surrounding Tochka began moving.

Orca stood on the wall.

His face—

heavier than anyone had seen it.

Not one drop of water remained.

Tomorrow no longer existed as a practical concept.

*We may not even last until midnight.*

Sade laughed quietly.

“Hehehe.”

“A man as old as you should have no reason to struggle for another twelve hours.”

not at all.

“No soldiers.”

“No water.”

“A true limit battle.”

“It’s been a while.”

“The Yuuni salt desert against the Baskerville dogs, maybe.”

“I don’t care about your ancient stories.”

“Hehehe.”

“Could be our final conversation.”

“And you’re still unpleasant.”

“…Die with dignity.”

“Hm?”

“I hate admitting it.”

“But you and I are from the same era.”

“Other than me—

“We represent that era now.”

“Don’t make the ending ugly.”

“Worry about yourself, fossil.”

“I was trying to give you sincere advice.”

defense.**
