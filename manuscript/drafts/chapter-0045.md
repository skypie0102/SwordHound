# Chapter 45: The Fiancée (3)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/045.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 45 is an independently aligned secondary witness only and contains substantial gender/name corruption. Established project terminology controls **Camus Morgue**, **Raspane Morgue**, **Adolf Morgue**, **Hugo Le Baskerville**, **Highsis / Middlesis / Lowsis Morgue**, and **Vikir Van Baskerville**. Chinese semantics control the childhood chronology, Camus's original marriage-alliance proposal, and the black cloak as her first treasured possession. The childhood clothing accident is retained as nonsexual embarrassment and an early crush without added detail or eroticization.

Seven years ago.

There was an exceptionally stubborn eight-year-old girl in the Morgue Clan.

Camus Morgue.

The youngest candidate to become clan head in the prestigious magical family of Morgue.

From the very beginning, there had been something extraordinary about her.

One day, the children of Morgue were gathered together for a lesson in magic.

“The path of magic is difficult,” their tutor warned. “When you gaze into the abyss, the abyss also gazes into you. Delve too deeply into the world of magic and you may be swallowed by something vast. You must always remain cautious.”

The children listening to him felt a vague, instinctive fear.

All except one.

Camus snorted.

“What nonsense.”

The tutor's brow furrowed.

“Lady Camus, why do you call it nonsense?”

“Because saying the abyss gazes back when I gaze into it is ridiculous.”

Camus continued with a perfectly straight face.

“The abyss of magic is so vast and deep that the human mind can't even comprehend it.”

“...”

“If I look into the abyss, why should the abyss notice me? An ant being able to see me doesn't mean I notice the ant. The abyss might not even know I'm looking at it, much less care. Claiming that it must gaze back is excessive self-importance.”

“...”

“Maybe things are different after reaching some extraordinary level. But children who can't even control their mana properly don't need to be frightened with ideas like that yet.”

The tutor closed his mouth.

Annoyingly enough, she had a point.

He himself had reached the Fifth Circle and could operate five mana circles, yet he had never felt himself being consumed by some mystical abyss.

Perhaps things changed at the Seventh Circle, which was regarded as the pinnacle of ordinary magic, or beyond it.

But that was another matter.

Once the tedious lesson ended, Camus rose expressionlessly from her seat.

*What is Uncle doing?*

There was only one person in the family who truly understood her talent and listened seriously when she spoke.

Camus liked her maternal uncle, Adolf Morgue, more than even her mother and clan head, Raspane Morgue.

As she passed the head's office, however, Camus heard Raspane and Adolf speaking inside.

“I hear the ruby vein extends beneath Baskerville territory?”

“Yes, Sister. Conflict may be unavoidable.”

“What a nuisance. I have no intention of yielding to that stubborn Hugo... and barbarians are active in the region as well. Are we going to end up in a three-way confrontation?”

“The fact that the vein extends into Baskerville territory puts us at a legal disadvantage. And the tribes there have troubled Baskerville for years. If a dispute breaks out locally, the clan could suffer enormous losses.”

Raspane and Adolf both looked grave.

That was when Camus stepped inside.

“Send me.”

They stared at her.

“Does this child even understand how serious the problem is...?”

“Wait, Sister. Let's hear her out. Camus is a genius. She may see something we adults don't.”

That was precisely why Camus liked Adolf.

After listening to her proposal, the two adults exchanged uncertain glances.

“Use a child's innocence to sound them out?” Raspane said.

“And then strengthen cooperation and push for joint development?” Adolf considered it for a moment. “That isn't bad.”

It was a startlingly shrewd suggestion for an eight-year-old.

But Camus was not finished.

“What about discussing a marriage alliance at the same time? Instead of one side yielding to the other, wouldn't it be better to cooperate as relatives?”

A marriage-alliance strategy.

Camus casually offered another useful idea.

Raspane's eyes sharpened with interest.

“A marriage alliance with Baskerville? Then whom should Morgue send?”

“Hm? Anyone. What about those useless triplets—Highsis, Middlesis, and Lowsis?”

Camus answered without the slightest hesitation.

Soon afterward, formal negotiations began.

Adolf brought Camus to Baskerville, and Camus used her position as a child to present the proposal to Hugo Le Baskerville.

She even used a metaphor to explain how the underground ruby vein crossed territorial boundaries.

Then, for the first time in her life, Camus tasted bitter defeat.

*That boy.*

An eight-year-old child had appeared on the other side as well.

She could not even properly explain how he had beaten her in the argument.

Logic aside, she had clearly lost in force of presence.

Camus eventually burst into tears from sheer frustration.

Nothing like that had ever happened to her before.

No child her age had ever been able to stand against her.

Neither had children several years older.

Even adults usually found themselves dragged along by her pace.

The world had always moved as Camus expected.

Until that day.

Everything had slipped beyond her control because of the black-haired boy she had just met.

*If it weren't for you...!*

Camus was furious.

She wanted to teach the first real rival of her life a lesson.

So, amid the clamor of the training grounds, Camus challenged him.

She attacked with everything she had.

The boy simply dodged.

Tap!

Tap, tap, tap!

Infuriatingly, all he did in return was flick her forehead as though they were playing some childish game.

Worse, he kept striking the exact same spot.

It hurt terribly.

The boy's irritating face seemed to fill her entire field of view.

Rage narrowed her world until there was nothing left but herself and him.

All Camus could think about was landing one good hit.

Then—

BOOM!

An accident erupted in the neighboring training ground.

Flames and debris swept through the area, ruining Camus's clothes.

She instinctively hid behind the dust and smoke, but she knew the cover would last only seconds.

Only then did panic hit her.

There were many people nearby besides her and the boy.

For Camus—the supernova of Morgue, the prodigy everyone admired, the future clan head—the thought of being exposed and humiliated in front of them was unbearable.

Eight years old or not, she had pride.

Camus fought back tears.

To be humiliated in front of peers she had always dismissed was bad enough.

Worst of all, Highsis, Middlesis, and Lowsis were training nearby.

If those three saw her crying in such a state, she would never hear the end of it.

*...What do I do?*

There was nowhere to run.

The dust would clear at any moment.

For one desperate instant, Camus thought she would rather disappear entirely than suffer the humiliation.

Then—

WHOOSH!

Her vision went dark.

Something heavy and warm settled over her shoulders.

Camus looked up.

The boy was standing in front of her.

He had immediately given her his own black Baskerville cloak so she could cover herself, apparently unconcerned about his own embarrassment.

*Doesn't he feel ashamed at all?*

Camus could not understand him.

He stood there without hesitation, as calm as if nothing unusual had happened.

Her heart began pounding.

Her first real rival.

Her first defeat.

The first person her own age who had faced her without fear.

And now this.

Too many firsts collided inside Camus's brilliant mind at once.

For the first time, her thoughts simply stopped.

She could only stare at him in stunned silence.

And, privately, she found herself very glad she had suggested the marriage-alliance strategy before leaving Morgue.

◆◆◆

After several more twists, the boy ended up facing Adolf.

Camus had always loved and respected her uncle.

At that moment, however, she found him strangely irritating.

*Adults always use being older as an excuse to bully children.*

Normally, Camus appreciated that Adolf treated her like an adult.

Now she found herself resenting the enormous difference in power between him and the boy.

Even so, the boy kept challenging him.

The gap in strength was obvious.

Yet he never backed down.

Camus's feelings became complicated.

She knew how powerful and exceptional Adolf was.

Still, she could not take her eyes off the boy.

To her own surprise, she even began hoping he would somehow win.

Then the impossible happened.

The boy turned what Camus had considered a hopeless situation around.

CRASH!

The water jar broke, soaking Adolf as water streamed down over him.

Her uncle's expression went completely blank.

Camus could not stop herself from cheering.

She even jumped up and down in excitement.

Adolf looked deeply wounded by both the result and her reaction, but Camus no longer cared.

She had always sneered at ordinary eight-year-olds for laughing too loudly and bouncing around over trivial things.

Yet now she was laughing and jumping exactly like one of them.

◆◆◆

After the negotiations ended successfully, Camus returned home and lay down on her bed.

She looked at the stuffed toy she usually hugged while sleeping.

From that day onward, she decided, she would put it away neatly instead.

“I'm not a little child anymore.”

In its place, she adopted a new habit.

A black cloak bearing the Baskerville insignia.

The cloak the boy had placed over her that day became Camus's first treasured possession.

Every night, she hugged it as she slept.

There was still a faint smell of sweat in the fabric.

It should have been unpleasant.

Instead, for some reason, Camus found the scent strangely familiar and reassuring.

Whenever she buried her face in the cloak, sleep came quickly.

Sometimes her heart would start beating faster for no obvious reason, leaving her restless beneath the blankets.

Sometimes she would catch herself biting the edge of the collar in frustration and immediately wonder what she was doing.

One day, her nanny reached for the cloak.

“Lady Camus, shall I have this washed?”

Camus snatched it back at once.

“No.”

“But it should be cleaned—”

“Something like this only has value if it isn't washed.”

The nanny could only stare at her.

Camus never allowed anyone to wash it.

Not once in seven years.

◆◆◆

The seasons changed.

Birthdays came and went.

Camus grew taller.

So did the questions she asked herself whenever she looked at the black cloak.

*Where is he now?*

*What is he doing?*

*Has he gotten taller too?*

Whenever Camus had an excuse to inspect the ruby mines near Baskerville territory, she went personally.

Officially, she was studying the mines.

In truth, she was always looking for one person.

She never found him.

All she heard was that Vikir Van Baskerville had been sent away for prolonged field training.

Then stories began spreading from Underdog City.

A young Deputy Magistrate had arrived and was causing one major incident after another.

The stories reached Morgue as well.

A public stake driven into the city square.

A reward offered to anyone bold enough to challenge the new order.

One remarkable achievement followed another.

Camus recognized the name immediately.

*You grew up.*

Not long after that, she went directly to Raspane.

“Send me on the joint operation with Baskerville.”

Raspane raised an eyebrow.

“You?”

“Yes.”

Camus made certain of one condition before anything else.

The newly appointed Deputy Magistrate of Underdog City had to be the representative sent by Baskerville.

Once that was settled, she threw herself into the preparations.

The day before Baskerville's delegation arrived, Camus personally chose the place where the two sides would meet.

A fortress surrounded by fields of beautiful cotton blossoms.

It was far more scenic than the other strongholds.

She also pushed Morgue's observers farther away than necessary.

There was no reason for everyone to crowd around the meeting point.

None at all.

After seven years, the wait was finally about to end.

Then the day came.

And everything immediately went wrong.

Highsis, Middlesis, and Lowsis picked a fight with the arriving Baskerville delegation and destroyed the atmosphere Camus had prepared.

She had intended to punish them properly for it.

Unfortunately, the three fled before she could.

Camus forced herself to let it go for the moment.

There was something much more important in front of her.

She rode toward Vikir.

“Welcome, partner.”

At first glance, he still looked much as she remembered.

But seven years had sharpened him.

His jaw was more defined. His nose was higher. The child she remembered had become unmistakably older.

Worse, he had somehow grown into an even more striking version of the boy Camus had replayed in her imagination all those years.

Heat crept into her face.

For one mortifying instant, her mind flashed back to the accident in the training ground seven years ago.

*How much has he changed since then?*

Camus immediately tried to crush the thought.

Without using so much as a spark of fire magic, she suddenly felt very warm.
