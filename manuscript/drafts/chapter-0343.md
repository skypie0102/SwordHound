Vikir’s vision slowly brightened.

When he looked up—

he was alone.

Dolores, who had been beside him a moment earlier, seemed to have been sent somewhere else.

“I landed somewhere unpleasant.”

Vikir surveyed his surroundings.

Leaves sharp as knives.

Dense thorn bushes.

Hot, humid air.

And beyond the jungle—

a broad river curved around the entire forest.

At first glance, it looked like an ordinary tropical rainforest.

There was one decisive difference.

**Daylilies of Blood.**

The grotesque aquatic plants covered the river.

A colony.

Vikir had landed in a region completely surrounded by them.

“…The entire jungle is enclosed.”

“Getting out directly won’t be easy.”

Every visible stretch of river was filled with Daylilies.

There were too many to count.

In case they released toxic gas—

Vikir put on the Picaresque Mask.

“Other than the Daylilies, it looks normal.”

He turned back toward the forest.

Then stopped.

“…!”

Something was wrong.

The enormous Daylily colony had distracted him.

Now that he looked again—

the jungle was changing in real time.

Sssssss—

Fresh leaves sprouted from withered branches.

Flowers bloomed.

Fruit formed.

The fruit fell.

Seeds germinated.

And the branch that had produced them shriveled and died.

Everything in the forest—

was being born and dying at astonishing speed.

As though organic time had been accelerated dozens of times over.

Vikir watched a muddy puddle.

The water visibly evaporated.

Tiny eggs inside hatched almost instantly.

Small insects took flight.

Circled above the puddle.

Then fell dead.

“…Mayflies?”

Even mayflies did not live **that** briefly.

*Time is passing quickly.*

Vikir looked again at the floor name.

**<Basement Floor 11 — River of Flow>**

Time flowed like water.

The name did not refer to the river.

It referred to the passage of time.

“A jungle where time moves fast.”

“Another strange floor.”

More precisely—

organic time moved fast.

The wind against his face was normal.

The river itself flowed at an ordinary speed.

Then Vikir noticed his body.

Sssssss—

The marks from deep wounds were fading.

Rapidly.

At a rate impossible even for the regeneration stored through Beelzebub.

*…Much faster than I thought.*

This was not merely double or triple speed.

“Then I need to hurry.”

Vikir moved.

◆◆◆

He paused farther inside the jungle.

Sunlight filtered through the canopy.

Green.

Red.

Blue.

Purple.

The vegetation changed color every few seconds.

The entire forest shimmered like a living stained-glass window.

“….”

Finding an enemy visually in this environment would be difficult.

So Vikir changed methods.

On a jungle battlefield—

nature itself was more dangerous than the enemy.

He had learned that thoroughly in Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

If you did not understand the ecosystem—

you did not move carelessly.

Fortunately—

only plant life seemed fully adapted to this accelerated aging.

For plants—

growth and decay were natural cycles.

“I’ll take higher ground.”

Vikir grabbed a vine.

Srrrk—

It broke apart in his hand.

It had already withered.

Another vine was growing from the roots.

“…Right.”

Vikir grabbed the tree trunk instead.

He had climbed countless trees during military service and during his time in Ballak.

The trunk was enormous.

That barely mattered now.

He started upward.

Then—

PING!

An arrow came from outside his vision.

Fast.

Fast enough that even Vikir reacted late.

He twisted his head aside.

The arrow missed—

but cut through hair beside his temple.

More arrows followed.

Puff!

Puff!

Puff!

The attacker knew the forest well.

The first surprise shot had been dangerous.

Now that Vikir had the direction—

the rest were easier to avoid.

*About seventy-four meters.*

One archer.

Every arrow carried unusual weight.

Imperial archery tended toward sharpness and precision.

These shots struck like cannon fire.

And yet—

they were not Ballak-style arrows either.

Still—

the technique felt familiar.

*This is…*

Before he could finish the thought—

another arrow drilled into the rock behind him.

Flash!

A point of dull metal appeared directly before his eyes.

A spear.

Extended perfectly straight—

so fast that from the front it looked like nothing more than a dot.

Like horizontal lightning—

it stabbed toward Vikir’s face.

Vikir shifted.

Two figures appeared.

A man and a woman—

both apparently in their twenties.

Animal skins.

Leaves.

Almost no conventional clothing.

The woman’s mud had already dried.

*One ranged.*

*One melee.*

The woman filled every opening with arrows.

No—

not merely filled openings.

A combination attack refined by long practice.

Crunch!

twisted—

and broke it.

The man tried to resist.

The strength difference was overwhelming.

Crack!

His wrist gave out.

Vikir pulled him forward—

then clamped a hand around his throat.

“Ghk!”

The woman rushed in.

Puff-puff-puff-puff!

Four arrows—

fired at once while running.

Vikir stepped back.

Raised one hand.

*Reflexes really does matter.*

Only one point.

The difference was enormous.
