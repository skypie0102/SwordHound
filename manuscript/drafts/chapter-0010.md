# Chapter 10: Hounds of Hell (3)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/010.txt`. Canonical English forms for Bloody Beans, Hellhound, Cerberus, Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, and the accepted Baskerville rank system are normalized from current project/Fandom evidence. Recovered English Chapter 10 is an aligned secondary reference only.

Vikir remembered something that had happened only a few days earlier.

Hugo Le Baskerville had given him a reward for placing first on the written examination.

‘Is there any snack you want?’

‘Chocolate!’

‘Butler. Take him to the pantry and give him what he wants. Don’t let him become absurdly greedy. Only what he can carry himself.’

That was when Vikir had received access to the pantry.

Following John Barrymore into the underground storehouse, he had acquired an entire pouch of raw cocoa beans.

‘Years ago, the Patriarch personally led clan forces against the barbarians on the western frontier and turned a vast stretch of jungle into farmland. One of that region’s specialties is the Bloody Bean. They say a single bean is concentrated enough to flavor roughly a hundred liters of chocolate.’

Bloody Beans.

Absurdly concentrated cocoa beans with an overwhelmingly strong flavor.

Vikir reached into the pouch at his waist and removed one.

The Hellhound had been forced to detour around the thin line of water and had lost much of its speed.

Even so, it continued charging with its jaws spread wide.

To an experienced hound like Vikir, the posture looked almost as though the beast were asking him to throw something directly into its mouth.

Flick!

Vikir snapped his thumb.

The Bloody Bean flew straight between the Hellhound’s teeth.

The reaction was immediate.

Yelp!

The monster swallowed what had flown into its mouth.

Then its body convulsed.

K-krrk! Krrk! Yip!

Foam gathered around its jaws.

Thick, sticky saliva and mucus dripped from its mouth.

Watery feces leaked from its rear.

Its entire body began to spasm.

Its heart hammered as though it might burst, and its eyes reddened until they looked ready to pop.

‘As expected.’

Vikir nodded.

Every hunter who had lived through the Age of Destruction knew one simple fact.

Canine monsters were highly sensitive to chocolate.

The substances contained in cocoa were practically poison to dogs.

Even a hound born from hell was still a hound in that respect.

‘Let’s see… What were the symptoms again? Mucus and vomiting. Difficulty breathing. Incontinence. Diarrhea. Rising body temperature. Accelerated heartbeat. Loss of appetite. Convulsions. Seizures. Extreme agitation… and eventually death.’

Vikir tightened his grip on the blunt shortsword.

He followed the staggering Hellhound deeper into the root-filled forest.

‘The stomach and heart are hit first. Then the liver.’

Unfortunately, the Hellhound’s stomach, heart, and liver were protected by ribs harder than steel.

The dulled examination sword could neither cut through those ribs nor slip cleanly between them.

So Vikir aimed for an organ left relatively exposed by the rib cage—one already being heavily strained by the poison.

‘… The kidneys.’

The lower abdomen.

The kidneys, overwhelmed by chocolate toxins.

Vikir turned the blunt sword sideways and drove it in with all his strength.

The edge could not slice or pierce the monster’s hide, but the force of the impact was more than enough to batter the organs beneath it.

Thud!

The more damage the kidneys suffered, the less effectively the Hellhound could handle the poison coursing through its body.

The beast expelled another stream of burning-hot waste and collapsed.

Its tongue, more than a meter long, lolled from its mouth.

Vikir did not stop there.

He thoroughly extinguished the life of the monster lying helpless before him.

Hellhounds were canine monsters, but they were also creatures that carried and consumed the fires of hell.

Unless one killed them completely, their vitality could flare back up like an ember hidden under ash.

‘Even a fire that looks dead needs to be checked twice.’

Vikir raised the blunt shortsword.

Thud! Thud! Thud! Thud!

With a blade this dull, beating the monster to death was the only reliable answer.

Before long, something remarkable happened.

Sssssss…

An invisible current flowed out of the Hellhound’s corpse and seeped into Vikir’s body.

People called such things by many names.

Experience.

Effort.

Karma.

Cause and effect.

Whatever one called it, the strange energy accumulated inside the body and was eventually converted into physical growth.

Vikir had single-handedly killed a high-ranking monster beyond anything children his age could normally imagine defeating.

As a result, his body grew stronger again.

The blessing of the River Styx combined with the power gained from killing the Hellhound.

His resistance to poison, magic, and physical force rose even further.

‘… My bones feel thicker. Have I grown a little taller too?’

The shortsword in his hand seemed noticeably lighter.

The weapon itself had not changed.

His strength had.

Whoosh—whoosh—

A sword that other eight- and nine-year-olds struggled to swing with both hands could now be moved effortlessly with one.

Even so, Vikir remained far from the strength he had possessed before his regression.

‘At this pace, I should be able to recover everything before the coming-of-age ceremony.’

He was only eight years old.

Yet he had already reached High Sword Expert.

Most Baskerville children would not approach such a level until much later.

Vikir was confident that within the next seven years he could surpass the power of his previous life entirely.

‘And if I take first place in this practical examination, there should be another reward.’

He looked down at the Hellhound’s still-warm corpse with satisfaction.

If he dragged a B+ monster back to the clan, first place was practically guaranteed.

Crossing the restricted boundary might create trouble, but most of that blame would fall on the Guide Dogs responsible for security.

From the examinee’s perspective, an eight-year-old entering forbidden territory alone and returning with a B+ monster corpse was more likely to earn praise than punishment.

Vikir could already imagine Hugo’s expression.

Crunch—

He grabbed the Hellhound by the tail and began dragging it uphill.

Monster corpses obeyed strange laws in the human world, their souls bound in some fashion to the Demon Realm.

The result was a body far heavier than its appearance suggested.

For Vikir’s strengthened body, however, the weight was manageable.

Scrape—crunch—drag—

The thorn-covered slope ahead looked almost like a flower-lined road.

All he had to do was return, hand the corpse over to the guardian knights, and claim first place.

The reward waiting for him was something he had never dared dream of receiving in his previous life—a treasure whose true value almost no one in this era understood.

Then Vikir stopped.

Grrrrrr…

Growls came from deep within the forest behind him.

More and more throats joined the sound.

‘… Damn.’

Vikir let go of the corpse.

For one moment, he had forgotten an important fact.

Hellhounds lived in packs.

Before long, yellow and red sulfuric flames began appearing throughout the darkness between the trees.

Eleven Hellhounds had surrounded him.

Yet Vikir did not look afraid.

He smiled faintly.

The monsters that saw that smile lowered their bodies and growled uneasily instead.

Could one hound recognize another?

Even after surviving the destruction of the world and passing through regression, the scent of blood engraved into Vikir’s soul had not vanished.

Just as the venomous snakes in his cradle had once frozen before him, the Hellhounds hesitated in front of the murderous intent he now released openly.

Vikir spoke.

“Come on. I still have plenty of beans.”

The Bloody Beans rattled inside the pouch at his waist.

One Hellhound had already strengthened his body significantly.

If he killed all eleven gathered here, the gain would be difficult to estimate.

Then—

Whine…

The Hellhounds suddenly tucked their tails between their legs and began backing away.

“…?”

Vikir frowned.

Once a Hellhound bared its fangs, it did not turn its back and flee.

But these monsters were unmistakably retreating in fear.

No matter how vicious Vikir’s killing intent was, he still possessed the body of an eight-year-old. It should not have been enough to send more than ten Hellhounds running.

Then what was frightening them?

What kind of external threat could make an entire pack retreat with their tails tucked?

Vikir soon received the answer.

Grrrrrr…

The sound of saliva boiling together with sulfuric fire.

The true master of this part of the forest emerged.

Six eyes burned in the darkness.

Three enormous heads pushed out between the twisted roots.

“… Something like this came all the way down here?”

Even Vikir looked surprised.
