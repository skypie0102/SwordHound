# Chapter 463: Infiltration of the Water Source (10)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/463.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 461 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established project terminology controls Tochka, Orca Montreuil-sur-Mer Javert, Nouvelle Vague, Poison Humans, Red Death, D’Ordume, Souare, BDISSEM, Flubber J. Tarbond, Sakkuth de Leviathan, and the displaced Nouvelle Vague wardens.

The world outside Tochka had turned red.

Inside the fortress—

the stored **Saintess’s Tears** were drying up.

Dolores and the other clergy had spent the holy water across the walls—

again—

and again—

and again.

The protective power weakened.

The Poison Humans attacked harder.

Even Tochka’s walls looked lower now.

Because corpses had piled so high beneath them—

the ground itself had risen.

The siege had begun at dawn.

Now—

the sun was already descending.

BOOM.

A blackened hand appeared over the parapet.

One enormous palm—

larger than an ordinary soldier’s torso.

Rotten.

Twisted.

Dripping poison.

[OOOOOOOO—!]

A mutant hauled itself up.

Far beyond ordinary Poison Human combat strength.

Oil.

Fire.

Explosives.

Nails.

All of them had torn it apart during the climb.

That only made it uglier.

“IT’S ON THE WALL!”

“Poison Human—no!”

“That thing is a monster!”

“Push it back!”

Soldiers rammed spears into it.

Poles.

Hooks.

It was like trying to move a boulder with toothpicks.

The six-meter mutant climbed fully onto the wall.

Its jaw was gone.

Its tongue hung loose.

And mouths had opened where mouths should never exist.

One in the palm.

Another along the arm.

Teeth.

Gums.

Tongues.

The nearest young soldier stumbled backward.

Tripped on a stone.

The mutant’s hand rose.

The mouth in its palm opened.

“No!”

Several soldiers rushed to help.

Too late.

Then—

BOOM!

Something hit hard enough to shake the battlement.

The mutant vanished.

The young soldier blinked.

Far away—

the Poison Human flew through the air like garbage.

CRASH!

It landed outside the wall—

flattened into meat.

A massive old man stood over the soldier.

**Orca.**

Former warden of Nouvelle Vague.

Huge club in hand.

“Hold your position.”

The boy stopped crying.

Saluted.

“Yes, sir!”

Then returned to the line.

Orca moved on—

as though nothing had happened.

His voice rolled over the walls.

“Sector commanders!”

“Report the condition of every tower!”

“Abandon damaged outer sections if necessary!”

“Hold the inner wall!”

“Pull the city-security militia up here too!”

“Whatever is happening inside the city no longer matters!”

He saw everything.

Where the wall was failing.

Where it remained firm.

Where reinforcements needed to go.

And Orca did not command from safety.

He fought at the front.

His chain-linked club smashed skulls from the wall.

Spears and arrows struck his heavy fur coat—

failed to penetrate.

CRASH!

One sweep knocked multiple mutants back over the edge.

Cheers erupted.

“General Orca!”

“The god of defensive warfare!”

“With Orca here—

Tochka won’t fall!”

The soldiers saw only his back.

Not his face.

Not the sweat under his white hair.

Not the cold running down his spine.

*This is ugly.*

Orca looked beyond the wall.

Red.

Nothing but red.

Poison Humans.

Mutants.

An army large enough to fuel an entire civil war.

Leviathan alone—

had somehow gained the strength to confront several great houses.

And the attackers now were not ordinary soldiers.

Most were larger—

stronger—

more evolved.

Tochka was famous as an impregnable fortress.

But even the best fortress failed under enough mass.

Especially when most defenders were refugee militia.

*If my Nouvelle Vague men were still here…*

Orca reached into his pocket.

For a cigar.

Nothing.

The old supply had vanished with the prison’s destruction.

His throat—

already dry from lack of water—

worked automatically.

Then—

rustle.

A cigar case appeared from the right.

Filled with thick cigars.

Orca pulled one out without thinking.

Then—

scratch.

A match appeared from the left.

Flame touched the cigar.

Orca froze.

Turned.

Two massive mutants leapt toward him.

ROAR!

Then—

BOOM!

Both disappeared.

One shredded by a storm of axe blades.

The other—

impaled by stone spikes erupting from wall and ground.

Two familiar men stood through the falling blood.

“So.”

“Who’s becoming the next warden?”

Orca stared.

“D’Ordume.”

“Souare.”

“How are you two here?”

Then a sharp voice came from behind.

“Am I so small you can’t see me?”

“I’ve grown a little, you know.”

A girl in a black helmet stood there.

**BDISSEM.**

She spread both arms.

Restraints flew.

THUD-THUD-THUD!

Poison Humans climbing the walls suddenly lost strength.

Mana vanished.

Bodies went slack.

They fell.

The suppression was weaker outside the deep sea—

but enough to break the momentum of the assault.

“…Huh.”

For once—

Orca looked genuinely surprised.

Then something even stranger happened.

Gurgle.

Hissssss—

Green spread across the wall.

At first—

like moss.

Then more.

And more.

It seeped from the cracks between Tochka’s bricks.

A vast slippery mass.

Poison Humans grabbed at the wall.

Their fingers touched the substance.

Then—

SSSSSS!

The green slime turned violently acidic.

Skin dissolved.

Flesh.

Bone.

Mutants screamed as the living liquid climbed over them.

Orca watched the enormous mass pour down the fortress wall.

“…That thing came too.”

**Flubber J. Tarbond.**

The most mysterious life-form of Nouvelle Vague.

Older than the prison itself.

And now—

here.
