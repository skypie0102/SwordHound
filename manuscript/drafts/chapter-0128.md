# Chapter 128: Admission to the Academy (3)

“…What?”

Pigi stared at Vikir.

Then his expression turned alarmed.

“You're not going to orientation?”

“No.”

Vikir continued unpacking.

“I'm not going.”

Pigi waved both hands.

“No, no! You have to go!”

“If you skip O.T., the upperclassmen will remember it.”

“And you'll get isolated from the other freshmen!”

“That makes it harder to get information and build connections too…”

An **outsider**.

Someone who failed to become part of the group and ended up on its margins.

Such students suffered in more ways than simple loneliness.

Sudden class cancellations.

Schedule changes.

Group assignments.

Any situation requiring shared information could become a problem.

Networking became harder.

So did gathering basic information.

For an ordinary freshman—

Pigi was correct.

Orientation was something one should attend.

But Vikir was not an ordinary freshman.

Being overlooked was exactly what he wanted.

The less attention people paid him—

the easier it would be to move outside their sight.

Vikir looked down at Pigi.

The concern in the boy's eyes was genuine.

He truly cared about the Academy life of someone he had only just met.

*Still almost foolishly kind.*

Pigi was timid.

Careful.

And extremely good at collecting and analyzing information.

If Cindywendy met him—

she would probably want to recruit him immediately.

But among Colosseo's freshmen—

that same personality made Pigi easy to exploit.

The Academy valued practical skill and real combat ability far more than theory.

That tradition was not unique to Colosseo.

The Magic Tower.

Varangian.

The continent's greatest institutions shared the same broad principle.

Ability ruled.

Not meaningless fistfights.

Not childish duels.

Practical-exam performance.

Without visible results—

it was difficult to receive respect.

Pigi was intelligent.

His information-gathering ability was excellent.

But he possessed little talent in either Cold or Hot Department practical disciplines.

His practical scores were poor.

Combined with his gentle—

or timid—

temperament—

that made him an easy target for ridicule and exclusion.

*And before regression, his first-semester roommate was Highbro.*

That had made everything worse.

Pigi spent much of his Academy life shrinking into himself.

Highbro rarely treated him with basic respect.

Students from other classes—

even upperclassmen—

learned that Pigi could be pushed around.

And yet—

he had remained kind.

Whenever he found someone weaker than himself—

Pigi reached out.

Helped.

Did everything he could.

“…”

Vikir watched him for a moment.

Then looked away.

Pigi noticed none of it.

He was still trying to sell orientation.

“A lot of amazing upperclassmen will be there!”

“Saintess Dolores, the student council president, is attending!”

“And our own class is incredible too!”

Pigi started counting on his fingers.

“You know Tudor, heir of Donquixote?”

“And Bianca Fo Usher?”

“The Baskerville triplets enrolled too, and all three are supposed to be monsters!”

“There’s also Sancho from the Northern Mercenary Guild Alliance!”

“He gave up a chance to enter Varangian at the top and came here instead!”

Pigi took a breath—

then immediately continued.

“And Sinclaire!”

“She could have gone to the Magic Tower as one of its top entrants, but chose Colosseo instead!”

“She's already first in the Hot Department practical ranking!”

Pigi's face became almost reverent.

“They're already being called the next generation of heroes.”

“You have to see them!”

“I can't believe I get to be in the same class as people like that…”

The remarkable part—

was how much Pigi already knew.

If an upperclassman heard him summarize the incoming class this accurately—

a club or noble house might try to recruit him for his information skills alone.

Pigi leaned toward Vikir.

“Well?”

His eyes sparkled.

“Don't you want to go now?”

Vikir shook his head.

“No.”

Pigi froze.

“Still?”

“Still.”

“What?”

His shoulders slumped.

“Why? Why not?”

Pigi had clearly imagined attending with his roommate—

someone familiar at his side while both navigated the crowd.

Almost no freshman openly skipped orientation.

Vikir's answer was brief.

“I just don't want to go.”

That ended the matter.

Vikir finished arranging his luggage.

Then left the room.

He disappeared down a corridor crowded with freshmen.

Pigi stared after him.

“…Cool.”

“My roommate is incredible.”

“I should learn to be decisive like that too!”

Pigi remained relentlessly positive.

◆◆◆

Evening descended.

The sunset burned red above Colosseo's high walls.

The roads sloping down toward the academic buildings filled with freshmen.

Girls dressed carefully.

Boys wearing their best clothes.

Yet no amount of preparation could hide how new all of them were.

Unsteady makeup.

Overworked hairstyles.

Awkward posture.

Young students drifted toward groups—

quietly assessing one another.

Upperclassmen guided them toward the orientation rooms.

“Cold Department, Class A—over here!”

“Hot Department, Class C! Gather beneath the red flag!”

“Student council members, guide the new students to their assigned rooms!”

Classes had not formally begun.

Empty classrooms filled with upperclassmen and new students.

Rumors spread through the corridors immediately.

“This year's freshmen are amazing.”

“The practical scores are ridiculous.”

“Tudor, Bianca, and Sinclaire are the obvious standouts, right?”

“Individually, maybe.”

“But what about the Baskerville triplets?”

“Their synergy as a group might be even more dangerous.”

Then someone's voice dropped.

“Actually…”

“I heard *that person* from Baskerville enrolled this year.”

The rumor spread instantly.

“Vikir?”

“Isn't that just a story?”

“Someone in his teens couldn't possibly have accomplished everything they say.”

“Maybe Baskerville invented him.”

“Noble families exaggerate their own prestige all the time.”

“And Baskerville is closed-off enough that no one can verify much.”

“There are more than ten freshmen named Vikir this year.”

“Every one I've heard about is a commoner.”

“Wait.”

“One of them did stand out.”

“The written-exam first place is named Vikir too.”

“A written exam?”

Someone laughed.

“Who cares about theory?”

“Practical monsters are much cooler.”

Gradually—

the hallway chatter died down.

Every classroom filled.

A few short conversations.

A few exchanged looks.

Students who had been strangers that morning were already forming loose circles.

Temporary friendships—

but friendships nonetheless.

A student council member raised a roster.

“All right, 20th class!”

“Let's take attendance!”

Names began echoing through the room.

Then—

“Vikir.”

“Here.”

“Vikir.”

“Here.”

“Vikir.”

“Here.”

“Vikir.”

Silence.

The student council member looked again.

“Vikir?”

No answer.

One Vikir was missing.

**Vikir Van Baskerville.**

Or—

for now—

simply Vikir.

As promised—

he had not come to orientation.

Instead—

a black cloak covered his body.

A beaked plague-doctor mask hid his face.

The **Night Hound** stood on the peak of one of Colosseo's towers.

Tap.

He stepped onto the roof.

Beyond the Academy's wall lay the Imperial Capital—

a beautiful maze of canals and bridges.

Vikir vanished silently into the dark city.

While the rest of the 20th class attended orientation and formed connections—

Vikir began working through the names on his blacklist.

Demons.

Collaborators.

People who had betrayed humanity in his previous life.

Faces of fallen comrades passed through his mind.

*Wait a little longer.*

*This time, I'll settle those old debts.*

Following the faint traces of demonic miasma spread throughout the capital—

the Night Hound began his hunt.
