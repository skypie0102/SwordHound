# Chapter 2: Hellhound (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/002.txt`. Canonical English proper nouns checked against the user-designated Fandom wiki. Recovered English Chapter 2 is an aligned secondary reference only.

‘Where am I?’

Vikir checked his body.

No mana. No strength.

Even the aura he had accumulated through countless brushes with death, paid for in blood, was gone without a trace.

‘Did I fall into hell?’

But that was not it.

This place… was too desolate even for hell.

A place so wretched it could make hell itself weep.

That was the Baskerville Clan.

There was no way he could fail to recognize the atmosphere of the place where he had lived as a hound for more than thirty years.

The smell of blood. The smell of pus. The stench of everything filthy and dead.

Vikir Van Baskerville instinctively realized that he had returned to shortly after his birth.

‘What do I do now?’

There was not much a body barely a hundred days old could do.

Roll over and signal the nursery wet nurse that he wanted milk?

Just then.

“Doesn’t look like there’s a single useful one among them.”

A familiar voice reached his ears.

Hugo Le Baskerville, patriarch of the Baskerville Clan, stood in the middle of the nursery.

The instant Vikir heard his voice, his body instinctively tried to rise and kneel.

Fortunately, an infant’s body made that impossible.

Grind—

The sight of the man chiefly responsible for framing and executing him made Vikir clench his jaw on instinct.

But he had no teeth yet, so not a sound came out.

‘Calm down.’

The days when he had struggled desperately to become a true Baskerville, to earn his father’s acknowledgment, and to escape the stigma of being an illegitimate child were over.

That life of inferior blood and a past dried dark like an old bloodstain was finished.

This life would be different.

He would never again live like a hunting dog discarded once the rabbit was gone.

Then came something that hardened Vikir’s resolve even further.

“Take the children to the Cradle of Swords.”

Hugo’s voice rang out.

As a wet nurse carried him away in her arms, Vikir thought,

‘The first trial?’

The Cradle of Swords was the first journey toward the River Styx that encircled the small hill.

Once a Baskerville child passed through the walls of blades and immersed themselves in the Styx, they were reborn as a warrior of the clan.

Of course, the power of the River Styx was not abundant enough for everyone.

The weak were meat for the strong. The fittest survived.

The strong took more.

Even at birth, that law did not change.

The Baskerville children had to escape the Cradle of Swords as quickly as possible and enter the River Styx.

The sooner they entered, and the longer they stayed submerged, the greater the advantage they would gain over their siblings.

So the instant Vikir was placed in the center of the Cradle of Swords, he moved.

… Grip!

His two tiny hands closed around the blades.

In some noble houses, infants were given objects to grasp on their first birthday—symbols meant to guide their futures.

The children of the Baskervilles, however, were made to grasp blades that threatened their futures from their very first trial.

Slice—tear—rip—

The blades cut across his entire body.

Every time he forced himself through the narrow spaces between them, burning pain surged through him.

But that did not matter.

He had long since grown accustomed to pain like this over the decades he had lived as a hound.

Biting, clawing, subduing others—he had endured the backlash of violence more times than he could count.

‘And the deeper the wounds, the better. That will let the Styx seep into my body more easily.’

Vikir knew the Baskerville Clan’s secrets, legends, myths, and ghost stories.

That was precisely why he knew how to draw the greatest possible benefit from the River Styx.

Wriggle… wriggle… wriggle…

The infant’s soft body was guided through hell by the hardened soul of a hound.

A road of blood.

A red road.

The blood running from his body streamed downhill, showing him the direction forward—the shortest route.

Vikir kept crawling along the path marked by his own blood.

Before long, he reached the Baskerville Clan’s sacred ground.

The River Styx flowed through the marshland.

Immersion in that river hardened the body like steel and made the soul extraordinarily clear.

Vikir curled into a fetal position and threw himself into the water.

Plop!

Heavy.

It felt as though he had been submerged in boiling molten iron.

The water steamed around him. Falling into it with countless blade wounds covering his body was naturally excruciating.

But Vikir endured the torment and waited.

Waited for the waters of the Styx to seep into his wounds.

Before long—

Sssssss…

His body began to change.

The Styx flowed through his wounds, into his marrow and internal organs, remaking Vikir’s body from within.

‘It really is different.’

Vikir was astonished that a better beginning could create such a dramatic difference.

His body in his previous life had been hardened by experience, but it had remained small and thin.

His bones themselves had been frail, limiting how much muscle he could build, and his mana channels had been narrow, preventing him from raising his mana to its full potential.

His brothers had been different.

They were tall, with thick bones and wide mana channels.

That was the difference in talent, aptitude, and starting point.

In his previous life, Vikir had been nearly the last child to escape the Cradle of Swords.

As a result, he had barely benefited from the River Styx.

His siblings had already absorbed most of its power.

After that, his illegitimate birth and poor natural aptitude had always pushed him to the very bottom.

While others ate clean food, wore clean clothes, slept in clean quarters, and received clean work—

Vikir ate filthy food, wore filthy clothes, slept in filthy places, and did filthy jobs.

The rewards he earned through his own labor were handed to his half-brothers time and again.

The war against the demons had been no different.

No matter how many demons and fiends he killed, the credit always went to his father or his half-brothers.

Vikir received almost nothing in return.

And demons were not the only targets he had been ordered to kill.

His father’s political enemies were human, too.

Vikir had moved among the Seven Great Families carrying out espionage, assassination, intelligence gathering, ambushes, threats, and other covert work, helping raise the Baskervilles to the foremost position among them.

… And what had been his reward?

When the rabbit is dead, the hunting dog is boiled.

The rabbit was gone. The hunt was over. So the hound was cooked.

Vikir was accused of colluding with the demons, made to bear Hugo’s sins in his place, and ultimately executed.

His only real crime had been one thing.

‘Knowing too much.’

Grind—

Vikir clenched his jaw.

Before he knew it, teeth were rapidly growing inside his mouth and striking against one another.

The River Styx surged through him like rage flooding his marrow, thickening his bones, toughening his flesh, and widening his mana channels.

The searing pain continued without pause.

It still did not matter.

Vikir began drinking the river water outright.

He intended to strengthen his internal organs as well.

An old legend passed down through the Baskerville Clan came to mind.

‘There was an invincible warrior in the clan long ago, wasn’t there?’

A peerless swordsman whose bones would not break even when struck with an axe.

Yet his end had been absurd.

A rival from another family shot him in the heel with a poisoned arrow, and he later died from the aftereffects.

The reason lay in his infancy.

His wet nurse had held him upside down by the ankles when she immersed him in the River Styx. The parts covered by her palms never touched the water and remained comparatively weak.

‘I can’t leave any place like that.’

Vikir twisted and writhed with everything he had beneath the water.

He did his best to make sure every part of his body touched the river.

If his wounds tore open further in the process, all the better.

That only allowed the Styx to seep deeper into him.

Bubble… bubble… bubble…

As he lost blood, his consciousness began to blur.

Breathing grew difficult. He desperately wanted to surface.

But he could not.

The River Styx would not accept a child again once they had left its embrace.

If he raised only his head above the surface to breathe, then his head would never receive the river’s protection again.

So Vikir clung desperately to a stone on the riverbed and endured.

At the same time, he kept moving his arms so even the palms gripping the stone would remain exposed to the water.

Then faint voices reached his ears.

“Young master! You have to come out!”

“If you stay under any longer, you’ll die!”

“Oh my god! What do we do?!”

“Save him! We have to pull him out!”

The people above seemed to be in complete chaos.

Naturally so.

Too much greed could become poison. Strengthening the body was one thing, but it meant nothing if he suffocated to death.

Then even Hugo’s voice reached him.

“Son. It’s time to come up.”

But the voice of Hugo Le Baskerville only stoked Vikir’s fighting spirit further.

Push—

Vikir rose.

But he did not simply come to the surface.

Gulp—gulp—gulp—gulp—

Until the very last instant, he filled his mouth with the water of the Styx and swallowed.

Then—

“Haaah—!”

The moment his face broke the surface, Vikir sucked in air to his heart’s content.

His mouth opened wide as he gasped for breath.

At the sight, Hugo Le Baskerville broke into a rare, radiant smile.

“Hahaha! Look at this little fellow. He’s already grown teeth!”

A hound in boiling water had bared its fangs.
