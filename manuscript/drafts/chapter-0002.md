‘Where am I?’

<!-- source-p:002 -->
Vikir examined his body.

<!-- source-p:003 -->
It held no mana and was utterly weak.

<!-- source-p:004 -->
Not a trace remained of the aura he had built up while repeatedly hovering at death’s door and coughing up blood.

<!-- source-p:005 -->
‘Have I fallen into hell?’

<!-- source-p:006 -->
No.

<!-- source-p:007 -->
This place… was too desolate even to call hell.

<!-- source-p:008 -->
Even hell would have seemed hospitable beside the Baskerville family.

<!-- source-p:009 -->
‘I could never mistake the atmosphere of the place where I spent thirty years as a hunting dog.’

<!-- source-p:010 -->
The smell of blood, of pus, of everything filthy and dead.

<!-- source-p:011 -->
Vikir Van Baskerville knew instinctively that he had returned to his infancy.

<!-- source-p:012 -->
‘What should I do now?’

<!-- source-p:013 -->
There was little he could do in a body only about a hundred days old.

<!-- source-p:014 -->
Roll over and signal the nursery nanny to nurse him?

<!-- source-p:015 -->
Just then—

<!-- source-p:016 -->
“None of them looks useful.”

<!-- source-p:017 -->
A familiar voice reached him.

<!-- source-p:018 -->
Hugo Le Baskerville, head of the Baskerville family, stood in the middle of the nursery.

<!-- source-p:019 -->
At the sound of his voice, Vikir almost rose and bowed out of habit from his previous life.

<!-- source-p:020 -->
Fortunately, his infant body made that impossible.

<!-- source-p:021 -->
Clench—

<!-- source-p:022 -->
At the sight of the man who had framed and executed him, he clenched his jaw.

<!-- source-p:023 -->
But he had no teeth yet, so he made no sound.

<!-- source-p:024 -->
‘Calm down.’

<!-- source-p:025 -->
In his previous life, he had struggled desperately to belong to the Baskerville family, to earn his father’s recognition, to cast off the stigma of being an illegitimate child.

<!-- source-p:026 -->
Inferior blood. No matter what he did, that label had clung to him.

<!-- source-p:027 -->
This life would be different. He would not be a hound boiled in the pot once the rabbits were gone.

<!-- source-p:028 -->
Just then—

<!-- source-p:029 -->
An opportunity arose that strengthened Vikir’s resolve.

<!-- source-p:030 -->
“Take the children to the Cradle of Swords.”

<!-- source-p:031 -->
Hugo’s order rang out.

<!-- source-p:032 -->
As the nanny carried him away, Vikir’s thoughts raced.

<!-- source-p:033 -->
‘The first rite of passage?’

<!-- source-p:034 -->
The Cradle of Swords wound around a small hill toward the River Styx.

<!-- source-p:035 -->
Once they passed through the maze of blades and plunged into the Styx, the Baskerville children were reborn as warriors.

<!-- source-p:036 -->
But the river did not have enough power to bless them all equally.

<!-- source-p:037 -->
The weak became prey to the strong. Only the fittest survived.

<!-- source-p:038 -->
The strong took the greater share. That rule held from birth.

<!-- source-p:039 -->
The Baskerville children had to escape the Cradle of Swords and enter the Styx as quickly as possible.

<!-- source-p:040 -->
To gain an advantage over their siblings, they had to enter the water early and stay submerged as long as they could.

<!-- source-p:041 -->
So Vikir began moving the instant he was thrown into the middle of the Cradle of Swords.

<!-- source-p:042 -->
Grip—

<!-- source-p:043 -->
He seized a blade with both tiny hands.

<!-- source-p:044 -->
Children of other noble families grasped objects meant to help their futures during their first grasping ceremony. Baskerville children had to grasp blades that threatened theirs.

<!-- source-p:045 -->
Slash—rip—tear—

<!-- source-p:046 -->
The blades sliced his skin.

<!-- source-p:047 -->
Each time he forced himself through a gap between the blades, searing pain coursed through his body.

<!-- source-p:048 -->
But that did not matter.

<!-- source-p:049 -->
Decades as a hunting dog had made him accustomed to such pain. He had endured it countless times while biting, clawing at, and subduing others.

<!-- source-p:050 -->
‘Besides, the deeper the wounds, the better the Styx’s water can seep into my body.’

<!-- source-p:051 -->
Vikir knew the Baskervilles’ secrets, legends, myths, and ghost stories.

<!-- source-p:052 -->
He knew how to make the most of the River Styx.

<!-- source-p:053 -->
Wriggle… wriggle… wriggle…

<!-- source-p:054 -->
The hound’s tenacious soul dragged the child’s fragile body toward hell.

<!-- source-p:055 -->
Blood marked a crimson trail. Flowing from his body and down the slope, it showed him the shortest way forward.

<!-- source-p:056 -->
Vikir crawled in the direction his blood flowed.

<!-- source-p:057 -->
Before long, he reached the Baskervilles’ sacred grounds.

<!-- source-p:058 -->
The River Styx flowed through the marsh.

<!-- source-p:059 -->
Its waters made the body as hard as steel and the soul remarkably clear.

<!-- source-p:060 -->
Vikir curled into a fetal position and threw himself into the river.

<!-- source-p:061 -->
Splash!

<!-- source-p:062 -->
The weight pressed down on him. The pain was like being plunged into boiling molten iron.

<!-- source-p:063 -->
Steam rose from the water. Falling into it with cuts all over his body was sheer torment.

<!-- source-p:064 -->
But Vikir endured the boiling pain and waited.

<!-- source-p:065 -->
He waited for the Styx’s water to seep through his torn and gaping wounds.

<!-- source-p:066 -->
Before long—

<!-- source-p:067 -->
Sss…

<!-- source-p:068 -->
His body began to change.

<!-- source-p:069 -->
The water seeped through his wounds into his bones and organs, transforming his body completely.

<!-- source-p:070 -->
‘It really is different.’

<!-- source-p:071 -->
Vikir marveled at how much a good start could change.

<!-- source-p:072 -->
In his previous life, his body had been tough and battle-hardened, but small and thin compared with the others.

<!-- source-p:073 -->
His weak bones limited how much muscle he could build, and his narrow mana channels restricted the growth of his aura.

<!-- source-p:074 -->
His brothers were different: tall, thick-boned, with broad mana channels.

<!-- source-p:075 -->
Their talent, their potential, even their starting point had been different.

<!-- source-p:076 -->
In his previous life, Vikir had been among the last to leave the Cradle of Swords.

<!-- source-p:077 -->
He had gained little benefit from the River Styx.

<!-- source-p:078 -->
His brothers had already absorbed most of its power.

<!-- source-p:079 -->
After that, his illegitimate birth and limited ability had always consigned him to last place.

<!-- source-p:080 -->
While the others enjoyed good food, fine clothes, comfortable lodgings, and clean work…

<!-- source-p:081 -->
He ate filthy scraps, wore shabby clothes, slept in squalor, and did the dirty work.

<!-- source-p:082 -->
His half brothers reaped the rewards of his labor.

<!-- source-p:083 -->
It had been no different during the war against the demons.

<!-- source-p:084 -->
No matter how many demons or evil spirits he killed, the credit went to his father or half brothers.

<!-- source-p:085 -->
He received almost nothing in return.

<!-- source-p:086 -->
Nor were demons his only targets. He had to kill his father’s political enemies, humans included.

<!-- source-p:087 -->
Through spying, assassination, intelligence gathering, ambushes, and intimidation among the seven great families, he had raised the Baskervilles above them all.

<!-- source-p:088 -->
…And what had it earned him?

<!-- source-p:089 -->
The rabbit was dead; the hound was cooked.

<!-- source-p:090 -->
Once the rabbits were gone and the hunt was over, the hound went into the pot.

<!-- source-p:091 -->
Accused of colluding with demons, Vikir had been executed, bearing the blame for all of Hugo’s dirty deeds.

<!-- source-p:092 -->
He had committed only one sin.

<!-- source-p:093 -->
‘Knowing too much.’

<!-- source-p:094 -->
Grind—

<!-- source-p:095 -->
Vikir clenched his teeth.

<!-- source-p:096 -->
Without his noticing, teeth had rapidly grown in his mouth. Now they ground against one another.

<!-- source-p:097 -->
The Styx’s water surged into his marrow with his fury, thickening his bones, toughening his flesh, and widening his mana channels.

<!-- source-p:098 -->
The burning pain continued, but it no longer mattered.

<!-- source-p:099 -->
Vikir even began drinking the water.

<!-- source-p:100 -->
He intended to strengthen his internal organs as well.

<!-- source-p:101 -->
An old Baskerville legend came to mind.

<!-- source-p:102 -->
‘Wasn’t there an invincible warrior in the family long ago?’

<!-- source-p:103 -->
Even an ax could not scratch the skin of that peerless swordsman, much less damage his bones.

<!-- source-p:104 -->
Yet his death had been absurd.

<!-- source-p:105 -->
A rival from another family had struck his heel with a poisoned arrow, and he had died from its effects.

<!-- source-p:106 -->
When he was a baby, his nurse had held him by the ankles and dipped him upside down into the Styx. Her palms had kept the water from touching part of him, leaving that spot vulnerable.

<!-- source-p:107 -->
‘I won’t leave a weak spot like that.’

<!-- source-p:108 -->
Vikir thrashed in the river with all his strength.

<!-- source-p:109 -->
He twisted his body, trying to let the water touch every part of him.

<!-- source-p:110 -->
His wounds pulled open, but that was all the better.

<!-- source-p:111 -->
The water could seep into his body more easily.

<!-- source-p:112 -->
Bubble… bubble…

<!-- source-p:113 -->
Blood loss clouded his consciousness.

<!-- source-p:114 -->
His breath was running out. He ached to reach the surface.

<!-- source-p:115 -->
But he could not.

<!-- source-p:116 -->
The River Styx would not welcome a child back once he had left its embrace.

<!-- source-p:117 -->
If he raised his head to breathe, that part would receive no further blessing.

<!-- source-p:118 -->
Vikir clung desperately to the stones on the riverbed.

<!-- source-p:119 -->
Even then, he kept shifting his arms so that the water could reach his palms.

<!-- source-p:120 -->
Just then—

<!-- source-p:121 -->
Faint voices reached his ears.

<!-- source-p:122 -->
“Young master! You must come out!”

<!-- source-p:123 -->
“You’ll die if you stay under any longer!”

<!-- source-p:124 -->
“Oh, heavens! What do we do?”

<!-- source-p:125 -->
“Quick, get him out! We have to pull him up!”

<!-- source-p:126 -->
There seemed to be an uproar above. That was only natural.

<!-- source-p:127 -->
Too much greed became poison. Strengthening his body was all very well, but what good would it do if he drowned?

<!-- source-p:128 -->
Then another voice joined them.

<!-- source-p:129 -->
“Son. Come up now.”

<!-- source-p:130 -->
It was Hugo Le Baskerville.

<!-- source-p:131 -->
But that voice only stoked Vikir’s fury.

<!-- source-p:132 -->
Whoosh—

<!-- source-p:133 -->
Vikir rose toward the surface.

<!-- source-p:134 -->
But he did not simply rise.

<!-- source-p:135 -->
Gulp—gulp—gulp—

<!-- source-p:136 -->
He swallowed the Styx’s water until the very last moment.

<!-- source-p:137 -->
Then—

<!-- source-p:138 -->
“Hah!”

<!-- source-p:139 -->
The moment he broke the surface, he drew a deep breath.

<!-- source-p:140 -->
Vikir opened his mouth wide for air.

<!-- source-p:141 -->
At the sight, Hugo Le Baskerville broke into a rare, broad smile.

<!-- source-p:142 -->
“Hahaha! Look at this boy! He’s already grown teeth!”

<!-- source-p:143 -->
The hound boiled in scalding water had bared his fangs.
