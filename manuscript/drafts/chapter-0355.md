# Chapter 355: Outside the Tower (5)

Was this how the legendary wooden horse—

the one said to have broken the impregnable fortress—

had finally ended?

Sssssss—

Amdusias’s enormous equine body began to collapse.

From the broken horn—

to the ends of his hooves—

he turned to ash.

The long nightmare was over.

The Fifth Corpse.

Amdusias—

the Unicorn of the Abyss—

was completely destroyed.

“…It’s over!”

“The demon is dead!”

“The Hell Tree is collapsing!”

Cheers erupted.

Parents who believed their children dead—

found them alive.

Children who thought they would never see their families again—

returned home.

Teachers recovered beloved students.

Students fell back into the arms of respected teachers.

Tears spread everywhere.

Then everyone looked toward the center of the crater—

where the Hell Tree had vanished.

Seven Baskerville knight orders stood in formation.

They had entered at the crucial moment.

Driven the final assault.

Without them—

the losses among the people gathered at Colosseo would have been far worse.

“Thank you!”

“Thank you, Baskerville!”

“I’ll never forget this!”

“But why are Baskerville’s knights here?”

“Isn’t the Iron-Blooded Sword House famously closed?”

“That boy summoned them.”

“I saw him blow the horn!”

The whispers shifted.

Then the crowd’s attention centered on one person.

The man who broke the Hell Tree.

The Sword Master whose aura pillar reached the sky.

A first-year Colosseo student.

And—

apparently—

someone able to summon the full military strength of House Baskerville.

Vikir.

Vikir **Le Baskerville**.

The identity he had hidden—

was now exposed.

And before him—

the hounds bowed.

“Your order has been carried out.”

Osiris Le Baskerville.

Behind him—

Isabella.

German.

Metzgerhund.

Cu-Chulainn.

Boston Terrier.

Great Dane.

All lowered their heads.

The crowd reeled.

This was Baskerville.

One of the Empire’s greatest and most secretive houses.

Its future head—

and almost its entire military leadership—

were bowing to a student most people had believed was a commoner.

Then the better-informed people began shouting.

“Wait…”

“Is that really Vikir Le Baskerville!?”

“The deputy magistrate from the dungeon city?”

“The one who received an Imperial commendation?”

“The legendary Baskerville prodigy?”

“I thought those stories were exaggerated!”

“There’s a golden statue of someone who looks exactly like him in Red Fang Castle!”

“So the hero who opened peaceful trade between Baskerville and the western tribes was real?”

“Wasn’t he also the one who recovered the Wraith Tree during the University League?”

More voices joined.

Students who had survived the tower began talking over one another.

“Dad!”

“He saved me from hellhounds!”

“He saved me from the demonic flames!”

“Vikir!”

“My brother!”

“How am I supposed to repay you!?”

Noble faction.

Powerful-house faction.

Commoner faction.

None of it mattered.

Everyone looked at Vikir with respect.

Envy.

Admiration.

Gratitude.

Inside the tower—

Vikir had almost always been at the front.

Taking the greatest risks.

Bearing the worst danger.

No one knew—

that part of his reason had been to monopolize the tower’s rewards.

Then—

“Mr. Vikir!”

“Please look this way!”

“We need a magic-screen capture!”

“We understand you were central to resolving the Hell Tree incident!”

“Are you really the hidden power behind Baskerville!?”

“Why did you conceal your identity!?”

Reporters swarmed.

Where had they been during the actual battle?

Nobody knew.

Now—

they appeared from every direction.

“Move!”

“I can’t see!”

“Let me through!”

Camus and Dolores both tried to force their way toward Vikir.

The sudden crowd blocked them.

Their eyes met.

Neither backed down.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

They were closer.

They waved.

“Vikir!”

“You did it!”

“You’re a hero now!”

“Don’t pretend you don’t know us!”

“You bastard!”

“You were Baskerville this whole time?”

“That’s tonight’s drinking topic settled.”

“Vikir!”

“I’m just happy you’re alive!”

“You scared the hell out of us.”

Sinclaire stood farther back.

Her expression—

complicated.

Vikir watched all of them.

Calmly.

Too calmly.

Then he turned away from the cheering crowd.

He lowered his left hand toward the ground.

Baby Madam still clung to his wrist.

Vikir spoke quietly.

“Go with Aheul and the others.”

“For now—

this is goodbye.”

“Hack…”

Baby Madam protested.

She did not want to leave.

Vikir urged her again.

Finally—

she dropped to the ground.

Rustle.

She crawled toward Aheul’s retreating group.

Stopped.

Looked back.

Moved again.

Looked back once more.

Then—

eventually—

disappeared.

Only after Baby Madam was completely out of sight—

did Vikir turn toward the crowd again.

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca approached.

“Reporters!”

“Line up!”

“Our Vikir is exhausted!”

“Vikir, are you all right?”

“You used an enormous amount of aura.”

“Do you need medicine?”

“You should rest before all these interviews.”

“Where did all these reporters come from anyway?”

Their concern was genuine.

Pure.

Unreserved.

Vikir answered with two words.

“Get lost.”

The four froze.

Tudor blinked.

“…What?”

“Who are you talking to?”

“Someone bothering you?”

“Tell us.”

Vikir looked directly at them.

“I said—

get lost.”

“Trash.”

Silence.

They finally understood.

Vikir’s eyes were hostile.

Not irritated.

Not tired.

Hostile.

“Vikir…”

“What’s wrong with you?”

“Why are you suddenly acting like this?”

“If you think we’re trying to ride your reputation—

you’ve misunderstood.”

“Yeah!”

“We were just worried!”

“Did you hit your head?”

Vikir cut through them.

“I don’t have weaklings like you as friends.”

“Know your place.”

“Stop flattering yourselves.”

A full stop.

A line drawn through every bond.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

All four stood frozen.

Tudor recovered first.

His face twisted.

“Why?”

“Why are you doing this?”

“Vikir…”

“Is this because of the final floor?”

“Because you wrote the order?”

“We didn’t have one then either—”

Vikir pushed him aside.

Then walked toward the center of the crowd.

His gaze—

cold and distant—

made the four feel as though an impossible wall had appeared between them.

Camus.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Everyone Vikir had met at Colosseo.

Everyone he had saved.

Everyone he had formed a bond with.

All of them—

felt that line.

Vikir stepped in front of the reporters.

“I am…”

The first row barely understood him.

People farther back leaned in.

Vikir reached into his clothes.

Then—

took out three things.

A crimson sword.

An inverted pentagram pendant.

And a plague-doctor mask.

The meaning was unmistakable.

The noise vanished.

Vikir raised the mask.

Then spoke clearly.

“I am Night Hound.”
