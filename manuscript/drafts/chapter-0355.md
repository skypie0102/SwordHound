Was this what happened—

when the legendary wooden horse finally broke the impregnable city?

Sssssss—

Amdusias’s enormous body began dissolving.

Broken horn.

Flaming mane.

Hooves.

Everything turned to ash.

The nightmare—

finally—

ended.

Amdusias.

Fifth Corpse.

The Unicorn of the Abyss.

Gone.

“…It’s over!”

“The demon is dead!”

“The Hell Tree is gone!”

Cheers erupted.

Parents who had believed their children dead—

found them again.

Children who thought they would never see home—

returned.

Teachers found students.

Students returned to teachers.

People cried everywhere.

Then attention shifted toward the crater.

Baskerville’s knight orders stood in formation.

They had entered at the critical moment.

Without them—

the casualties around Colosseo would have been far greater.

“Thank you!”

“Baskerville saved us!”

“I’ll never forget this!”

“But why were Baskerville’s knights here?”

“That family is famously closed.”

“The boy summoned them!”

“I saw him blow the whistle!”

The crowd’s eyes turned.

Toward one person.

The one who had broken the tower.

The Sword Master whose aura had risen toward the heavens.

A first-year student.

And the person who had summoned Baskerville’s hounds.

**Vikir Van Baskerville.**

The identity he had hidden—

was hidden no longer.

Before him—

the hounds lowered their heads.

“Your order has been carried out.”

Osiris.

Isabella.

German.

Metzgerhund.

Cu-Chulainn.

Boston Terrier.

Great Dane.

Every present Baskerville commander bowed.

The crowd froze.

This was **Baskerville**.

One of the most closed and militarized great Houses in the Empire.

Its future head—

and nearly all of its active great military leaders—

bowing to a student.

Then someone shouted.

“Wait!”

“Vikir Van Baskerville?!”

“The Deputy Magistrate from Underdog!”

“The Baskerville legend commended by the Emperor!”

“I thought he was made up!”

“There’s a golden statue with his face!”

“The hero who opened peaceful trade between Baskerville and the western tribes!”

“That was real?!”

“Isn’t he also the student who recovered the Wraith Tree during the League?!”

More voices joined them.

Students from the Hell Tree.

“Dad!”

“He saved me from hellhounds!”

“He got me away from the Daylilies!”

“Vikir!”

“My brother!”

“How do I even repay this?”

“He forgave us!”

“He’s the reason we’re alive!”

Nobles.

Commoners.

Old families.

New money.

Everyone looked at Vikir with the same mixture.

Respect.

Admiration.

Gratitude.

They had seen him take the front.

Take the risk.

Make the sacrifice.

None of them knew—

that gathering tower rewards had also been part of his purpose.

Then—

the reporters arrived.

“Mr. Vikir!”

“Look this way!”

“Mana-screen capture!”

“We understand you played the decisive role in the Hell Tree incident!”

“Please give us a statement!”

“Are you really Baskerville’s hidden power?”

“Why conceal your identity?”

They swarmed like insects.

Camus pushed from one side.

“Husband!”

“Move!”

“I can’t see!”

Dolores pushed from another.

“Van-nim!”

“Please let me through!”

Their eyes met.

Neither moved aside.

Meanwhile—

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

They were already close enough to approach.

“Vikir!”

“You did it!”

“You’re seriously a hero now.”

“Don’t pretend you don’t know us!”

Sancho laughed.

“Unbelievable.”

“You were a Baskerville all along?”

“That alone gives us enough drinking stories for the entire night.”

Pigi looked close to tears.

“I’m just glad you’re alive.”

Bianca exhaled.

“You really know how to make people worry.”

Behind them—

Sinclaire approached more slowly.

Her expression complicated.

Vikir watched all of it.

Calm.

Too calm.

Then he turned his back to the cheering crowd.

Lowered his left hand.

“Follow Aheul.”

“For now—

we part here.”

Baby Madam clung to his wrist.

[Hack… hack…]

She did not want to go.

Vikir insisted.

Finally—

she dropped to the ground.

Rustle.

Baby Madam hurried after Aheul and the Ballak children—

already distant.

She looked back several times.

Vikir watched until she disappeared.

Then turned toward the crowd.

His friends were still trying to help.

“Reporters!”

“Line up!”

“Vikir is exhausted!”

Sancho looked him over.

“You all right?”

“Need medicine?”

Pigi glanced at the crowd.

“He should rest before interviews.”

Bianca scowled at the journalists.

“Where were all of you during the battle?”

“Should I have Father clear them out?”

They were proud of him.

Happy.

Relieved.

After everything they had survived together—

their affection was uncomplicated.

Vikir answered with one sentence.

“Get lost.”

They froze.

Tudor blinked.

“…What?”

“Who are you talking to?”

Sancho turned around.

“Someone bothering you?”

Pigi looked toward the professors.

“I can ask them to control the reporters—”

Vikir repeated himself.

Clearer.

“Fuck off.”

“Scum.”

Silence.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Only now did they understand.

Vikir was looking at **them**.

With hostility.

“Hey…”

“What happened?”

“Do you think we’re trying to take credit?”

“We’re not.”

“Vikir!”

“We’re just worried about you!”

“Did you hit your head?”

Vikir cut them off.

“I don’t consider weak trash like you my friends.”

“Know your place.”

“Stop flattering yourselves.”

A full stop.

A sentence designed to sever.

Affection.

Connection.

Relationship.

Tudor stood stunned.

Then his face twisted.

“Why?”

“Why are you doing this?”

“Is it because only you used the scroll at the end?”

“But we didn’t even have—”

Vikir shoved him aside.

Walked past.

The four felt the boundary in his eyes.

Something absolute.

A distance he was deliberately forcing between them.

Tudor sat where he had fallen.

Speechless.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

The same.

And not only them.

Camus.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Everyone whose life had crossed Vikir’s inside the Academy—

felt that sudden cold line.

Vikir stepped in front of the reporters.

The crowd leaned closer.

“I am…”

The people in front barely understood him.

Those farther back strained to hear.

Instead of repeating himself—

Vikir reached into his clothing.

He removed three things.

A crimson demonic sword.

An inverted-pentagram pendant.

And a plague-doctor mask.

The crowd stepped backward.

Everyone recognized those symbols.

Silence spread.

Then Vikir spoke.

Low.

Clear.

“I am Night Hound.”
