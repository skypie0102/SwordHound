# Chapter 63: The Protagonist of Hunting (3)

Vikir was sitting inside his shelter, feeding the fire.

“……Are you there?”

A quiet voice came from outside.

Vikir pushed aside the leaf curtain.

A black-haired, dark-eyed girl stood there, looking up at him shyly.

He recognized her.

She had been one of the girls waiting outside the chieftain's tent since early morning the day before.

“The chieftain is asking for you. I came to deliver the message.”

Vikir immediately headed toward Akwilla's tent.

Then—

“W-wait!”

He turned.

The girl's face had gone red.

“Thank you for the roasted meat during the festival. It was really good.”

She hesitated.

“And… you looked impressive.”

Vikir gave a brief nod and continued walking.

The chieftain had summoned him.

There was no reason to delay.

◆◆◆

Ballak warriors moved freely throughout the jungle.

Inside the chieftain's tent, however, even they behaved carefully.

Vikir entered.

An eagle trophy hung at the far end.

Beneath it sat Akwilla.

Her face was hard.

Scars marked her body.

She was not particularly tall, yet the eagle-feather cloak and the pressure of her presence made her seem enormous.

The great scar crossing one eye only strengthened the impression.

The **Night Fox**.

Current leader of Ballak.

Its strongest warrior.

The woman who had successively defeated Hugo Le Baskerville and Adolf Morgue.

A major reason a tribe of only a few hundred people was feared by the Empire.

“Chieftain.”

Vikir dropped to one knee.

Akwilla had been called Ballak's greatest archer since the legendary **Divine Archer Adonai**.

Her remaining eye studied him.

Ballak speech wasted little time on ceremony.

“I hear you brought down an Oxbear. We ate well because of you.”

Akwilla smiled.

Her voice carried authority, but not Hugo's sharp coldness.

“No, Chieftain. I only did what was necessary.”

“No need for false humility. Good work is good work.”

Akwilla's smile widened slightly.

Vikir was still not used to seeing warmth on the face of the Night Fox.

Then she spoke.

“For what you did yesterday, I am removing your slave status. From today onward, you are one of Ballak.”

Vikir was no longer a slave.

He was officially a member of the tribe.

“……Hmph.”

A dissatisfied cough came from the side.

Vikir turned.

A wrinkled old man stared at him.

*The elder who smeared ash on the hunters' faces.*

**Ah'Heman**.

Ballak's chief shaman.

Aside from Akwilla, he held enormous influence among the older generation.

The young hunters disliked his obsession with old rites and superstition.

Older hunters trusted him.

People who survived enough danger often looked for something spiritual to lean on.

Ah'Heman understood that better than anyone.

*So Ahun is that old man's grandson.*

The family resemblance was faint but visible.

Ah'Heman clearly disliked Akwilla's decision.

Akwilla noticed.

“Shaman.”

“……Yes, Chieftain.”

“You object to my accepting him into Ballak?”

She asked without softening the words.

However powerful Ah'Heman was, Akwilla's authority as chieftain came first.

He lowered his head.

“Of course not. I only worry about Imperial blood entering the tribe.”

“That is not your concern. Pray for rain when we need rain. Bless hunters before they leave. Conduct the rites of war, death, and plague. That is your duty.”

Ah'Heman bowed more deeply.

Aiyen, standing nearby, quietly stuck out her tongue behind him.

Akwilla looked back at Vikir.

“You've recovered quickly.”

“Thanks to the tribe's care.”

“I did little. If anyone deserves that gratitude, it is my daughter.”

Aiyen immediately looked away.

Then Akwilla continued.

“Now that you are officially one of us, tell me what you want.”

She paused.

“You are family now, but I did not raise you. Consider this compensation.”

The offer became more concrete.

“There are many suitable women in the village. If you want, I can choose one as your mate. If you do not want a mate, I can give you a slave instead. We have women taken prisoner from other tribes.”

Murmuring came from outside.

The same women who had lined up before the tent were still waiting.

Vikir shook his head.

“I appreciate it, but I do not need a mate.”

Mate or captive woman—the practical purpose was the same.

Increase births and give an outsider a reason to settle permanently.

Akwilla looked surprised.

“Nothing? Most outsider men ask for women.”

Ballak men and women tended to be physically healthy and attractive.

Their bodies were shaped by hunting and training.

Their eyes and hair showed little of the chronic sickness seen in more isolated tribes.

Outside blood periodically entered Ballak through its customs, preventing the worst effects of close inbreeding.

That had also made Ballak women targets for outsiders.

Those outsiders usually ended up as wolf food.

Akwilla thought again.

“Then half the Oxbear hide?”

Vikir almost objected on instinct.

A whole Oxbear hide was enormously valuable.

Cutting it in half could reduce its worth to a fraction.

Then he felt Aiyen staring at him.

Her expression said exactly one thing.

*You didn't forget.*

Akwilla noticed her daughter signaling frantically.

Aiyen did not notice that she had been noticed.

Vikir spoke.

“I don't need a material reward.”

Aiyen's eyes brightened.

“But may I enter the **Fountain of Valor**?”

Before this audience, Aiyen had repeatedly told him to ask for that specific reward.

For the first time, Akwilla looked troubled.

“……Hmm.”

The elders reacted even more strongly.

Ah'Heman stepped forward.

“You insolent brat! Do you know what you're asking?”

He knelt before Akwilla.

“Chieftain! Only the honored warriors of Ballak have ever been permitted to enter the Fountain of Valor!”

Murmurs filled the tent.

Ahun sneered from near the entrance.

“The Fountain of Valor belongs to warriors who have proven exceptional strength. Know your place.”

Aiyen snapped back.

“He *did* prove himself. He helped bring down an Oxbear for the festival!”

“He followed behind real hunters and threw stones.”

Aiyen pointed at Ahun's bandaged waist.

“Then did one of those stones do that to your back?”

Ahun began shaking with anger.

Akwilla returned her attention to Vikir.

“The Fountain of Valor. Do you know what it is?”

Vikir nodded.

Deep in Ballak's sacred territory stood a small spring among the memorials of the tribe's greatest warriors.

Tradition claimed that the protection of those ancestors filled the water with healing power.

Internal injuries.

External wounds.

A soak in the spring was said to mend both.

But the spring was shallow.

It took a long time to refill.

And if too many people entered, its effect weakened.

For generations, only warriors with exceptional achievements were allowed access.

“Chieftain, this is too much!”

“One Oxbear does not justify letting a newly accepted outsider enter our holiest place!”

“He is talented. Rewarding him could strengthen the tribe.”

“Since when did the Fountain of Valor become a public bath?”

“He's still young. If he's one of us now, we should treat him like one of us.”

The elders split into argument.

Some respected Vikir for bringing in desperately needed food.

Others distrusted him because he was still an outsider in all but name.

Ah'Heman and his supporters opposed the request most strongly.

Akwilla herself seemed willing to grant it.

The political resistance made the decision less simple.

Then—

CLANG! CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!

An alarm bell rang from the watchtower.

A sentry shouted across the village.

“Outsiders have appeared!”

Something had happened at the village boundary.
