# Chapter 18: Bared Teeth (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/018.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 18 is an aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English terminology for Orc, Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, Beelzebub abilities, Sword Expert ranks, and Baskerville Fangs follows current project/Fandom evidence. The Chinese source contains one isolated age contradiction; Vikir remains eight, while the contradictory fifteen-year phrase is treated as a comparison to the normal Baskerville milestone established by the surrounding chapter and Chapter 20.

“Well? Did you gain anything from it?”

Hugo Le Baskerville had summoned Vikir to his study the moment he left the library.

Judging by how perfectly the timing lined up, Hugo had clearly been waiting for him.

Aside from his eldest and second sons, Vikir had never seen Hugo take this much interest in one of his children.

That did not make Vikir proud or nervous.

In his previous life, he had lived longer than the Hugo standing before him now.

“A little.”

“Oh?”

A light flashed in Hugo’s eyes.

A single day in the library should not have been enough to produce any real insight.

Most Baskerville children Vikir’s age had only just begun sensing mana, and most had never experienced genuine combat.

What could an eight-year-old possibly comprehend from reading advanced sword manuals under those conditions?

And yet Hugo stared at this son—one of so many he could hardly have counted them—with an anticipation even he seemed unable to explain.

“What did you feel? What did you understand?”

“It was… warm… pointed… sharp… soft… tingly? Something like that.”

“…”

Hugo rubbed his head for a moment.

Children often answered in ways adults could barely understand.

But perhaps this was the intuition of a warrior who had already reached a certain realm.

Something in Vikir’s disordered description made Hugo hesitate.

“…No. Surely not.”

He murmured to himself, then gave a slight shake of his head.

Aura.

The sign that a swordsman had truly stepped into the world of the blade.

A realm most ordinary swordsmen could never reach in their entire lives.

Even among the prodigies of the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan, reaching the first stage of Sword Expert generally required relentless training until around fifteen.

Hugo frowned again.

The child before him was only eight.

What exactly was he hoping to see?

Still, he could not take his eyes off Vikir’s unguarded face.

Perhaps that very expectation made Hugo impatient.

“…Would you show your father what you mean?”

Baskervilles instinctively disliked revealing their true level to one another.

Parents and children were no exception.

Neither were siblings.

That instinct was so deeply rooted that Hugo used a careful tone even with his eight-year-old son.

Vikir answered without hesitation.

“Of course.”

◆◆◆

The Baskerville training ground.

A single child stood in the middle of the broad field, surrounded by spectators.

Vikir held a shortsword proportioned for his body and kept his eyes closed.

Word that Hugo intended to test him had spread quickly, and people crowded into the training ground to watch.

“That’s the child who stayed under the River Styx for seven minutes, right?”

“I heard he strangled two venomous snakes while he was still in the cradle.”

“And he caught a Cerberus during the practical examination.”

Surprisingly, Hugo did nothing to disperse the crowd.

He merely kept his eyes fixed on Vikir.

John Barrymore stood beside him.

“My lord, how do you intend to test Young Master Vikir’s ability?”

“…I’ll have him face a suitable monster.”

Hugo remained merciless even here.

He was going to make an eight-year-old child fight a demonic creature.

Before long, Vikir’s opponent was brought forward by the Guardian Knights.

A huge green-skinned humanoid.

Even unarmed, an Orc could match four or five ordinary adult men in combat.

Dense muscle covered its entire body, and it possessed tremendous natural strength, resistance to disease, and regenerative power.

This particular Orc had once belonged to a warlike tribe before being captured alive by the Baskervilles.

By now it looked ready to hate the entire world.

The spectators began murmuring.

“Even so, making an eight-year-old fight an Orc is…”

“You normally need to be at least fifteen to handle one.”

“If something goes wrong, the Guardian Knights will intervene.”

Even among the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan, most people could not understand this matchup.

“…”

Hugo offered no explanation.

The test proceeded.

Clang!

The restraints came off.

Driven forward by the Guardian Knights’ spears and swords, the Orc bared its teeth and gums and staggered toward the center of the field, drool running from its mouth.

Vikir waited there with his sword in hand.

ROOOAAARGH!

The Orc lunged with both arms spread wide.

It saw only an eight-year-old child and assumed the kill would be easy.

Vikir ducked beneath its hands.

The movement was polished and economical, making perfect use of the difference in their height and build.

“Oh?”

Surprise flickered through Hugo’s eyes.

The other Baskervilles reacted the same way.

Before that surprise could settle, Vikir moved again.

Whoosh!

He turned with the shortsword raised.

Eight-year-old or not, his body carried considerable weight and strength.

He put all of it behind the strike.

The blade severed one of the Orc’s wrists.

ROOAAARGH!?

For an instant, the Orc could not understand what had happened.

Its opponent had vanished from directly in front of it.

Its wrist was gone.

Before it could process either fact, Vikir slipped between its legs and cut through one ankle as well.

THUD!

By the time the Orc realized its wrist had been severed, its ankle was already gone and its body had crashed to the ground.

Silence spread across the training ground.

Every Baskerville watching stared with widened eyes.

“…He’s eight?”

“What kind of movement was that? He fights like a veteran.”

“He hasn’t even used mana yet. What was I doing at that age…?”

More spectators arrived with every passing moment.

Guardian Knights came to watch.

So did servants.

The gathering grew enormous.

But Hugo alone remained silent.

Vikir understood why.

‘Because I still haven’t shown him what he thinks I realized in the library.’

Cutting off the Orc’s wrist and ankle had been pure physical technique.

He still had not shown any supposed enlightenment from his time among the sword manuals.

Vikir drew a slow breath.

Then he gave the shortsword a light swing.

The weapon weighed around four kilograms—heavy for a child.

In Vikir’s hands, it moved like a twig.

That alone would have been impressive.

What came next was more shocking.

Flash.

A faint light appeared at the tip of the blade.

Everyone who recognized what it was cried out at once.

“Aura!”

Gaseous aura.

The hallmark of a Sword Expert.

It flickered around the tip of Vikir’s blade like a ghostly flame.

Crimson, like blood being burned for fuel.

An unmistakably Baskerville aura, sharpened by killing intent.

The Orc sensed danger on instinct and recoiled.

It tried to use its natural **High-Speed Regeneration** to restore the wounds at its wrist and ankle.

It could not.

Beelzebub was already lodged within Vikir.

The Hellhound’s **Hemorrhage** overwhelmed the Orc’s High-Speed Regeneration.

Now the Orc would experience the full lethality of a Hellhound’s bite.

Even a shallow wound that would normally shed little blood would begin bleeding heavily with time, and the bleeding would persist far longer than an ordinary injury.

A severed wrist and ankle were far worse.

Gulp… gulp… gulp…

The blood staining Vikir’s shortsword slowly vanished.

The blade, carrying Beelzebub’s power, drank the Orc’s blood greedily while the creature could do nothing but continue supplying it.

Then—

Thrust!

Vikir moved his sword.

Everyone gathered around the training ground saw it.

A single terrifying fang forming in the air.

The **1st Fang**.

The **Stabbing Fang** drove deep into the Orc’s neck.

The creature died almost instantly.

Along with the fatal wound came pain like the fires of hell.

Vikir had deliberately shown only the level of strength normally expected from a Baskerville youth around fifteen: the first stage of Sword Expert and a single Fang.

The spectators erupted.

“Impossible. I couldn’t even sense mana at eight.”

“At that age I could barely gather it into my palm.”

“That child looks like a fully formed Low Sword Expert. How is that possible?”

Until now, the youngest recorded Sword Expert had been fourteen.

Vikir was eight.

Even among the prodigies of House Baskerville, this was unprecedented.

Barrymore spoke with a mixture of shock and delight.

“C-congratulations, my lord. A once-in-a-generation genius has appeared. It is a blessing to the Clan.”

“…Hmm.”

Hugo nodded without changing expression.

Barrymore nevertheless caught the faint upward twitch at the corner of his mouth beneath his beard.

Since there seemed to be no limit to how much praise Hugo would tolerate for his son, Barrymore continued.

“An Orc can fight four or five grown men without being overwhelmed, yet an eight-year-old killed one this easily. No other family in the empire could possibly have another genius like—”

“Ahem. Enough, Butler.”

Hugo cut him off, looking almost embarrassed.

Then his expression turned dissatisfied.

“That Orc seemed strange.”

“What?”

“Orcs are famous for regeneration, yet that one never stopped bleeding. Sudden heavy blood loss would naturally slow it down. Was there poison on Vikir’s sword?”

“That is impossible, my lord. The Guardian Knights inspected the weapon. It was an ordinary shortsword.”

“Ahem. Then the Orc must have been weakened already.”

Hugo was, in short, a man incapable of accepting praise without qualification.

Barrymore gave a faint smile and shrugged.

Whatever Hugo said, a prodigy had appeared in the Baskerville Clan.

An eight-year-old warrior publicly recognized as a Low Sword Expert.

Just as everyone—including Hugo—was beginning to digest that fact, Vikir spoke again.

“Please wait.”

Every eye turned toward him.

Hugo had already started to turn away, but stopped.

Their gazes met.

Vikir spoke calmly.

“Is there anything bigger than an Orc?”
