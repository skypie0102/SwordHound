The Old Testament priests erupted.

“Saintess!”

“Do you understand what you're doing!?”

“The late Lord Quilt was a devout believer!”

“How can you publicly read his private correspondence?”

“Those include conversations with Cardinal Humbert!”

“Confidential correspondence!”

“If you release all of this—”

Part of their anger was political.

Disaster brought opportunity.

Heal the injured.

Comfort victims.

Lead reconstruction.

Earn public trust.

The Old Testament priests had wanted to enter the ruins quickly—

and prevent the New Testament faction from monopolizing that role.

Dolores and Mozgus had stopped them.

But their protests did not last.

“Quiet.”

Dolores looked at them.

Coldly.

The normally gentle Saintess had never looked like this.

Some people became more frightening—

not less—

when anger finally broke through kindness.

The priests stopped.

Dolores continued reading.

“**Baron Gorg.**”

“Killed a groom who resisted the exercise of the so-called right of first night.”

“The bride later took her own life.”

“Her family was punished and financially destroyed.”

“Sin forgiven after a donation of **one billion gold**.”

Faces paled.

“**Viscountess Moscoe.**”

“Used improper influence to place her sons in the Imperial Central Bank and accelerate their promotion.”

“Qualified applicants lost positions to unqualified family appointments.”

“Sin forgiven after **two hundred million gold per son**.”

“**Chairman Guerrasso.**”

“Embezzlement.”

“Misappropriation.”

“Stock-price manipulation.”

“Approximately twenty billion gold in shareholder losses.”

“Sin forgiven after **fifty million gold**.”

“**Madame Pierre.**”

“Kidnapped homeless underage boys.”

“Imprisoned them.”

“Sexually exploited them.”

“Forced them into illegal labor.”

“Sin forgiven after **one hundred fifty million gold**.”

“**Cult leader Manimani.**”

“Deliberately dispersed infected followers across multiple regions.”

“Spread disease.”

“Paralyzed local economies.”

“Sin forgiven after **one hundred million gold**.”

The scale of Quilt's indulgence business became impossible to dismiss.

Crimes that ordinary society regarded as unforgivable—

reduced to fees.

Fees often laughably small compared with the harm.

Even Old Testament priests stared at one another.

“They sold indulgences to people like *that*?”

“For those amounts?”

“There have to be missing zeroes.”

“Manimani deserved execution.”

“How could Cardinal Humbert and Quilt approve this?”

“But…”

“The seals are real…”

The whispers spread outward.

From priests—

to Quovadis retainers—

to ordinary people.

“How could Quovadis do this?”

“This is corruption of the faith!”

“Calm down!”

“This concerns the Old Testament faction—not every New Testament priest!”

“Don't condemn every Old Testament believer either!”

“This is a faction inside a faction!”

Voices collided.

Some priests looked ready to fight one another.

Dolores kept reading.

The Night Hound's evidence documented:

transactions between Quilt and Humbert—

high-society crimes—

offerings to Rune diverted into investment schemes—

funds siphoned into politics—

illegal lobbying—

contacts with regional cults.

Then—

false relics.

Farm tools.

Wood scraps.

Straw.

Even pig bones.

Sold as holy objects.

Each supposedly reducing purgatorial suffering by one year.

More than thirty thousand relics sold.

The crowd went silent.

Even the Academy's young reporters stopped writing for several seconds.

A scandal inside Quovadis—

exposed by a Quovadis Saintess.

Dolores closed the last ledger.

Then opened another document.

Something she had composed through the night.

“I—Dolores of the New Testament Faction—”

“answer the abuses of the Old Testament indulgence system as follows.”

Pens rose.

Notebooks opened.

Mana-recording screens flashed.

Dolores began.

“**First.** When Rune calls believers to repent, repentance concerns the whole life—not a purchased transaction.”

“**Second.** Repentance cannot be reduced to confession and absolution performed merely through priestly office.”

“**Fifth.** The Pope cannot cancel punishments beyond those placed within lawful church authority.”

“**Sixth.** The Pope does not create forgiveness of sin; at most, the office can declare or recognize forgiveness granted by Rune.”

She continued.

“**Twenty-seventh.** The claim that a soul escapes purgatory the instant money rings in a collection chest is human teaching—not divine truth.”

“**Forty-fifth.** Anyone who ignores the poor yet spends money buying an indulgence does not purchase Rune's favor.”

“They invite Rune's anger.”

“**Forty-sixth.** Those without abundant wealth have a duty to provide for their families.”

“They must not waste necessary savings on indulgence certificates.”

The crowd remained silent.

“**Fiftieth.** If indulgence sellers have stripped ordinary people of their wealth—”

“the Church should repay them even at the cost of selling church property.”

“**Ninetieth.** If objections from ordinary believers are suppressed by power rather than answered with reason—”

“the Pope is made ridiculous and the faithful are harmed.”

“**Ninety-fifth.** Believers should place deeper trust in reaching heaven through sincere endurance and repentance—”

“not through purchased comfort.”

Those were only selections.

The rebuttal contained **ninety-five articles**.

Dolores delivered all of them.

Without stopping.

Without retreating.

Even the priests who opposed her—

fell silent beneath the force of it.

When she finally finished—

the strength left her legs.

Fortunately—

the full white skirts of her priestly robes concealed the trembling.

Mozgus caught her arm.

“Magnificent, Saintess.”

“Every point has reason behind it.”

“This Mozgus is deeply moved.”

Dolores whispered back:

“…I may actually faint.”

Her thoughts returned to the night.

No—

to the early morning.

The Night Hound.

After he disappeared—

the extraordinary strength that had filled her body disappeared too.

The soul-resonance buff was gone.

She had drawn courage from remembering it—

but the power itself had not remained.

*The Pope once said…*

*A Saintess awakens only after meeting a true soulmate.*

Soul resonance.

Emotional synchronization.

Divine power multiplying beyond its normal limit.

How could that awakening become permanent—

instead of a momentary phenomenon?

The Night Hound was gone.

No one to ask.

No one to test with.

*The older Saintesses used to talk about awakening.*

*I thought it was old-fashioned nonsense.*

*I should have listened.*

Dolores still did not understand why the Night Hound—

specifically—

had become her awakening condition.

She did not know what Vikir knew.

That among the people alive in this era—

he alone carried complete memories of the Age of Destruction.

He understood destruction—

because he had lived through it.

Whatever the reason—

Dolores now knew one thing.

Rune had led her soul to resonate with him.

At this moment—

he alone qualified as the counterpart capable of awakening her power.

Dolores lifted her head.

Then murmured—

“The Night Hound.”

“I have to see him again.”

Crack!

Crack-crack!

Mana-camera flashes erupted behind her.

Dolores turned.

Reporters had gathered much closer than she realized.

They heard only the line.

Saw her severe expression.

Connected it to the destruction around them.

Then shouted over one another—

“THE SAINTESS HAS DECLARED WAR ON THE NIGHT HOUND!”

“QUOVADIS HOLY WAR AGAINST THE NIGHT HOUND!”
