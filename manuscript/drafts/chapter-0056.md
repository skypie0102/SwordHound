# Chapter 56: The Hunter and the Hunted (2)

“……”

Vikir sat silently on the wolf's back.

Aiyen, riding in front, kept turning around to study his face.

“Does it hurt?”

“……”

“Are you angry?”

“……”

She twisted farther around.

Vikir answered flatly.

“Watch where you're going.”

The wolf stumbled over a root and lurched.

Aiyen hurriedly faced forward again.

Even then, she kept glancing over her shoulder.

At this rate, it was difficult to tell which of them was the master and which was the slave.

The roots eventually thinned and the jungle opened into a broad stretch of ground.

Aiyen slowed the wolf.

“I didn't have a choice. Ahun is petty and stubborn. If I hadn't handled it that way, he'd have kept coming after you.”

“……”

“Why won't you say anything?”

“It hurts.”

That was the truth.

The inside of Vikir's mouth was caked with blood, and even speaking pulled at the injuries.

Aiyen stiffened.

“It… hurts that much?”

Her gaze fell to Vikir's neck.

The collar had rubbed a red line into his skin.

She stared at it for a while, leaned closer, and briefly ran her tongue over the abrasion.

Vikir immediately pulled his head away.

Aiyen froze too, apparently realizing what she had just done.

Vikir frowned.

*Do Ballak lick each other's wounds?*

Canines did.

Ballak lived closely with wolves, so perhaps the habit was not unusual among them.

To Vikir, it was simply unsanitary.

*Saliva can infect a wound. If she does that again, I'll stop her.*

But Aiyen only turned forward in silence.

The tips of her pointed ears had gone red.

◆◆◆

Like most Ballak warriors, Aiyen's emotions were straightforward.

Once she saw that Vikir was behaving as usual—aside from speaking less because of the pain—her mood quickly recovered.

“I'll give you something to make up for hitting you. Ask for anything I can reasonably do.”

Vikir answered without hesitation.

“Teach me archery.”

“……Archery.”

For some reason, Aiyen sounded disappointed.

Then she smiled.

“Fine. We're hunting anyway. A bow suits your current condition better than a sword.”

She moved behind him and demonstrated the stance by guiding his shoulders and arms from close range.

Vikir's damaged spine protested.

“Do you have to teach me like this?”

“You learn fastest by doing it with your own body. Any complaints?”

In his present condition, resistance was more trouble than it was worth.

Aiyen handed him a spare bow.

The unfamiliar balance triggered old memories.

Young Baskerville hounds received basic weapons training, so Vikir already knew how to use a bow.

And in the Age of Destruction, a swordsman who knew only swords did not live long.

Vikir could use most common weapons at least passably.

*But archery was never one of my specialties.*

He could hunt small game.

He could not have called himself a true combat archer.

Living among Ballak—masters of the bow—offered a chance to correct that weakness.

Aiyen adjusted his posture again.

“We need every hand we can get for this hunt, even a wolf pup's.”

Ballak had nearly three hundred mouths to feed.

Even with mushrooms, berries, roots, and other gathered food, the tribe needed the equivalent of more than two hundred kilograms of meat a day.

Recent hunts had gone poorly.

Some people had spent days eating little besides gathered plants.

“If you distinguish yourself here, life in the village will be easier.”

Vikir nodded.

Then Aiyen's wolf abruptly stopped.

“……!”

Its name was **Bakira**.

The animal lowered its snout to the ground.

Aiyen crouched and scooped up the damp soil Bakira had been smelling.

Rotten wood.

Leaves.

Moss.

Sand.

And a harsh, rank odor.

“Oxbear.”

Vikir knew the monster.

> **Oxbear**  
> **Danger Rating:** A  
> **Size:** 5 meters  
> **Found at:** Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, 7th Ridge  
> An enormous bear with two massive horns. A near-perfect terrestrial predator at the top of the 7th Ridge ecosystem, with few natural enemies other than its own kind.

The beasts Vikir had encountered during Baskerville training had been captured specimens awaiting execution.

Wild Oxbears in their own territory were another matter.

The one whose trail they had found deliberately marked its range with strong-smelling urine.

Most predators would turn away.

Aiyen did not.

Neither did Vikir.

“Not afraid?” she asked.

“Not especially.”

“Good. Even experienced Ballak hunters are careful around Oxbears.”

Aiyen seemed pleased.

They began moving upwind.

Her tracking was almost animalistic.

At times her nose seemed more useful than Bakira's.

Vikir was skilled at tracking fleeing humans.

Monster tracking was different.

*This is worth learning.*

Before long they found the animal on a brush-covered rise.

It was enormous.

Two curved horns.

Dense, coarse fur.

Muscle and fat packed together under the hide.

The old individual stood close to eight meters—larger than any Oxbear Vikir remembered.

“That should probably be rated higher than A.”

A frontal battle would be foolish.

“We should choose another target,” Vikir said.

Aiyen did not retreat.

“Follow it.”

“That thing is abnormally strong.”

“Idiot. Who said we're hunting *that* one?”

Vikir looked at her.

Aiyen smiled.

“Where there's a female, males will come. Look.”

Another Oxbear emerged from the brush.

The first giant was female.

The newcomer was male—and much smaller.

Perhaps four meters at the shoulder, with roughly half the female's mass.

Breeding season.

The male approached cautiously, sniffed her fur, and began rubbing against her chest and flank.

Aiyen lowered Vikir into the brush beside her.

“That's our real prey.”

The two hunters stayed hidden while the Oxbears tested each other.

Aiyen held Vikir still so he would not disturb the vegetation.

Vikir felt the pressure against his injured back and arrived at one practical conclusion.

*Heavy.*
