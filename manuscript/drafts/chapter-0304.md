The prophet Rune walked toward execution under persecution.

His disciples followed him—

crying.

Rune said:

**‘As it is written: I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep will scatter.’**

**‘You too will all abandon me.’**

A young apostle—

who would later become Pope—

stepped forward.

**‘Even if everyone else abandons you, I never will.’**

Rune answered:

**‘Listen carefully.’**

**‘Before the rooster crows today, you will deny me three times.’**

Later—

after Rune was seized—

people confronted the young apostle.

**‘You were with that criminal too.’**

**‘I don’t know what you mean.’**

First denial.

A prostitute passing by pointed at him.

**‘This man always begged for that criminal’s teaching and volunteered to run errands for him!’**

**‘I do not know that man.’**

Second denial.

Then several executioners surrounded him.

**‘Your accent gives you away.’**

**‘You were one of his people.’**

The apostle swore.

**‘I do not know him.’**

Third denial.

Then—

a rooster crowed.

**—The Gospel of Rune 26:69–75—**

◆◆◆

“Damn.”

Vikir grabbed Nabokov’s hand.

Pulled her backward.

The old woman covered her mouth and blushed.

“Oh my.”

“Old man~”

“I’m a nun.”

“You can’t just grab me.”

“….”

Vikir sighed.

If Nabokov had been fully lucid—

her presence might have changed everything.

But in this state—

expecting meaningful help seemed impossible.

*Get her out.*

That was the priority.

Winston—

or the thing inside him—

looked toward the Pope.

His expression changed slightly.

“The Pope.”

“Perhaps she knows.”

“Which is closer to a demon?”

“Demons?”

“Or humans?”

Vikir ignored him.

Winston continued.

“Quovadis.”

“Descendants of the one who denied God three times.”

“A family that lost a prophet while claiming to protect humanity.”

Amdusias tightened the reins around Winston.

Black sparks scattered.

Memory fragments.

Pieces scraped away as demon and human overlapped.

Vikir saw them.

◆◆◆

A small boy.

Crying.

His parents had made a modest fortune selling beans.

Then famine came.

Neighbors starved.

His parents could not bear to watch.

So they opened their stores.

Made tofu from the beans.

Gave it away.

One piece per person.

A simple act of charity—

based on religious teaching.

The crowd ignored the rule.

Cutting lines.

Lying.

Stealing.

Threatening.

Fighting.

Some took extra by force.

Then food ran out.

The people who arrived late became angry.

“Why didn’t you save any for us?!”

“You’re rich!”

“You’re stingy!”

“We heard there was food!”

People who had already eaten—

did not help.

Some denied receiving anything.

Some criticized the taste.

Some said it was too little.

Some complained the tofu lacked enough beans.

The crush became violent.

A fence collapsed.

The boy’s parents—

were buried beneath it and died.

*What is God?*

That was the first time the boy asked.

And from somewhere deep inside—

a voice answered.

[Deny God.]

[Deny humanity.]

[Deny yourself.]

Three denials.

And—

the cry of a rooster.

◆◆◆

The boy became a young man.

He trusted people less and less.

Built a wall between himself and the world.

Then—

at school—

someone broke through it.

A beautiful young woman.

When he was with her—

old wounds felt smaller.

Warmth returned.

Faith—

slowly—

returned too.

Perhaps his parents had been right.

Perhaps people were fundamentally capable of goodness.

The woman believed that.

So he wanted to believe it too.

Years passed.

The young man became middle-aged.

During the suppression of a rebellion—

he was badly wounded.

His health deteriorated.

Eventually—

he took a recovery journey.

He traveled toward the territory where his parents had died.

The woman—

now his partner—

went with him.

They crossed the sea.

Then came a storm.

The ship began to sink.

Cargo was thrown overboard.

Not enough.

So the sailors decided—

people would have to go too.

Their eyes turned toward the couple.

The man hesitated.

His partner did not.

“Throw **him**.”

“Not me.”

“He’s sick.”

“He can’t fight back.”

The sailors obeyed.

The weakened man—

too ill to resist—

was thrown into the black sea.

As he sank—

he thought:

*What is humanity?*

What makes a person abandon being human?

Or—

were people ever what the word **human** was supposed to mean?

At the bottom—

the voice came again.

[Deny God.]

[Deny humanity.]

[Deny yourself.]

The second denial.

No rooster this time.

But dawn was close.

Then—

he woke on a beach.

Alive.

His wounds—

healed.

Only then did an old memory return.

As a child—

he too had been beneath the collapsed fence with his parents.

Yet somehow—

he survived.

Now—

the mystery had an answer.

Something had been with him since then.

He returned to the Academy where he had spent half his life.

Body changed.

Mind changed.

Surroundings changed.

He adapted.

Doubted God.

Doubted humanity.

Still—

he continued living.

◆◆◆

Then—

the final memory.

His heart—

pierced.

The delinquent child he had sponsored.

Protected.

Raised.

A descendant of an infamous war criminal.

A child he believed education and kindness could save.

Because God taught compassion.

Because his parents believed humans were good.

Because watching the child grow—

had made him proud.

And now—

that grown child stabbed him.

*What am I?*

Memories flew past—

like scenery seen from the back of a galloping unicorn.

*What did any of it mean?*

*Was everything useless?*

*Who am I?*

*Where am I going?*

The voice came a third time.

[Deny God.]

[Deny humanity.]

[Deny yourself.]

Winston closed his eyes.

By then—

human or demon—

the distinction no longer mattered to him.

◆◆◆

[Humans always blame their ugliness on demons.]

Amdusias pulled the reins.

[Shameless species.]

Winston’s face twisted.

Something demonic.

Something exhausted.

Vikir remained calm.

“I don’t care about philosophical rankings between humans and demons.”

[… ]

“I am human.”

“And most of the things I want to protect—

are on humanity’s side.”

“That’s enough.”

War did not divide neatly into good and evil.

Only interests.

Survival.

People.

Things worth protecting.

A veteran of decades on the battlefield understood that too well.

Amdusias smiled faintly.

[Then the argument has lasted long enough.]

[Die.]

The huge hoof came down.

Vikir reached for Decarabia—

then—

BOOM!

A white barrier intercepted the strike.

Dolores.

She stood between them.

“Mr. Van!”

Only one person called Night Hound by that fragment.

The barrier cracked.

Amdusias pushed through it almost immediately.

The Fifth Corpse’s brute force—

far beyond Dantalian or Belial.

Dolores gritted her teeth.

*Again.*

She had trained.

Adjusted her mindset.

Tried to improve.

And still—

when true evil appeared—

she felt weak.

Night Hound’s pilgrimage would only grow harsher.

At this level—

could she even stand beside him without becoming a burden?

Then—

rustle.

A black cloak covered her vision.

Night Hound stepped in front of her.

“…Don’t die.”

Her heart was beating too hard.

“What?”

His voice came again—

low and urgent.

“Don’t look.”

Dolores froze.

The tone told her everything.

Something had appeared—

that she was not supposed to see.
