# Chapter 96: Madam Eight-Legs (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/096.txt` — `a44930a8cfb09a245054bb64c5b4fad7c53de2e6`. Recovered English Chapter 95 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Madam Eight-Legs**, **Akwilla**, **Aiyen**, **Bakira**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Adonai**, **Beelzebub**, and **Baskerville**.

“Madam crossed the line.”

Vikir looked at the ruined village.

“I'll confront her.”

Ballak's warriors stared.

They had thought about revenge.

Children had died.

Elders.

Companions.

Wolves.

Food and shelter were gone.

But confronting Madam Eight-Legs was something else.

“No one does that.”

“Even you shouldn't.”

“Madam is a god of this jungle.”

“A disaster.”

“Forgetting is safer.”

These were warriors who had followed Vikir almost without hesitation for years.

For the first time, they opposed him together.

That alone showed how deeply they feared her.

A hand landed on Vikir's shoulder.

Akwilla.

Her face was pale.

The Night Fox had defeated Adonai's corpse in direct combat.

The price showed everywhere.

Arrow wounds covered her.

The poison carried by Adonai's Madam-made bow still resisted even her master-class recovery.

“Vikir.”

Her voice was quieter than usual.

“Do not let anger decide this.”

Aiyen agreed.

“Madam isn't only Ballak's fear.”

She looked toward the jungle.

“Every tribe treats her like an unavoidable disaster.”

Then something crawled from the wreckage.

[Whine…]

Aiyen stopped speaking.

“Bakira?”

The wolf dragged herself forward.

Born the same day as Aiyen.

Her companion for almost her entire life.

Before the Illiad, Aiyen had sent Bakira back to the village with a simple message.

*Prepare to receive the winner.*

Now Bakira's right hind leg was ruined.

Bones shattered.

Black poison had entered deep into the limb.

Madam's work.

Aiyen knelt beside her.

The damage was severe enough that the leg might have to be removed.

Vikir looked down at them.

“Still think anger is meaningless?”

Aiyen did not answer.

Akwilla exhaled slowly.

“Later.”

She was badly wounded herself.

Yet her first concern remained everyone else.

“Treat Bakira. Then the other injured.”

The tribe returned to work.

◆◆◆

That night was almost completely black.

Rain hammered the jungle.

Lightning provided the only light.

A shadow moved alone through it.

Vikir.

He had left without announcing it.

Again.

Madam Eight-Legs had always been one of the reasons he entered this region.

The Empire did not understand how dangerous she was.

Vikir did.

In the Age of Destruction, monsters of that scale would no longer be unique.

Right now—

she was.

And she had helped force the tribes into constant migration.

Madam expanded from hunger.

Baskerville advanced from the other direction.

Vikir intended to break that pressure.

He also wanted an answer to another question.

*How strong am I now?*

The battle with Adonai had shown him how close the next wall was.

Then a familiar scent appeared through the rain.

Aiyen stood in his path.

Soaked.

Waiting.

“You knew I'd leave?”

No answer.

“How long have you been here?”

Silence.

Then—

“Wolves can't be tamed.”

Vikir frowned.

“No matter how tightly you tie them, they'll break the rope.”

Aiyen met his eyes.

“A wolf only comes back when it chooses to.”

She smiled faintly.

“You're not a hound, Vikir.”

He looked at her injured leg.

Leaves and leather wrapped it tightly.

She still limped from Adonai's poisoned arrow.

“You can't help me in that condition.”

His voice was dry.

“Go home.”

Aiyen had expected the answer.

She held out a package.

Dried meat.

Fruit.

Nuts.

Vikir took it.

“Looks like an offering for the dead.”

“Don't.”

Her eyes flashed.

Then softened.

“Promise me something.”

Vikir knew the scene.

“Promise you'll come back.”

He did not answer.

Aiyen swallowed.

“No.”

She corrected herself.

“You don't have to come back.”

Her voice became unsteady.

“Just stay alive.”

Vikir stayed silent.

Then answered.

“Of course.”

Aiyen finally breathed.

Vikir turned away.

The wolf disappeared into the rain.

There was one thing he did not tell her.

*Just stay alive.*

For the first time since his second birth—

Vikir had lied.

He did not know whether he could keep that promise.
