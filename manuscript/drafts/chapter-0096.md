# Chapter 96: Madam Eight-Legs (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/096.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 95 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Madam Eight-Legs**, **Akwilla**, **Aiyen**, **Bakira**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Adonai**, **Beelzebub**, and **Baskerville**.

“Madam crossed a line.”

Vikir looked across the ruined village.

“I'll go to her and demand an answer.”

His final words horrified every Ballak warrior who heard them.

It was not that none of them had thought about revenge.

Innocent children had died.

Elders.

The sick.

Companions.

Wolves.

Their homes and food—the foundations needed to survive—had been destroyed.

Still—

the idea of confronting Madam Eight-Legs directly was something else entirely.

“Confront Madam? That's impossible. No warrior has ever done such a thing.”

“Vikir, even for the hero of the floodlands, that's reckless.”

“Madam Eight-Legs is a god here.”

“One of the worst evil gods.”

“We can recover from this damage eventually. Forgetting it as quickly as possible is the only answer.”

These were warriors who had trusted Vikir without reservation again and again.

For the first time—

they all shook their heads.

That alone showed how deeply Madam's existence terrified them.

Then—

tap.

A hand settled on Vikir's shoulder.

Akwilla.

The Night Fox looked pale.

Her usual overwhelming dignity had been badly diminished.

She had fought Divine Archer Adonai—an ancient legend—one-on-one and defeated his corpse.

But she had paid for it.

Cuts from arrows covered her body.

Worse, the poison carried through the bow made from Madam's remains had entered those wounds.

Even a master-level warrior's natural recovery could not clear it easily.

“Vikir.”

Akwilla looked directly at him.

“Young hero of the floodlands. Newly risen sun.”

Her voice was grave.

“Don't let the anger in front of you control your judgment. Anything involving Madam requires extreme caution.”

Aiyen stood beside her and agreed.

“Madam Eight-Legs isn't only Ballak's fear.”

She looked toward the jungle.

“Every tribe fears her. She's an unavoidable fate. The core of the darkness. Something like a natural disaster.”

Her expression tightened.

“To think of revenge against something like that—”

She stopped.

[Whine… whimper…]

Something was crawling painfully out from beneath the collapsed wreckage.

“Bakira?”

Aiyen froze.

Bakira.

The wolf born on the same day as Aiyen.

Her companion for almost her entire life.

Before Vikir's Illiad with Ah'Heman, Aiyen had sent Bakira back to the village with one simple instruction.

> *Go tell them to prepare for the winner of the Illiad.*

Such an easy task.

And this was what had happened.

[Whine… whine…]

Bakira dragged herself toward Aiyen.

She reached her feet and rubbed a wet nose against her.

The wolf's right hind leg was a ruin.

Bones shattered.

Black poison already driven deep into the marrow.

There was no need to ask what had caused it.

Bakira had resisted Madam Eight-Legs.

And the poison was still spreading.

The damage was so severe that amputation might be the only way to save her.

Aiyen stared at the ruined leg in silence.

Vikir answered the argument she had been making only moments earlier.

“Do you still think the desire for revenge is meaningless?”

“…”

Aiyen had no answer.

Akwilla looked from her daughter's face to Bakira's whimpering body and released a long breath.

“We'll talk about this later.”

She was badly injured herself.

Still—

her first concern was everyone else.

“Treat Bakira first. Then attend to the other wounded. Quickly.”

The chieftain's order sent everyone moving again.

There were survivors to find.

Wounds to treat.

Food and shelter for that very day had to be secured.

◆◆◆

That night was unusually dark.

Heavy rain fell again.

There was no moon.

Only occasional lightning illuminated the black floodlands.

A shadow moved through the hanging curtains of rain like a ghost.

Vikir.

He had left the tribe without telling anyone.

Again.

The hound was already on his way to meet Madam Eight-Legs.

*I would have had to fight her eventually anyway.*

Madam had been one of Vikir's real objectives in coming into these wetlands from the beginning.

The Empire still did not understand the true danger of this monster.

An ultra-high-threat creature whose danger had not yet been properly recognized.

If he hunted her before the Empire even understood what she was, the benefits could be enormous.

Beelzebub pulsed against the artery in his wrist.

Vikir listened to it while moving through the rain.

*Once the Age of Destruction begins, monsters on Madam's level won't feel quite so unique.*

*But right now—she is.*

Madam was also one of the reasons the tribes had been forced to keep abandoning their homes.

As hunger drove her territory outward, they fled.

When Baskerville cut deeper into the jungle and expanded Imperial territory from the opposite direction, they fled again.

Caught between Madam Eight-Legs and Baskerville—

the tribes had nowhere stable to live.

*I can give them their freedom back.*

Madam.

Baskerville.

Vikir intended, in time, to bring both forces that oppressed the tribes under control.

That would repay the people who had sheltered and protected him.

And it would benefit the future of both sides.

There was another reason to fight.

Vikir wanted to know how strong he had become during the last two years.

*The fight with Adonai taught me something.*

He could feel it.

*I'm close to the next level.*

Vikir continued forward through the rain.

Then stopped.

A familiar scent had reached him through the downpour.

One he had smelled almost every day for two years.

Aiyen stood ahead of him.

Drenched from head to toe.

Waiting.

“You expected me?”

“…”

“How long have you been standing here?”

“…”

She did not answer.

After a while, Aiyen spoke.

“A wolf can't be tamed.”

“…?”

“No matter how tightly you tie it, eventually it'll break the rope and run.”

She lifted her eyes to Vikir's.

“A wolf stays near you only when it chooses to come back.”

A small smile touched her face.

“You aren't a hound, Vikir.”

“…”

“You call yourself one.”

Her smile became sadder.

“But no hound moves the way you do.”

Vikir looked down at her leg.

Her right leg had been wrapped tightly in leaves and leather.

During the battle with Adonai, Aiyen had taken a poisoned arrow while saving Vikir.

The lingering damage still made her limp.

After a short silence, Vikir answered.

“With your leg like that, you won't help me.”

His voice was dry enough to sound strange in air heavy with rain.

“Go back to the village.”

Aiyen had clearly expected exactly that.

She pressed her lips together.

Then held something out.

A leaf-wrapped bundle.

Dried meat.

Fruit.

Nuts.

Food for the road.

Vikir gave a faint laugh.

“Looks like a sacrificial offering.”

“Don't joke about that.”

Aiyen glared.

Then her expression changed.

“Promise me something.”

The scene felt familiar.

Vikir had experienced something like this before.

“Promise you'll come back.”

“…”

He said nothing.

Aiyen held her breath.

Then—

“No.”

She hesitated.

“No. If you don't want to come back, that's fine.”

Her voice had become faint.

Uneasy.

Pressed flat by anxiety.

Then she looked directly at him, the words almost tearing out of her.

“You don't even have to come back.”

A pause.

“Just live.”

Vikir remained silent for a while.

Then—

after a small hesitation—

“…Of course.”

At last, Aiyen exhaled.

White breath escaped her lips.

Dark clouds swallowed what little dawn-light remained.

The world went black.

Aiyen stood where she was and watched the back of the hound—

no.

The wolf—

as he disappeared into the rain.

Once again, the wolf ran beyond the dawn.

There was one thing Vikir had not told her.

> *You don't have to come back. Just live.*

Women had a habit of asking for difficult things.

Since being born a second time—

this was the first promise Vikir had made while knowing it might be a lie.

Because even he did not know whether he would survive long enough to keep it.
