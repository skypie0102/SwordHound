Vikir stared at the weapon.

He could not identify it with absolute certainty at first.

But he recognized what kind of thing it was.

Ancient calamities.

Seven demonic beings whose infamy had spread across both the Demon World and the human world.

The **Seven Great Plagues**.

Their remains—

material or conceptual—

had persisted even after death.

They could no longer freely interfere with causality.

But fragments of their old power survived.

One such remnant had become **Beelzebub**.

And there were six others.

Humans called the weapons created from those remains—

the **Seven Demon Swords**.

Beelzebub was one.

The blade in Black Tongue’s hands—

was another.

**Asmodeus.**

A demonic sword just as vicious as Beelzebub.

In Vikir’s previous life—

it had appeared among the demons’ final weapons late in the Age of Destruction.

*I never expected to find it here.*

Black Tongue gazed at the wrinkled blade lovingly.

“Once upon a time—

I had a leech I adored.”

“Though now that I think about it—

maybe calling it a leech wasn’t quite right.”

He kissed the blade.

“It wanted blood more than anything.”

“Too much.”

“It kept drinking—

growing—

becoming stronger.”

“Eventually I couldn’t control it.”

“So I tricked it.”

“Locked it in isolation.”

“And starved it.”

“….”

“But it refused to die.”

“No matter how long I kept it sealed away.”

“It only shriveled.”

“Like hibernation.”

“So eventually—

I ground it on a whetstone.”

Black Tongue laughed.

“So much meat powder came off.”

“But look at it now.”

“Sharp enough to be called a sword.”

Vikir almost admired the madness required to turn an immortal blood-parasite into a blade.

Almost.

“You should be careful.”

“Asmodeus does not serve an unworthy master.”

“The moment it gets the chance—

it will turn around and eat you.”

Vikir had seen exactly that happen before regression.

Human masters.

Demonic masters.

Asmodeus devoured both.

Black Tongue only smiled.

“This one still wants blood.”

“Especially yours.”

The sword swelled.

It drank directly from Black Tongue as he held it.

Its flesh softened.

Its blade writhed.

Then—

CLANG!

Asmodeus and Beelzebub collided.

The impact drove weight through Vikir’s entire arm.

The dried weapon was surprisingly small—

but unbelievably dense.

Harder than ordinary metal.

Then—

creak.

Asmodeus bent.

Not like steel.

Like living flesh.

It curved around Beelzebub.

WHIP!

Vikir thrust Beelzebub into Black Tongue’s chest—

and at the same moment—

Asmodeus pierced Vikir’s thigh.

“Agh!”

Black Tongue screamed.

Then laughed.

“You bastard!”

“Protect your master before chasing food!”

The sword was as insane as its owner.

Vikir frowned.

Asmodeus attacked on its own—

yet every movement chose an efficient killing line.

And Black Tongue himself possessed excellent swordsmanship.

Purely in combat terms—

he might even overwhelm D’Ordume or Souaré.

Vikir pulled power through Beelzebub.

Asmodeus answered by drinking deeper.

Blood.

Toxins.

Even the venom stored in Vikir’s body.

“What a troublesome weapon.”

Vikir stepped backward.

Black Tongue followed.

“Scared?”

“Afraid?”

“Why are you running?”

Vikir did not answer.

He kept retreating—

along the exact route he had taken earlier.

Black Tongue pressed harder.

“Fear blinds the eyes and deafens the ears, my friend.”

His arm twisted.

Asmodeus twisted farther.

“The more blood it drinks—

the more flexible it becomes.”

Like dried meat softening after absorbing water.

The sword’s trajectory became impossible to predict.

Vikir jerked his head aside.

Too late.

RIP!

His ear tore free.

Not only the outer ear.

The internal structures were damaged too.

Pain exploded through his skull.

His regeneration immediately began closing the wound—

but complex organs took longer to restore.

Balance vanished.

Distance became difficult to judge.

Black Tongue rushed him.

“Hehehe!”

“Die, Night Hound!”

Vikir slashed wildly.

Left.

Right.

Up.

Down.

Every attack missed.

Some flew in obviously wrong directions.

Black Tongue laughed.

“You’re completely useless now!”

BOOM!

He slammed Vikir down.

His body had swollen to nearly twice its earlier size.

The leeches covering him boiled with excitement.

“With all this noise—

someone may come.”

“D’Ordume.”

“Souaré.”

“Maybe Orca himself.”

“How does that feel?”

“Nervous?”

“Cold sweat?”

“The whole world spinning?”

Vikir answered calmly.

“Hm.”

“That would be inconvenient.”

D’Ordume and Souaré were manageable.

Orca—

was not someone he wanted appearing here yet.

Black Tongue grinned.

Vikir raised his left hand.

“I wanted your parasites.”

“So I spent a little time playing along.”

“But I don’t have any more time.”

“…!?”

Black Tongue’s eyes bulged.

The leather bag containing his precious doppelganger-leech eggs—

was in Vikir’s hand.

“When did you—”

“Goodbye.”

BOOM!

The corridor split.

The strange slashes Vikir had thrown while apparently disoriented—

had never been aimed at Black Tongue.

They had cut the walls.

Floor.

Ceiling.

And Flubber’s mucus layer sealing the outer structure.

CRACK!

An entire section of corridor separated from Nouvelle Vague—

like a slice cut from a cake.

Black Tongue felt the whole space move.

Then—

ROAR!

Deep-sea water crashed in.

“What!?”

“This is nothing!”

“I can swim back!”

Black Tongue flailed as the severed corridor fell into the abyss.

Then he saw Vikir.

The veteran hound’s eyes were perfectly focused.

“…Annoying bastard.”

Vikir released the power he had been saving.

**Baskerville Eighth Form.**

**Black Sun.**

Eight fangs curved into a compressed black sphere.

The rushing ocean became a vortex.

Black Tongue—

caught at its center—

could no longer move freely.

Vikir looked down at him.

The hunt was over.

“Die.”

Simple.

Final.
