“Clap!”

The guards pushed Vikir's hands together.

“Harder!”

Every midnight, Red Salt Castle's guards applauded Vikir's statue twelve times.

People nearby had begun using the sound to tell time.

Vikir stared at the men forcing him to applaud himself.

Some faces were familiar.

Soldiers from the retreat two years ago.

The bearded guard laughed.

“Anyone around here who doesn't know Lord Vikir is suspicious.”

He raised his mug.

“The stories reaching the capital are probably exaggerated. But Morgue used that much gold for his statue. That should tell you how grateful they are.”

Local nobles and officials even visited annually for ceremonies.

Vikir pulled his hood lower.

The actual events had already mutated.

Adolf Morgue had done most of the direct fighting that night.

Vikir had made pursuit through darkness possible.

He had genuinely stayed behind against Madam.

Everything else had expanded through retelling.

Cold wind cleared smoke from the room.

Three women entered.

Red hair.

Similar faces.

Formal robes.

**Highsis Morgue.**

**Middlesis Morgue.**

**Lowsis Morgue.**

The **Three Flowers of Morgue**.

Plant.

Earth.

Water.

The guards stood instantly.

“Loyalty! Greetings, Commanders!”

Vikir looked up.

The triplets sat near the other guards.

Their personalities had not become any gentler.

“What are you talking about?”

“Midnight applause?”

“Lord Vikir again?”

“Yes!”

Highsis smiled over her beer.

“His accomplishments deserve it.”

She sighed theatrically.

“The first time I saw him, my heart practically stopped.”

New guards leaned closer.

“You met him?”

“Of course.”

The three sisters began remembering their encounters with Vikir far more fondly than Vikir remembered them.

“A beautiful young man.”

“Kind.”

“Charismatic.”

“I watched him whenever I could.”

“We should have spoken longer.”

Vikir nearly laughed.

Their first meetings had been considerably less pleasant.

Still—

the chatter was useful.

He remained quiet and listened.

Two years had changed more than he expected.

After Vikir disappeared—

Hugo repeatedly went to Morgue in fury.

Morgue paid Baskerville enormous compensation.

Hugo remained angry.

Camus personally led search parties into the floodlands for almost two years.

Only a few weeks earlier—

she stopped.

Then vanished into closed-door training.

Vikir absorbed the information.

Hugo's anger was easy to explain.

A useful hound had disappeared.

Morgue's compensation had apparently not satisfied him.

Camus was harder to understand.

She had continued searching for two years.

The triplets returned to boasting.

“If he were alive, we'd make a fine couple.”

“You? I'd be the one.”

“Why fight? There are three of us.”

Then all three deflated.

“What does it matter?”

“He's dead.”

Vikir stood.

Enough.

He wanted sleep.

Highsis noticed the hooded stranger moving upstairs.

“You!”

He stopped.

“Come here.”

A guard tried to intervene.

“Commander, he's only a kid I bought milk for—”

“Quiet.”

Highsis raised one hand.

“Bind.”

The wooden stair rail split.

Vines surged toward Vikir.

He stepped over them with almost insulting ease.

The tavern went silent.

Middlesis and Lowsis stood.

Guards drew weapons.

Highsis's face sharpened.

“Who are you?”

Vikir sighed.

He could escape.

Doing so would put the entire fortress on alert.

And he was already returning to Baskerville.

Whether Morgue learned first or second no longer mattered.

Vikir raised his hands.

Then pulled back his hood.

Highsis blinked.

“……Oh.”

Middlesis followed.

“……Oh.”

Lowsis stared.

“……Oh.”

At first—

they reacted only to his face.

Then one guard slowly looked toward the statue outside.

Back to Vikir.

Back to the statue.

His expression froze.

Others followed.

The resemblance was impossible to miss.

Fingers began pointing.

“Vi…”

“Vi… Vi…”

The missing hound of Baskerville.

The dead hero of Red Salt Castle.
