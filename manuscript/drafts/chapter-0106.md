# Chapter 106: The Returned Hero (2)

“Come on, clap! You too! Clap!”

“We applaud Lord Vikir’s statue every night.”

“We’re alive because he held that monster back!”

“Come on, kid. Why are you so weak? Put some strength into it! Clap! Like this!”

At midnight, the guards of Red Salt Castle clapped twelve times toward Vikir’s statue.

People passing nearby could apparently tell the hour from the applause.

Vikir studied the faces of the guards forcing him to join in.

Two years ago.

The Depths.

The retreat from Madam Eight-Legs.

Some of these men did look like soldiers he had seen that night.

The hairy guard who had ordered Vikir milk laughed.

“Anyone around here who doesn’t know Lord Vikir’s name has to be a spy. Saying his fame reached every corner of the Empire might be an exaggeration, but he really is famous. Morgue used that much gold for his statue. That tells you how grateful they are.”

Every year, politicians and nobles from nearby towns supposedly came to pay respects and hold memorial ceremonies.

Vikir pulled his hood lower and sighed.

He had certainly done a great deal in the Depths two years ago.

But most of the story had been grotesquely inflated.

Single-handedly killing thousands of tribesmen.

Saving the Morgue-Baskerville allied army.

Rescuing Morgue’s delicate young lady.

In truth, Adolf Morgue had been the most active person that night.

Vikir had mainly made the nighttime pursuit possible and bought them time.

*…Though fighting the monster at the end really was me.*

He drank his milk and looked back on the past.

Somehow, everything Adolf and the allied force had accomplished had been gathered together and credited to Vikir alone.

While he was still wondering how the story had grown this distorted—

Creak.

The tavern door opened.

The simmering sound of fish soup, the smell of roasting skewers, and the greasy heat of sizzling pork seemed to vanish beneath a rush of cold night air.

Three women in long robes stepped inside.

Thick eyebrows.

Catlike eyes.

Beautiful faces so similar they could only belong to triplets.

The guards jumped to their feet.

“Loyalty! Greetings to the fortress commanders!”

Their abrupt change in posture caught Vikir’s attention.

He looked toward the three women.

“…!”

Familiar faces.

The Three Flowers of Morgue.

Or, depending on whom one asked, the Three Plagues.

Highsis, Middlesis, and Lowsis Morgue.

Plant, earth, and water magic were their respective specialties.

Their distinctive red hair swayed as they crossed the tavern and took a table.

The men around them stared for a moment, but nobody behaved foolishly.

The triplets were beautiful.

Their personalities were also infamous.

“What were you all talking about so loudly?”

“What else? It’s midnight. They were clapping again.”

“Were you talking about Lord Vikir?”

The guards answered at once.

“Yes!”

Highsis, the eldest, picked up a cold mug of beer and smiled.

“Ho ho ho. His accomplishments really were magnificent. To be honest, I still remember the way my heart raced the first time I saw him.”

The newer guards leaned forward.

“Commander, you met him personally?”

“Of course.”

Highsis and her sisters narrowed their eyes with pleased smiles.

“At first, I thought he was only a handsome boy. But after sharing the fortress with him, I came to understand how kind and charming he really was.”

Vikir almost laughed from his distant seat.

He remembered their first meeting rather differently.

Highsis, Middlesis, and Lowsis, however, seemed to have polished those memories into something beautiful.

“He really was wonderful. Honestly, I spent most of his time at the fortress secretly watching him.”

“We only crossed paths a few times in the dining hall.”

“We saw him longer while patrolling the cotton fields. Those three vicious Baskerville dogs kept getting in the way, so we never had a proper conversation. If I had known what would happen, I would have talked to him more.”

The sisters chatted beside the guards.

The server who had been rude only moments before now brought them drinks and food with impeccable manners.

Vikir remained alone at his table and listened.

He wanted to know what had happened to Morgue and Baskerville while he was gone.

The triplets talked enough to answer quite a few questions.

The changes following Vikir’s disappearance and presumed death had been more dramatic than expected.

Hugo Le Baskerville had repeatedly gone to Morgue in a rage.

Morgue had paid Baskerville an enormous compensation.

Even that had not calmed Hugo.

Most surprising of all, Camus had continued personally leading search parties for clues in the Depths until only a few weeks ago.

Vikir accepted the first two facts without much trouble.

If Hugo was angry that Vikir had vanished, it was probably because he had lost a useful hound.

If he was still angry, perhaps he simply thought Morgue’s compensation insufficient.

Camus was the unexpected part.

Two years later, she still had not forgotten him.

She had kept searching until only weeks ago.

Then, one day, she stopped.

Rumor said she had withdrawn deep into the family estate for closed-door training.

The triplets continued telling stories about Vikir while showing off their own connections to him.

“By the way, that golden statue outside doesn’t capture Lord Vikir’s true appearance at all. Such a shame. If he were still alive, we would make a wonderful couple.”

“Who would make a couple with whom? Don’t be ridiculous. If he were alive, I’d be the one to win him over.”

“Stop fighting, sisters. The three of us could just date him together. What man would refuse all three of us?”

Then all three drooped.

“What’s the point of arguing? He’s gone.”

A melancholy silence settled over the tavern.

Vikir quietly stood and headed upstairs.

He wanted to lie down.

Listening to people discuss him as though he were a legendary dead man was exhausting.

Then—

Highsis happened to turn her head.

Her eyes fixed on Vikir’s hood.

“You! You look suspicious. Come here.”

Apparently eager for something to do, she immediately invoked her authority as fortress commander.

Vikir stopped on the stairs.

Taking off his hood would create chaos.

Ignoring her would only make her angrier.

“Hey! Stop! Didn’t you hear me?”

The bearded guard hurried to intervene.

“Commander, that’s the kid I bought milk for earlier. He’s not suspicious.”

“Quiet! Especially now. The tribes outside have been strangely quiet. That means we should be even more vigilant!”

Highsis shot to her feet.

She stretched out a hand.

Powerful mana surged toward Vikir.

“Bind!”

The wooden stair rail creaked.

Long vines burst from it and whipped toward Vikir.

But—

Swish.

Vikir sprang lightly aside.

The vines closed on empty air.

Highsis stared.

So did the normally composed Middlesis and Lowsis.

The nearby guards spat out their drinks.

The three Morgue witches were already approaching the Third Circle, and Highsis was the strongest among them.

Yet the hooded stranger had evaded her spell as if it were nothing.

“…This is getting troublesome.”

Vikir immediately understood what was coming.

He had hoped to pass through unnoticed, but the Morgue triplets were not people he could casually brush aside without consequences.

Highsis, Middlesis, and Lowsis rose together and gathered mana.

The guards who had been laughing moments earlier drew swords, bows, and spellbooks.

They surrounded Vikir.

“Who are you? Remove your hood and give us your name!”

Highsis snapped.

Vikir sighed.

He could escape.

Doing so would throw the entire fortress into chaos.

He also could not guarantee that nobody would be hurt.

More importantly, there was no real reason to hide.

*I’m heading to Baskerville to tell them I’m back anyway.*

Whether Morgue learned first or Baskerville did was only a matter of order.

Vikir stopped.

He raised both hands.

Under the wary gaze of everyone in the tavern, he slowly pushed back his hood.

…

The moment his face appeared—

“…Oh.”

“…Oh my.”

“…Oh my, oh my.”

The triplets gasped together.

Not because they had recognized him yet.

For one brief moment, they were simply reacting to his face.

Then people began noticing that Vikir looked remarkably like someone else.

Several guards slowly turned toward the golden statue outside the window.

“…?”

Then back toward the young man standing on the stair.

“…!”

They froze.

The realization spread through the guards.

Then the tavern.

Then Highsis, Middlesis, and Lowsis themselves.

Soon everyone who understood was staring openmouthed, trembling fingers pointed at him.

“Vi… Vi… Vi… Vi…”

Vikir Van Baskerville.

The missing hound of the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan.

The hero of Red Salt Castle had returned.
