# Chapter 220: That Day, Him and Me (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/220.txt` — `8f768db48cb1b62930c38bf5f924bb5176a6e63c`. Recovered English Chapter 218 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Morgue Clan, Camus Morgue, Adolf Morgue, Raspane Morgue, Rosie Morgue, Vikir Van Baskerville, Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, Red Salt Castle, and Dark/Light Faction continuity. The flashback begins roughly three years earlier, immediately after Camus returns from the mountain believing Vikir dead.

—About three years earlier—

Whenever three or more servants gathered in the Morgue estate—

they talked about *that day*.

Rumor spread—

mouth to mouth.

“Did you hear?”

“Everyone heard.”

“She went into the depths of Le Rouge et Le Noir.”

“With Morgue mages and Baskerville swordsmen.”

“To rescue Lady Camus.”

“At least she came back alive.”

“Then why hasn't she left her room?”

“Would you?”

“After what happened out there?”

One maid—

who had helped with the expedition's supplies—

lowered her voice.

“…I heard something different.”

The others leaned closer.

“It wasn't fear.”

“It was love.”

“Love?”

“The Baskerville man she had liked for years disappeared during the expedition.”

“The one from before?”

“Yes.”

“In those mountains?”

“At night?”

“With that monster rampaging?”

“Then he's dead.”

“What else could have happened?”

The maids fell quiet.

Camus—

normally blunt and reserved—

had always treated servants well.

Now—

she barely ate.

Barely slept.

Cried through the night.

Today—

they could hardly hear even that.

“Maybe she finally fell asleep.”

“No.”

“I listened at the door.”

“She was still crying.”

“Her voice is just gone.”

“She's been crying until she's hoarse.”

One maid sighed.

“All this over one man…”

“If I had Lady Camus's face, talent, and position—”

“I wouldn't ruin myself like that.”

“What sort of man could make her this obsessed?”

“Whoever he was, he wasn't worth—”

She stopped.

All three maids—

went pale.

A man stood behind them.

Long red beard.

Heavy presence.

**Adolf Morgue.**

The servants bowed so fast their heads nearly collided.

“Marquis!”

“We were only worried about Lady Camus!”

“We meant no disrespect!”

Adolf's usual gentleness—

was gone.

“These are the tongues—

spreading their master's private grief?”

He snapped his fingers.

Three tongues—

shot forward.

Slap.

Slap.

Slap.

Their tips stuck together.

“Mmph!?”

“Mmm—!”

The three women were forced into a tight circle—

faces almost touching.

Adolf clicked his own tongue.

“A servant's mouth should be heavy.”

“You made yours light as birds.”

“So I added weight.”

The spell—

would last roughly a month.

They would need help—

water.

soft food.

everything.

Unless—

they chose to cut the joined tips free.

Adolf walked away.

Toward Camus's room.

At the door—

he stopped.

Listened.

Nothing.

Then—

faintly—

a broken sob.

Barely human.

Like someone buried alive—

still trying to cry.

Adolf sighed.

Knock.

Knock.

“Camus.”

“It's me.”

“Your uncle.”

No answer.

He waited.

Still—

nothing.

“I'm coming in.”

Silence—

accepted as permission.

The door opened.

Dark room.

Bed—

at the center.

Blankets piled like a grave mound.

Adolf sat beside it.

A thin voice emerged.

“The maids did nothing wrong.”

“Undo the spell.”

Adolf froze.

“Your voice…”

He pulled the blanket back.

Camus lay beneath it—

hollow-eyed.

thin.

drained.

Her throat—

ruined from crying.

“Camus.”

“What happened to your voice?”

She closed her eyes.

“Rosie.”

“Then Vikir.”

“They both became like that because of me.”

Adolf—

said nothing.

He was not good at empty comfort.

But neither—

could he tell his niece that everything was truly her fault.

So he tried.

“Camus.”

“You cannot stop here.”

“Rosie and Vikir would not want this.”

“The living carry the lives of the dead with them.”

“That makes your life heavier—

not meaningless.”

Camus's eyes—

opened.

“…What?”

Adolf blinked.

“What?”

“Say that again.”

“You must live for those who died?”

“Yes!”

She sat up so violently—

Adolf nearly fell backward.

“That's it.”

“The living carry the dead's share.”

Adolf nodded—

relieved that something had reached her.

Camus—

reached a completely different conclusion.

“Then if the living return that share—”

“the dead can live again.”

“…What?”

“The total remains the same.”

“Life as a state function.”

“Magic is just a method of calculating and moving that value.”

“If the order changes—”

“if negative dimensions can supply entropy—”

“if the positive-dimensional remainder can be exchanged…”

Adolf's relief vanished.

“Camus.”

“What are you thinking?”

Too late.

Camus threw off the blanket.

Stood.

Her weakened body—

immediately swayed.

Adolf caught her.

She smiled.

For the first time in days—

life in her eyes.

“Food.”

“Bring me food.”

◆◆◆

The servants—

who had been watching for any sign of recovery—

set a table in less than a minute.

Camus ate.

Fast.

Desperately.

No spoon.

No fork.

Hands—

food—

mouth.

Then she saw—

a potato.

A Morgue-grown variety.

Memory struck.

*Your family doesn't have these, right?*

Vikir's voice.

Camus's eyes filled.

She bit into the potato.

“…No earthy taste.”

Then swallowed.

More food.

“Again.”

Raspane herself—

left an important meeting about **Red Salt Castle** and the ruby mine—

just to see her daughter eating again.

“Bring more.”

More arrived.

Camus finished it.

“More.”

Again.

“More!”

Again.

Eventually—

even Raspane's relief became concern.

Camus ate until she vomited.

Then—

continued eating.

“More!”

“Camus, stop.”

“More!”

“I need strength.”

“I need to build it back.”

She forced down another mouthful.

Tears—

mixed with sweat.

Adolf and Raspane—

could only watch.

Finally—

when the table had been emptied again—

Camus stood.

She looked directly at the head of Morgue.

“Mother.”

“Give me command authority.”

“Let me search the mountains.”

Vikir's body—

had not been found.

Camus—

intended to find it.
