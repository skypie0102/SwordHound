# Chapter 416: End game (8)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/416.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 414 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Orca Montreuil-sur-Mer Javert, Sadi, Aiyen, D’Ordume, Souaré, Flubber, Poseidon, Nabokov I, Cane Corso, Baskerville Eighth Form — Black Sun, and the Forty-Seven Riot. **Angajumang Cedric Sadi de Sade / Marquis de Sade** follows the aligned witness as a source-local form pending direct Fandom confirmation.

All of Nouvelle Vague’s monsters had converged on Level Ten.

Warden Orca.

D’Ordume.

Souaré.

Flubber.

The only missing Wardens—

BDISSEM and Black Tongue—

had already been removed by Vikir.

Yet Orca alone dominated the space.

He watched Vikir.

Vikir watched him.

Two old fighters—

one wearing a young body—

measuring each other.

Then Sadi screamed:

“How dare you ignore me?!”

Her whip tore free from the ground.

Wrapped around Orca.

He did not move.

Only clicked his tongue.

“Winston was too soft.”

“Perhaps because he was young.”

“I told him—

do not reap.”

“Crush the seed.”

“What benefit is there in leaving a baby that carries hatred?”

“Eventually—

it becomes trouble.”

A memory from long ago.

Spoken almost to himself.

It still drove Sadi into fury.

“DIE!”

“I’LL KILL YOU!”

Her demonized power surged into the whip.

BOOM!

Air ruptured.

Ground tore open.

Orca drew the chain at his waist.

The club attached to it rose into his hand.

He swung once.

WHUM.

The pressure alone scattered Sadi’s attack.

Aiyen stared.

“At his age?”

“He should be bedridden.”

“Akwilla wasn’t weak at old age either.”

“Our mother was never that old.”

Aiyen smirked—

then pulled a stolen bow to its limit.

Her strength had grown enormously through the Hell Tree.

TWANG!

The arrow screamed toward Orca’s temple.

CLINK.

He blocked it with the club.

“A sniper.”

“Annoying.”

Aiyen clicked her tongue.

“If I had a proper bow…”

Orca moved.

“D’Ordume.”

“Souaré.”

“Clear the surroundings.”

“I’ll inspect that suspicious sphere.”

Both Wardens nodded.

Even Flubber seemed to stiffen in agreement.

Orca started toward Poseidon.

Vikir stepped in front of him.

“No.”

Beelzebub glowed deep red.

Orca frowned.

“Baskerville brat.”

“How do you possess this much strength at your age?”

“Enough about age.”

Neither of them matched the body age others saw.

Then they collided.

BANG!

Black force burst from Orca’s club.

Crimson aura from Beelzebub.

The shockwave traveled through the ground—

and into Poseidon.

Its blue light intensified.

Orca’s eyes sharpened.

“So this is deliberate.”

“You caused that riot—

then came here like a rat.”

“I won’t deny it.”

Club.

Sword.

Every clash cracked air and warped stone.

Vikir shouted over his shoulder:

“I’ll stop him.”

“Charge Poseidon.”

“Understood!”

Aiyen knew instantly that Orca was beyond her.

She moved to the sphere.

Sadi tried to flank him.

“Damn old man—”

D’Ordume.

Souaré.

Flubber.

All blocked her.

So Vikir could focus entirely on Orca.

That did not make the fight easy.

BOOM!

The club hit Beelzebub’s spine.

Vikir’s entire frame shook.

Each strike was absurdly heavy.

One bad adjustment—

and he would be crushed.

*Orca.*

*So this is Nouvelle Vague’s true symbol.*

Vikir withdrew by centimeters.

Cold sweat slid down his skin.

He was using power beyond the ordinary Sword Master threshold—

yet neither side gained ground.

*Stronger than Nabokov I.*

*Probably slightly below Cane Corso.*

Nabokov had already been beyond her prime—

and as a cleric—

had not devoted her life purely to combat.

Cane Corso—

meanwhile—

had abandoned ordinary humanity as a Death Knight.

Which meant—

Orca might be the strongest **living human** Vikir had personally fought.

Orca seemed equally disturbed.

The young-looking swordsman moved like someone who had survived several lifetimes.

BOOM!

Club and sword met.

Auras tangled.

Mountains of prison stone collapsed.

Vikir blocked a kick with his knee.

CRACK.

Orca turned his head away from Vikir’s left fist.

Behind them—

Poseidon’s blue flames roared.

“Brother!”

Aiyen shouted.

“Not much longer!”

Vikir tried to disengage.

CLATTER!

The chain attached to Orca’s club wrapped around his ankle.

“Where are you going?”

“See the fight through.”

“Young people have no patience.”

Vikir glanced back.

Poseidon was vibrating violently.

The lava-root explosions below it had nearly reached a critical rhythm.

*Now?*

*Or wait?*

Too early—

and activation might fail.

Too late—

and Vikir could exhaust himself against Orca before escaping.

There was another issue.

Vikir did not want Orca dead.

In the old future—

the Warden had made enormous contributions to the Human Alliance.

And later—

Orca himself had destroyed Nouvelle Vague.

Vikir now understood why.

The Demon War.

The worsening collapse.

Demons turning toward the prison.

And—

separate from all of that—

**the Second Forty-Seven Riot.**

Then Orca’s club came down.

Vikir raised everything he had.

**Baskerville Eighth Form.**

**Black Sun.**

BOOM!

The same attack that had sent Black Tongue into the abyss—

hit Orca.

The Warden held.

Veins burst across his forehead and jaw.

Blood sprayed.

Yet he did not take a step backward.

“…Is that your full strength?”

“….”

“Then it is my turn.”

Orca wiped sweat and blood from his face.

CRACK.

Bones and muscle shifted inside his body.

Vikir remembered something from the previous timeline.

*Of course.*

*Orca isn’t an ordinary human either.*

Every one of Nouvelle Vague’s great Wardens was abnormal.

Why would the man above them be different?

Vikir reset his stance.

Then—

RIP!

Orca’s black coat tore across the back.

A whip mark opened beneath it.

Sadi.

Bloodied.

Shaking from battle and the cost of demonization.

But still standing.

Behind her—

D’Ordume and Souaré had collapsed again.

Flubber had been battered so thoroughly that its main mass was no longer visible.

“…Thank you.”

Sadi was looking at Vikir.

Not Orca.

Vikir frowned.

“For what?”

“Keeping your promise.”

Then he remembered.

When he had asked Sadi for Winter Sword Orwell—

she had demanded one thing in return.

*If you ever escape Nouvelle Vague—*

*take my grandfather out too.*

“…!”

Vikir looked around.

His fight with Orca had ruined the Level-Ten terrain.

Hills.

Stone formations.

Walls.

Cracks ran everywhere.

Even the structures extending upward toward the isolated cells of Level Nine had been damaged.

BOOM!

Far away—

solitary cells began collapsing.

“No…”

Souaré’s face whitened.

D’Ordume went silent.

Even Orca’s expression became worse than it had during Black Sun.

Then—

from beneath the fallen stone—

came a weak laugh.

“Puhihihihi…”

A sound like air leaking from a balloon.

Vikir knew that laugh.

He had heard it from solitary confinement before.

A tall old man rose from the rubble.

Thin as a corpse.

Almost skeletal.

Yet relaxed.

Completely unhurried.

“…Angajumang?”

Vikir asked.

The old man smiled.

“Perfect timing.”

“I was getting tired of my vacation.”

He looked toward the bloodied Sadi.

Warmly.

Only then did Vikir confirm it.

**Angajumang Cedric Sadi de Sade.**

The **Marquis de Sade**.

The warmonger who had shaken the Empire more than forty years ago—

during the Forty-Seven Riot—

was free.
