The Imperial bloodline possessed strange gifts.

Astrology.

Astronomy.

Prophecy.

Future sight.

The First Crown Prince—

Jack—

And Andras—

inhabiting Jack’s body—

had pushed that bloodline to its limit.

[COUGH—!]

Broken organs.

Black blood.

Chunks of flesh.

They spilled from Andras’s shattered body.

Cracks across him glowed red.

Sssssssss—

He was crumbling.

Like volcanic ash after a fire.

Yet—

[…I see it!]

Dark mana still poured from his eyes.

Clouds gathered.

Twisted into red veins.

The veins converged—

forming a gigantic eye in the air.

The eye looked down.

Andras—

already dying—

forced the Crown Prince’s prophetic power beyond anything it had ever reached.

[…I SEE IT!]

[YOUR FUTURE!]

The vision struck.

A swamp of blood.

Hills built from bones.

An atmosphere emptied of mana.

A massive mushroom cloud rising beyond the horizon.

Scenes Vikir had seen before.

The Age of Destruction.

The same ruined mental landscape glimpsed in Amdusias’s residual memories.

But then—

something new.

A desert.

Endless.

Empty.

A giant tower standing at its center.

An old man in black—

walking toward it alone.

The images came as fragments.

Sharp flashes.

Memories Vikir had never lived.

And yet—

somehow—

they felt faintly familiar.

*The Hell Tree.*

*When Pigi’s blood closed it.*

*And when Chimeries showed me visions.*

The burning world.

The final destruction of everything.

But Andras was showing something farther away.

Much farther.

[I SEE IT!]

[YOU ARE OLD!]

[ALONE!]

A barren desert stretching without end.

An old man walked through it.

Gray beard.

Black cloak.

Dry wind.

A cough.

No one around him.

Nothing.

More images flashed—

too quickly to understand.

Andras laughed.

[I SEE IT CLEARLY!]

[YOU SAY YOU CARE ABOUT THE PEOPLE AROUND YOU NOW—]

[BUT YOU END UP ALONE!]

[OLD!]

[ROOTLESS!]

[WANDERING!]

The old man crossed the desert.

Sandstorms tore across his clothes.

He kept walking.

Alone.

[YOU’RE RUNNING!]

[RUNNING FROM THOSE WHO CHASE YOU!]

The old man’s beard whipped in the wind.

Ahead—

something appeared on the horizon.

A tower.

Sharp as a sword.

[YOU’LL SPEND YOUR OLD AGE FLEEING!]

[CHEWING ON LONELINESS!]

[THEY’LL REACH YOU!]

[YOU CAN’T ESCAPE THEM!]

[NOT EVER!]

[HAHAHAHA!]

[I CAN SEE THEIR ANGRY FACES!]

[YOUR MISERABLE FUTURE—]

[BOUND TO THEM FOREVER!]

Vikir’s old trauma surfaced.

Running while falsely accused.

Baskerville hounds catching him.

Dragging him through blood.

The execution ground.

The guillotine.

His own severed head.

Cold sweat ran down his face.

Then Andras declared the final image.

[IN THE END—]

[YOU’LL BE TORN INTO FIVE PIECES!]

Five-horse dismemberment.

A brutal execution once popularized in the Warring States Era.

Vikir had nearly suffered that punishment before being sentenced to beheading instead.

His spine went cold.

“….”

The hound clenched his teeth.

Breathing became difficult.

His lungs tightened.

His consciousness blurred.

The **Cradle**.

The **Grave**.

The two ends of a life.

Everything between them—

life itself.

What kind of life had his been?

Again—

grave.

Then cradle.

Vikir remembered the strange connection between the end of his first life—

and the beginning of the second.

He had struggled.

Always.

Illegitimate son.

Child of a concubine.

That was why he had carried **Van**.

Not the privileged names of siblings born from recognized wives.

So he worked harder.

A hundred times harder.

And still—

the ending had been miserable.

Countless battles.

Countless near-deaths.

Then—

betrayal.

A false charge of collaborating with demons.

Execution.

At the very end—

*I want to live.*

*I want another life.*

a miracle.

A tiny butterfly effect.

One small change from the previous life.

Then another.

Then another.

Until the whole world changed.

Not through Vikir alone.

Never alone.

Faces rose in his mind.

The people whose struggles had carried him this far.

And outside this palace—

they were still fighting.

Still risking their lives.

Vikir could not sit here—

drowning in prophecy.

“Demons die.”

The old phrase returned.

Simple.

Absolute.

Even as Andras’s predictions struck with terrifying accuracy—

Vikir did not bend.

His companions still fought.

There was no time for self-pity.

A toad could not stop the giant wheel of causality.

Fight what stood in front of it.

That was better than surrendering to a demon.

[THINK CAREFULLY!]

[THERE IS ONLY A GRAVE IN FRONT OF YOU!]

[YOUR BODY!]

[YOUR HEART!]

[FIVE PIECES!]

[CHANGE YOUR MIND NOW—]

[AND YOU CAN STILL CHANGE THE FUTURE!]

Nymphet.
