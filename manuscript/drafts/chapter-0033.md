# Chapter 33: The Social Club (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/033.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 33 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. The seven local-house names follow the Chinese transliterations and aligned witnesses where no dedicated Fandom canonical entry is currently indexed.

The seven young masters gathered in the VVIP room were clever and ruthless men.

They came from seven old local houses:

The **Montblanc Family**.

The **Pierre Family**.

The **Louis Vuitton Family**.

The **Chanel Family**.

The **Ferragamo Family**.

The **Hermès Family**.

The **Prada Family**.

For generations, those seven houses had lived without needing to bow their heads to anyone except the Baskerville Clan.

Even Baskerville officials had often been drawn into friendly relations with them because of their enormous wealth and the local support behind them.

Things had almost never gone against them.

Naturally, they assumed this time would be no different.

Yes, they had played a little prank on the new Deputy Magistrate.

But—

This time was different.

Crash—!

Crash—!

Crash—!

Countless shards of glass and champagne foam poured through the VVIP room like rain.

The fragments glittered beneath the chandeliers while the champagne splashed hot and frothing across the floor.

The seven heirs stood soaked beneath that sharp, scalding downpour with vacant expressions.

‘He just blew apart the champagne tower through mana resonance?’

‘Just by touching the table?’

‘And from the top down instead of the bottom up?’

Every one of them had at least basic martial training.

That was why they understood just how absurd Vikir's feat had been.

Sending mana into an object, forcing it into resonance, and making it rupture at selected points required exceptional control.

But the glasses had not broken from the place nearest Vikir's hand.

The glasses resting directly on the tabletop had survived until the very end while the highest ones shattered first.

That meant the force had traveled upward through the structure before being released exactly where he intended.

And it had not spread horizontally.

It had moved vertically, layer by layer.

The difficulty was on another level entirely.

It demanded control over strands of mana finer than thread, manipulated as freely as one's own limbs.

A truly monstrous degree of precision.

Only one thought remained in their minds.

‘A **Sword Graduator**!’

Graduators were monsters capable of cleaving apart boulders the size of houses and dropping birds from the sky with killing intent alone.

The young masters, most of whom had barely reached the lower reaches of Sword Expert, could not even imagine standing on equal footing with such a person.

“...”

Vikir's expression had not changed.

Only the dark crimson aura rolling over his shoulders moved, hanging around him like the breath of death itself.

If death possessed a visible shape, perhaps this was what it would look like.

The seven men who had intended to toy with him stood drenched in champagne and began to tremble.

‘Are you insane? They told us he was just a fifteen-year-old kid!’

‘Hey! You're a Mid Sword Expert! At least try something!’

‘Shut up! If we resist, we won't last even one second!’

Their eyes darted desperately between one another.

Then one of the young masters muttered in a daze.

“...Wait. Vikir. I think I've heard that name before.”

Everyone turned toward him.

He swallowed and continued.

“Vikir Van Baskerville. The Baskerville Clan's supernova. They say he survived seven minutes in the River Styx as a baby, strangled two venomous snakes in his cradle, hunted a Cerberus when he was eight, and even won a confrontation with the Morgue Clan's magic fortress...”

The others stared at him as if he had just recited an absurd urban legend.

But what if those stories were true?

Their eyes slowly returned to Vikir.

That dense, almost liquid aura was the sign of a Sword Graduator.

If the stories were all nonsense, then how else were they supposed to explain the deathly pressure pouring from a fifteen-year-old boy?

What fifteen-year-old in the entire world reached the Graduator realm at that age?

Even among the geniuses of Baskerville, it was unheard of.

Perhaps one could search all of **Colosseo Academy**, or even the entire Imperial Capital, and fail to find another example.

The seven young masters finally forced awkward smiles onto their faces.

“...We'll pay for the drinks.”

Did they realize that the servile smiles they had spent their lives seeing on other people's faces were now carved into their own?

Vikir answered without interest.

“Of course you'll pay for the alcohol you drank.”

The seven fell silent.

‘Right... he hasn't drunk anything with us yet.’

They had accidentally revealed their plan to stick him with the bill before they had even carried it out.

Perhaps they could still talk their way out of it.

If the person across from them had been anyone other than Vikir.

His body was fifteen.

His soul had already lived through decades of blood and war.

He saw through their intentions immediately.

“What? Were you planning to get me drinking with you and then dump the bill on me?”

The seven men shook their heads frantically.

“H-How could we possibly do that?”

“Who would do something so shameless?”

“We would never dare treat someone from Baskerville that way...”

They understood something else now.

Vikir's combat strength was terrifying, but his mind was no easier to deceive.

In the end, they had no choice but to lower their heads.

Only minutes ago, they had joked that Baskerville was their hunting dog.

Standing before Vikir, none of them dared repeat it.

Apparently the hounds of Baskerville were not so easily leashed.

Even so, the spoiled heirs of old local houses still wanted to preserve the last scraps of their pride.

“Actually, Deputy Magistrate, we only wanted to test you.”

They composed their expressions and spoke with exaggerated respect.

Vikir remained silent.

Taking that silence as permission to continue, they hurried on.

“To be frank, Underdog City has become terribly corrupt.”

“We thought a leader capable of cleansing the city should be someone who wouldn't be shaken by this degree of temptation.”

“You've proven yourself worthy to lead us. We will follow you.”

“Your noble spirit today will remain a lesson to me for the rest of my life!”

“We'll make sure to repay today's impression properly in the future.”

They bowed deeply.

One of them even winked at Vikir, pretending he was still relaxed.

Then—

“...”

One corner of Vikir's mouth slowly rose.

A smile.

That tiny change transformed the entire room.

Until now, the atmosphere had merely felt like a cold graveyard.

That alone had frightened the seven men.

Now the pressure became almost unbearable.

It crushed down on them like earth being shoveled onto a living grave.

Their breathing turned ragged.

Their forced smiles vanished.

They began to shake.

Vikir slowly approached the seven heirs, who had gone rigid like frogs before a snake.

“A master may test his dog whenever he wishes.”

“...”

“But the reverse is forbidden. A dog does not test its master. Not under any circumstances.”

Hugo's old teaching surfaced in Vikir's mind.

He paced slowly in front of the frozen young men.

“You have a little money and power, so you stopped being afraid of anything?”

No one answered.

Vikir continued.

“That's because you've never seen anything truly frightening.”

They had spent their lives pretending they understood everything.

Pretending to be refined.

Pretending to be calm.

Pretending nothing could touch them.

The price of that pretense was about to become very heavy.

Vikir reached inside his clothes and pulled out a sturdy wooden stake.

The same stake that had earned Judy ten billion gold only days earlier.

**THUD!**

A dull impact rang through the room.

The stake seemed to bend for an instant before whipping forward with terrifying speed.

It smashed into the face of the nearest young master and turned his features into a mass of blood and crushed flesh.

There had been no warning.

Teeth scattered across the floor.

Blood, saliva, tears, and mucus sprayed through the air.

In the space of a few heartbeats, Vikir struck the man's face, head, neck, and shoulders again and again.

Then he did the same to the others.

Before long, all seven heirs lay flattened across the VVIP room like slabs of beaten meat.

**Thud! Thud! Thud! Crack!**

Vikir kept swinging the stake.

“Do you know why you're being beaten?”

“AAAGH! I don't know! I don't know! Why are you hitting us!?”

“If you don't know, then you need to be beaten.”

His voice remained completely indifferent.

The seven men looked at his expressionless face and realized that if this continued, they really could die here.

“I-I know! Ugh! I know why! We understand!”

They begged through split lips and torn tongues, their teeth broken and blood pouring from wounds all over their heads.

Vikir's expression did not change.

“If you understand, then accept your punishment.”

The stake continued to fall through the spray of red.
