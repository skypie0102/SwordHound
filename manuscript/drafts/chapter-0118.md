# Chapter 118: Falling in Love (2)

A chill ran through Vikir's entire body.

Goosebumps rose along his arms.

*…What did I just hear?*

Love.

**Love.**

Even a veteran warrior who had survived battle after battle might have doubted his own ears after hearing such an unexpected word.

Of all people in the world, Hugo was the one least suited to saying it.

And yet Vikir had just heard it from his mouth.

Every strategy he had prepared seemed to collapse at once.

Hugo, meanwhile, spoke with a mixture of irritation and anger.

“Yes. She was the only woman I ever loved in my life. Strange as it may sound, we fell in love at first sight.”

The answer was practically a monologue.

Vikir thought—

*I didn't ask for that much.*

He did not need a detailed account.

He was just about to move to his next question when Hugo continued.

“I was born into a noble marquis house. Roxana was a commoner with nothing to her name. But the moment our eyes met, we knew. Birth and background could not separate us.”

Vikir thought again.

*I really didn't ask.*

Hugo kept going anyway.

“Yes. Our love was difficult. The first moment I saw her, I felt something shake my soul. She didn't feel the same way at first. She tried to distance herself because of her humble birth, and I abandoned everything to follow her.”

His voice grew heavier.

“For her, I could have given up anything. Not merely my body. My soul as well.”

“Mm. Yes. Father. Thank you for answering. Now, about the next—”

“But!”

Hugo cut him off.

“Our love was tested again. Baskerville sent a pursuit party after us.”

“…”

Vikir was not particularly curious anymore.

Still, he decided to let Hugo finish.

Hugo continued through clenched teeth.

What followed was the story of a difficult romance.

A classic romance-fantasy plot, almost embarrassingly so.

But the emotion behind it had survived the years.

Hugo Le Baskerville—

a wealthy, gifted, coldly handsome heir of a powerful house.

Roxana—

poor, spirited, cheerful, and stubbornly independent.

Their relationship survived every objection placed before it.

They quarreled over small misunderstandings.

They cried and laughed through the schemes of people who tried to break them apart.

They railed against cruel circumstances.

And in the end, they overcame the opposition around them and made their love real.

Vikir quietly nodded.

“…And the result was Penelope?”

Penelope La Baskerville.

The instant Hugo heard the name, his pupils trembled again.

More violently than when Roxana had been mentioned.

“What answer are you trying to get from me?!”

“…”

Vikir remained perfectly calm.

Hugo let out a helpless breath.

“Yes. That's right.”

His voice dropped.

“My first child. Penelope.”

Hugo's body trembled slightly as he said her name.

Vikir stared at him.

He had never seen an absolute powerhouse so thoroughly exposed by a single memory.

Perhaps even John Barrymore, who had served Hugo for most of his life, had never seen him like this.

Hugo continued.

“Roxana was physically frail. Not long after Penelope was born, she died of illness.”

His gaze lowered.

“I raised the child alone after that.”

Penelope had been bright and lively.

She inherited Hugo's unbreakable will and Roxana's gentle heart.

Everyone in Baskerville loved her.

Then—

one day—

*that incident* happened.

Penelope went out for a walk.

A Rokoko hunting party captured her.

No one ever discovered how the raiders learned the route she would take.

The Rokoko Clan, infamous for hunting and eating captives, abducted Penelope and vanished into the depths of the Red and Black Mountains.

From that day forward—

Hugo changed.

A Sword Master with the **Furnace Heart** and the **Blood of Iron** abandoned his wealth, influence, and position in the Imperial Capital.

He moved the entire Baskerville family to the frontier.

Publicly, the reason was simple.

Exterminate the monsters and hostile tribes of the Depths.

Expand the Empire's territory.

Hugo threw away luxury and political comfort and buried himself in work.

The cold weapons in his hands took countless enemy heads.

But one man's strength had limits.

If he wanted to destroy as many threats in the Depths as possible, he needed more warriors who carried his blood and martial talent.

So Hugo entered one political marriage after another.

He took more wives.

He fathered more children.

The Emperor gave full support to the marquis who had voluntarily abandoned the center and gone to the frontier.

Restrictions on private troops were lifted.

Surveillance was minimal.

Taxes were waived.

Funds and military support arrived under every possible justification.

Hugo poured all of it into the frontier.

Decades passed.

A great house rose far from the center of the Empire—

yet no one could ignore it.

That was how the **Ironblood Sword House, Baskerville**, became what it was.

Vikir nodded slowly.

*…So that's why something felt strange when he agreed to join hands with the Morgue Clan.*

He remembered something Barrymore had once told him in confidence.

Hugo had agreed to the joint campaign at Red Salt Castle after hearing that a girl connected to Morgue had been taken by the Rokoko Clan.

Had that reminded him of Penelope?

Vikir remembered Camus grieving after learning that Rosie—the younger girl she had regarded as family—had been taken by the Rokoko and killed.

Barrymore had once reflected on Hugo's past.

*My lord became the cold man he is today after that incident.*

*If Lady Penelope had still been alive, perhaps he would never have become this way…*

Vikir had never known what Hugo was like before Penelope vanished.

That had happened before he was born.

But across two lives, Vikir knew the Hugo who came afterward very well.

A ruthless warrior of iron and blood.

A man who seemed to care for nothing except the Empire's glory and the restoration of his house.

Seeing that same man visibly suffer over an old love felt profoundly strange.

“…Yes.”

Even Hugo's voice trembled.

His eyes had gone wet.

Vikir nearly laughed aloud from sheer disbelief.

But he did not let the sight lower his guard.

This was still the man who, after losing his eldest daughter, sent the sons born afterward into war against the tribes and monsters of the frontier one after another.

If Hugo learned that his granddaughter carried tribal blood, Vikir still could not rule out a violent reaction.

So he awakened Beelzebub.

If matters went wrong, he was prepared to draw.

“First, thank you for answering.”

“…”

Hugo wiped the moisture from his eyes almost instantly and glared at him.

“Why are you asking me this?”

His expression hardened.

“If this is only to satisfy some petty curiosity of yours—”

Vikir raised one hand and cut him off.

Then he asked the real question.

“What would you do if the bloodline of your first wife's daughter—Penelope La Baskerville—still continued?”

“…What?”

Hugo frowned.

He looked as if Vikir had just said something absurd.

But Vikir did not lie without reason.

And as Hugo slowly remembered that fact—

Thud.

Vikir reached into his pocket and placed an object on the desk.

Hugo's eyes widened.

“…That—what is that?!”

A pendant.

A keepsake recovered from a Rokoko village that had nearly been destroyed by the Red Death.

Inside were three faded portraits.

Hugo and Roxana in their youth.

And Penelope as a little girl.

“I made this!”

Hugo's voice cracked.

“I made it myself in a workshop. I gave it to Roxana, and later—later it was around Penelope's neck!”

His hand trembled violently as he reached for the pendant.

The shaking was so severe that his fingers missed it.

Clatter.

The pendant slipped from the desk.

Vikir moved quickly and caught it before it struck the floor.

Hugo shouted as if he had lost his reason.

“Give it back!”

His hands clawed at empty air.

“Give it back to me!”

“Calm down, Father.”

Vikir obediently returned it.

“…Ah.”

Hugo held the pendant with both hands.

The portrait inside had faded with age.

The metal lid bore wear that looked like the result of being touched again and again for years.

Penelope.

His lost first child.

Where had she gone?

Had she lived?

If she was dead, where were her remains?

What had happened to her before the end?

What had she thought?

How lonely had she been?

How helpless?

How afraid?

Had she resented the father who never came to save her?

Had she died believing he had forgotten her?

Those questions had followed Hugo for decades.

He had always been afraid that his daughter hated him at the end.

A father who had never spoken warmly.

A father who had always been strict.

A father who had failed to find her.

A father who had never sent her so much as a word.

And the longer that fear lasted—

the harsher Hugo became toward himself.

He stopped giving affection freely to any of his children.

If they grew up without loving him, that was fine.

It would be easier.

Whenever he imagined Penelope suffering somewhere beyond his reach, he could think of little else.

And when he looked at sons who shared his face and his temperament, self-loathing followed.

But now—

he was holding the pendant.

A pendant worn smooth from being touched so many times.

The thought that Penelope might have clutched it again and again—

even near the end—

seemed to melt something that had been frozen inside Hugo for decades.

His head lowered.

A single heavy tear fell onto the cold stone floor.

Then Vikir spoke.

“Penelope had a daughter.”

“?”

Perhaps Vikir's voice was too dry.

Too matter-of-fact.

Hugo stared blankly.

“??”

He still did not seem to understand.

“???”

So Vikir said it again, more plainly.

“You have a granddaughter.”

For a heartbeat, Hugo did not move.

Then—

“!”

His eyes widened farther than Vikir had ever seen them.
