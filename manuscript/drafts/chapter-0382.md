# Chapter 382: Kennel (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/382.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 380 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Decarabia, Garam Nord, Picaresque Mask, Camus, BDISSEM, Flubber, Nouvelle Vague, and Gulper Eel / Elevator Eel.

A low-ranking guard with no visible name or unit.

That was Vikir now.

Nobody inside Nouvelle Vague knew the identity or purpose of the hound moving through its darkness.

*Maybe handing the Picaresque Mask to Camus was premature.*

*Keeping it a little longer would have been convenient.*

Vikir moved along the ceiling.

A smaller body would have made avoiding patrols easier.

For a moment—

he considered how useful puppy form would be.

Then rejected the thought.

Nouvelle Vague had no dogs.

No cats either.

A stray puppy wandering the corridors would be more suspicious—

not less.

Some guards did keep pets.

Crabs.

Lobsters.

Octopuses.

Occasionally gulls—

water cats—

or manatees.

*If people keep pets down here—

of course there has to be an aquarium.*

This was the deep sea.

Once Vikir confirmed the corridor was empty—

he dropped from the ceiling.

Level Five.

The first major destination in his escape plan.

Before him—

a sea.

The enormous plaza was divided into circular pools.

Narrow, steep passages ran between them.

An aquarium.

A breeding facility.

The only true aquarium inside Nouvelle Vague.

“Let’s see what they raise here.”

Vikir took a thick fishing line from a pile in one corner.

The hook attached to it—

larger than the anchor of an ordinary ship.

He baited it with salted cod.

Then threw it into the pool.

Splash.

The bait sank.

A shadow approached.

Long.

Enormous.

Then—

SNAP.

The line pulled tight.

Vikir gripped it.

Yanked.

WHOOSH!

The water bulged upward.

A giant eel came out on the hook.

“…A Gulper Eel.”

Its shape was bizarre.

Huge head.

Huge belly.

Tiny, thin tail.

Its mouth consumed almost the entire head—

wide enough to resemble the gate of a cathedral.

Grotesque.

But not demonic.

A strange deep-sea species native to the ocean.

One-bite eel.

Gulping eel.

Elevator eel.

It had many names.

[GRRROOOO—!]

The creature thrashed the instant it left the water.

Its gigantic mouth opened.

Rows of hook-shaped teeth lined the gums.

Barbs filled the spaces behind them.

A bite built not to release prey.

“…Annoying.”

SLAP.

One blow from Vikir shattered most of its teeth.

The eel went limp.

Tears welled in its eyes as Vikir dragged it fully onto land.

“There are quite a few.”

He looked across the pools.

Countless Gulper Eels circled through the water.

The one he caught happened to be among the largest.

Nouvelle Vague bred them deliberately.

Their bodies were enormous—

but their fins and tails were small.

They were poor swimmers.

Instead—

they floated with deep currents.

Most of the body was water.

What remained was bone and skin.

Little usable meat.

Strong fishy odor.

Bad food.

Ugly pets.

So why raise them?

“Because they’re the only elevator back to the surface.”

Vikir looked at the eel panting before him.

Coming **down** to Nouvelle Vague was simple.

Sail far into hostile waters.

At midnight—

the Maelstrom opened.

Put a prisoner into an Orichalcum-alloy coffin.

Drop it.

The coffin followed the descending current until it reached Nouvelle Vague—

ten thousand meters below the surface.

Returning upward—

was different.

That required these eels.

Huge mouth.

Larger stomach.

Enough room for several riders.

Soft flesh.

Extremely tough skin.

A body capable of surviving crushing deep-sea pressure.

When someone had legitimate reason to return to the surface—

an eel of the right size was selected.

Its mouth forced open.

The rider entered the stomach.

Then the eel was released into a specific rising current.

It could barely swim—

but it did not need to.

The current carried it slowly upward.

Hence—

**Elevator Eel**.

“…So if I cross that membrane—

the outside is right there.”

At the end of the breeding pools—

the corridor looked directly into black deep sea.

Only a transparent mucus wall separated inside from outside.

A membrane like an enormous soap bubble.

Nouvelle Vague guards called it **Flubber**.

BDISSEM.

Flubber.

The two mysterious substances keeping the prison alive.

BDISSEM restrained mana and physical strength.

Flubber kept the ocean outside.

Neither substance had been properly identified.

Sticky.

Vikir studied the translucent mucus coating the windows—

and, in truth, much of Nouvelle Vague’s outer wall.

Flubber was part of the prison’s security.

But it was far weaker than BDISSEM.

Now that Vikir had escaped his restraints and regained mana—

breaking through Flubber would not be difficult.

He grabbed the Gulper Eel’s tail.

Dragged it toward the membrane.

The eel resisted.

That did not matter.

Its body was slick enough to fit through narrow openings.

And without swimming ability—

it would simply follow current once outside.

“This one is enormous.”

“Was it raised for a giant?”

The eel looked almost like a living transport weapon.

Most eels in the pools were much smaller.

Only a handful approached this size.

Vikir looped reins around its tongue—

then extended control lines along the body toward the fins.

It could not truly swim.

But with the fins—

its direction could be influenced.

Then—

THUMP.

Vikir kicked it through the membrane.

SPLASH!

Flubber tore.

The eel disappeared into the black ocean.

Its stomach—

empty.

Vikir caught more.

Three medium eels large enough to hold one person each.

Two larger than the first.

He fitted each with reins.

Then pushed each one outside.

The Flubber membrane healed rapidly after every tear.

No continuous flood followed.

After the sixth eel vanished into the sea—

Decarabia finally asked:

[Human.]

[Why are you releasing them?]

[We are not riding any of them.]

“Distraction.”

“Recovering six escaped eels will consume manpower and time.”

[Ah.]

[Impressive.]

“An escape plan should always include distractions.”

“Especially this one.”

Decarabia paused.

[Then are we leaving on the seventh eel?]

[…Wait.]

[Why did you only catch six?]

[Shouldn’t we prepare one more?]

Vikir answered simply.

“I’m not leaving.”

[…What?]

Decarabia sounded genuinely confused.

[Then why release the eels at all?]

“I already told you.”

“Distraction.”

Vikir smiled faintly.

“Riding one comes later.”

“The escape is not at its final stage yet.”

Leaving now would be stupid.

The rising current was slow.

Even with six decoys—

guards would eventually catch him.

He had no proper water supply.

No reliable food for an unknown-length trip.

A larger deep-sea monster might eat the eel.

Currents might trap it.

Reefs might tear it apart.

Vikir intended to eliminate uncertainty first.

Then—

“What’s this?”

“An escaped prisoner?”

A strange voice came from behind him.

“…!”

Vikir sensed danger instantly.

He erased the artificial burn scars from his face—

returning to his own appearance.

Footsteps approached.

“I thought you were a guard because of the uniform.”

“Apparently not.”

The speaker sounded interested.

“Hm.”

“So this is the face of the person who broke my restraints.”

Green hair.

Lazy eyes.

A body that looked like a young teenage girl.

But the layers of familiar restraints around wrists and ankles—

told another story.

She wore a guard uniform.

And on her chest—

one bright star.

A brigadier general.

Higher than colonel.

Vikir narrowed his eyes.

At last—

one of the oldest mysteries of Nouvelle Vague made sense.

“…So.”

“You’re the one who made them.”
