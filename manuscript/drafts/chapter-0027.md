# Chapter 27: The Graduate (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/027.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 27 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English forms for Staffordshire Baskerville, Pit Bull Knights, Underdog City, Deputy Magistrate, Colosseo Academy, and established Baskerville terminology follow current project/Fandom evidence.

It had been a long time since Vikir last entered Hugo's study.

The room still carried the same cold, bloody atmosphere.

Dust lay thick over old books. Acrid smoke hung in the air beside the smell of cold coffee and a faint trace of blood.

Like a seasoned hound, Vikir read the mood through scent alone.

Then the middle-aged man seated behind the desk turned away from the window and faced him.

Hugo Le Baskerville.

Patriarch of the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan.

After so many years, his face looked older than the Hugo Vikir remembered from before his regression.

Then Vikir noticed a relatively fresh scar running across the bridge of Hugo's nose.

The scar had existed in Vikir's previous life too, but he had never known how Hugo had gotten it.

That made him curious.

“Greetings.”

“Mm.”

Hugo answered briefly and nodded.

Vikir continued in the same calm tone.

When he asked where the scar had come from, Hugo dismissed it as nothing worth discussing.

“I took an arrow while subjugating barbarians in Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.”

Vikir was mildly surprised.

Someone had managed to wound Hugo Baskerville with an arrow.

A Sword Master of Hugo's level would never be struck by a random shot.

Someone had deliberately aimed at him.

Hugo gave a short laugh.

“There was a woman who was very good with a bow.”

“Did you catch her?”

“No. But I carved the same mark into that fox-like woman's face.”

A swordsman of Hugo's caliber could project aura from the tip of his sword even against an enemy at long range.

Hugo rubbed the scar crossing his nose as though it still ached faintly.

After a moment of silence, he spoke again.

“Anyway. Congratulations on completing your training.”

“Thank you.”

“It gets harder from here.”

Vikir nodded.

Just as Staffordshire Baskerville had told them, the real trial was only beginning.

Three and a half years had gone into basic education—combat theory, physical conditioning, and developing the instincts needed to read an enemy.

Another three and a half years had gone into practical training—fighting monsters and people, wilderness survival, and real combat fundamentals.

But none of that could fully prepare a hound for the depths of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

The frontier overturned every comfortable rule of survival they had learned.

It was hell.

Either the blood you were born with carried you through, or you fought desperately enough to survive on your own strength.

‘The next twenty-one months should have been spent opening up the frontier.’

Vikir recalled the path he had taken before regression.

At this point in his first life, he had become an apprentice knight of the Pit Bull Knights and been sent into Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

For twenty-one months, he had crossed the line between life and death again and again.

He had been thrown directly into battle like a disposable hound.

...But this time, Hugo gave him an unexpected order.

“Take a post as a lower councilor.”

Vikir had not expected that.

Hugo was placing him on the civil-administrative track.

Of course, in a clan where nearly everyone was a trained warrior, being assigned to administration did not mean abandoning combat.

Among Baskerville youths, those with exceptional talent and strength were sometimes given an administrative posting midway through their development before later returning to military duty.

Hugo's eldest and second sons had followed the same path.

“A man must be strong.”

Hugo's voice remained flat.

“A sword is power. Authority is power too.”

Vikir nodded.

While expendable hounds died and accumulated battlefield experience, the elites remained within the clan or moved through outside institutions, learning politics and administration.

Then, when those elites eventually entered real combat at an older age, the expendable hounds who had survived countless battles became their subordinates.

The elites started later.

But they commanded those hardened hounds like dogs, piled up achievements through them, and combined that strength with political and administrative experience.

That was how they grew into something even larger.

‘...And only the elites are allowed to learn the Fifth Fang and beyond.’

That was the real reason expendable hounds were denied the higher Baskerville sword techniques.

It ensured that direct-line elites entering combat later could still catch up quickly with the disposable hounds who had spent years surviving in the field.

It was no different from a ruling class refusing to teach common people how to read.

“I will obey your order.”

Vikir lowered his head respectfully.

He had originally intended to move farther away from Hugo.

Instead, he had somehow ended up even closer.

But his true strength was still hidden well enough that this was not necessarily a problem.

If anything, gaining access to the center of Baskerville power offered advantages of its own.

Hugo briefly explained the position.

“A lower councilor assists with local administration throughout Baskerville territory. Executive work, legislation, judicial matters, development projects, disputes—everything large and small that keeps the territory functioning.”

A lower councilor who produced enough results could eventually rise to the upper council and handle matters beyond the clan's internal territories.

That part did not interest Vikir very much yet.

Creak—

Hugo opened a drawer and took out several documents.

John Barrymore immediately stepped beside him with the seal and ink pad.

“You'll serve as Deputy Magistrate of Underdog City.”

Underdog City.

Vikir knew it well.

It was the most prosperous settlement in the Baskerville Clan's vast territory.

Calling it a town was almost misleading.

It was a true metropolis.

The Magistrate governed the city's legislative and judicial affairs.

A Deputy Magistrate assisted that office across a wide range of administrative duties.

Vikir looked down at the appointment document.

“So I'll be making and enforcing laws.”

“Correct. At the moment, both the Magistrate's seat and the Deputy Magistrate positions are vacant.”

There was a reason an entire administrative structure had been left empty at once.

Underdog City's previous Magistrate had been Hugo's second son.

Several relatives by marriage who had been granted the Baskerville surname had served beneath him as deputy magistrates.

But Hugo's second son had since entered a long period of secluded training.

The deputies left behind had then been removed together after being implicated in bribery.

Hugo intended to hand the authority and obligations of those vacant offices to Vikir.

“It's a city without a master. Can you handle it?”

Vikir nodded.

Deputy Magistrates were normally around thirty years old.

Even rare prodigies usually did not receive such a post until their early twenties.

Those young elites often went on to hold major positions representing the clan.

‘...For example, entering the Academy.’

After four years at the Academy, an elite could return and move directly into a Magistrate's seat.

It was an elite course reserved for the elite.

Hugo's appointment of Vikir was extraordinary even by those standards.

“You seem to be giving me too much authority.”

“And an equally heavy responsibility.”

A dry smile touched Hugo's mouth.

“Your achievements are at least ten years ahead of the children this clan calls geniuses. You will be treated accordingly.”

The treatment included both privileges and obligations.

Greater authority meant heavier responsibility.

Seen from the other direction, heavier responsibility also meant greater authority.

“I will do my best not to disappoint you, Patriarch.”

Hugo nodded silently.

A moment later—

THUMP!

His seal came down on the document.

Just like that, fifteen-year-old Vikir became Deputy Magistrate—and in practice the acting ruler—of Underdog City, a metropolis of five hundred thousand people.

‘This is useful.’

Vikir considered the possibilities.

If he used the authority of Deputy Magistrate properly, he could strengthen himself far more safely than if he were thrown directly into the frontier.

His hidden fangs and claws would grow sharper still.

After a moment, Vikir spoke again.

“Patriarch.”

“...?”

Hugo looked up.

The faint warmth that had cooled after the appointment was completed returned to his eyes.

Vikir noticed it immediately.

“I'd like to make a request concerning the ruby mine dispute with the Morgue Clan.”

Seven years earlier, when he was eight, Vikir had dealt boldly with the Morgue delegation and left Hugo deeply satisfied.

Hugo had promised him a reward then.

Vikir had never specified what he wanted.

Now he was finally calling in that promise.

“...Could you lend it to me once?”

Hugo hesitated for a moment.

“Mm. Right. I did promise you a reward back then. I told you to ask when there was something you wanted.”

“If it's difficult, you don't need to grant it.”

One of Hugo's thick eyebrows shifted.

“Difficult?”

He nodded once, took out another document, and pressed the Patriarch's seal onto it.

“I trust you won't do anything foolish. Son.”

No matter how many times Hugo called him that, Vikir still could not get used to it.

More surprising was how readily Hugo had granted the request itself.

‘Before regression, I couldn't even have imagined asking him for something like this.’

Things were progressing more smoothly than expected.

◆◆◆

Vikir left the Patriarch's chamber and walked down the long corridor.

John Barrymore followed behind him with a gentle smile.

“Young master.”

“Mm.”

“The Patriarch cares for you very deeply.”

Vikir did not answer.

Barrymore continued anyway.

“Instead of sending you straight into the field, he has given you authority equivalent to a city's ruler. Isn't this training for leadership? It's clear that the Patriarch intends to use your talents in a major way.”

If Hugo had wanted nothing more than another disposable hound, Vikir would have been sent immediately to the frontier to be sharpened through battle.

While expendable hounds matured at the edge of death, future masters remained somewhere safe, arming themselves with thought and knowledge.

Then those masters used that foundation to leap toward heights ordinary hounds could never reach.

In the end, even if someone born below awakened to their situation, the structure was designed to keep them from surpassing those born above—whether through administration or through force.

Vikir understood that truth more deeply than anyone.

It left a bitter taste.

“That's assuming I do my job as Deputy Magistrate properly.”

“You will, young master. If you produce even a respectable result there, I'm certain you'll be among the next entrants to the Academy.”

Barrymore had realized that Vikir was interested in the Academy.

He simply had no idea why.

Meanwhile, Vikir's mind moved through the policies, reforms, and eccentric methods of famous officials he had seen or heard about in his previous life.

The appointment document bearing Hugo Le Baskerville's seal rested securely inside a pouch hidden beneath Vikir's clothes.

‘I need results as quickly as possible.’

There were countless ways to gain recognition and rise further in a short period of time.

Vikir chose the fastest and most reliable of them.
