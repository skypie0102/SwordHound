# Chapter 105: The Returned Hero (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from source/chinese/chapters/105.txt — 6154ca5fc0ee9cc3d67f0c1c6ca00b62b9493dab. Recovered English Chapter 104 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls Red Salt Castle, Morgue, Baskerville, Adolf Morgue, the Depths, and Vikir Van Baskerville. Chinese controls the hero legend and deliberately exaggerated retellings.

Red Salt Castle.

A massive man-made fortress built by the mages of Morgue, surrounded by high earthen walls and watchtowers.

Blood-red ruby veins ran through the ever-expanding mines around it.

On a dark, rainy night, Vikir climbed over the enormous wall and slipped inside.

Guards paced beneath jutting iron frameworks and uneven shelves of rock.

Vikir melted into the shadows with his usual soundless movements, crossed the wall, and slid down an iron support.

Before long, the interior of Red Salt Castle opened before him.

Rain dampened the streets. Faint light leaked from the narrow alleys.

Vikir headed into one of them in search of an inn where he could spend the night out of the cold drizzle.

Then—

“…?”

Something rose above the buildings in the distance.

Even through darkness and rain, the statue was enormous and imposing.

A human figure faced west, apparently cast entirely in gold.

Golden light shone from its base, illuminating the surrounding square.

The statue’s face, however, remained buried in darkness.

“…Why would they put something like that there?”

Vikir tilted his head.

There had been no such statue when he left the fortress two years ago.

Morgue administered this place, but the land ultimately belonged to Baskerville. A monument this conspicuous should have required agreement between both houses.

Who could possibly merit it?

And who was important enough to be rendered from head to toe in gold?

Vikir moved closer.

Just as he was about to identify the person the statue honored—

“Hey! You there, vagrant! Get away from it!”

A guard stationed beside the monument waved him off.

Apparently, enough rats had tried to gnaw gold from the statue that the guards had become unusually sensitive.

“This monument was raised by Morgue in honor of the hero who defended Red Salt Castle! Don’t come any closer. Look from a distance!”

Vikir backed away.

He had no reason to quarrel with a guard. Revealing his identity here would only create trouble.

*Even if everyone has forgotten me after two years, there’s no reason to make a scene.*

He was not curious enough to force his way closer.

Vikir turned into an alley and resumed his search for an inn.

He wanted food, a dry room, and sleep.

◆◆◆

Before long, Vikir stepped into a tavern.

Rainwater covered the floor, muddied by footprints streaked with dirt and coal dust.

The interior was stiflingly warm compared with the cold wind outside. Moisture briefly fogged Vikir’s vision.

Roasting meat, simmering fish soup, clattering mugs, shouting, and laughter filled the room.

Vikir found an empty seat.

A moment later, a cup of hot rum and three skewers of salted turkey landed in front of him.

The server turned away without a word.

Vikir raised a hand.

“I didn’t order these…”

“Those are the basic drink and snacks.”

The answer came from a bearded man who had somehow taken the seat beside him.

Vikir lifted his hooded head and looked over.

The man squinted at him.

“We saw each other in the alley earlier, didn’t we?”

Only then did Vikir recognize him.

He was the guard who had shouted at him beside the statue.

“Ha ha ha! Sorry about that. It was my job. You get jumpy when you spend your shift guarding something like that.”

Laughing, he slapped Vikir on the shoulder.

Perhaps because Vikir looked like a boy in his late teens, the guard had already relaxed.

Vikir merely nodded.

“…I understand.”

Apparently satisfied, the guard raised a hand for the server.

Then he glanced through the window toward the distant golden statue.

“You should understand. That statue commemorates *that man*.”

“That man?”

The guard brightened at once.

“That’s right. The hero who saved Red Salt Castle two years ago. We survived the great barbarian attack because of him. Morgue’s mages and Baskerville’s swordsmen owe him their lives too.”

“…?”

Vikir tilted his head.

This was a story he had never heard.

*A hero like that existed?*

Two years ago meant just before he disappeared.

There had been a massive barbarian attack?

And someone powerful enough to save both Morgue and Baskerville?

The more Vikir listened, the less familiar the story became.

While he searched his memory for some forgotten hero—

“Hey! Get my young friend a drink already! Damn it, did you stew a turtle’s legs before walking over here?”

The guard caught the surly server by the back of the neck and ordered salted pork and a bottle of rum for himself.

Then he turned to Vikir.

“What do you want, kid? My treat, since I startled you earlier.”

“Then I’ll have rum too—”

“Ha ha ha! You sound like a child. What rum? Bring him a cup of hot milk!”

The guard shooed the server toward the kitchen.

Several men at the next table, all wearing the same uniform, burst out laughing.

“Hey! Telling the story of *that man* again?”

“This guy sees one outsider and immediately starts spreading the legend.”

“Can you blame him? We’re alive because of that man.”

“Exactly. You can’t praise his achievements enough.”

The guards grew louder.

Vikir stayed silent beneath his hood.

The bearded guard leaned toward him.

“You’re from out of town, right? Even so, you should know his name. His fame has spread through Underdog City below us, across Baskerville territory, even as far as the imperial capital!”

“…”

“What, too shocked to speak? Hard to believe a hero from a frontier town like this could shake the whole Empire?”

“…”

“It sounds ridiculous, but it’s true. A cousin of mine went to the capital not long ago. She said the nobles in high society won’t stop talking about him.”

The guard drained a cup of rum. His cheeks reddened.

“Two years have passed, but I still remember that night! The excitement. The terror. The chills!”

“…I see. So a great hero really did appear two years ago.”

Vikir shook his head.

The room erupted.

The guards at the rear table lifted beer, vodka, and whiskey.

“Ha ha ha! You don’t know *that man*? Are you a spy? Even an idiot should have heard his name once!”

“That’s right! That day he pursued the barbarians when nobody else dared follow them!”

“He led the Morgue-Baskerville allied force straight into the dark Depths! At night! Into enemy territory and black floodlands! It was practically suicide, but he did it! Even the invincible Archmage Adolf was only a supporting act beside him!”

“That’s nothing! He slaughtered thousands of terrifying barbarians single-handedly and rescued Morgue’s delicate young lady! She had been poisoned too, and she survived because of him!”

“Bah! If you weren’t there, keep quiet! Did any of you see his courage when that gigantic monster appeared? He fought it alone so the allied force could retreat safely. I saw his heroic sacrifice with my own eyes!”

…?

Something began to feel strange.

A hero who single-handedly repelled countless tribesmen.

A knight who charged deep into enemy territory to rescue Morgue’s young lady.

A sacred sacrifice who stood against a gigantic monster and bought the allied army time to escape.

“The great hero who fought beside us only two years ago! His name was—!”

The guard sprang to his feet and lifted a lantern toward the window.

At that moment the clouds parted.

Moonlight reached the face of the golden statue.

The guards stared at the solemn figure and shouted.

“Vi!”

“Ki!”

Every eye in the tavern turned toward Vikir.

Judging from their expressions, failing to recognize the name now might actually get him pelted with stones.

Vikir finally answered.

“…r?”

The guards roared in triumph.

“See! You know him too! Lord Vikir Van Baskerville!”

The enormous golden statue standing in the central square of Red Salt Castle—

was Vikir himself.
