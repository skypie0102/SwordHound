# Chapter 105: The Returned Hero (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/105.txt` — `6154ca5fc0ee9cc3d67f0c1c6ca00b62b9493dab`. Recovered English Chapter 104 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Red Salt Castle**, **Morgue**, **Baskerville**, **Adolf Morgue**, **Madam Eight-Legs**, and **Vikir Van Baskerville**. The guards' hero story is intentionally exaggerated folklore, not narrator-certified fact.

**Red Salt Castle.**

A massive artificial fortress built by Morgue mages.

High earthen walls.

Watchtowers.

Ruby mines cutting red veins through the ground.

Vikir entered under cover of rain and darkness.

Silent Heel erased his steps.

He scaled the wall.

Dropped along iron supports.

Then moved through the inner streets without drawing attention.

He intended to find a cheap room and sleep.

That was all.

Then he saw the statue.

Huge.

Golden.

Facing west toward the floodlands.

Its lower half glowed beneath lamps.

Its face disappeared into shadow.

Vikir stopped.

“That wasn't here two years ago.”

Red Salt Castle belonged administratively to Morgue.

The larger territorial claim still involved Baskerville.

A monument this large should have required agreement between both houses.

Who deserved that?

Vikir moved closer.

A guard waved him away.

“Hey! Get away from there!”

Vikir stopped.

The guard pointed toward the statue.

“That is a monument erected by Morgue to honor the hero who saved Red Salt Castle.”

He narrowed his eyes.

“Look from a distance. Don't touch it.”

Vikir backed off.

There was no reason to start trouble.

If anyone recognized him—

the night would become exhausting.

He entered a nearby alley and found a tavern.

◆◆◆

Heat met him at the door.

Wet floorboards.

Mud.

Coal dust.

Roasting meat.

Fish soup.

Beer.

Laughter.

Vikir sat alone.

Hot rum and salted turkey skewers appeared in front of him.

“I didn't order—”

“Basic service.”

A bearded man sat beside him.

Vikir recognized him.

The statue guard.

The man laughed.

“Sorry about earlier. Guard duty makes a man suspicious.”

He slapped Vikir's shoulder.

The hood and Vikir's young appearance had lowered his caution.

Vikir nodded.

“I understand.”

The guard called for more food.

Then looked through the window toward the golden statue.

“You should understand. We have to protect *his* monument.”

“His?”

The guard brightened immediately.

“The hero who saved Red Salt Castle two years ago.”

Vikir tilted his head.

“Saved it?”

“From the great barbarian attack.”

The man leaned closer.

“Morgue. Baskerville. Everyone here survived because of him.”

Vikir searched his memory.

Nothing.

A great hero.

Two years ago.

A massive tribal raid.

Someone powerful enough to save both houses.

Had Vikir forgotten someone?

The guard ordered salted pork and more rum.

Then offered Vikir a drink.

“Rum.”

The man laughed.

“With that voice? Warm milk.”

The nearby guards roared.

Then conversation returned to the unknown hero.

“He chased the raiders when no one else dared.”

“He led Morgue and Baskerville into the floodlands at night.”

“Even Adolf Morgue was just support beside him!”

“He slaughtered thousands of savages!”

“He rescued Morgue's young lady after she was poisoned!”

“He stood alone against a giant monster so the alliance could escape!”

Vikir listened.

Slowly—

something began to feel wrong.

These were not separate stories.

They were distortions of one night Vikir remembered very well.

The pursuit.

Camus.

Madam Eight-Legs.

The retreat.

The heroic stranger sounded increasingly familiar.

The bearded guard stood.

Lantern raised toward the window.

“That great hero who fought beside us two years ago!”

Moonlight finally reached the statue's face.

The guards shouted.

“Vi!”

“Ki!”

Every head turned toward Vikir.

They waited.

Vikir stared at them.

Then at the statue.

Then back.

“……R?”

The tavern erupted.

“Exactly!”

“Vikir Van Baskerville!”

The enormous golden statue in the center of Red Salt Castle—

was him.
