By the next morning—

Vikir’s final words from the previous night covered every front page.

**Night Hound Arrested.**

**The identity of Night Hound revealed.**

**The criminal who terrorized the Imperial Capital was a first-year Colosseo student.**

And beneath those headlines—

one sentence appeared again and again.

**“I am Night Hound.”**

The papers identified him as Vikir of House Baskerville.

Then listed his charges.

Murder within House Baskerville.

Flight from the family.

The suspected killings of Cardinal Humbert and Evangelist Quilt of Quovadis.

The killing of Bartolomeo Bourgeois.

And involvement in numerous assassinations and disappearances across the Empire.

Even that—

the papers claimed—

was only a partial list.

For years—

Night Hound had been presented to the public as a terrifying criminal.

A murderer.

A destabilizing force.

Now that the mask had been removed—

the place most violently affected was Colosseo Academy.

Every group of students talked about him.

“I can’t believe that monster was in our year.”

“He hid it so calmly.”

“That’s the frightening part.”

“But he saved people inside the Hell Tree.”

“Maybe that was coincidence.”

“Or maybe murderers just have two faces.”

“He was even in Ryukeion.”

“Did he write articles condemning himself?”

“I lived near his room.”

“I used to hear him sharpening a sword.”

“I worked with him in class once.”

“He always looked strange.”

The general mood—

was hostile.

That was not surprising.

The public image of Night Hound had been built for years.

But a few students disagreed.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Tudor clenched his jaw.

“Did everyone already forget the Hell Tree?”

“We’re alive because of him.”

“Ungrateful bastards.”

Sancho nodded.

Unlike Tudor—

he had already thought through what happened yesterday.

“Vikir cut us off first.”

“That was deliberate.”

“He wanted to throw away every remaining burden before surrendering.”

Pigi’s voice was low.

“He did it so none of this would spread to us.”

Bianca snorted.

“Of course he did.”

“Who did he think he was fooling?”

“Vikir and Sinclaire both.”

“Trying to hide what they really feel.”

Tudor gave her a sidelong look.

“You’re calling **him** immature?”

“You were crying and calling him a traitor.”

“I was not!”

Normally—

that argument would have lasted for hours.

This time—

it died almost immediately.

No one had energy.

Tudor looked exhausted.

“What can we actually do for him?”

Sancho crossed his arms.

“One thing is certain.”

“We can’t just sit here.”

Bianca nodded.

“The trial is starting soon.”

“Could we go as observers and protest?”

“No.”

“We’d only get thrown out.”

“We need something practical.”

The four fell into thought.

Then—

“We’ll help.”

A low voice came from behind them.

Several students approached.

Gordon.

Underdog.

Granola.

Highbro.

Middlebro.

Lowbro.

All of them—

people who had formed some connection to Vikir inside the Hell Tree.

Gordon spoke first.

“All of us survived because of him.”

“We owe him.”

Underdog lowered his head.

“I was able to repent because I met him.”

“If not—

I might have remained a demon’s toy forever.”

Granola clicked his tongue.

“I don’t even like Vikir that much.”

“But there’s someone I care about—

and she’s worried sick over him.”

The triplets answered in sequence.

“We protect our lord.”

“We protect him.”

“We protect him.”

They were not alone.

Then—

Dolores appeared.

Student Council President.

Head of Ryukeion.

And—

long before most people knew the truth—

one of Night Hound’s few secret supporters.

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca knew that much.

Their expressions brightened.

Dolores did not smile.

“The situation is bad.”

Everyone went quiet.

“Vikir cut every connection before surrendering.”

“That protected us.”

“But it also left him with no one publicly defending him.”

“He is carrying every accusation alone.”

She continued.

“House Baskerville has officially distanced itself.”

“They claim the murder and escape were the actions of an illegitimate son acting alone.”

“They also claim the red military horn was stolen.”

“The knights—

officially—

were mobilized to retrieve it.”

Cutting off the tail.

Baskerville had formally denied responsibility.

Dolores bit her lip.

“That means one of the strongest arguments for noble treatment is gone.”

“Vikir may be sentenced to death.”

“So before the jury decides—

we need to influence the Seven Families.”

The judge would be the Emperor—

or an Imperial representative.

The jury—

one representative from each of the Seven Great Families.

Dolores looked around.

Fortunately—

many of the students gathered here had extraordinary social positions.

She began assigning work.

“Tudor.”

“Speak to House Donquixote.”

“Bianca.”

“House Usher.”

“Sancho.”

“Take the truth north to the mercenary guilds.”

“Pigi.”

“You and I will use the newspaper.”

“I’ll also speak directly to the Pope of Quovadis.”

“Gordon.”

“Underdog.”

“Reach the noble faction, the powerful-house faction, the commoner faction, and the student council.”

“Highbro.”

“Middlebro.”

“Lowbro.”

“Gather every piece of information you can from Baskerville.”

“There may be something that helps reduce the sentence.”

Dolores lowered her head.

“Please.”

Everyone nodded.

“You don’t have to ask.”

“We want this too.”

“We’ll do everything we can.”

Dolores looked around at them.

Her eyes were wet.

For the first time since the arrest—

the pressure in her chest eased slightly.

Only slightly.

Still—

it felt like a step forward.

Then—

“What a childish plan.”

A mocking voice came from behind them.

A red-haired female student stood there—

smiling.

“Hello.”

“Vikir told me to come get acquainted with you.”

The name tag on her chest read:

**Camus Morgue.**

Dolores narrowed her eyes.

Camus came straight to the point.

“Convincing the jury to reduce Vikir’s sentence sounds nice.”

“But do you really think that’s what he wants?”

Dolores’s expression hardened.

“What do you mean?”

Camus tapped her temple.

“Think.”

Everyone sucked in a breath.

Speaking to Dolores like that—

was not something most people dared.

Dolores stayed calm.

“I thought this was the best thing I could do.”

“You disagree?”

“Completely.”

“What you’re planning isn’t the best option.”

“And it isn’t what Vikir wants.”

Dolores’s voice cooled.

“Then what **does** he want?”

“And what is your plan?”

Camus smiled.

Then the smile vanished.

“I used to be like you.”

“I didn’t understand him either.”

“I didn’t know what he wanted.”

“What he was preparing.”

“What he was waiting for.”

“What he was fighting.”

“But now—

I think I understand.”

Dolores stared.

Camus spoke quietly.

“Leave Vikir alone.”

“He isn’t a man who needs you to worry about him.”

Tudor frowned.

“How would you know?”

Camus looked disappointed.

“Don’t you?”

“He’s smarter than any of us.”

“And he’s carrying a mission none of us can fully understand.”

No one argued.

They had seen too much.

At Colosseo.

Inside the Hell Tree.

Every action Vikir took—

might have been calculated.

Not for days.

Not for months.

Possibly—

for his entire life.

Dolores spoke.

“So we do nothing?”

Camus shook her head.

“No.”

“We do what we **can** do.”

“And what is that?”

“Simple.”

“We do what Vikir has always been doing.”

“We stop it.”

“…Stop what?”

Camus reached inside her clothes.

Placed something on the table.

A bloodstained mask.

The Picaresque Mask.

One of Night Hound’s symbols.

Everyone stiffened.

Camus looked at them.

“The Gate that is about to open.”

“The **Gate of Destruction**.”
