# Chapter 153: The Drinking Game (2)

Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Dolores L. Quovadis, Sinclaire, Pigi, Tudor Donquixote, Bianca Fo Usher, Colosseo Academy, Magic Tower, and Varangian. Go terminology follows the source rather than corrupted recovered-English renderings: `阿达里` → **atari** and `万霸不听` → **manpae bulcheong**.

A white fortress rose across the board.

Dolores's stones formed strongholds throughout the field.

One fortress linked to another.

Territory expanded gradually—

carefully—

securely.

An amateur fifth-dan player.

Dolores dominated through defense so solid it seemed unbreakable—

paired with steady, measured attacks.

The board was slowly turning white.

At least—

that was how it should have gone.

“…Mm.”

A quiet groan escaped her.

The clean white fortress—

perfectly built—

had been under attack for some time.

Black kept appearing where it should not.

Vikir.

His black stones split into several groups—

then drove into the white territory from multiple directions.

They slipped through tiny openings.

Destroyed the interior.

Then escaped through a completely different gap.

Like black cavalry appearing and vanishing across a battlefield—

they refused to fight where Dolores expected.

Her white Paladins could not pin them down.

*This isn't second-dan strength.*

*He lied to me!?*

Dolores gathered what remained of her forces.

She reinforced the walls.

Linked her strongholds more tightly.

Strengthened the corners.

Thickened every defensive line.

Then—

black spears broke through again.

The white wall collapsed.

The territory behind it—

once secure—

became empty.

Then black.

*No.*

*If he gets inside again…!*

Dolores concentrated everything she had left.

A final defensive line.

A Maginot Line.

A position that—

if held—

would guarantee victory.

The condition was simple.

*If held.*

Vikir's black stones bared their fangs once more.

They pierced the white wall like awls.

Pushed deep inside.

Tore apart the territory Dolores had protected so carefully.

The white interior—

was covered by black scars.

The shape on the board became unmistakable.

A black hound—

jaws spread wide.

“Atari.”

Vikir had caught the Saintess's arm.

One more bite—

and her right arm would be severed.

Then the left arm.

The right leg.

The left leg.

The torso.

And finally—

the neck.

Sweat appeared on Dolores's forehead.

*What do I do?*

*How do I get out of this?*

No matter how long she stared—

she could not find a route out of the black beast's jaws.

Defeat was already approaching.

Dolores refused to accept it.

Go players were competitive by nature.

A single game could send them between heaven and hell again and again.

And tonight—

for the first time in a long time—

Dolores had found a genuine opponent.

Vikir looked at the board.

“You can also resign after thinking it through.”

A mental attack.

Dolores's brow twitched.

Vikir—

meanwhile—

was mildly surprised.

*…In this era, her skill is probably around fifth dan.*

Dolores was not weak.

Vikir was simply stronger than he had expected himself to be.

Before his regression—

during long periods of stalemate on the battlefield—

he had played Go with many soldiers.

One sergeant had been particularly good.

*What's my rank?*

*About first dan.*

*That was my rating before the war, anyway.*

The man had once told Vikir—

*At your level, maybe second or third dan.*

Vikir remembered that clearly.

The sergeant had considered himself first dan—

with Vikir a little below him.

But after the war against the Demon World began—

certification organizations disappeared.

So did tournaments.

Official rankings stopped meaning much.

That was why Vikir still thought of himself as roughly second dan.

If evaluated properly in the present era—

perhaps the result would be higher.

Finally—

click.

The last stone came down.

Dolores lowered her head.

“…I lost.”

There was no point counting territory.

The difference was too great.

The room erupted.

“Whoa!”

“I've never seen the Saintess lose at Go!”

“President Dolores was in the newspapers when she was younger, wasn't she?”

“As a Go prodigy!”

“Back when Colosseo still had a Go club, didn't she beat the Magic Tower and Varangian clubs practically by herself?”

“How did this happen?”

Because the loser was Dolores—

everyone chose their wording carefully.

With anyone else—

the room would already have been full of harsher phrases.

*Destroyed.*

*Crushed.*

*Torn apart.*

Vikir reached for the stones.

“Wait!”

Dolores grabbed both of his hands.

“One more.”

“This time—speed Go.”

All Go players hated losing.

Prodigies—

especially so.

And Dolores's competitive streak in this area was enormous.

Vikir accepted.

The rule was simple.

Three seconds per move.

No long calculations.

No extended pauses.

The second game would end quickly.

So they moved the board beside Dolores's bed—

away from the middle of the party.

The other students followed anyway.

Glasses in hand—

they formed a circle around the new battlefield.

Watching turned out to be entertaining.

“This is more fun than I expected.”

“Go always looked boring when my father played it.”

“I don't really understand what's happening.”

“Was the first game close?”

“I don't understand either…”

“But it definitely wasn't close.”

Black and white faced one another again.

Click.

Click.

Click.

Click.

Click.

Click.

Stones struck the board at frightening speed.

Then—

Vikir spoke.

“Manpae bulcheong.”

A ko so decisive—

there was no need to answer the opponent's threats.

Dolores stared at the board.

For one terrifying moment—

a thought entered her head.

*…Should I flip it?*

It was the most violent impulse Go had given her in over a decade.

But everyone was watching.

So Dolores forced the words out.

“…I lost.”

The spectators finally exhaled.

Sinclaire wiped sweat from her forehead.

“I thought I was good at Go…”

“But I can't understand Bro's moves at all.”

Pigi nodded.

“Vikir's style isn't anything like modern play.”

“No one plays like this now.”

“There aren't even proper patterns.”

“So how does it work?”

Dolores could not answer.

The more she replayed the game in her mind—

the stranger Vikir's style seemed.

The foundation of Go was territory.

Build your house.

Secure it.

Expand.

But Vikir seemed almost indifferent to building a home.

His games were chaotic battles.

Brawls.

He cared less about creating territory—

than destroying the opponent's.

Attack.

Break.

Invade.

Destroy.

Dolores had been forced to surrender beneath relentless pressure.

“…”

Vikir looked at her dejected face.

A faint, bitter smile crossed his own.

People who had never lived through war—

could not completely understand those who had.

In the Age of Destruction—

building a home had little meaning.

Maintaining a family settlement had little meaning.

At any moment—

demons could arrive.

A household could disappear overnight.

So people moved.

Struck first.

Destroyed enemy bases before the enemy could destroy theirs.

Survival favored motion—

and aggression.

That atmosphere—

that momentum—

those values—

had entered Vikir's Go.

Dolores and the others had no way to know that.

Tudor scratched his head.

“I don't understand Go.”

“But I can tell he plays like a mad dog.”

Bianca nodded.

“Mad-dog style.”

“…It has a certain charm.”

Dolores heard none of it.

*Who am I?*

*Where am I?*

*Where am I going?*

Everyone had at least one game they took pride in.

Until—

one day—

they met someone much stronger.

A single overwhelming defeat could turn an entire world upside down.

Dolores had been undefeated for so long—

that losing once was enough to shake everything.

And now—

she still had to take the penalty.

“…Ugh.”

She drank.

Then—

hic.

Dolores stiffened.

She had already consumed far too much of the “soda.”

Her stomach felt swollen.

She tried to suppress the next sound.

Hic!

“…What?”

Dolores turned toward the others.

Their faces were red.

They were smiling far too broadly.

Dolores grabbed one of the glass bottles.

Read the label.

Her eyes widened.

This was not harmless soda.

It contained plenty of alcohol.

“You tricked me…”

Hic!

Her face grew redder.

Heat spread through her body.

Sweat gathered.

Around the room—

empty bottles had begun to pile up.

Boys and girls alike were flushed.

The atmosphere was getting louder—

warmer—

less restrained.

Then—

thud.

Thud.

Thud.

Footsteps.

Outside the door.

The drunkenness vanished from Dolores's mind.

“The supervisor is coming!”

Everyone froze.

Then panic exploded.

“Hide the bottles!”

“The ceiling panel won't open!”

“The window is too narrow!”

“Where do we hide the boys!?”

“Under the beds!”

“No—inside the beds!”

“Under the blankets!”

“But boys and girls together—”

“Don't start worrying about that now!”

“Shut up!”

“Lights!”

“Turn off the lights!”

The room descended into chaos.

Then—

click.

A key turned in the lock.

Creak—

the door began to open.
