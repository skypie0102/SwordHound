# Chapter 91: The Ghosts of the Ancestors (2)

Established terminology controls **Death Knight**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Akwilla**, **Aiyen**, **Ah'Heman**, **Adonai**, **Tomb of the Brave**, and **Beelzebub**.

The dead apes moved.

Their bodies had been dead only a short time.

They were already decaying.

Skin darkened.

Flesh softened.

Bone emerged.

Living things were born from nature.

Then returned to it.

That was the ordinary law.

Undead broke that law.

A corpse should be reclaimed by the world.

A ghost that refused to release it forced nature to work harder.

Flesh rotted.

Organs failed.

Bone remained.

That was why ordinary undead decayed so quickly.

But some dead had possessed such power in life that even decay could not immediately erase them.

Those were different.

CRACK.

CRACK.

CRACK.

The graves opened.

Aiyen stared.

“No way…”

Figures climbed from stone tombs and earth mounds.

Some were skeletal.

Others still wore dried flesh.

Black-panther shrouds hung from their bodies.

Bows.

Spears.

Swords.

Axes.

These had once belonged to Ballak's greatest warriors.

Now they advanced together.

> **Death Knight**  
> **Danger Rating:** A–S  
> **Size:** Unknown  
> **Found at:** Unknown  
> **Alias:** ‘Death Knight’

Warriors who had reached extraordinary heights in life could sometimes become Death Knights after death.

Dark power chained soul and body together.

Most lost personality and reason.

What remained was hatred.

Rage.

Violence.

Rare intelligent Death Knights had existed in human history—

but only in a handful of recorded cases.

These Ballak ancestors wore no heavy armor.

Only burial shrouds.

They moved like wind.

All answering Ah'Heman's hatred.

Akwilla raised her voice.

“Everyone. Prepare for battle.”

Aiyen fired immediately.

Her arrow targeted Ah'Heman.

A Death Knight moved first.

Its own arrow struck hers aside.

Ah'Heman laughed behind the dead.

“Die! All of you!”

His body aged by the second.

He was spending his remaining life to control them.

The past faced the present.

Ancient Ballak against living Ballak.

Aiyen drew again.

“Time to show the old heroes what their descendants can do.”

Her silver-aura arrow screamed forward.

A Death Knight caught it barehanded.

CRACK.

The arrow stopped.

The hand holding it disintegrated.

Even ancient muscle memory could not fully compensate for decayed flesh.

Another Death Knight swung.

A black slash tore forward.

Aiyen reacted too late.

CLANG.

Vikir intercepted.

Then he threw white powder into the faces of two charging undead.

HISSSS.

Smoke rose.

Skin blackened.

The Death Knights recoiled.

“Careful.”

Vikir watched them closely.

“Every one of these was a monster in life.”

Aiyen grinned despite the situation.

“You're still the only man I trust with my back.”

Vikir gave her a flat look.

“In battle.”

She smiled.

“Obviously.”

The next attack erased the joke.

Aiyen shouted.

“What was that powder?”

“Salt.”

Vikir grabbed another handful from the mineral-heavy riverbank.

“Undead hate it.”

He threw.

The white crystals darkened on contact.

The Death Knight convulsed.

Vikir stepped in and drove Beelzebub through its temple.

Ballak warriors understood immediately.

“Salt!”

“Use salt!”

“It works!”

Most still hated striking their ancestors directly.

So they minimized close combat.

Salt first.

Only enough force afterward to let the dead rest again.

One by one, the Death Knights fell.

Akwilla stared at Ah'Heman.

“Our ancestors aren't toys for someone like you.”

Ah'Heman laughed.

“Then quality over quantity.”

He smeared his own blood across his face.

Complex symbols formed.

The mana around him changed.

This was no ordinary spell.

He was spending not only life—

but whatever came after it.

Dark power surged.

Some of the Death Knights collapsed as he withdrew energy from them.

Ah'Heman's eyes remained closed.

Then they opened.

“I found him.”

His voice trembled.

“I finally found him.”

The storm of black mana intensified.

“In the deepest place. Alone. Without a grave.”

Akwilla steadied herself.

“Enough.”

She drew.

Silver light flashed.

Her arrow crossed the darkness toward Ah'Heman's forehead.

Then stopped.

A hand had caught it.

Every Ballak warrior froze.

The man standing there was thin.

Ancient clothes.

Dry skin clinging to bone.

No lips.

Teeth exposed.

Tall.

Broad-framed despite death.

And strong enough to catch Akwilla's arrow with one hand.

No one recognized him.

Almost no one.

Akwilla did.

Her face changed.

She had grown up hearing stories of legendary chieftains.

Usually she thought she could have matched them.

Her own talent justified that arrogance.

There was only one exception.

The hero who had declared war on the Empire alone.

Forced it to yield.

Subjugated the surrounding tribes.

Led Ballak into its greatest age.

The greatest archer in recorded Ballak history.

**Divine Archer Adonai.**
