# Chapter 91: The Ghosts of the Ancestors (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/091.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 90 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Death Knight**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Akwilla**, **Aiyen**, **Ah'Heman**, **Adonai**, **Tomb of the Brave**, and **Beelzebub**.

Gurgle… crack…

The orangutan corpses continued moving.

They had not been dead long, yet their skin was already turning blue-green and rotting away.

White bone showed through ruptured flesh.

All living things were born from nature.

And, eventually, all living things returned to nature.

A body was not separate from the world.

It was part of it.

Birth from nature and return to nature were pieces of the same great law—

a kind of contract accepted by everything that belonged to the ecosystem.

But occasionally—

something refused to honor that contract.

The undead.

A life ended.

Its body should have returned to the world.

Instead, a lingering spirit occupied it and refused to leave.

It was like a tenant whose lease had expired but who still refused to vacate the house.

By the law of life, the body belonged back to nature.

The undead held it by force.

So nature acted to reclaim what was hers.

All things returned to dust.

To recover a corpse occupied by a spirit, nature accelerated the turning of that wheel.

Flesh decayed.

Organs stank and collapsed.

Muscle sloughed away.

Eventually only clattering bone remained.

That was why ordinary undead appeared rotten or skeletal.

But—

there were exceptions.

Some warriors had possessed such extraordinary power in life that even in death, the remnant occupying the body could resist nature's law for a time.

Their bodies decayed less completely.

Their strength remained terrifying.

They fought the world's attempt to reclaim them with the same power they had once used against living enemies.

Crack!

Crack!

Crack!

The things climbing out of the Tomb of the Brave were such beings.

“…No way.”

Aiyen's mouth fell open.

Stone tombs split.

Earthen graves opened.

Figures climbed out.

Some were nearly bare skeletons.

Others still carried dried skin and strips of ancient flesh.

Black-panther pelts that had once been burial shrouds fluttered around them.

Empty eye sockets stared from beneath those funeral robes.

These were warriors and leaders from Ballak's distant past.

They had been buried with the weapons they loved most.

Bows.

Spears.

Swords.

Axes.

Now those same weapons emerged with them as the dead marched in unison toward the living.

> **Death Knight**  
> **Danger Rating:** A–S  
> **Size:** Unknown  
> **Found at:** Unknown  
> **Alias:** ‘Death Knight’

A Death Knight was what could remain when a warrior who had reached extraordinary heights in life fell into undeath.

Soul and body became collateral for dark power.

That dark mana bound the corpse until muscle, blood vessels, and flesh finally ruptured under the strain.

Most Death Knights lost the personalities they had possessed while alive.

What remained was hollow hatred.

Blind rage.

Aggression without purpose.

Rare exceptions retained intelligence.

Human history recorded only seven such cases.

Normally, Death Knights compensated for their mindless aggression with thick, heavy armor.

The resurrected Ballak ancestors wore no armor at all.

Only their shrouds whipped in the black current as they approached with the speed of the wind.

They were answering Ah'Heman's madness.

Akwilla's voice cut through the clearing.

“Everyone! Prepare for battle!”

Aiyen was already moving before the last word left her mother's mouth.

She drew and fired straight at Ah'Heman, who was still struggling on the ground.

But a Death Knight sprinted forward at astonishing speed.

It fired first.

Fwoosh! Fwoosh! Fwoosh!

Arrows packed with tremendous force crossed the field and intercepted Aiyen's shot.

The dead began forming a wall around Ah'Heman.

“Hah… hahaha!”

Ah'Heman laughed from behind them.

“Die! All of you!”

His face was visibly changing.

He was aging by the second.

Controlling warriors of this level required more power than he possessed.

So he was burning his own life to make up the difference.

Soon skeleton-faced Death Knights carrying swords and spears stood directly before the living Ballak.

Past faced present.

Warriors of the old age confronted warriors of the new.

Aiyen raised her bow again.

“Then it's time to show the heroes of the past how frightening today's young people can be!”

She fired.

Fwoosh!

A silver aura wrapped the arrow.

A Death Knight reached out and caught it barehanded.

Crack!

The arrow stopped.

Its hand did not survive.

The fingers and palm that had seized the aura-charged shaft crumbled into powder.

The Death Knight tilted its head, almost puzzled.

Perhaps in life it could have caught such an arrow without difficulty.

Its instincts still remembered that strength.

Its decayed body did not.

Then—

BOOM!

A second Death Knight swung its sword.

A black slash tore across the clearing, cleaving everything in its path.

Aiyen had not expected it to move so quickly.

For one dangerous instant, she was late.

CLANG!

Someone stepped in and knocked the slash aside.

Vikir.

Then two more Death Knights rushed him.

Vikir opened one hand.

White powder scattered through the air.

It struck their faces.

HISSSSS.

Smoke rose with the sound of flesh scorching.

Both undead recoiled in visible pain.

“Be careful,” Vikir said. “Every one of these was a famous monster in life.”

Aiyen smiled at once.

She bent slightly, pushed her hips back, and lightly patted Vikir's thigh.

“As expected. You're the only man I can trust with my back.”

“…”

“Oh, obviously I mean guarding my rear in battle.”

She grinned at his reaction.

“Don't misunderstand.”

Even with Death Knights charging them, teasing Vikir still amused her.

But the next wave of attacks ended the joke.

The undead were getting sharper.

Faster.

More dangerous.

Aiyen's expression hardened.

“Vikir! What was that?”

“What?”

“That powder. What did you throw at them?”

“Salt.”

He reached toward the bank beside the waterfall.

“Undead are sensitive to it.”

There was no better symbol of purification than salt.

Along the bank outside the Illiad ground, natural salt lay in pale drifts like white sand.

Vikir grabbed another handful and threw it.

The crystals touched a Death Knight.

Crackle!

The white salt immediately darkened.

The undead convulsed and staggered backward.

That opening was enough.

Vikir stepped in.

Beelzebub punched through the Death Knight's temple.

The Ballak warriors saw it too.

“Salt!”

“Use the salt!”

“Vikir's right! It works!”

Until now, many of them had hesitated.

Even as undead, these were the bodies of their ancestors.

They did not want to hack them apart.

So they adapted.

Avoid direct clashes whenever possible.

Throw salt.

Use only as much close combat as was necessary to let the dead return to rest.

As Ah'Heman's last burst of life reached its peak—

the Death Knights began falling one by one.

Akwilla looked toward the dying shaman and sneered.

“Our ancestors won't be puppets for someone as insignificant as you.”

For a moment—

Ah'Heman only laughed.

“Hehehe…”

His voice was low and ugly.

“So quantity isn't enough.”

Blood ran across his face.

“Then I'll use quality.”

He smeared his own blood over his skin and drew a complicated ritual array.

The power gathering around him exceeded ordinary life force.

He was spending something beyond the limits of his body.

Even if the spell succeeded, his soul would suffer for an eternity after death.

Ah'Heman no longer cared.

BOOM.

BOOM.

BOOM.

The Death Knights still standing around him began dropping one after another.

Power was being pulled away from them.

Ah'Heman kept his eyes closed.

He muttered continuously.

Then—

“…Found you.”

His face twisted with ecstasy.

“I found you!”

The dark mana around him accelerated into a violent spiral.

“Deep down…”

His voice shook.

“At the bottom of that endless abyss…”

The storm grew.

“You had no grave.”

The black current became so strong that several Ballak warriors struggled to keep their footing.

“You were resting there alone…”

Ah'Heman's eyes flew open.

“Yes.”

He laughed.

“You really were there!”

Akwilla alone stood unmoved inside the storm.

“…It's time to end this.”

The Night Fox raised her bow.

Her arrow aligned with Ah'Heman's forehead.

Twang!

Silver light tore through the black gale.

At that distance, from Akwilla's bow, the arrow should have punched a hole straight through Ah'Heman's skull.

But—

CRACK!

The arrow stopped in midair.

No.

A hand had caught it.

“…!?”

Every Ballak warrior looked up.

A thin man stood between Akwilla and Ah'Heman.

His clothes were ragged and ancient.

Only dry skin and bone remained on his face.

His lips were gone, leaving teeth and gums exposed.

The burial cloth whipping around him belonged to an age long past.

His frame was tall.

His bones were thick.

And he had just caught the current Night Fox's arrow with one bare hand.

No one could immediately identify this ancestor.

No one except Akwilla.

“…!”

Her eyes trembled.

Since childhood she had heard legends of Ballak's old chieftains and heroes.

Most had never impressed her as much as the storytellers intended.

Akwilla had been born with overwhelming potential.

Talent that appeared only once in centuries.

Nearly limitless room to grow.

She had become one of the most capable chieftains Ballak had ever produced.

So when she heard stories of ancient warriors, part of her often thought—

*I could have done that too.*

There was only one exception.

A hero who had declared war on the Empire by himself.

A warrior who forced the Empire to surrender.

A conqueror who unified the surrounding tribes and led Ballak into its golden age.

An eternal myth.

The supreme archer of the spirit world.

The strongest bowman since Ballak's history began to be recorded.

**Divine Archer Adonai.**
