# Chapter 260: The Grave of Swords (6)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/260.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 258 is the verified shifted secondary witness.

**<Death Knight>**

**Danger Rating:** A+ ~ ?  
**Size:** ?  
**Discovery Location:** ?  
**Alias:** ‘Death Knight’

A noble knight whose overwhelming strength and spirit were swallowed by the abyss after death.

The dark mana drawn from beyond the gate of death continues to sustain the body until both flesh and soul are completely worn away.

What lies beyond the next gate—

after a being that has failed to repay that power crosses death once more—

remains unknown.

Death Knight.

A noble knight who transcended ordinary limits—

died—

and transformed.

Their power varied so greatly from one individual to another that assigning a fixed danger rating was almost meaningless.

The result depended on who the knight had been.

How powerful their body was.

How strong their spirit.

And what circumstances had produced their transformation.

Any Death Knight was at least A+.

But no truly extraordinary one had appeared in nearly a century.

Vikir knew immediately—

Cane Corso was far beyond that minimum.

Far beyond even S-rank monsters such as Madam Eight-Legs or the Basilisk.

[I crossed the threshold of death.]

Cane Corso spoke about his own death without hesitation.

He had walked toward it alone.

In a remote place.

Unknown to the world.

And even at the final moment—

he had never regretted his choice.

In exchange for that lonely death—

he had touched a core of the higher realm that no human had approached in centuries.

“…A realm you can only reach by actually experiencing death.”

Vikir swallowed.

The path now made a terrible kind of sense.

The Sixth Fang.

A state reached by transcending emotion.

The Seventh.

A state reached by regaining the emotions once discarded.

The Eighth.

A state reached by gripping the sword as if reborn—

then surviving countless brutal battles with a desperate attachment to life.

And the Ninth—

the incomprehensible core of the higher realm.

A place only someone who had truly crossed death could enter.

Vikir had spent one life rejecting emotion.

That had taken him to the Sixth Fang.

His reunion with Camus had opened the path to the Seventh.

Cane Corso’s guidance—

and the edge of death—

had now brought him to the Eighth.

But the Ninth was something else entirely.

*To go beyond Sword Master, I need to be reborn and then die again?*

*What kind of training method is that?*

Even after reaching the Eighth Fang—

the idea felt absurdly distant.

And yet—

the man before him had done it.

By surrendering his life.

Cane Corso’s dead eyes seemed deeper than darkness.

[That is why I told you that you cannot reach the Ninth while alive.]

[This realm rejects ordinary understanding.]

[Empathy.]

[Acceptance.]

[Belief.]

[Common sense.]

[Probability.]

[Causality.]

[A being who has never experienced death cannot set foot here.]

“…”

[You still cling strongly to life.]

Cane Corso almost sounded amused.

[Understandable.]

[You are far too young to contemplate death seriously.]

He settled into the iron throne.

[You are not ready.]

The words drew a clean boundary.

This side—

and the other.

The living world—

and what lay beyond it.

Vikir frowned.

“What do you mean by ‘ready’?”

[Complete the Eighth Fang.]

[Become the master of this tower.]

That answer made no sense to Vikir yet.

But another question mattered more.

“…Then what about the Tenth?”

“Does it truly exist?”

“Have you understood the principle behind it?”

[It exists.]

Cane Corso’s voice grew heavier.

[But even I, master of this tower, have not comprehended it.]

He paused.

[I said the Ninth requires one to experience death.]

“Yes.”

[To reach the Tenth—]

[one must die and then awaken again.]

Vikir stared at him.

There was already a realm one could not reach while alive.

Now there was another—

one could not reach merely by dying.

The Ninth required death.

The Tenth required death—

and return.

“…Not simply to die.”

Vikir frowned.

“To experience death.”

“And then wake from it.”

“What kind of absurd principle is that?”

Cane Corso smiled.

[I do not understand it either.]

[That is why I remain at the Ninth.]

[How does a dead man wake again?]

[Perhaps I will never reach the Tenth.]

For Cane Corso—

failure to reach the Tenth meant something more than stalled swordsmanship.

It meant he could not rest.

He had become a Death Knight.

But he had no idea how to escape death’s chains—

or become human again.

Perhaps he had never even considered that possibility.

Would he remain here forever?

Alone through endless years?

At an age when he should have been buried in his own tomb—

had he instead become the guardian of someone else’s?

Only then did Vikir fully understand the tower.

Cane Corso spoke as if reading the thought.

[This Grave of Swords is the tomb of a great Baskerville.]

Vikir’s attention sharpened.

The **Cradle of Swords** was said to be the birthplace of the Baskerville ancestor who created the family’s swordsmanship.

This tower—

was where that same figure had died.

[His birth was the birth of the sword.]

[His death was the death of the sword.]

Vikir nodded.

The Lurking Ambush swordsmanship he had inherited—

may well have originated with that ancestor too.

Cane Corso continued.

[This is his tomb.]

[And the final destination of those who seek the ultimate truth of the sword.]

Then—

[A true Baskerville comes here at the end of life.]

All Baskervilles were born from the Cradle of Swords.

But only the greatest among them—

the truest Baskervilles—

closed their eyes in the Grave of Swords.

[All great Baskervilles end the same way.]

[From his cradle—]

[to his grave.]

[You will too.]

Children born from the Cradle of Swords—

destined eventually to sleep in the Grave.

Vikir snorted.

“I still have too much to do.”

“I’m not coming back to a silent wasteland like this.”

Cane Corso laughed softly.

[Too much to do?]

[Life is not as important as you think.]

[It is only a form between a question and an answer.]

People passed through countless rites while alive.

Birth.

Coming of age.

Coronation.

Marriage.

Funeral.

How many things did anyone really “have to do”?

Life was only a long equation—

between the question of birth—

and the answer of death.

[At my age—]

[those little ceremonies stop meaning much.]

Cane Corso stroked his beard.

Then—

boom.

The tower moved.

The Grave of Swords began to shake.

“…What?”

A violent wind struck Vikir.

He raised an arm over his eyes.

The storm intensified.

Cane Corso’s form grew faint.

*We will meet again someday.*

Those were his final words.

Then Vikir noticed the white grains within the wind.

Salt.

The desert was pouring into the tower.

The storm swallowed everything.

Then—

silence.

“…!”

Vikir opened his eyes.

He was standing in the empty desert.

The Grave of Swords—

the enormous tower—

was gone.

Not ruined.

Not hidden.

Gone.

As if the entire encounter had been a long dream.

But it had not been.

Vikir looked down.

Two solid saplings rested in his hands.

Wraith Trees.

The strange plants that had rooted behind the iron throne.

Artifacts for black mages.

Exactly what Pomeranian had wanted.

“…Not a dream.”

Power moved through Vikir’s body.

Different now.

Higher.

The Eighth Fang.

A Sword Master who had stepped beyond his previous realm.

Vikir now believed that even if he fought Hugo directly—

he would not lose.

He looked up.

Cane Corso was gone.

So was the tower.

Only sunlight and white desert remained.

No mirage.

No illusion.

Vikir turned toward the place where the iron throne had stood.

He bowed once.

Then tightened his grip on the two Wraith Tree saplings.

And left.

Back toward House Baskerville.

And then—

Colosseo Academy.
