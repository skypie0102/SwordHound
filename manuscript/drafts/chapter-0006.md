# Chapter 6: The Baskerville Dog (3)

John Barrymore.

He was the head butler who had served the Baskerville family faithfully for four generations.

“My lord. I am here to give today’s report.”

Seated before Barrymore was a middle-aged man with a cold expression.

A blade-sharp nose.

A thick beard.

Icy eyes.

A Sword Master. A sword saint. One of the seven great pillars credited with saving the nation.

Hugo Le Baskerville, patriarch of the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan, watched Barrymore without expression.

“First report. Regarding the bloodshed with the Morgue Clan at the ruby mines of Red Fang Mountain…”

The Morgue Clan, famed for magic, was one of the Seven Great Clans alongside the Baskervilles.

Recently, a territorial dispute had erupted between the two houses over rich ruby mines near the western edge of their respective domains.

After a moment, Hugo finally spoke.

“We’ll have a chance to discuss that soon enough. Morgue will come to us first.”

He made a small gesture with one hand.

Barrymore continued.

For most of the report, Hugo remained expressionless, listening as though the matters scarcely interested him.

An occasional frown of irritation was the most reaction he showed.

Then Barrymore reached a report that finally changed Hugo’s expression.

“… And so, first place in the written portion of this midterm evaluation at the nursery castle went to Young Master Vikir.”

Hugo lightly touched his chin.

Barrymore knew the habit well.

It was what Hugo did when he was pleased.

It was rare enough for Hugo to remember the names of young children in the clan at all, so Barrymore’s own expression brightened slightly.

Hugo asked,

“Butler. When is the children’s practical examination?”

“Soon. It begins in five days. Most of the guardian knights assigned to the nursery castle have already gone out to prepare the grounds.”

“Then the place should be mostly empty.”

Barrymore’s expression shifted.

“As it happens, my lord, the next matter I intended to report also concerns the nursery castle.”

“What is it?”

“Well… there is good news and bad news.”

“Let me hear the good news first.”

Hugo’s interest made Barrymore’s voice a little firmer.

“They say two suns appeared in the sky above the nursery castle.”

“Two suns?”

Hugo’s eyes widened slightly.

The Baskervilles were surprisingly superstitious.

“An auspicious sign?”

“At the very least, two suns appeared. Among the household servants, there is already a rumor that a small sun has been born to stand beside the clan’s future heir.”

“… No one was playing tricks with mana?”

“Everyone skilled enough to produce such an illusion was away on official business preparing the young masters’ practical examination.”

“Hm. Then it isn’t a bad omen. Another sun.”

Hugo gave a quiet nod.

The appearance of two suns had long been regarded as a favorable sign by the clan.

“Whenever an omen like that appears, something fortunate tends to happen to the family. I wonder what occurred at the nursery castle.”

Barrymore’s expression darkened.

“That… is the problem. Something did happen.”

“The bad news you mentioned?”

“Yes, my lord.”

“Let me hear it.”

Barrymore continued in a heavier tone.

“There was a serious fight among the young masters.”

Hugo’s expression changed immediately.

“A serious fight? How many died?”

“… No one died. But Young Master Highbro’s teeth were badly damaged, Young Master Middlebro suffered a fractured jaw, and Young Master Lowbro’s right index finger was severed. They have all received treatment and recovered physically.”

“Then it was nothing more than children scuffling. Brothers grow up fighting.”

Hugo relaxed and leaned back in his chair.

He flipped through the report on the triplets, then muttered,

“They were always close. Surely they didn’t turn on one another?”

“No. The conflict began with another young master.”

“Idiots. In this family, if someone is older than you, they are usually stronger than you as well. Even pups should know enough to measure an opponent before they bite.”

Barrymore corrected him carefully.

“The young master who did this to them is younger.”

“What? Younger than nine? Did an entire class of eight-year-olds gang up on them?”

“No, my lord.”

“Then what?”

“One person.”

A strange light returned to Hugo’s eyes.

Barrymore’s next words made it deepen.

“The culprit was the same Young Master Vikir I mentioned earlier—the one who placed first in the written examination.”

◆◆◆

Vikir Van Baskerville.

Eight years old.

He had been summoned by the patriarch and now walked toward Hugo’s chamber deep within the main castle.

When he pushed open the door, Hugo Le Baskerville’s large frame was waiting inside.

“Sit.”

Hugo was not intentionally releasing his presence, yet the pressure radiating from him was still immense.

Vikir moved carefully, making certain that none of the mana hidden inside his body leaked out.

‘I’m confident when it comes to concealing mana.’

Warriors who had survived the conflict with the demons and the long Age of Destruction had refined the art of suppressing their presence to an extreme.

The method Vikir used to hide his mana completely was beyond warriors of this era.

It was not a question of raw strength.

It was experience.

Even so, there were limits.

If Vikir continued growing at this speed, eventually he would become too powerful to escape Hugo’s notice.

‘I need to find a way to protect myself before that happens.’

With that thought in mind, Vikir sat in the small chair prepared for him.

Hugo spoke.

“It’s been a while. You’ve grown quite a bit.”

Vikir was surprised.

Hugo’s voice was normally so sharp it seemed capable of cutting flesh merely by being heard.

Today, it sounded strangely subdued.

The words themselves were even more unusual.

‘Piglets grow quickly. Why do these brats always look so small and scrawny every time I see them?’

That was the sort of thing Hugo used to say during Vikir’s childhood in his previous life.

Back then, Hugo had watched the children like livestock, impatiently measuring their growth and wondering when they would become useful.

Now, however, his gaze was oddly gentle.

Almost expectant.

“Greetings, Patriarch.”

Vikir greeted him with the bright innocence of a child.

Hugo seemed oddly dissatisfied with the title.

“Patriarch…? Hm. Yes. I suppose I am.”

He appeared to think about something, then abandoned it and changed the subject.

“I heard you crippled the triplets from the nine-year-old senior class.”

“As far as I know, they received proper treatment afterward.”

“I’m not talking about their bodies.”

Hugo frowned.

“I’m talking about their minds.”

“…”

“Since that day, they eat separately. Sleep separately. Barely even speak to one another. Their coordinated technique used to be excellent, but now their unity has completely collapsed.”

And that was not all.

These days, the three boys wet themselves from fear whenever they caught sight of Vikir.

A dog that had lost its fighting spirit was useless on a battlefield.

Vikir did not bother to say so.

Instead, he gave Hugo his conclusion.

“Carrying three soft, poorly made swords only makes you heavier while giving you nothing useful in return. One properly forged sword is better.”

A glimmer of approval passed through Hugo’s eyes.

“… True.”

He stroked his chin and looked down at the eight-year-old before him.

The corner of his mouth twitched upward.

“But don’t you think hurting your older brothers was wrong?”

Vikir looked genuinely puzzled.

“Wrong? What did I do?”

“What did you do? You beat your brothers until they were broken.”

Vikir tilted his head as though he could not understand the question.

“What was wrong with that? I was stronger.”

“… What?”

Vikir looked directly at the stunned Hugo and asked,

“How can the strong be wrong?”

The strong were revered.

The weak were guilty simply for being weak.

Was that not the Baskerville way?

For a moment, Hugo Le Baskerville said nothing.

Then the corners of his mouth began to twitch.

The child in front of him was becoming almost painfully endearing.
