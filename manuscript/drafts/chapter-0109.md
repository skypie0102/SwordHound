Established terminology controls **Hugo Le Baskerville**, **John Barrymore**, **Vikir Van Baskerville**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Night Fox**, **Bourgeois Clan**, **Rokoko**, **Pomeranian La Baskerville**, **Penelope La Baskerville**, and **Lady Roxana**. Vikir's assumptions about Hugo's motives and likely treatment of Pomeranian remain explicitly his interpretations.

Hugo Le Baskerville came personally.

So did **John Barrymore**.

Hugo entered City Hall with one question.

“Where is my son?”

Chihuahua led him straight to the office.

Vikir barely had time to stand.

The door opened.

Father and son faced each other.

Hugo's eyes widened.

Vikir's did too.

“You're back.”

“I am.”

“Well done.”

“Thank you.”

Four short lines.

Dry.

Formal.

Barrymore looked close to tears.

Hugo had not smiled during the two years Vikir was gone.

Not once.

Now—

he was smiling.

Barely visible.

Still unmistakable to the old butler.

Vikir noticed too.

That made him more suspicious, not less.

Pomeranian had been hidden hurriedly in the adjoining room.

Hopefully—

she would stay there.

Hugo put one hand on Vikir's shoulder.

“How have you been?”

Vikir tensed.

He had spent two years growing strong enough to challenge the future.

And just as importantly—

learning to hide it from Hugo.

But Hugo did not press mana into him.

Did not probe.

Did not test.

His voice carried something almost warm.

Vikir studied his face.

Surprise.

Relief.

Curiosity.

Joy?

*Like an ordinary father.*

That thought was dangerous.

Vikir remembered the previous life.

Rain.

Execution.

The man who condemned him for colluding with demons.

The king of the Baskerville hounds.

He sharpened his caution again.

“My injuries took time to heal.”

Vikir kept his tone respectful.

“I also established friendly relations with some native tribes.”

Hugo's expression brightened.

“Friendly relations?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Vikir almost relaxed.

Then Hugo continued.

“You must know their strength and locations now.”

His eyes sharpened.

“That will make extermination easier.”

There it was.

Vikir suppressed a sigh.

“They're stronger than I expected.”

“Especially the Night Fox.”

“But I found a way to control the tribes.”

Hugo leaned forward.

“What?”

“Trade.”

Vikir explained Imperial manufactured goods.

Glass beads.

Textiles.

Vegetables.

Grain.

Objects inexpensive inside the Empire could have enormous value inside the floodlands.

He also mentioned Bourgeois merchants who had already entered secretly.

Hugo's expression darkened.

“Those money-worshiping merchants traded with external enemies?”

His voice became dangerous.

“I should cut their heads off.”

“No need.”

Hugo looked at him.

“I already did.”

“What?”

“As Acting Magistrate of Underdog, I had them executed.”

Vikir's voice remained flat.

“No witnesses.”

Hugo stared.

Then smiled with open approval.

“Good.”

He nodded.

“My son indeed.”

They returned to the trade proposal.

Hugo disliked any plan that did not draw blood.

Vikir adjusted the presentation.

“Use the tribes as the front line against monsters.”

That fit Hugo's worldview.

Cheap resources.

Native hunters.

Monster suppression.

Baskerville gains territory without direct war.

Hugo became interested.

Vikir's real plan was different.

With Madam gone—

Ballak could migrate deeper into its old western floodland range, away from Baskerville pressure.

Baskerville could occupy the vacated frontier.

Trade could continue.

War could be avoided.

Hugo would interpret the migration as retreat.

Everyone gained what they wanted.

“Fine.”

Hugo nodded.

“You will manage native trade.”

“If you order it.”

“One exception.”

His expression hardened.

“Rokoko.”

Vikir waited.

“No trade.”

Hugo's voice became absolute.

“That tribe is to be exterminated.”

Vikir did not argue.

Not yet.

The rest of the report passed without incident.

Then Hugo said something Vikir did not expect.

“Your disappearance brought Baskerville enormous diplomatic gains.”

Morgue compensation.

Imperial recognition.

Awards for chivalry.

Hugo dismissed all of it.

“What use was any of that to a father who lost his son?”

Vikir stared.

Barrymore did too.

“But you're back.”

Hugo's voice settled.

“Things are in their proper place again.”

He told Vikir to step away from Underdog duties temporarily.

Rest.

The house would decide an appropriate reward.

Vikir bowed.

Then remembered Pomeranian's pendant.

Roxana.

Penelope.

Pomeranian.

Should he reveal them now?

Barrymore's prior-life account had connected Hugo's hardening personality to the loss of his first wife and first daughter.

The pendant was probably a relic from that family.

Still—

Vikir hesitated.

He expected Hugo to reject Pomeranian.

A girl.

A mage.

Black magic.

Mixed tribal blood.

That expectation came from the Hugo Vikir knew.

It was not certainty.

Then the side door burst open.

“Uncle!”

Pomeranian ran in crying.

Thunder had frightened her.

She buried herself in Vikir's arms.

That was the first surprise.

The second—

was Hugo.

Vikir looked up.

Hugo Le Baskerville had gone completely still.

Eyes wide.

Mouth open.

A face Vikir had never seen—

not in this life.

Not in the last.

Hugo stared at Pomeranian.

And for once—

Vikir had no idea what he was thinking.
