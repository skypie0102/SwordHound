It was a reunion after a very long absence.

The moment he heard that Vikir was alive, Hugo Le Baskerville—patriarch of the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan—came in person.

Even Vikir had not expected such an intense reaction.

John Barrymore accompanied him to Underdog City Hall.

Hugo went straight to the office.

“Where is my son?”

Chihuahua immediately led him inside.

Vikir barely had time to prepare.

Hugo pushed open the office door just as Vikir was about to step out.

“…!”

Hugo’s eyes widened.

Vikir’s did too, if only slightly.

“You’re back.”

“I’m back.”

“Well done.”

“Thank you.”

The reunion between father and son was brief and dry.

Barrymore, standing behind Hugo, watched with open relief.

*It has been a long time since I’ve seen the Patriarch this happy.*

There was a reason the old butler thought so.

In the two years since Vikir disappeared, Hugo had not smiled once.

Not even faintly.

Now he was smiling at his son.

The expression was almost impossible to recognize on Hugo’s habitually cold, controlled face.

Meanwhile—

Vikir glanced toward the side door.

Hugo had arrived too quickly for him to hide Pomeranian properly.

He had rushed her into the adjoining room.

That should be enough.

Then—

Grip.

Hugo placed a hand on Vikir’s shoulder.

“So. How have you been?”

Vikir tensed.

He had become much stronger during his years in the Depths.

He had no intention of letting Hugo discover how much stronger.

Drawing on the experience of someone who had survived the Age of Destruction, Vikir concealed his mana with practiced ease.

But—

“…?”

Hugo’s hand applied no pressure.

He did not probe Vikir’s internal mana.

He did not seem to be testing him at all.

There was even a faint warmth in his voice.

*What is this?*

Vikir was genuinely unsettled.

Had Hugo simply asked because he wanted to know?

Vikir searched his face again.

He could read little.

Surprise.

Joy.

Curiosity.

Relief.

Almost like—

*an ordinary father.*

Vikir frowned.

There were only two explanations.

Either Hugo’s thoughts had become so deep and devious that even Vikir could no longer read him—

or Vikir himself had grown soft.

The night before his regression.

Pouring rain.

Countless fangs piercing his body.

Vikir had never forgotten it.

How could he forget the man who branded him a collaborator of demons and ordered his execution?

Master of the hounds.

King of all hounds.

Hugo Le Baskerville.

Vikir sharpened the poisoned fangs that had almost grown dull.

The sharper his thoughts became, the gentler his voice sounded.

“I spent most of my time recovering from the wounds the monster gave me. Along the way, I also established friendly relations with some of the people in the Depths.”

Hugo brightened slightly.

“Oh? Friendly relations with the barbarians?”

“Yes.”

“That’s good.”

The answer was unexpected from a man who despised the natives of the Depths.

Hugo’s next sentence explained it.

“If you became friendly with them, you should know their positions and strength. That will make large-scale extermination much easier.”

Hugo still viewed them much as he viewed monsters.

Vikir suppressed a sigh.

“Living among them taught me that… they are stronger than I expected.”

“Indeed. The Night Fox in particular is dangerous.”

“But I found a way to control them.”

“Oh? What is it?”

Hugo leaned in with visible interest.

When he asked Vikir a question, he usually received a useful answer.

Apparently he expected the same now.

“The people of the Depths have a strong interest in small manufactured goods from the Empire.”

“Manufactured goods?”

“Yes. I confirmed that merchants from the Bourgeois Clan were secretly smuggling goods into the Depths and trading with them.”

Hugo’s face hardened.

His voice dropped to a growl.

“Those coin-worshipping merchants. Now they collude with enemies beyond our border? I should cut off their heads before long—”

“You don’t need to worry, Father. I already eliminated them.”

“What?”

“I was Deputy Magistrate of Underdog City. I used the Acting Magistrate’s authority to execute them immediately. There were no witnesses.”

Hugo’s eyes widened again.

Then his gaze toward Vikir became almost affectionate.

“Good. As expected of my son. That is how a Baskerville acts.”

His eyes gleamed.

“So the Bourgeois merchants bought the barbarians with cheap trinkets?”

“Yes. Glass beads and wool crafts that cost almost nothing in the Empire are valuable to them. So are vegetables and grain. Those goods can be used to influence them, and the arrangement can produce considerable diplomatic benefit.”

“…Hmm. But that sounds like trade, not subjugation.”

“A way to win without fighting.”

Hugo looked dissatisfied.

“Victory without battle is meaningless. Baskerville swords exist to draw blood from barbarians.”

*What a warmonger.*

Vikir kept the thought to himself.

“Of course, the barbarians will still shed blood.”

“Hmm? You just said we would trade with them.”

“I did not say we should live with them in peace.”

“Then?”

Vikir’s eyes sharpened.

“Use them as the vanguard for hunting monsters.”

It was one of Hugo’s favorite strategies.

Hugo immediately became interested.

“Hmm. Lure cheap manpower with cheap goods and send them against monsters. Not bad. The monsters have to be eliminated anyway.”

“Yes. I’ve already laid the necessary groundwork. You only need to use it.”

Vikir had no intention of letting Baskerville harm Ballak.

Madam Eight-Legs was gone.

Without the monster that had pressed them from one side, Ballak could withdraw deeper into its old territory, farther from Baskerville.

Baskerville could expand into the land Ballak left behind.

Hugo knew nothing about Madam.

He would interpret Ballak’s withdrawal as retreat.

For Vikir, the arrangement did two things at once.

It prevented war.

And it opened trade.

Hugo eventually nodded.

“Very well. I’ll leave trade with the barbarians to you. Can you handle it?”

“If that is your order, I will.”

“Good.”

Hugo smiled, satisfied that his son’s loyalty appeared as firm as ever—perhaps firmer.

For Vikir, claiming credit for a plan already prepared was equally convenient.

Then—

“…But.”

Hugo’s tone changed.

“Among the barbarians is a tribe called Rokoko.”

“Yes. I know them.”

“There will be no trade with them. Rokoko is to be completely exterminated. Remember that.”

His voice left no room for negotiation.

If Vikir objected now, everything else they had agreed on might collapse.

He simply nodded.

The conversation continued.

Most of Vikir’s report was the same account he had already given Chihuahua.

Hugo listened from the sofa, usually satisfied and occasionally surprised.

Eventually he said—

“After you disappeared while searching for Morgue’s daughter, our family gained enormous diplomatic concessions. Even the imperial court praised Baskerville’s chivalry and granted us honors.”

“…”

“But what use is any of that to a father who lost his son?”

Vikir found the conversation stranger with every sentence.

Behind Hugo, even Barrymore’s widened eyes mirrored his surprise.

At last Hugo brought the report to an end.

“But you’re back. That is enough. Things can finally return to their proper place.”

“…”

“Set aside your duties as Deputy Magistrate for now and rest. The family will decide on a suitable reward for you.”

Vikir answered briefly and bowed.

Then another question returned to him.

*Should I tell him about the portrait in Pomeranian’s pendant?*

Vikir believed the pendant had originally belonged to Hugo’s first wife, **Lady Roxana**.

Roxana’s daughter was **Penelope La Baskerville**.

Penelope’s daughter was **Pomeranian La Baskerville**.

Before regression, Barrymore had told Vikir that Hugo’s personality had grown far harsher after Roxana died and Penelope disappeared into the Depths under mysterious circumstances.

The old butler’s testimony made the story credible.

Should Vikir return what appeared to be Roxana’s keepsake?

And beyond that—

should he reveal Pomeranian herself?

*No.*

Vikir shook his head slightly.

*That would only tear open an old wound. I can’t expect anything from this cold-blooded man.*

If Vikir had ever seen Hugo deal with a close female relative, perhaps he could have predicted his response.

He had not.

Women were extraordinarily rare in the immediate Baskerville line.

*He might say women are unsuited to the sword. If he learns she carries Rokoko blood, he may even draw his weapon.*

Given Hugo’s usual character, saying nothing seemed safer.

That was Vikir’s conclusion.

…And then two things happened at once.

Both shattered his prediction.

“Uwaaah! Uncle!”

The side door flew open.

Pomeranian burst into the room.

“Uncle! Cold! Rain outside! Thunder! Boom, boom! Waaah!”

Her face wet with tears and snot, she threw herself into Vikir’s arms.

The first variable.

Then—

“…!”

Vikir’s eyes widened.

The second variable was far stranger.

Hugo’s face.

“…! …! …! …!”

His eyes were perfectly round.

His mouth had fallen open farther than Vikir had ever seen.

Across two lifetimes—

Vikir had never seen Hugo Le Baskerville make that expression.
