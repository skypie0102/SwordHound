# Chapter 69: Blood Relatives (2)

Aiyen frowned.

“She's still searching?”

Camus Morgue had not stopped for two years.

Officially, Morgue parties entered the jungle to suppress hostile tribes and survey territory.

Aiyen knew better.

Camus was looking for someone.

Vikir.

Aiyen remembered the slave auction.

A cage.

Her own body near death.

A boy who had opened that cage and saved her.

She had never forgotten his face.

When she met him again, another woman had been in his arms.

Camus.

That same woman had returned again and again to this dangerous jungle.

Perhaps she believed Vikir was alive.

Perhaps she wanted his body if he was dead.

Either way, two years of searching required an obsession even Aiyen respected.

*If she came for rubies, she should just dig for rubies and leave.*

Aiyen crossed her arms.

Then remained silent.

She was deciding whether to tell Vikir.

◆◆◆

Aiyen tracked him through the forest.

Bakira carried her easily over mud and roots.

She found Vikir crouched beside enormous tracks.

He pressed his fingers into one print and measured its depth.

“Mushuhushu.”

Aiyen looked down.

Vikir continued.

“Very old. Very large. If we kill it, the village eats for at least three days.”

He could read more than direction now.

Weight.

Age.

Health.

Speed.

Mood.

The Ballak hunters had trained those skills into him.

Aiyen watched without speaking.

Two years had changed Vikir.

His face was still young.

Everything else about him had sharpened.

Even before, he had handled tasks alone.

Now he moved like someone born to the jungle.

Aiyen understood, reluctantly, why Camus refused to forget him.

“Slave.”

Vikir did not turn.

Aiyen had never stopped using the old nickname even after he was freed.

“What?”

“What was the place you came from like?”

Vikir paused.

Which place?

Baskerville?

Or the world before regression?

He answered both at once.

“Hell.”

Aiyen scratched her head.

That was not the question she truly wanted answered.

“Do you want to go back?”

Vikir finally turned.

He studied her expression.

“Why are you asking that?”

Aiyen hated hiding things.

She hated lying.

She especially hated doing either to him.

Her hands tightened.

“Someone is looking for you.”

Vikir waited.

“That woman.”

Aiyen forced the words out.

“Camus Morgue.”

The tension in her body remained until Vikir answered.

“I'm not going back.”

Aiyen blinked.

“Really?”

“Yes.”

Vikir had no reason to return yet.

If he went back, it would be after recovering at least the strength of his previous life—

or after becoming capable of hiding his true strength completely from Hugo.

He thought about Camus.

*So she still remembers that I saved her.*

Perhaps gratitude.

Perhaps diplomacy.

Whatever the reason, if Morgue search parties could repeatedly operate in Baskerville territory, then Baskerville and Morgue relations had not collapsed.

More importantly, Baskerville had apparently not stopped looking for him either.

The outside situation had changed while he lived here.

Sooner or later he would need to see it for himself.

Aiyen's voice became lighter.

“One more thing.”

She pulled out a dagger.

A single large serpent had been engraved on it.

“The outsiders from the patrol report left this. Know the mark?”

Vikir's eyes narrowed.

“I do.”

The serpent belonged to one of the Empire's Seven Great Clans.

**Leviathan Clan.**

A house famous for poison.

Baskerville had the sword.

Morgue had magic.

Quovadis had faith.

Bourgeois had wealth.

Leviathan had toxins.

Why were Leviathan agents secretly entering this jungle and pouring red liquid into a river source?

Vikir's thoughts accelerated.

“Can I keep this?”

“Take it.”

He tucked away the dagger.

Then—

[GURRRRGL.]

Something huge moved through the mangroves.

A smooth, scaleless body dragged itself through the mud.

A lungfish.

This one was monstrous.

More than eight meters long.

Its mucus-coated body and foul appearance made even Ballak reluctant to regard it as food.

Aiyen grimaced.

“So the rainy season really is close.”

“If specimens this large are already moving, the rains may last a long time.”

Aiyen pointed toward a fallen tree.

Dried aquatic plants clung tens of meters above the present waterline.

“Last year it reached that high.”

“This year may be worse.”

Then Aiyen stopped.

Something protruded from the lungfish's body.

A harpoon.

She killed the creature with an arrow through the head and cut open its belly.

A half-digested human body slid out.

Aiyen's expression changed.

“Rokoko.”

Rokoko were famous for raising warriors on bear blood and milk.

They were Ballak's greatest local rivals.

Their tribe practiced cannibalism.

Most people in the jungle feared confronting them directly.

Ballak did not.

Vikir looked at the corpse.

“A Rokoko hunter inside Ballak territory. War?”

“No.”

Aiyen examined the harpoon.

It had not pierced the lungfish from outside.

It had punched from the stomach outward.

The hunter had been swallowed first, then stabbed through the creature's body from within.

That was the strange part.

A lungfish of this size was powerful but slow.

A healthy Rokoko hunter should never have been caught by it.

The remains suggested he was just over twenty—squarely in his physical prime.

Aiyen frowned.

“Why would this thing manage to eat him?”

“He was already weak.”

“Then why was someone that weak hunting?”

Vikir looked toward the direction from which the dead man had come.

“Maybe the tribe was in such bad condition that he had no choice.”

That was only one possibility.

The hunter might have weakened after leaving home.

But there were no obvious combat injuries.

Scratch marks inside the lungfish's throat and stomach showed that he had fought desperately.

The marks also showed how little strength he had left.

Aiyen's instincts reacted.

“Something's wrong.”

They tracked backward.

The dead hunter's movements had been erratic.

He had staggered.

Wandered without recognizing Ballak's territorial signs.

Searched for small animals and fallen fruit.

That behavior was completely unlike Rokoko hunters, who normally targeted large prey.

The closer Vikir and Aiyen came to Rokoko territory, the more disturbing the trail became.

Then they found a small camp.

Several Rokoko hunters were there.

Rokoko carried traces of ancient dark-elf blood.

The tribe was famous for striking faces and bodies, as well as black magic, curses, witchcraft, and summoning arts.

Their appearance had also made them frequent targets of slave hunters.

None of that mattered now.

Every hunter in the camp was dead.
