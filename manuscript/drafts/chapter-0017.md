# Chapter 17: Solitary (4)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/017.txt`. Canonical English forms for *Lurking Embedded Teeth*, Baskerville Fangs, Sword Expert, Sword Graduator, Beelzebub, and the 10,000 Book Library are normalized from current project/Fandom evidence. Recovered English Chapter 17 is an aligned secondary reference only.

‘Done.’

Vikir closed the book.

He had memorized every word.

So thoroughly, in fact, that he could recite the entire text with his eyes closed—and even work backward through it if necessary.

The results appeared almost immediately.

Whoosh—thrust—swish!

Vikir raised one hand like a blade and traced sword paths through the air exactly as the scripture taught.

The first Fang appeared cleanly in the motion.

Then the second.

Then the third.

Those were all within what his eight-year-old body could already reproduce.

Snap!

The fourth Fang followed.

The Baskerville Fourth Fang.

The technique Vikir had relied upon throughout his previous life.

But then—

Whip!

His hand continued past the Fourth Fang and carved another, sharper trajectory through the air.

A fifth Fang.

Smaller than the others.

Duller.

Unsteady.

But unmistakably there.

The Baskerville Fifth Fang.

Vikir had reproduced it with his own hand.

“… Incredible.”

He repeated the motion.

Once.

Twice.

Again.

Each attempt succeeded.

His child-sized hands and short arms prevented the sword path from reaching its complete shape, but Vikir could feel it clearly.

The fifth Fang had begun to grow.

“… Is there a sword around here?”

If he had possessed a blade, he would have poured aura into it immediately and tested the result.

Naturally, there was no weapon lying around the library.

And drawing Beelzebub from the blood vessel in his wrist was absolutely out of the question in a place like this.

Still—

Vikir nearly felt like crying.

Something hot surged through his chest and struck the back of his throat.

He forced the emotion down.

‘As long as my mana keeps pace, I can definitely exceed the level I reached in my previous life.’

In swordsmanship alone, he had already obtained a technique more advanced and refined than anything he had possessed before regression.

The Fifth Fang was no longer the end.

Sixth.

Seventh.

All the way to the Tenth Fang.

His future had opened in every direction.

Vikir closed his eyes and examined the mana within his body.

The mana pool enlarged by the River Styx continued to fill little by little.

He could not measure his state with perfect precision, but the realization he had just experienced had unmistakably pushed him across another threshold.

‘… Sword Graduator.’

He had advanced from **High Sword Expert** into **Low Sword Graduator**.

The difference was fundamental.

A Sword Expert produced gaseous aura.

A Sword Graduator could condense that aura until it behaved like liquid.

Rather than merely coating the edge or tip, a Graduator could envelop the entire blade in dense aura and shape it far more freely.

When a liquid aura blade met a Sword Expert’s gaseous aura, the result was as decisive as water colliding with steam.

Sword Graduators possessed physical capabilities beyond normal human limits.

The number a family could field was not merely a measure of prestige.

It was an index of military power for the empire itself.

In his previous life, Vikir had not reached this realm until around thirty years old.

Now he was eight.

The difference was enormous.

For many swordsmen, Sword Graduator was a dream they would never reach in an entire lifetime.

By a mage’s scale, Vikir’s current state roughly corresponded to the Fourth Circle.

Among the Baskervilles of the last century, there could not have been many—if any—who had reached such a level at his age.

“… And the character of the swordsmanship itself has changed.”

The technique Vikir had used before was the **Stabbing Fang** style taught to Baskerville hounds.

Its four Fangs had carried him through countless battles and near-death encounters.

But Stabbing Fang swordsmanship carried serious weaknesses.

It allowed a hound to become combat-effective quickly.

Its growth ceiling was obvious.

And because it pursued certainty in killing the enemy, the wielder often had to accept enormous personal risk.

In other words, it was ideal swordsmanship for hounds expected to be used as disposable pieces.

What Vikir had learned from *Lurking Embedded Teeth* was different.

Its very nature was concealed and vicious.

The sword moved slowly without openly displaying its power.

At first glance, the technique could even look graceful and dignified.

Beneath that calm surface, however, lay deliberate cruelty.

Once a strike landed, the following sequence relentlessly compounded the wound until the victim might begin to think death preferable to enduring the pain.

Savage offense.

Complete protection of the wielder.

A swordsmanship that joined attack and defense while hiding the true danger of both.

*Lurking Embedded Teeth*.

“Perfect for hiding my strength.”

Vikir murmured.

At a glance, the technique still resembled the Stabbing Fang style enough that disguising his true swordsmanship would not be difficult.

Concealment was built into its nature.

He looked around one last time and confirmed that no one was watching.

His heart was still pounding with the exhilaration of advancement.

Bzzzz—

A fly’s wings buzzed faintly near his ear.

Vikir glanced at his wrist.

The black tip of Beelzebub was beginning to protrude from beneath his skin.

“Go back.”

He reprimanded the relic sharply.

Beelzebub withdrew.

At the same time, Vikir coolly calculated how much the demonic sword changed his true combat strength.

Vikir Van Baskerville.

Age: eight.

He could reliably manifest four Fangs.

He now possessed the liquid aura of a Low Sword Graduator.

On top of that came decades of combat experience, an unstable but usable Fifth Fang, and Beelzebub’s monster-absorbing abilities.

‘If I truly commit to a fight, I should be able to deal with a Mid Sword Graduator.’

An assassination?

He estimated his success rate at essentially one hundred percent.

A direct confrontation?

About fifty percent.

“… The problem is how much of this I should reveal.”

Who would believe an eight-year-old had reached Sword Graduator?

Showing everything would be foolish.

Anyone who intended to survive in this world should keep roughly thirty percent of their strength hidden.

“…”

Vikir considered what level of ability would look appropriate for an eight-year-old.

The answer came quickly.

Enough to make Hugo expect more from him.

Not enough to make Hugo feel threatened.

And not enough to make every surrounding enemy become overly cautious.

There was no reason to hide so completely that he remained trapped and powerless.

Showing the right amount of talent could create opportunities to escape Hugo’s constant observation.

‘A provincial assignment, perhaps.’

‘Or admission to an academy.’

As soon as Vikir left the library, he would have to report the result of his visit to Hugo.

He had already decided exactly what performance to give during that test.

Before leaving, however, there was one final task.

Vikir took an old reading magnifier from his pocket and positioned it beside the window.

Sssssss…

Concentrated sunlight darkened the cover of *Lurking Embedded Teeth*.

Fwoosh!

Flame rose from the paper.

Vikir quietly watched the swordsmanship scripture burn.

The book containing the road toward the Baskerville 10th Fang disappeared from the world.

Its contents now existed only inside Vikir’s head.

Before long, panicked voices sounded in the distance.

“What’s that smell?!”

“Sniff— The miscellaneous section!”

“There isn’t even preservation magic over there! Hurry!”

Servants came running with buckets and found Vikir waiting with an apologetic expression.

“I’m sorry. I carelessly left the reading magnifier by the window… If this absolutely must be reported, I’ll explain it properly to the butler.”

The servants immediately waved their hands.

“Oh, Young Master, there is no need for you to apologize.”

“It was our responsibility to watch the area properly.”

“And thank goodness it was only the miscellaneous shelves.”

They had all noticed the phrase *if this absolutely must be reported*.

Vikir looked down at the ashes scattered across the floor.

“They were only worthless miscellaneous books anyway. It may be easier to pretend they never existed.”

He paused.

“And as you know, the butler is already busy with the ruby-mine dispute against the Morgue Clan. Does he really need to be bothered over something this trivial?”

The servants nodded vigorously.

“Ah! Exactly, Young Master.”

“We servants only want the people above us to remain in a good mood.”

“As long as Young Master is willing to overlook this, we would be very grateful.”

For them, avoiding an annoying report was an obvious benefit.

“I understand. You already have enough work maintaining the library, so I’m sorry for adding to it. I memorized the contents of the burned book. I’ll rewrite enough of it later to fill the gap appropriately.”

With that, Vikir turned and walked away.

Behind him, the servants whispered.

‘What a considerate young master. We should tell the other children about this.’

‘Hey! Then you’d also have to mention that a book burned!’

‘Just tell them the good part! Say he was kind to us and made a good impression!’

Vikir’s hearing had grown sharper with his new advancement.

He heard every word.

He did not care.

His attention had already shifted to the next problem.

The moment he left the 10,000 Book Library, Hugo would summon him.

Vikir needed to rehearse exactly how he would answer.
