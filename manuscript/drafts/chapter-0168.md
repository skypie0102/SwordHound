The next morning—

Indulgentia Orphanage woke to devastation.

Every major building—

ruined.

Walls collapsed.

Roofs torn open.

Stone and timber everywhere.

And yet—

everyone who had slept through the night said the same thing.

They had heard nothing.

Not one explosion.

Not one collapse.

Nothing.

As though the entire night had been a dream.

By sunrise—

the place was boiling with activity.

Investigators.

Priests.

Laborers.

Reporters.

Academy volunteers preparing to return to Colosseo stood among the rubble—

stunned.

“What happened here?”

“This is unbelievable.”

“How did none of us hear the buildings collapse?”

Tudor, Sancho, and Pigi stared around them.

They should have been leaving.

Instead—

their feet felt heavy.

For ten days—

they had cleaned these buildings.

Repaired them.

Worked beside the children.

Seeing the orphanage reduced to ruins hurt.

Then Bianca and Sinclaire arrived.

“I heard the Night Hound appeared before dawn.”

“While everyone was asleep…”

The volunteers immediately began talking about the masked criminal who had supposedly attacked the orphanage.

If circumstances had been only slightly different—

any one of them might have crossed his path.

Tudor clenched a fist.

“What a waste!”

“That could've been my chance to catch him and prove I'm a great hero!”

Sancho snorted.

“A man who hides behind a mask and only moves at night?”

“Suspicious by definition.”

Pigi looked worried.

“Aren't you two scared at all?”

“Several people have already died around him.”

Bianca folded her arms.

“If he appears in front of me—”

“one arrow.”

Sinclaire watched the investigation site.

“…He's an interesting villain.”

“There are too many strange things about him.”

The young students clustered together—

talking bravely.

Nearby, puppies gathered together and whimpered.

Elsewhere—

staff were calling groups into order.

“Academy volunteers returning to school—gather here!”

“Quovadis personnel—this side!”

Dolores moved through the disaster zone.

Supervising repairs.

Counting casualties.

Checking the children.

The most astonishing result—

was that there were no casualties.

No dead.

No injured.

Not among the people who had been sleeping there.

But everywhere the Night Hound had passed—

investigators found something else.

Human remains.

Many of them.

Bones belonging to children around thirteen years old.

They were concentrated beneath the residence of **Quilt Rune Indulgentia**.

Reporters flooded the site.

Colosseo's newspaper students joined them.

Dolores did not obstruct the coverage.

If anything—

she quietly encouraged it.

Quovadis had also dispatched investigators immediately.

Young priests from the **New Testament Faction** examined the children—

treated anyone who needed care—

and searched the ruins.

The **Old Testament Faction** tried to enter as well.

They were stopped.

“Her Holiness has not authorized your investigation.”

A massive man blocked their path.

**Mozgus Quovadis.**

Archbishop.

Inquisitor.

New Testament figure.

Old Testament priests protested.

“We are Quovadis too!”

“You cannot discriminate against us because of faction!”

“We have both the right and duty to investigate and aid people in distress!”

“Archbishop Mozgus!”

“Saintess Dolores!”

“This is outrageous!”

“If Cardinal Humbert hears—”

Mozgus simply stood there.

A human wall.

Then he looked toward Dolores.

“Saintess.”

“You should return to the Academy.”

“Leave the site to me.”

“Thank you, Lord Mozgus.”

Dolores touched his arm.

Then lowered her voice.

“…I'm sorry about your brother.”

Mozgus's face tightened.

“We were twins.”

“But we were raised apart from birth.”

“We were never especially close.”

“And our faiths diverged completely.”

He paused.

“So… I will be fine.”

The worry on his face contradicted the words.

Learning that his elder twin had been turned into **Ephebo**—

used as an undead servant—

had struck deeply.

Quilt—

Dantalian—

and the four undead guards had disappeared beneath the ruins.

Quovadis was about to begin a massive internal investigation.

Mozgus surveyed the excavation.

“Quilt was suspicious from the beginning.”

“I still cannot say I trust the Night Hound.”

“Attacking our branches was unlawful.”

“But…”

His jaw tightened.

“At this point, I cannot dismiss his judgment.”

“In one sense, he cut rot out of our family before we even admitted it was there.”

“Personally…”

“I find that difficult not to appreciate.”

Coming from Mozgus—

a rigid man of principle—

the admission carried weight.

He had personally witnessed the Night Hound's work during the Red Death.

Whatever he said publicly—

some familiarity and respect remained.

Dolores nodded.

“Please handle the aftermath.”

“I will.”

Mozgus looked at her.

“Will you return directly to the Academy?”

“No.”

“There is one more matter.”

“What matter?”

Dolores only smiled.

Mozgus studied her.

Something had changed.

Previously—

Dolores had looked strong on the outside while carrying conflict and doubt within.

Now—

she seemed brighter.

Lighter.

Stronger in a different way.

*She has broken through something.*

The soul resonance with the Night Hound—

brief though it had been—

had given Dolores the wonderful, exhilarating sensation of being united with another person in body and mind.

For years—

she had listened to the grief of others.

For the first time—

she had felt completely understood and supported.

The feeling left her deeply stirred.

Dolores stepped forward.

“Everyone from Quovadis!”

“Please listen!”

Priests turned.

So did Academy volunteers.

Dolores swallowed.

**Quovadis.**

An ancient clerical house.

Part of the Rune faith since before the Empire's founding.

A family that had guided rites—

politics—

religious authority—

for generations.

What she was about to do—

could wound that pride badly.

But Dolores now believed something else.

*If our pride has value—*

*we must be able to survive the truth.*

The night had changed her.

She was done hesitating because of other people's eyes.

Done carrying every worry alone.

Old Testament priests still shouted.

“Saintess!”

“Why are we being excluded?”

“Let us participate!”

“We are all brothers and sisters under Rune!”

“If Cardinal Humbert hears about this—”

Dolores reached inside her robes.

Rustle.

Letters.

Stamped envelopes.

Recipients.

Senders.

The seal of House Indulgentia.

Documents sent between Indulgentia and Quovadis.

The Night Hound had handed them to Dolores before leaving.

*Use these somewhere useful.*

That was all he had said.

Dolores opened the first document.

Her voice trembled—

but did not stop.

“Dated Month Zero, Day Zero.”

“Record of indulgence and remission sales.”

“Sender—Quilt Rune Indulgentia.”

“Recipient—Cardinal **Humbert L. Quovadis**.”

She gripped the paper hard enough to wrinkle it.

> **Indulgence Sales Record**
>
> **Viscount Becoin** — murdered parents who planned to donate their inheritance rather than leave it to their children; disguised the deaths as an accident and seized the inheritance.  
> **Absolution price:** 1.5 billion gold.
>
> **Baron Lagso** — sexually assaulted and murdered twin sisters employed in his household; secretly buried their bodies in a sewer.  
> **Absolution price:** 200 million gold.
>
> **Sir Finigig** — murdered a business partner and took sole possession of the investment funds; the partner's surviving family later died by collective suicide under financial pressure.  
> **Absolution price:** 800 million gold.
>
> **Count Ezel** — large-scale tax evasion approaching 100 billion gold.  
> **Absolution price:** 300 million gold.
>
> **CEO of Quica Corporation** — stock-price manipulation associated with the suicides of more than ten thousand small investors.  
> **Absolution price:** 5 billion gold.

It was only part of the ledger.

Quilt—

Dantalian—

had sold absolution through the Old Testament network to nobles and merchants.

Tax evasion.

Market manipulation.

Sex crimes.

Murder for hire.

Arson.

Violence.

Parricide.

Crimes against ordinary law.

Morality.

Humanity itself.

And behind them—

financial links to the Old Testament establishment.

Evidence.

Not rumor.

Not suspicion.

Documents carrying seals.

Records.

Amounts.

Dolores—

a Saintess of Quovadis—

read them aloud to the world.
