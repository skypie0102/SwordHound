**Saint Mecca.**

Population: roughly one hundred sixty thousand.

A resort-like holy city of dense woodland, clean water, white stone, and strict Quovadis order.

People walked solemnly through marble streets.

When the clock tower rang, pedestrians stopped and bowed toward the distant Quovadis residence.

The city's moral rules reached into ordinary life.

Lost money often remained untouched.

Public behavior was tightly controlled.

Dress was restrained.

White cloth dominated the streets.

Vikir watched from above.

“Stifling.”

After two years among Ballak, Saint Mecca felt even more rigid than he remembered.

He waited until night.

Then moved toward the city's outer slums.

Broken windows.

Cracked walls.

Stagnant water.

Steep stairways.

Poverty looked similar under every great house.

Quovadis merely added religion to it.

People guilty of minor offenses—not serious enough for prison, but enough to be excluded from the holy center—were pushed outward.

Checkpoints and patrols kept them there.

Vikir climbed toward the darkest part of the district.

The clock struck twelve.

Those still outside bowed toward Quovadis.

The Hound of the Night did not.

◆◆◆

At a neighborhood well, Vikir removed a small vial.

It contained contaminated material taken from Aheul's illness.

His purpose was brutal and calculated.

He introduced the Red Death into the well.

Not to start an uncontrolled massacre.

To force Quovadis to recognize the disease immediately, draw its healers into action, and expose the Leviathan connection before the epidemic devastated the region.

Vikir knew he was creating danger to innocent people.

That was why timing mattered.

The holy house needed to react before anyone died.

Then he heard voices.

Children were approaching with insect nets and collecting jars.

One peered into the darkness.

“Who's there?”

Vikir remained almost invisible under his cloak and mask.

He could have simply disappeared.

Instead he chose something the children would remember.

His eyes flashed behind the lenses.

“I curse this well.”

The children collapsed to the ground in terror, some of them wetting themselves.

“Anyone who drinks from it will die.”

They screamed.

“Ghost!”

“The well is cursed!”

“I'll never drink from it!”

They ran.

Vikir watched until they were safely gone.

Then he buried a marker near the well.

A serpent emblem.

**Leviathan Clan.**

By now Vikir's memories had aligned with the evidence.

The suspicious agents at the jungle's water source.

The red liquid.

The poison-house emblem.

The Red Death.

Leviathan had been involved.

Quovadis and Leviathan had been enemies for generations.

If Red Death appeared at Quovadis's home—

Quovadis would suppress the outbreak quickly.

Then it would ask who caused it.

Vikir wanted that conflict pointed in the correct direction.

He looked toward people already approaching the well with buckets.

“Time.”

He needed Quovadis mobilized immediately.

◆◆◆

The Hound of the Night moved to the main Quovadis residence in central Saint Mecca.

Carriages still crowded the entrance despite the hour.

Sickness did not respect bedtime.

Nobles and wealthy merchants had come seeking treatment.

Their clothes were immaculate.

Their faces were not.

“Saintess! Please let me in!”

“I heard you were resting here!”

“Please examine me only once!”

Rumor had spread that **Dolores L. Quovadis** was staying in Saint Mecca during the academy break.

Direct-line Quovadis.

A young house head within the family.

Saintess.

A **second-year** student at Colosseo Academy.

Already vice president of the student council.

Every person waiting outside believed status or money should move them to the front.

At last a gentle but firm voice came from beyond the door.

“Who seeks me at this hour?”

The visitors erupted.

One recited his noble family connections.

Another listed commercial offices, honorary titles, and past service under Boston Terrier Le Baskerville.

A third promised estates, villas, and carriages in exchange for treatment.

The door remained closed.

“I do not see why status or wealth gives me reason to open my door in the middle of the night.”

Eventually the disappointed petitioners left.

Only Vikir remained.

He knocked.

The same voice answered.

“Who seeks me at this hour?”

Vikir considered what he knew about Dolores.

Before regression, he had seen her on battlefields.

White divine power.

Robes soaked in blood that was not her own.

Endless wounded soldiers.

A saintess who worked where suffering was worst.

Titles would not impress her.

Money would not impress her.

So Vikir answered simply.

“Only a lost lamb.”

Silence.

Then—

CREAK.

The door that had remained closed to nobles and merchants opened for him.
