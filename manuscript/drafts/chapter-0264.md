# Chapter 264: The Season of Redemption (1)

After the long trip—

Vikir finally returned to his dormitory at Colosseo Academy.

“You go wash up first, Vikir.”

“I’ve got some things to take care of.”

“What things?”

“Some personal work I had to put off because of the University League.”

The moment Pigi entered the room—

he started organizing a pile of papers.

Numbers.

Tables.

Charts.

Something financial.

Vikir said nothing.

He went straight to the bathroom.

◆◆◆

Warm water poured down.

Baby Madam sat beneath the stream.

“Hack, hack.”

The water flattened her dark body until she almost seemed to melt into it.

Normally—

Vikir might have patted her head.

Today, however—

he stood in silence with a faint frown.

Because something else would not shut up.

The red-chain pendant hanging from his neck.

The inverted pentagram.

The Seventh Corpse—

Decarabia.

[HAHAHAHA!]

[I AM DECARABIA!]

[A FORBIDDEN EXISTENCE!]

[SAVIOR OF A SPECIES!]

[THE UNION OF GREAT DESTRUCTION AND GRAND CREATION!]

[AND IN THIS CYCLE I HAVE BEEN BLESSED WITH A BEAUTIFUL AND ADORABLE MISTRESS!]

Only Vikir could hear its voice.

Which meant—

only Vikir had been suffering through the nonstop monologue all the way back.

Decarabia seemed extremely pleased to hang around his neck and chest.

Its excitement remained high.

Right up until Vikir entered the bathroom—

and removed his clothes.

[Contractor!]

[Do not forget that anyone could steal me!]

[You should keep me with you even while bathing—]

[…?]

Decarabia’s eye opened wider.

Its pupil trembled.

Then—

slowly—

it looked down.

Vikir continued washing.

Decarabia fell silent.

Completely silent.

The eye that had been glancing up and down—

closed.

Vikir noticed the sudden peace.

He tapped the pendant with a mana-filled finger.

[…I am Decarabia.]

[…Do you have an order?]

“No.”

[…Then.]

Its eye opened briefly—

then snapped shut again.

The answer was dry.

Almost bureaucratic.

“…Good.”

“At least it’s quiet.”

Vikir nodded with satisfaction.

◆◆◆

When Vikir stepped out—

Pigi was already preparing to leave.

Steam rolled from the bathroom behind him.

Vikir dried his hair with a towel.

“Vikir!”

“I’m heading out!”

Pigi carried a stack of papers so high that it nearly blocked his face.

“Where?”

“I’m going to show my independent work to a professor.”

Independent work.

Student projects outside the Academy’s assigned curriculum.

Pigi grinned.

“There’s going to be a **Mock Investment Competition** soon.”

“A mock investment competition?”

“Yeah.”

“You research promising businesses, industries, or opportunities and decide where to invest.”

“It’s sponsored by House Bourgeois.”

“If you win—or even place high—you get all kinds of benefits.”

“Didn’t the Survival Contest just end?”

“How many competitions does this school have?”

“A lot.”

Pigi began counting.

“Swordsmanship.”

“Archery.”

“Magic.”

“Dance.”

“Eating.”

“Boxing.”

“Theater.”

“Singing.”

“Raising exceptional beetles…”

“There’ve been even more lately.”

Ever since Vikir had donated the fortune from his Gnoll-pelt sale to the scholarship foundation—

the Academy had begun inventing competitions under every possible pretext.

All of it—

a way to distribute scholarship money.

*Professor Banshee works quickly.*

Vikir silently approved.

He had doubted whether the scholarship foundation would function properly.

The speed of the results after the Survival Contest suggested otherwise.

Pigi scratched his cheek.

“My grades aren’t great.”

“So I have to make up credits with bonus points from competitions like this.”

In combat—

Pigi was ordinary.

Possibly a little below average.

In almost everything else—

he was talented.

Administration.

Law.

Medicine.

Art.

Finance.

Information gathering.

Analysis.

Summarization.

He even kept track of the latest news about important people.

Apparently—

he had also won several insect-raising competitions.

*If Cindywendy saw him, she’d recruit him immediately.*

Vikir nodded.

“You’ll do well.”

Pigi froze.

Vikir rarely encouraged people verbally.

That made the words hit harder.

“…Thanks, Vikir.”

“You’re always encouraging me.”

His eyes reddened.

Then he clenched both fists.

“All right.”

“I’m definitely beating that person this time.”

“…Who?”

“Sinclaire!”

“She’s entering too!”

Sinclaire.

Top first-year of the Hot Department.

A commoner by public record.

A prodigy even among Colosseo’s prodigies.

Her magical talent rivaled students from House Morgue.

She could also use swords, spears, bows, and other cold weapons.

Theory.

Written tests.

Certificates.

Part-time work.

She seemed capable of everything.

Even while working—

she continued placing first or second in practical and written subjects.

And not long ago—

as a first-year—

she had finished eighth overall in the University League.

Pigi’s competitive spirit burned.

“Apparently she’s also excellent at accounting and paperwork.”

“That’s part of why she works in the library.”

“I see.”

Vikir had not known Sinclaire was talented in economics and investing.

Pigi continued.

“House Bourgeois is sponsoring the competition.”

“People are entering for prize money, scholarships, and even possible jobs after graduation.”

“Sinclaire probably is too.”

“I’m not losing.”

“I’ll be back!”

Pigi waved.

Several papers slipped from his arms.

He gathered them—

then hurried down the hall.

One sheet remained behind.

Vikir picked it up.

Pigi looked back.

“Oh, that one doesn’t matter.”

“It’s just a competition poster.”

Then he vanished around the corner.

The room became quiet.

Vikir sat at the desk.

Baby Madam circled Decarabia.

“Hack, hack.”

[Do not drool on me, filthy puppy—]

[No.]

[Spider?]

[What are you?]

Baby Madam climbed closer.

[Stop.]

[Do not touch me.]

[Do not climb on me.]

[Keep your distance—]

Decarabia paused.

[…Female?]

“Hack…”

[Oh.]

[A lady.]

[Then I shall overlook this small offense.]

[In fact, perhaps I should simply close my eye and surrender myself—]

Vikir poured mana into the pendant.

“Open your eye.”

[…]

Decarabia hesitated.

Then obeyed.

[Fine.]

[My eye is open.]

[Perhaps I am discovering a new preference.]

[And maybe that face works better as a man after all—]

“What are you talking about?”

“Open your eye and tell me about the Ten Corpses.”

Vikir slapped the pendant lightly.

Decarabia corrected itself immediately.

[Ah.]

[The thing you asked earlier.]

[The Sixth Corpse?]

“Yes.”

Vikir wanted information about his next prey.

The Sixth Corpse.

Decarabia thought.

[The Sixth was close to the Ninth.]

[Those two were almost always together.]

Information Cindywendy had never uncovered.

Even a tiny piece mattered.

*Ninth Corpse.*

*Dantalian.*

*He wore Quilt Rune Indulgentia’s skin.*

Dantalian—

through Quilt—

had been deeply involved with Quovadis’s Old Testament Faction.

And the Sixth Corpse had been close to Dantalian.

*Then investigating the Old Testament Faction may lead to the Sixth.*

House Indulgentia was gone.

Dantalian was dead.

Yet the Old Testament Faction still issued indulgences.

Dolores’s Ninety-Five Refutations had briefly disrupted the trade.

But public demand returned.

The sale had resumed.

A new season had begun.

New indulgences were circulating.

And—

absurdly—

they were being discounted.

*They must be making a fortune.*

Vikir decided he needed to investigate Quovadis again.

Maybe the clue would be inside the Old Testament Faction.

Maybe it would lead somewhere else.

Then—

something like intuition touched him.

A feeling with no logical basis.

“….”

His gaze shifted toward the door.

The poster Pigi had dropped lay there.

**<Mock Investment Competition>**

**The current of the economy is created by humans—yet cannot be predicted by human strength alone.**

**Only the intuition of a rare few geniuses can touch that enormous current.**

**If you believe you are one of them, pay attention.**

**This competition will be held at a place called the ‘spine of the economy.’**

**It may become the stairway that leads you onto the great stage of finance.**

**Eligibility:** All Academy students  
**Registration:** Within one week of poster distribution  
**Note:** The poster will naturally disappear after one week  
**Organizer:** House Bourgeois

Vikir stared at the poster.

For some reason—

the timing bothered him.
