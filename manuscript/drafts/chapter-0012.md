# Chapter 12: The Gluttonous Flies (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/012.txt`. Canonical English forms for Cain Baskerville, Abel Baskerville, Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, Red Fang Mountain, Cerberus, and other already established entities are normalized from current project/Fandom evidence. Recovered English Chapter 12 is an aligned secondary reference only.

Cerberus lay sprawled on the ground.

Greasy saliva and feces leaked from its three mouths and its rear.

At the same time—

Sssssss…

The soul drawn out of the dead Cerberus flowed into Vikir’s body.

That mysterious force—called karma, cause and effect, experience, and many other things—refined the body and spirit of the one who slew a monster.

“This thing is going to be a pain to carry.”

Vikir tried moving Cerberus’s corpse, then quickly gave up.

He decided to leave the body where it was for now.

Its cause of death was obvious enough, and plenty of Guide Dogs had seen Vikir sharpening wooden spears around this area.

He could simply remove several important parts and organs, hide them somewhere safe, and use those later as proof if necessary.

More importantly—

“This isn’t what matters right now.”

Vikir stepped past Cerberus and looked toward the deep hollow from which it had come.

Cerberus was fundamentally a territorial monster.

Whether barbarians had driven it down here or some other circumstance had forced it from its usual range, one thing was almost certain.

Somewhere nearby was a dungeon Cerberus had been guarding.

A dungeon was generally a cave-like place containing treasure, though most such places also served as the lairs of powerful high-ranking monsters.

‘Cerberus is dead. Whatever was inside may already be gone.’

Monsters were naturally drawn toward dense demonic energy.

That meant a dungeon capable of attracting Cerberus had a good chance of containing a relic saturated with that kind of power.

Cerberus was not the sort of creature that simply chose any random cave as its home.

Vikir sharpened the senses peculiar to a Baskerville hound and carefully searched the territory beyond the examination boundary.

The filth clinging to his body carried enough of Cerberus’s scent that Hellhounds and most ordinary monsters kept their distance.

His hunter’s eyes followed traces left among the dry earth, rotting leaves, damp roots, and shadowed ground.

Footprints.

Saliva.

Broken brush.

He pushed through withered thorns, charred roots, and old trees collapsing into rot.

Before long, the depression ahead opened into something deeper.

A dungeon.

It was a cave sunk into low ground between enormous earthen mounds, hidden so cleverly from above that a traveler could pass nearby without ever noticing it.

Cerberus had not built this place.

The dungeon had plainly existed long before the monster arrived.

Vikir even remembered it from his previous life.

It would not be discovered for roughly another ten years.

“… But I remember it being empty by then.”

Now, however, something was different.

Behind the smell of rot lingered a faint but unmistakable trace of demonic energy.

An inexperienced hound might have overlooked it.

Vikir did not.

Crack!

He smashed through part of the dried mound and slid down with the collapsing earth.

A dungeon of red soil and stone appeared beneath him.

The place seemed connected to Red Fang Mountain, one of the branches of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Veins of raw ruby protruded from the earthen walls.

Beyond the entrance stretched a winding passage so dark that Vikir had to feel his way forward by hand.

Unexpectedly, the darkness did not last.

The passage opened into a broad stone chamber illuminated by fist-sized chunks of raw ruby embedded in the walls.

Their dim red glow stained the chamber the color of blood.

Vikir watched his long shadow stretch across the stone.

“… An empty dungeon?”

At first glance, there was nothing there.

No.

Not quite nothing.

At the far end of Vikir’s shadow lay a skeleton.

Many of its bones were broken, as though it had been cut and battered in a brutal fight.

The ruby light also revealed old bloodstains covering the chamber floor.

The stains had dried to a deep brown long ago.

Beside the skeleton, Vikir found a single page of writing.

The note began:

‘My real name no longer matters. For convenience, call me Cain.

Why stain clean white paper with my name?

After thinking on it for a long time, I decided to leave these words so that those who come after me will not repeat the same mistake.’

An unusual beginning.

Vikir continued reading by the red light.

It did not take long for him to recognize the script.

The writing used an old hand passed down through the Baskerville Clan.

‘A Baskerville ancestor?’

It was a reasonable conclusion.

The note continued.

‘This stone chamber belongs to an ancient dungeon spoken of in our family legends.

My younger brother and I found it by chance.

We endured countless trials and finally reached this “last room.”

We killed innumerable monsters on the way here.

But the final task given to us in this chamber held us here for three whole years.’

Vikir raised his head.

What kind of task could trap two Baskervilles in one room for three years?

The answer was written on the wall toward which the skeleton’s skull pointed.

‘One when entering.

Two once inside.

One when leaving.’

A strange riddle.

Vikir returned to the note.

‘My brother and I pondered those ominous words for a very long time.

But this dungeon has been handed down as a Baskerville legend, so surely the meaning is obvious.

Does it not mean that only one of us may obtain what we desire here and leave?

My brother and I were twins.

Inside our mother’s womb we began as one, then entered the world as two.

If we wished to obtain this dungeon’s reward and leave, we believed we had to become one again.

Two bodies cannot literally merge back into one.

So only one answer remained.

One of us had to kill the other.’

Encouraging rivalry among siblings was an old Baskerville tradition.

The two brothers had begun a long battle.

It ended with the death of the younger twin.

‘Then the younger brother here would be Abel.’

Cain and Abel.

An elder brother who killed his younger brother because he believed only one could be chosen.

Vikir looked at the skeleton lying on the floor.

Even in this windless chamber, the bones had nearly weathered away with age.

But the note contained one more surprise.

‘I killed my brother after a terrible battle.

Now I am the only one left in this chamber.

And still nothing has changed.’

The handwriting below that line became increasingly distorted.

The letters twisted so badly that Vikir had to decipher them one by one.

‘There were two and now there is one, but I received nothing!

If I receive nothing, I cannot leave!

Nothing! Nothing!’

The final section appeared to have been written much later.

The handwriting had become neat again.

‘I will stop wasting my time and leave this place.

If some brave descendant finds this chamber in the distant future, I have only one piece of advice.

Leave immediately.

This devil’s den does nothing but confuse the mind and leave you empty-handed.’

The meaning of Cain’s warning was clear.

He and Abel had overcome every trial in the dungeon and gained nothing for it.

Cain wanted whoever came next to turn around rather than waste the same years and blood.

Vikir reached a different conclusion.

‘I’m not a twin.’

The riddle still applied even though he had entered alone.

That meant Cain and Abel had misunderstood it from the beginning.

‘One when entering. Two once inside. One when leaving…’

Vikir looked behind him.

The tunnel was utterly black, like an enormous open mouth staring back at him.

Ahead lay the chamber drenched in ruby light.

Then he looked at the skeleton again.

Something extended from the end of the bones across the floor.

His own shadow.

‘… I see.’

Vikir clenched his fist.

Then he smashed the largest ruby illuminating the chamber.

Crash!

The gem shattered.

The weak magic contained in it vanished with the light.

Complete darkness swallowed the room.

Then—

Rrrrrrrrrumble—BOOM!

One side of the stone chamber collapsed.

The wall was astonishingly thick—tens of meters of stone.

No one looking at it from inside the chamber would ever have imagined it was a door.

Vikir nodded.

The answer had been simple.

A shadow.

In the pitch-black entrance tunnel, a person and his shadow were effectively one.

Upon entering the ruby-lit chamber, the light separated the shadow from the body.

Two.

Destroy the ruby and restore complete darkness, and the shadow returned to the body.

One again.

Only then did the final section of the dungeon open.

Vikir carefully felt his way forward through the darkness.

Fortunately, the space beyond the hidden wall was a flat, straight passage with no further traps.

Cain and Abel had apparently cleared away all the monsters long ago.

Only the dungeon’s reward remained.

Before long, Vikir’s fingertips touched something cold.

A sword hilt.

The weapon stood vertically embedded in stone.

An inscription had been carved in front of it.

Vikir traced the letters with his fingers and slowly read them.

‘Only one who carries the blood of Baskerville may draw this sword…’

The inscription included the Baskerville name.

And the name of the sword itself.

The instant Vikir understood that name, shock struck him like lightning.

“… So this is the relic that was found here.”
