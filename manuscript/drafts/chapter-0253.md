The assignment from the Iron-Blooded Sword House Baskerville.

Even without opening the scroll, everyone thought they knew what it would be.

Some savage monster rampaging along the western frontier.

After all—

what else would interest the bloodthirsty mad dogs of Baskerville?

That was how the world saw the family.

Vikir chose it anyway.

*I need to go home.*

He had business at the main estate regardless.

For someone who hated House Baskerville as deeply as Vikir did, going home could hardly be called a vacation.

Still—

it was an opportunity to see how thoroughly the family had been cleaned up.

After Andromalius had worn the family’s skin like a disguise, Vikir wanted to know how many demonic remnants still survived inside the House.

*And I can deal with the assignment while I’m there.*

Getting Hugo’s seal should be easy enough.

Honestly—

the task looked almost free.

◆◆◆

Vikir reassured his worried friends that monster hunting was his specialty.

That was easy for them to believe.

He consistently scored well in Introduction to Monster Studies.

And not long ago, he had returned with a large haul of Gnoll pelts.

By the next morning—

before most of the students were even awake—

Vikir left Mage Tower.

He concealed his route completely.

Whenever the highest-ranked students departed to begin the League’s second-stage missions, all kinds of people followed them.

Reporters.

Paparazzi.

Guild recruiters.

Scouts.

And people with less innocent motives.

Vikir carefully checked every shadowing presence for signs of demonic involvement.

Nothing stood out.

Only after confirming that did he begin the journey home.

Of course—

Vikir was exceptionally skilled at hiding his tracks.

Anyone trying to follow him would lose him quickly.

At dawn, fog lay across the station.

Vikir had already bought his ticket for the train heading toward Baskerville territory when—

“…!”

A familiar face caught his eye.

On the neighboring platform—

in the last row of a train—

sat a white-haired girl staring quietly through the window.

Sinclaire.

Vikir stood on the platform for trains traveling in the opposite direction.

*East?*

He moved behind the iron framework of an unfinished station expansion and watched her through a narrow gap.

Sinclaire had not noticed him.

She simply sat there—

looking out at the pale dawn.

The train she had boarded was heading east.

There were no famous Great Houses in that direction.

*Didn’t Sinclaire choose the Quovadis assignment?*

Her mission was supposed to involve proposing a method of suppressing heresy and cults from the perspective of House Quovadis.

Quovadis territory lay to the west.

Yet Sinclaire was traveling in the exact opposite direction.

Only she knew why.

“…”

Vikir did not investigate.

He turned away.

That, to him—

was the proper distance between acquaintances.

◆◆◆

Hugo looked very different from how Vikir remembered him.

The old Hugo had possessed the cold eyes of a reptile.

A thin face.

A sharpness that seemed to hang over him like a veil.

But now—

“Heheheheh.”

The man sitting before Vikir looked almost absurd.

He had gained weight.

His beard had grown back.

And it had been braided like a little girl’s hair.

A clumsy ribbon was tied at the end.

“It has been a long time, my son.”

Hugo greeted him with an attempt at solemnity.

The solemnity lasted only a few seconds.

“Look at this.”

He proudly lifted his beard.

“Pomeranian braided it for me.”

“I have no idea how she managed something this beautiful.”

“There has never been a genius like her in the history of Baskerville.”

“Divine talent.”

Hugo held the sleeping Pomeranian in a cloth sling and grinned from ear to ear.

Vikir silently looked around.

“…”

Hugo’s study had changed just as much.

The cold black-stone floor was now covered with soft, colorful puzzle mats.

They would cushion a child if she fell.

And could be used for play at any moment.

Wind chimes shaped like stars and planets hung from the ceiling.

Glow-in-the-dark stars had been stuck overhead.

The pink curtains depicted a magical princess defeating monsters with a wand—

a scene from a currently popular children’s story.

Charts of Imperial letters covered the walls.

The shelves that had once been packed with sword manuals and military texts—

were filled with children’s books.

And then came the greatest change of all.

“You have returned, Young Master?”

Butler Barrymore entered carrying tea.

He was wearing cow-pattern pajamas.

Something like a nose patch sat over his nose.

A rattle rested in one hand.

Pomeranian, naturally—

showed no particular interest in any of it.

Hugo spoke with complete seriousness.

“My granddaughter grew up among barbarians without being able to enjoy even the most basic things.”

“As her grandfather, I must make up for what she missed.”

Vikir could only nod awkwardly.

The documents Hugo had been reading looked like ordinary Baskerville administrative paperwork.

They were not.

Every page was about Pomeranian.

**<Report on Lady Pomeranian’s Growth>**

— On Month 0, Day 0, at 00:00, Lady Pomeranian saw the ornament on a teacup during morning tea and said, “Ruby pretty.”

**Emergency Action:** Terminate the alliance agreement with House Morgue and immediately advance on Red Fang Mountain to seize the ruby mines.

— On Month X, Day X, at XX:XX, Lady Pomeranian ate the chocolate pudding served after dinner and said, “Chocolate yummy.”

**Emergency Action:** Recapture the coffee-growing territory beyond the Red Witch’s River from the barbarians, secure the blood-bean plantations, and construct a logistical fortress there.

— On Month #, Day #, at ##:##, Lady Pomeranian looked at the Monster Encyclopedia during reading practice and said, “Bear cute.”

**Emergency Action:** Dispatch a special force to the Seventh Ridge of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain. Kill and skin as many Oxbears as possible.

**Urgent Revision 1:** Report corrected. Lady Pomeranian appears to prefer living creatures.

**Emergency Action 1:** Dispatch a special force to the Seventh Ridge and capture as many Oxbears alive as possible.

**Urgent Revision 2:** Report corrected. Lady Pomeranian appears to require only one small, young, living specimen.

**Emergency Action 2:** Dispatch a special force to capture one newborn Oxbear. Due to the gravity of the operation, force size is not to be reduced.

**Urgent Revision 3:** Report corrected. Lady Pomeranian said, “If the baby bear comes here, its mama will be sad, so I’ll just use a teddy bear!”

**Emergency Action 3:** No matter how I think about it, my granddaughter is too kind and adorable.

**This document is an official operational record of House Baskerville and a first-class military secret. Unauthorized viewing is punishable.**

**Head of House Hugo Le Baskerville — Seal**

Vikir glanced over the document.

Then looked away.

*…What did I just read?*

That was unquestionably the head-of-house seal.

Within Baskerville—

its authority was comparable to the Emperor’s seal itself.

Before his regression, Vikir had spent countless sleepless nights guarding documents stamped with that mark.

He had fought enemies to keep them safe.

And now—

this was what the seal was being used for.

Vikir shook his head.

Then—

he felt Hugo’s eyes on him.

*Careless.*

No matter how ridiculous Hugo looked now—

he was still a Sword Master.

The Sword of the Empire.

Head of House Baskerville.

King of all hounds.

Vikir straightened.

Hugo finally spoke.

“It is time to give the Academy student his Baskerville assignment.”

Five-star difficulty.

The worst category of task.

A special order issued by House Baskerville.

Apparently Hugo had no intention of going easy on Vikir simply because he was his son.

*Monster subjugation?*

*Assassination?*

*Frontier development?*

Vikir did not know what the task would be.

But it would not be simple.

Hugo opened his mouth.

“I have a task for you.”

He paused.

“It is…”

“Childcare.”

Vikir wondered if he had misheard.

“…Childcare?”

“Yes.”

Hugo nodded.

“Childcare.”

Vikir frowned.

“Did you say ‘flesh fangs’?”

“What sort of Fang is that?”

Hugo clicked his tongue.

“What are you talking about?”

“Childcare.”

“Looking after a child.”

He lifted the sling in front of him.

Pomeranian stirred in her sleep.

Hugo’s expression turned grave.

“I’m worried.”

“She has been so listless lately.”

“She barely smiles.”

“She used to laugh every time I showed her my braided beard.”

“…”

“I summoned every doctor in the west just in case.”

“They all said nothing was wrong.”

“Quacks.”

“My granddaughter isn’t smiling and they claim nothing is wrong?”

“I nearly had them all cut down, but Barrymore stopped me.”

Hugo looked directly at Vikir.

“Can you make her laugh?”

Vikir remembered the writing on the assignment scroll.

**Iron-Blooded Sword House / Difficulty: ★★★★★**

*So this is what five stars means?*

*Is childcare really that difficult?*

Vikir sighed and stepped forward.

At that exact moment—

Pomeranian opened her eyes.

“Mm… ah!”

“Samchun!”

“Samchun!”

“…Not samchun.”

Vikir held out his hands.

“Uncle.”

Pomeranian immediately stretched both arms toward him.

Then—

“Oho!”

“She smiled!”

“My granddaughter smiled!”

Hugo began laughing too.

The instant Pomeranian saw Vikir—

she had broken into a huge smile.

At the same time—

**THUMP!**

Behind them, Barrymore—

still wearing cow pajamas—

nodded with satisfaction.

He slammed Hugo’s seal onto Vikir’s mission scroll.

Completion confirmed.

A five-star assignment no Academy student would willingly challenge—

had been cleared instantly.
