# Chapter 403: Jailbreaker (1)

Mealtime.

Vikir ate alone in the mess hall on one of his rare off-duty days.

Black bread.

Pickled sardines.

Wilted greens.

Nothing else.

Even for a Major—

the meal was miserable.

*Surface supplies must be failing.*

If this was what a Major received—

lower-ranking guards had to be eating worse.

And prisoners?

Vikir had recently noticed fewer familiar faces in the labor areas.

Some had probably already starved.

At a table ahead of him—

several lieutenant colonels spoke in low voices.

“Is it true?”

“Yes.”

“The surface is in chaos.”

“Demons and monsters are appearing everywhere.”

“I heard enormous Gates opened.”

“And monsters poured out of them.”

“That explains why prisoner numbers are exploding.”

“Prisoner numbers?”

“The whole Empire may be in danger.”

Some guards dismissed the rumors.

“Demons?”

“How old are you?”

“That’s Quovadis superstition.”

“I believe what I see with my own eyes.”

“And we’re ten thousand meters underwater.”

“How exactly are we supposed to see the surface?”

“The new prisoners all claim they saw Gates and monsters.”

“They’re prisoners.”

“They went insane inside Iron Maidens on the way down.”

Others were less dismissive.

“Something is wrong.”

“Nouvelle Vague has never received this many prisoners at once.”

“Warden Orca himself went to the surface.”

“And Colonels D’Ordume and Souaré went with him.”

“Two of the Five Wardens and the Warden himself leaving together?”

“That has never happened.”

“I heard the surface is practically at war.”

“Not gang fighting.”

“Nation against nation.”

“And there are rumors of demon invasion.”

“Even the patriarchs of the Seven Great Families are under threat.”

Most of the prison guards still did not care.

“Our job is here.”

“The surface has nothing to do with us.”

“If monsters are everywhere above—

isn’t Nouvelle Vague safer?”

The prison’s guards had been cut off from society for too long.

Many had been exiled here in one form or another.

They had little attachment left to the outside world.

Vikir thought differently.

*Nouvelle Vague won’t escape the Demon War forever.*

In his previous life—

the prison remained safe for a time after the war began.

Its location ten thousand meters beneath the sea protected it.

Eventually—

the demons discovered it.

Orca Javert—

the old hero who had suppressed the Riot of Forty-Seven—

and the Five Wardens resisted.

They failed.

Then Nouvelle Vague itself collapsed for a reason unrelated to the demons.

Most guards and prisoners died.

The survivors returned to the surface with a hatred of demons—

joining the Human Alliance or fighting independently.

*That point is coming earlier this time.*

Vikir chewed the limp vegetables.

If produce from the surface had already deteriorated this badly—

the situation above was advancing faster than expected.

“There you are.”

A chair scraped beside him.

Captain Kirko Grimm sat down.

She had fully recovered.

“Promotion really does change the quality of information you receive.”

“What did you hear?”

“Warden Orca is returning.”

“Colonels D’Ordume and Souaré too.”

The three highest figures in Nouvelle Vague.

And they were escorting a huge wave of prisoners.

“How are the guards coming down?”

“D’Ordume and Souaré should use Gulper Eels.”

“Warden Orca is coming **personally**.”

“Personally?”

“As he always does.”

Kirko moved to another rumor.

“One of the incoming prisoners is supposedly a monster.”

“How so?”

“She attempted to assassinate the head of one of the Seven Great Families.”

“She was sent here without trial.”

“Straight to Level Nine.”

“And once Level Ten is finished—

she’ll be transferred there.”

Vikir did not care about the new prisoner.

“When do they arrive?”

“Within a week at most.”

“Then the induction ceremony will happen at the same time.”

Vikir’s eyes cooled.

That ceremony—

was his opportunity.

Bastille oversaw admissions.

As a recently trusted Major—

Vikir could be assigned to assist.

More importantly—

every guard’s attention would be focused on the new arrivals.

Existing prisoners would be watched less carefully.

And Vikir’s plan was not a quiet escape through some forgotten crack.

“…I’ll be busy.”

He stood with his tray.

Kirko immediately stood too.

Food still remained on hers.

“You aren’t finished.”

“Eat.”

“My superior stood up.”

“So I stand.”

“…Why?”

“I’m your adjutant.”

She said it as if nothing could be more obvious.

Ever since the Black Tongue incident—

Kirko had stayed almost constantly at Vikir’s side.

Duty.

Extra assignments.

Even their rooms had been placed next to each other.

Aside from sleeping, bathing, and using the bathroom—

they were rarely apart.

*That will interfere with tonight’s preparations.*

Vikir thought for a moment.

Then deliberately raised his voice.

“So.”

“Are we together tonight too?”

Several guards nearby heard him.

Kirko turned bright red.

“W-what does that mean?”

“That night.”

“You don’t remember?”

“I-I don’t!”

“You said you didn’t want to be alone.”

Kirko’s eyes sharpened.

She answered in an aggressively official tone.

“Do not misunderstand.”

“That night—

my body and mind were unusually weak—”

“You said you didn’t remember.”

“….”

Her face reddened all the way to her ears.

“I thought it was a dream!”

That explanation only made the surrounding guards misunderstand more.

“N-no!”

“I mean I literally thought I was dreaming!”

Kirko fled.

Only then did Vikir return to the matter at hand.

The quiet days of Nouvelle Vague were ending.

*It’s time for the mass jailbreak.*

There was little time left.

An unprecedented storm was about to swallow the prison.

A signal flare for humanity’s counterattack—

on the eve of the war to come.
