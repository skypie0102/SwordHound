House Donquixote’s Invincible Armada.

In naval warfare—

the strongest force in the Empire.

Countless ships advanced through the flood.

Ahead—

darkness swallowed everything.

Vikir stood at the bow of the lead ship.

Sea wind hammered his cloak.

*Endless darkness covering the world.*

*This must be one of the First Corpse’s abilities.*

The Imperial Capital’s sky—

normally bright—

showed neither moon nor stars.

Only—

BOOM!

CRASH!

Enormous tsunamis.

Bianca stared at the approaching wall of water.

“At this rate we’ll capsize.”

Tudor snorted.

“A Donquixote ship?”

“Capsize from a wave like that?”

“Impossible.”

The Invincible Armada justified his confidence.

Not one ship ran aground.

Not one overturned.

Massive vessels were linked by heavy chains—

many ships functioning as one.

A chain formation.

One of Vikir’s proposed tactics.

It dramatically increased the Armada’s ability to break through rough seas.

Camus nodded.

“Good formation.”

“As long as no one uses fire against it.”

“With this rain?”

Vikir looked upward.

“Fire isn’t much of a concern.”

Camus grinned.

“Exactly.”

“My boyfriend really is impressive.”

She punched his arm playfully.

Then—

“…!”

Vikir’s expression changed as she moved closer.

“The smell.”

“…What?”

Camus froze.

Then hurriedly sniffed herself.

“W-what?”

“I smell?”

“I bathe every day!”

“And I brushed my teeth right before coming!”

“I mean, if I’m meeting my boyfriend then maybe I should’ve brought perfume, but bringing perfume to a battlefield seemed strange, and maybe I sweated during morning exercises—”

“Not you.”

Camus went quiet.

“It’s demonic.”

“…!”

“A faint scent like a half-demon.”

Camus’s embarrassment vanished.

“That’s strange.”

“Seere barely occupies any of my body now.”

“I shouldn’t smell strongly enough for you to notice.”

“It isn’t coming from you.”

Vikir remembered the old days.

When Camus—

as the Corpse Queen—

had shared half her body with Seere.

He had memorized that half-demonic scent.

Now—

something similar rode the sea wind.

From straight ahead.

The Imperial Palace.

The heart of the Imperial Capital.

“…!”

Camus noticed it too.

Even from far away—

the smell was dense.

Powerful.

Aiyen dropped from the lookout.

“Something malformed.”

“Not an ordinary demon.”

Her time inside the Hell Tree—

killing demons, monsters, and spirits—

had trained her senses too.

Dolores joined them.

“The feeling is different.”

“More ominous.”

“Unstable.”

“Not just stronger.”

Vikir looked at Decarabia.

“What does that mean?”

Decarabia hesitated.

[Think of humans using yourself as the standard.]

[You are one.]

[If another separate human exists, there are two.]

[Then three.]

[Four.]

[Five.]

Vikir frowned.

[The First Corpse does not work that way.]

[Two can become one very quickly.]

[And one can become two just as quickly.]

[The standard separating “self” from “not self” lies outside ordinary human concepts.]

[So normal reasoning cannot measure it.]

“…I have no idea what you’re talking about.”

Vikir’s expression hardened.

“So it has a strange body like yours?”

Decarabia itself—

an object-type demon—

was already an exception among demons.

If the First Corpse was similarly anomalous—

the battle could become troublesome.

[I’m not entirely sure either.]

[Number One has always been full of secrets.]

Decarabia lowered its voice.

[But there is one thing I know.]

[The role of the **one who closes the gate**—

and the role of the **one who opens the gate**—

are separate.]

“That’s all?”

[That is all I know.]

Vikir disliked conversations like this.

He had spent his life preferring direct answers.

He was about to press Decarabia again—

when Cindywendy shouted from another ship.

“Emergency!”

“Tsunami ahead!”

Everyone grabbed the railings.

A colossal mass of water rose.

Like a black mountain lifting out of the sea.

Almost touching the sky.

Even Tudor’s face tightened.

A wave of that scale could threaten any fleet.

Then—

“Hehehe.”

Marquis de Sade sat at the bow—

fishing.

Completely relaxed.

“What are you all afraid of?”

He tilted his head.

“Have you forgotten who is traveling with you?”

Before anyone could answer—

BOOOOM!

The tsunami split.

Something seized the wave from below—

and dragged it down.

Water collapsed.

Then—

a hand rose along Sade’s fishing line.

A massive black-and-white killer whale surfaced beside the ship.

Orca—

in his full aquatic beast form.

“Sometimes I need to loosen up in this body.”

He accepted a pipe from Lieutenant Bastille.

The broken pieces of tsunami still became enormous pillars of water.

Those—

the allied leaders destroyed one by one.

SLASH!

A black bird landed on the railing in front of Vikir.

Osiris.

After cutting apart another wave—

he felt Vikir watching him.

Perhaps because they were brothers—

Osiris understood immediately.

“You’re thinking about Father.”

Vikir did not answer.

Osiris did not push.

He only said:

“I don’t understand what he did either.”

“….”

“But I can empathize with it.”

Vikir looked at him.

Understanding—

and empathy.

Different things.

Rarely separated so clearly.

Osiris turned away.

His gaze went toward Cindywendy on the neighboring ship.

“Maybe one day you’ll understand too.”

Then—

quietly—

“Though understanding isn’t always necessary.”

He vanished into the storm.

Vikir stood still.

The women following him.

His father—

now ash.

The cooled Gate of Destruction.

The final conversation still waiting with the First Corpse.

The ship rocked.

His thoughts rocked with it.

Vikir inhaled.

A ship could sway.

His mind could not.

Not now.

He shook away everything unnecessary.

The future could wait.

Only the enemy ahead mattered.

“Demons must die.”

That was enough.

Then—

between the black sky and black water—

a sharp structure rose on the horizon.

Kirko shouted.

“Is that the Central Clock Tower?”

“I’ve seen it in books!”

The Central Clock Tower—

symbol of the Imperial Capital—

still stood.

Nearly submerged.

But upright.

The fleet moved through streets that had once been the center of fashion and wealth.

Past the district containing Colosseo Academy.

Toward the Imperial Palace.

A formal entry into the capital—

and a bitter one.
