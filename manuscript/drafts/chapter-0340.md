# Chapter 340: Trap (2)

**<Floor10 — Lost Paradise>**

A translucent screen floated on the wall.

More catalog items.

**Hot-water faucet — 3 red candies**

**Cedar sauna — 1 red candy**

**Marble bathtub — 2 red candies**

**Luxury bath salts + shampoo + conditioner + body wash — 1 blue candy**

**Oak barrel of aged wine — 1 blue candy**

**Fresh fruit platter — 1 blue candy**

**Braised pork hind leg — 1 red candy**

**Latest card game — 1 blue candy**

**Letter to someone outside the tower — 1 golden candy**

A resting room where candy could buy almost anything.

Comfort everywhere.

The prices were low enough that even challengers with weak stats and low Levels could afford to rest.

Good food.

Soft furniture.

Entertainment.

Contact with the outside world.

The letter system even allowed replies.

Reading one immediately cost another golden candy.

Waiting thirty days—

free.

And once per month—

sending one letter cost nothing.

Fewer than one in a million challengers reached this place.

The next floor was advertised as at least ten times harder.

To most people—

arriving here would feel like incredible luck.

Vikir’s evaluation:

*Obvious trap.*

Almost insultingly obvious.

And still—

astonishingly effective.

Without knowledge from before regression—

even Vikir might have fallen for it.

*Camus suffered badly on this floor too.*

Vikir remembered something Hugo Le Baskerville once taught the young hounds.

*Do you know how to kill a great monster?*

One child answered:

*Attack it with many people.*

Hugo shook his head.

*I mean a monster too strong even for that.*

*A creature ruling an entire mountain.*

*What then?*

Such monsters had once existed throughout Baskerville’s western frontier.

One creature.

One territory.

Too powerful for ordinary armies.

Hugo’s solution—

had not been brute force.

*If you have enough time—*

*even an ordinary person can do it.*

He scattered meat throughout the monster’s territory.

No poison.

No iron traps.

Anything unnatural would only create suspicion.

Instead—

fatty meat.

Rich.

Seasoned.

Fried.

Placed along every hunting route.

At first—

the monster ignored it.

Then tried one piece.

Then another.

Soon—

food appeared everywhere.

No hunt required.

Walk a familiar path.

Eat.

Sleep.

Wake.

Eat again.

Time passed.

The creature grew fat.

Its hide softened.

Claws once sharpened by constant hunting—

dulled.

Teeth once strengthened by crushing bones—

weakened.

Eyes and ears that could once detect prey from enormous distances—

lost their edge.

Fat accumulated around its belly and organs.

A body once built to hunt—

became heavy.

Then Hugo said:

*Now.*

*Now you hunt it.*

Once the monster had lost its wildness—

Baskerville hounds broke their chains.

From that day—

the mountain had a new master.

*This floor works the same way.*

Vikir closed the catalog.

Lost Paradise was dangerous—

not because it contained lethal monsters.

Not because it attacked the challenger.

Exactly the opposite.

Nothing here threatened survival.

That was the trap.

After everything below—

the room felt impossibly peaceful.

For a tiny portion of the stats earned through blood and death—

a challenger could buy comfort.

Leave—

and never return.

Stay—

and avoid a next floor ten times harder.

And then—

the letters.

Family crying outside.

Friends.

Lovers.

One chance to say:

*I’m alive.*

And—

receive an answer.

So challengers stayed.

Then spent candy.

Then more.

Stats disappeared slowly.

So slowly it barely felt like loss.

Strength.

Agility.

Stamina.

Even Level.

And the offer to extract already-consumed stats was worse.

On this floor—

the shop returned full candy value.

Even gave a bonus.

Why *not* cash them out?

Stay long enough—

and the challenger became weaker.

Body first.

Then mind.

Eventually—

too weak to go to the next floor at all.

Trapped forever.

A prison without bars.

The body remained because it was comfortable.

Then the mind chose the prison too.

*I can grow old here.*

*Fat.*

*Weak.*

*And keep writing outside.*

Even more dangerous—

the tower would end if someone else cleared it.

So another thought followed naturally.

*It doesn’t have to be me.*

That thought made leaving even harder.

Vikir looked toward the letter function.

*There’s another trap hidden here too.*

He pressed the free-send button.

Words appeared.

**To: Corporal Janet — 1st Platoon, 4th Company, 207th Regiment**

**I miss you, comrade.**

**Are you doing well over there?**

Send.

A reply arrived within seconds.

**<Read reply immediately?>**

**Cost: 1 golden candy**

**Free after 30 days**

Vikir spent the candy.

The reply opened.

**From: Corporal Janet — 1st Platoon, 4th Company, 207th Regiment**

**Company Commander?**

**Is that really you, Commander Vikir?**

**You’re alive?**

**Everyone is waiting for you!**

**We knew you couldn’t be dead—**

Vikir closed the message.

*As expected.*

*A trap.*

Janet—

the soldier from Vikir’s pre-regression future—

had already died in that timeline.

But the current future had changed.

With Cindywendy’s support—

the woman who might once have become Corporal Janet should now be living an entirely different life.

Wealthier.

Safer.

Never becoming the same soldier Vikir remembered.

The specific **Corporal Janet** in his memory—

no longer existed anywhere.

Yet the reply came instantly.

Perfectly tailored.

Exactly what Vikir wanted to hear.

*Fabricated.*

Something on this floor read the challenger’s memories.

Emotions.

Longing.

Then wrote the answer most likely to make them stay.

The once-a-month restriction made the trap stronger.

A challenger would choose only the person who mattered most.

Write only the words that mattered most.

And expose the deepest emotions attached to them.

Then the tower answered with those emotions.

A demon-written letter—

designed to make the challenger content.

Safe.

Unwilling to descend.

*Petty.*

*Cowardly.*

*Exactly what demons do.*

Vikir stood.

No one outside the tower was waiting for him in the same way.

No one whose longing could hold him here.

So leaving would be easy.

*I’ll stay only long enough to recover my body and mana.*

His wounds were already healing quickly.

Baby Madam and Decarabia—

asleep beneath the bed—

would wake soon.

Then a thought occurred.

*Wait.*

*Who put me on this bed?*

“Oh?”

“You’re awake?”

A familiar voice beside him.

Vikir turned.

Dolores stood there.

Colosseo’s student council president.

Watching him from the other side of the bed.

Then Vikir remembered another detail of this floor.

*There are always two residents.*

A man and a woman.

The tower did not want its prisoners escaping because of loneliness.

So it gave them company.

A companion.

Someone who might become another reason to stay.

Apparently—

Dolores had arrived before Vikir.

She brushed the wound on his forehead gently.

“Lie down a little longer.”

“Most of the external injuries are healed.”

“But the internal damage is still severe.”

Vikir stiffened.

*What is this?*

How was she seeing him?

As Vikir—

the first-year Colosseo student?

Or as Night Hound?

Her speech sounded like she was addressing Vikir.

But the warmth in her eyes—

looked much more like the way she treated Night Hound.

*Did she discover my identity?*

Vikir started to rise.

Dolores grabbed him.

“Oh, no you don’t.”

“I told you to lie down.”

She hooked her arms under him—

and lifted him.

For one instant—

Vikir saw his reflection in her clear eyes.

It was neither Vikir—

nor Night Hound.

Dolores smiled.

“Listen to your big sister, Choco.”

“Before I have you neutered.”

**Choco.**

The name Dolores had once given Vikir’s black-dog form.

His third identity.
