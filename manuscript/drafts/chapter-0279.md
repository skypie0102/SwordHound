# Chapter 279: What Money Can't Buy (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/279.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 277 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Damien Bourgeois, Bartolomeo Bourgeois, House Bourgeois, Wraith Tree, and the Seven Great Houses. Juliet Bourgeois / Romeo remain source-local forms pending direct Fandom access.

Once—

Damien Bourgeois had challenged for the throne of House Bourgeois.

He had lost.

Second place.

A silver medalist in the ugliest contest imaginable.

Now he was Director of the Imperial Money Manufacturing Bureau.

An outside director of the Bourgeois Landowners Guild.

Wealth.

Status.

Authority.

A vast mansion.

Fine carriages.

A beautiful wife.

Everything a person was supposed to want.

So why—

had he lost the will to live?

“…It happens.”

Damien spoke quietly.

As Vikir had guessed—

the answer was his daughter.

“No matter how rich you are—

you cannot force your children to live the way you want.”

“I learned that too late.”

Damien had one child.

One daughter.

**Juliet Bourgeois.**

Long—

wild white hair.

Clear eyes.

A smile as bright and guileless as a puppy’s.

“Like every true Bourgeois heir—

my daughter had to undergo the family test.”

Damien explained.

Collateral children could grow normally inside the family.

Illegitimate children too.

But **true successor candidates** were different.

Like young Baskervilles abandoned to the wilderness—

future Bourgeois heirs had their surnames sealed away.

Then they were dropped into common society.

No family name.

No parental support.

No inherited capital.

Nothing.

They had to climb from the bottom of Imperial society to the top through their own ability.

Survival in society—

Damien said—

was no easier than survival in the wild.

The point was simple.

A child who inherited limitless wealth without understanding ordinary life—

could become useless.

So the future ruler had to experience the world without privilege.

Juliet entered society as a commoner.

For more than a decade—

she never once used Damien’s name.

Never called on House Bourgeois.

Never revealed who she was.

**Esse, Non Videri.**

*To be—

without being seen.*

The principle expected of a Bourgeois heir.

“House Bourgeois always raises at least two possible leaders.”

“So they can restrain one another.”

“Compete.”

“In my generation—

it was my brother and me.”

Bartolomeo.

Damien.

Two brothers fighting for one throne.

Bartolomeo won.

Damien did not give up entirely.

Both brothers—

by coincidence—

had only one daughter.

Damien believed Juliet was more capable than Bartolomeo’s daughter.

More talented.

More adaptable.

He believed that when Juliet eventually returned from her test—

she would reclaim everything he had lost.

Perhaps—

one day—

even rise to the top of the family.

“But that expectation ended.”

“She abandoned the test midway.”

“And returned.”

Juliet had revealed her identity while living as a commoner.

That alone disqualified her from succession.

Then she came home—

and asked Damien for help.

*Father.*

*I’m in love.*

Damien closed his eyes.

“…She met a sick man while volunteering.”

“A man from the bottom of society.”

“And fell in love with him.”

“I sent her there to understand the lower classes.”

“I never imagined she would put down roots there.”

His name was **Romeo**.

Low birth.

No prestige.

No special wealth.

No extraordinary looks.

No notable talent.

No promising future.

A man not much different from an ordinary servant.

His only unusual trait—

was illness.

A weak body.

A short life.

Perhaps that was why he had grown detached from the things other people chased.

Money.

Fame.

Power.

Advancement.

Recognition.

Display.

Success.

Romeo seemed to care about none of them.

He called himself a bard.

Loved nature.

Loved art.

Helped his neighbors.

Spoke kindly.

Served others.

Juliet loved him.

Damien dug his fingers into his hair.

“I was furious.”

“To me—

he looked lazy.”

“Spineless.”

“An incompetent man pretending he was above success because he lacked the ability to climb.”

“A parasite trying to change his life by seducing a rich girl.”

“That was what I believed.”

Juliet did not yield.

*He’s a good person, Father.*

*He has something we don’t.*

*Maybe he can help you recover the happiness you lost.*

*If it’s him—*

Damien refused to listen.

His carefully raised daughter—

his one great hope—

had abandoned her succession test.

Exposed her identity.

And now—

wanted enormous medical expenses for a poor man who was dying.

Damien rejected her request.

Then—

worse.

He hired men.

They dragged Romeo into the rain.

Beat him severely.

Then forced him to break things off with Juliet.

That night—

through lightning and torrential rain—

Juliet confronted Damien.

*…Was it you, Father?*

Damien said nothing.

Juliet’s voice trembled.

*He still never gave me your name.*

*When I pressed him, he laughed.*

*He said every father thinks his son-in-law is a thief.*

*He said he would probably feel the same.*

Juliet lowered her head.

Then turned away.

Damien—

angry—

disappointed—

hesitated.

Then realized something.

If she left like this—

he might never see her again.

He followed.

Juliet climbed into a carriage.

She left.

Damien quickly organized a pursuit.

Later—

they found the carriage again.

Juliet was driving.

Romeo—

barely able to move—

lay in her arms.

The two were trying to disappear.

Far away.

Somewhere no one knew them.

Damien ordered trained riders to chase.

Then—

the obvious tragedy happened.

Thunder.

Rain.

Slippery road.

A frightened horse.

The carriage overturned.

“Unfortunate.”

Vikir’s voice stayed flat.

He had heard the public version before regression.

Hearing it from Damien—

felt different.

Damien spoke again.

“But the rumor is wrong.”

“…?”

“My daughter did not die.”

Vikir’s eyes sharpened.

That—

was new.

“When I reached the wreck—

my daughter was in that man’s arms.”

“Romeo was covered in wounds.”

“Juliet was completely unharmed.”

“I still don’t know how he managed it.”

Juliet regained consciousness.

Immediately—

she asked about Romeo.

Damien chose cruelty.

He told her Romeo was dead.

He hoped the shock would sever her attachment.

He wanted her to move on.

To find someone suitable.

A prince.

A noble heir.

Someone from another Great House.

Someone worthy.

That was when the true tragedy began.

Juliet collapsed.

Later—

she woke.

Then fainted again.

Woke.

Fainted.

Again.

Again.

Dozens of times.

Then—

she stopped waking entirely.

“My daughter isn’t dead.”

“She breathes.”

“But she closed her eyes—

and never opened them again.”

No medicine helped.

No poison.

No divine power.

No alchemy.

No treatment.

Damien spent fortunes.

Called experts.

Bought elixirs.

Collected forbidden knowledge.

Asked priests.

Witches.

Doctors.

Pharmacists.

He even bowed his head to other Great Houses.

Failure.

Failure.

Failure.

The wound was not physical.

The soul itself refused contact.

Damien looked into the night.

“The human heart…”

“Love…”

“I did not understand how powerful it could be.”

“I tried to tear it apart—

for money.”

“For status.”

“For family.”

“For things that now feel microscopic.”

His eyes reflected no starlight.

“I don’t understand why I did it.”

Blood-tinged tears ran down his face.

If Juliet smiled once—

what would all his money matter?

His title?

His position?

He would abandon everything.

Even seeing her awake once—

long enough to apologize sincerely—

would be enough.

But he had already failed every known method.

Vikir—

however—

knew one possibility.

A power that could touch the boundary between life and death.

The **Wraith Tree**.

An artifact capable of reaching the souls of the dead—

the living—

and those trapped between.

Vikir spoke.

“What if I can give you one chance to talk to your daughter?”

Damien’s eyes widened.

Vikir asked again.

“If I do—

what will you give me?”
