A child from the two years Vikir had lived deep in Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

*The girl you helped today is his younger sister.*

*Her name is Aheul.*

After searching his memory—

Vikir finally remembered.

Aheul.

The girl who had once lined up outside his tent—

wanting to become his wife.

The girl Vikir had protected from abusive Bourgeois merchants.

They had grown closer after that.

Aheul had always been physically weak.

An unexplained illness.

Gradually worsening health.

Now—

Vikir understood that the glass earrings brought by those merchants had likely been responsible.

He had found the earrings.

Removed the drugs hidden inside them.

And Aheul recovered.

But after Vikir left Ballak—

he never saw her again.

“…You grew up.”

Vikir sounded almost dazed.

The woman before him looked around thirty.

In truth—

she was close to sixty.

Aheul’s eyes became wet.

“It has been a long time.”

“Big brother.”

◆◆◆

“What happened?”

At Vikir’s question—

Aheul told him everything.

After Vikir left—

trade expanded.

Ballak began formal peace negotiations with the Empire.

For a while—

everything seemed to be improving.

Then—

one day—

Amdusias appeared.

Not through Winston’s body.

Not through a human host.

The demon manifested using its own power.

And attacked Ballak.

*I came looking for the Madame of the Depths.*

*But I can’t find her.*

*So I suppose I’ll take you instead.*

Amdusias—

apparently—

had been collecting terrain and people for the abyss.

For entertainment.

But demonic entertainment demanded blood.

Ballak fought desperately against the unicorn demon destroying their village.

And during that battle—

Akwilla died.

The chieftain—

wounded Amdusias severely before falling.

But she had never fully recovered from her earlier battle with Adonai.

Those lingering wounds became fatal.

“The other warriors scattered too.”

Aheul lowered her eyes.

“That unicorn was too strong.”

Vikir asked:

“How did **you** end up here?”

“I was protecting the babies in the ruins.”

“Then I lost consciousness.”

“When I woke up—

I was here.”

Vikir nodded.

Everything now fit.

Ballak’s disappearance from Cindywendy’s trade network.

Aheul—

a young teenager when swallowed into the abyss—

aging into adulthood here.

And the middle-aged warriors around her—

not recognizing Vikir because they had been infants at the time.

“That’s why the tattoos are faded.”

Vikir looked at them.

“The markings were made when you were babies.”

“As your bodies grew—

the ink stretched and faded.”

Several warriors stiffened.

Vikir pointed.

“You’re Ajin.”

“And you’re Ami.”

“W-what?!”

“H-how do you know?!”

The two middle-aged warriors stared at him.

Vikir remembered.

When he had once demonstrated Baskerville body techniques to Ballak—

the adults had shown little interest.

But several children had eagerly followed him.

Now—

those children stood before him as seasoned warriors.

Behind Vikir—

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca were even more confused.

“How do you know these people?”

“Wait.”

“You actually lived with Ballak?”

“Imperials can do that?”

“I heard they barely accept outsiders.”

“No wonder your archery is so strange…”

Vikir gave them only a brief explanation.

Enough to establish one thing.

To Vikir—

Ballak had once been family.

Aheul looked apologetically toward the four Academy students.

Because Ballak’s mission—

was the same.

**<Mission — Kill or Be Killed!>**

**Never lower your guard!**

**Latecomers grow stronger as time passes!**

**Even if you arrived first, keep improving so they don’t catch up!**

**Recognized only when everyone completes the mission!**

Aheul and the other Ballak warriors had also been ordered to kill Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca.

But Aheul—

unable to bring herself to murder them—

had reduced the conflict to repeated food raids and symbolic fighting.

Vikir frowned.

“You never tried cooperating?”

“We did.”

Aheul sighed.

“But if the floor becomes obviously peaceful—

the fairies intervene.”

“They change the mission.”

“Or give direct kill orders.”

Tudor and the others nodded.

They had experienced the same thing before—

trying to preserve peace with Sinclaire—

only to be punished and separated.

Aheul’s expression darkened.

“I’m more worried about the others.”

“Especially my brother.”

Ahun.

And another person remained even more vivid in Vikir’s memory.

**Aiyen.**

Vikir rubbed his chin.

They had not been taken into the tower.

Then—

what had happened to them?

Aheul swallowed.

“We survived because we ended up here.”

“But the ones who didn’t…”

“What if something worse happened to them?”

Vikir put a hand on her shoulder.

“Don’t imagine the worst.”

“Go outside.”

“See with your own eyes.”

Aheul looked at him.

“…We can leave?”

“Of course.”

Vikir explained what he knew.

The Hell Tree.

The abyss.

The Worldview Absorber.

Amdusias’s habit of collecting fragments of worlds and people.

Aheul stared.

“So this place…”

“is a patchwork made from pieces of worlds that demon stole?”

“And when one floor is cleared—

the world and its inhabitants are freed?”

“Half right.”

“Half wrong.”

Vikir remembered the Beetlemen who had tried to leave.

Lightning.

Ash.

“There is no true liberation—

until the entire tower is cleared.”

This tower was especially cruel toward people who abandoned the process halfway through.

Vikir warned Aheul and the other Ballak warriors.

Do not stop midway.

Do not assume an apparent exit is safe.

Whatever happens—

finish the tower.

Aheul looked at him with deep trust.

“Big brother is still the same.”

Then Tudor raised one hand.

“…Sorry to interrupt.”

“But what do we do about the mission?”

A fair question.

above Ballak.

One group must kill the other.

Otherwise—

nobody cleared the floor.

Nobody left.

“…Then eventually—

we have to choose.”

Colosseo.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Aheul.

“Do you trust me?”

Legends.

Myths.

“Obviously.”

**<Mission — Kill or Be Killed!>**
