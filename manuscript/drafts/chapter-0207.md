# Chapter 207: Festival Night (2)

Festival night.

Stalls—

everywhere.

Music.

Colored lights.

Food piled high.

Students and visitors—

moving through the Academy in costumes.

Zombies.

Skeletons.

Vampires.

Mummies.

Ghosts of every kind.

And—

the Ryukeion tavern.

“Welcome!”

“A ghost tavern full of romance and chivalry!”

“Good food!”

“Better hosts!”

Sancho shouted—

“Hey!”

“You changed the slogan!”

Tudor—

Death Knight—

was already attracting customers.

Black armor.

Long blond hair.

Sword.

A knight's smile.

“Beautiful lady.”

“Would you join me at Ryukeion—

for wine and poetry?”

Several women—

immediately agreed.

Sancho—

Snow Maiden—

had found a different audience.

Muscular men.

A lot of them.

“Hahaha!”

“Come in!”

“Until nine—

blood stew is free!”

“Hotter than a warrior's boiling blood!”

Someone flexed.

“Protein and iron?”

“Perfect!”

Another laughed.

“You're dressed as a woman—

and somehow look *more* masculine!”

Sancho approved.

Pigi—

Fairy—

was handing out flyers.

“Um.”

“Please take one.”

Women stopped.

“Look at him.”

“So cute.”

“Are you really an Academy student?”

“Come on.”

“We'll help you hand these out.”

The tavern—

filled quickly.

Behind it—

Dolores hesitated.

Black gloves.

Black cloak.

Boots.

Plague-doctor mask.

A remarkably accurate—

**Night Hound** costume.

“…This is too provocative, isn't it?”

Tudor happened to pass.

“Socially?”

“Probably.”

Dolores stiffened.

Tudor shrugged.

“But plague-doctor masks existed before the Night Hound.”

“People have worn them during Halloween for years.”

“Banning an entire costume because one criminal uses it—

would be strange.”

He was right.

Several nearby festivalgoers—

already wore similar masks.

Dolores still hesitated.

Because—

she knew something most people did not.

The Night Hound—

was not the monster the newspapers described.

He had saved people.

Saved her.

But—

she could not say that publicly.

Then—

“What are you wearing?”

Dolores turned.

Professor Banshee Morgue.

Hot Department professor.

Ryukeion faculty advisor.

He stared at her outfit.

“President Dolores.”

“I did not expect this degree of poor judgment from you.”

“….”

“Even at a festival—

you should consider the social meaning of your clothing.”

“Why imitate an irredeemable criminal?”

That last word—

hit differently.

*He isn't a criminal.*

*He's the opposite.*

*He is—*

Dolores stopped herself.

The Night Hound—

did not want his identity exposed.

So she swallowed the defense.

Then—

made a different choice.

“I like this costume.”

Banshee blinked.

“…What?”

“I'll serve in it.”

Dolores bit her lip.

But did not retreat.

Banshee looked—

genuinely disappointed.

“I expected better judgment.”

“Still.”

“You are responsible for your own actions.”

“As advisor—

I do not have authority over what students wear.”

“Do as you wish.”

He clicked his tongue.

Entered the tavern.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi—

watched.

“Why is Professor Banshee here?”

“Other professors too.”

“Festival tradition.”

Professors—

visited the stalls run by their students.

Bought food.

Drank.

Talked.

Attracted more customers.

A way—

to support sales.

Banshee—

therefore—

sat.

Then—

spat out the first bite Tudor served him.

“Pah.”

Tudor froze.

Banshee stared at the chicken.

“This is so raw—

a competent veterinarian might still save it.”

The kitchen—

went silent.

Then Banshee continued.

“This pancake contains enough grease to make a foreign nation invade us for oil.”

“This beef should be kept away from the salad.”

“It is so undercooked—

it appears to be eating the salad itself.”

He picked up bread.

“A fine mace.”

“Dense.”

“Heavy.”

“Excellent for breaking a skull.”

“Where is the bread I ordered?”

He drank water.

“The best-tasting item on the table—

is this water.”

A pause.

“Even this water—

is dry.”

Beans.

“Cooked so little—

plant them next spring.”

Pork.

“Can you hear that?”

“The pig is still singing *Hakuna Matata*.”

Banshee—

notoriously picky.

The kitchen students—

visibly wilted.

Dolores finally stepped in.

“If you're going to complain about everything—

why come?”

“I didn't want to.”

Banshee answered immediately.

“But professors are expected to support their students' stalls.”

“So here I sit—

in a dirty chair—

eating cheap ingredients—

prepared under questionable sanitation—

served by unlicensed workers—

for prices that aren't low—

paid in cash—

without a receipt.”

Dolores—

could not find an obvious counterargument.

Banshee stared at the next plate.

Chicken.

Potatoes.

“Of course—

if the food tasted good—

I could forgive some of that.”

“Taste is the foundation.”

“If there is no taste—

every other flaw becomes impossible to ignore.”

“So this dish will probably be—”

He ate.

Stopped.

Chewed.

Again.

“…What?”

Another bite.

“Why is this good?”

Fire-charred aroma.

Chicken—

rich with bone-broth flavor.

Morgue potatoes—

soft.

sweet.

And a mysterious red seasoning—

spicy.

salty.

balanced.

Banshee stared.

“I know this kind of flavor.”

“From restaurants in the Imperial Capital.”

“How is it coming out of a school-festival tavern?”

He ate several more spoonfuls.

Then looked up.

“Who made this?”

“Did you hire a professional chef?”

Dolores frowned.

“Obviously not.”

Tudor—

passing nearby—

answered.

“Oh.”

“You like it?”

“We changed the person running the kitchen.”

Banshee narrowed his eyes.

“To whom?”

“One of us is bizarrely good at cooking.”

“He took over.”

“There.”

Tudor pointed.

Banshee.

Dolores.

Both turned.

A figure stood over a large pan—

controlling the flame.

Black witch hat.

Black cloak.

Straight nose.

Dark brows.

Pale skin.

Red eyes.

A student—

in full witch costume—

stirring food with absurd competence.

Sweat—

at the brow.

Firelight—

across the face.

Banshee stared.

Dolores—

also stared.

“Do we have a girl like that in the department?”

Then Tudor spoke.

“Professor.”

“You forgot the Academy tradition.”

Banshee looked at him.

“…?”

Then—

understanding.

“…!”

Right.

Festival tradition.

Boys dressed as girls.

Girls as boys.

So the witch in the kitchen—

was not a girl.

And there was only one student—

everyone had been waiting to see like this.

“Order!”

“Three Vikir-special chicken stews!”

“Two seafood pancakes!”

“Six sausage-and-vegetable plates!”

“Five beef-bone rice plates!”

“Another new menu!”

“What is this called?”

“No name?”

“You made it from leftovers?”

“Add it!”

“That's special menu number thirty-two!”

The open kitchen—

drew more customers.

First—

men.

Someone suggested announcing that Vikir was male—

to calm things down.

It did not.

Women arrived—

in greater numbers.

The men—

did not leave.

More number tickets—

needed.

More orders.

More food.

At the center—

quietly processing all of it—

was Vikir.
