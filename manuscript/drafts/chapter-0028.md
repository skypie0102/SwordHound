# Chapter 28: Special Laws of Vikir (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/028.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 28 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English forms for Underdog City, Vikir Van Baskerville, Chihuahua Baskerville, Deputy Magistrate, and established Baskerville terminology follow current project/Fandom evidence.

Vikir made his way toward City Hall in the center of Underdog City.

Beyond the cluster of buildings where countless employees worked, the Magistrate's office stood at the very back.

“Clean this place properly. The new Deputy Magistrate arrives today.”

“I wonder who it is.”

“I don't care who comes. I just hope it's someone who understands actual administrative work.”

“Don't get your hopes up. I heard he's only fifteen.”

Employees were cleaning everywhere.

High on the wall hung a banner welcoming Vikir Van Baskerville, the new Deputy Magistrate and Acting Magistrate.

Vikir walked past them without a second glance.

He was not officially due to begin until tomorrow, so none of the City Hall employees paid much attention to him.

“Whose kid is that? Did one of the staff bring him?”

“Hey, you can't come in here.”

“This area is temporarily closed because an important person is arriving tomorrow.”

“Go play outside. There's a playground for the employees' children.”

Several workers stepped in front of Vikir.

“Move.”

Then he showed them his letter of appointment.

Their faces went pale.

“Y-You're early, Acting Magistrate.”

The employees dropped flat to the floor as though they had seen a ghost.

Vikir entered the office and sat in its soft chair.

The first order he issued after taking the Magistrate's authority in all but name was simple.

“Bring me wine and women.”

◆◆◆

Chihuahua Baskerville had worked at City Hall for decades.

At that moment, he felt sick.

‘A drinking party on his very first day.’

And the new Deputy Magistrate was not even fifteen yet.

The previous officeholders had all been suspended or dismissed over bribery allegations, leaving an enormous backlog of work behind them. Now, at the worst possible time, some wet-behind-the-ears brat had arrived and immediately begun fooling around.

‘And in a place of public service, no less. How ridiculous can this kid be?’

Chihuahua Baskerville pressed a hand to his forehead.

He had been born outside the clan. His exceptional administrative ability had earned him the Baskerville surname.

But without a middle name such as Le, La, or Van, he remained an administrator rather than a member of the bloodline.

Even so, he had always worked hard.

“Damn it. There's already more work than we can handle, and now some playboy shows up... I actually had a little hope when I heard he came from the main Baskerville family.”

Even when Chihuahua heard that a fifteen-year-old would take the post, he had refused to abandon that last sliver of hope.

Now the clinking of glasses, women's laughter, and entertainers' songs still drifted in through the window.

Chihuahua irritably twisted his goatee and tossed aside a folder.

“Fine! Let it all fall apart! Let Underdog City go to hell for all I—”

He never finished the complaint.

Creak—

The office door opened and a boy appeared.

Vikir Van Baskerville.

The newly appointed Deputy Magistrate.

At last, he had come into the office to work.

“You're the General Secretary?”

Chihuahua straightened and put on a respectful expression.

“Yes. Chihuahua Baskerville.”

“You have no middle name.”

Chihuahua's expression stiffened slightly.

Having no middle name such as Van meant that he came from outside the family.

Ordinarily, outsiders recruited into Baskerville service worked under contract for three years. If they proved themselves, they received the Baskerville surname and became formal members of the clan's organization.

Staffordshire and Chihuahua were both examples of that path.

Strictly speaking, they were not blood relatives. They were retainers.

New Deputy Magistrates had often used that distinction as an excuse to discriminate against people like him.

So Chihuahua braced himself for the same treatment again.

Instead—

“Good.”

Vikir's response caught him completely off guard.

Chihuahua's eyes widened.

Vikir, meanwhile, searched his memories.

‘Chihuahua Baskerville. Right. I know that name.’

He was thin, with a crafty-looking face made even more suspicious by his goatee.

In reality, however, he was remarkably straightforward.

Because he had come from outside the clan, he had endured constant discrimination, and Magistrates and Deputy Magistrates had stolen credit for many of his accomplishments.

That was something Vikir could understand.

Vikir trusted blood relatives within the clan very little.

Someone from outside who had already proven his ability was another matter.

“I skimmed your personnel record on my way in. You were born in Underdog City?”

“Ah? Y-Yes. I was born and raised here.”

“Then you understand this place better than I do. I'm the newcomer.”

“...Yes, sir.”

The answer escaped Chihuahua before he realized what he had said.

Past experience told him exactly what should come next.

‘If you know so much, do it yourself.’

‘If you're so capable, why are you working under me?’

‘Are you trying to steal my achievements?’

‘Fine. Then you do everything.’

He had heard every variation.

Yet Vikir surprised him again.

“I know how hard the people who actually work in the field have it. I have no intention of interfering with the principles, habits, or working styles they've developed for themselves.”

Chihuahua stared blankly.

He had never expected to hear something like that from a member of the main family.

He had assumed the boy would arrive full of entitlement and tear apart every existing practice simply because it was old.

‘At least he doesn't seem like the kind of superior who makes everyone's life harder.’

Maybe he would not help.

But perhaps he would not make things worse either.

As Chihuahua finally relaxed, Vikir's gaze moved to the documents piled on the desk.

Rustle—

Pages began turning.

Chihuahua did not expect Vikir to read them seriously.

To his surprise, Vikir went through every one.

Before long, he closed the files.

“The legislative framework itself is fine. The judicial system is where the irregularities are.”

“What? You read all of that already?”

Chihuahua gaped at him.

The files amounted to hundreds of pages.

But Vikir already knew a great deal about Underdog City.

Before his regression, he had carried out extensive intelligence work and operations here.

“Illegal gambling houses, entertainment establishments, slave auctions, and every variety of illegal finance are flourishing throughout the territory. Underdog City's underground economy must be enormous.”

Chihuahua's eyes widened further.

Vikir continued.

“The previous Deputy Magistrates were probably removed because they became entangled in the same interests. Once officials start compromising with that system, the cycle becomes almost impossible to break.”

“...”

“You were born and raised here. What do you think?”

Faced with the direct question, Chihuahua could only open and close his mouth.

The boy had absorbed an enormous stack of documents almost instantly, identified the city's core problem, and then asked the opinion of an outsider subordinate instead of looking down on him.

And this was his first day—one day before his official appointment even began.

The situation was so far outside Chihuahua's expectations that he could not form a proper answer.

Vikir smiled faintly.

“It's actually simple. To destroy an underground economy, you find the core linking all those illegal businesses that look separate on the surface.”

In his previous life, Vikir had spent years fighting Underdog City's criminal organizations.

He knew where nearly every kind of crime took place.

He knew where the criminals hid.

He even remembered the locations and personal details of officials who took bribes.

‘Those black ledgers we seized when we crushed the criminal organizations in my previous life are finally useful.’

Making and enforcing law was part of the Deputy Magistrate's job.

Vikir intended to begin immediately.

“I'll respect every law already in force. I'm only going to add one.”

“One new law?”

“Yes. It won't be long. Just a few lines.”

A sharp light flickered in Vikir's eyes.

“Within one week, I'll tear Underdog City's underground economy out by the roots.”

It was a short, forceful declaration.

Then Vikir turned his attention to the next step.

A war on crime was coming, and he needed a law that would justify what followed.

“To create and enforce that law, I need a proper reason.”

Chihuahua found himself being drawn into the strange atmosphere surrounding the boy.

Without thinking, he asked a question of his own.

“A reason? Why would you need a reason to crack down on illegal activity?”

“I'm young. And officially, I don't even start as Deputy Magistrate until tomorrow.”

Vikir looked back at Chihuahua and smiled.

“At the very least, I should give them a warning. That's common courtesy in this business, isn't it?”

“...?”

Chihuahua tilted his head, completely unable to follow.

Vikir offered no further explanation.

Instead, he looked through the Magistrate's office window at the noisy celebration below and rubbed his chin.

Eat.

Drink.

The banquet welcoming the new Deputy Magistrate, Vikir, had already grown extravagant.

The party continued splendidly even without its supposed guest of honor.

And Vikir, the person the banquet was meant to celebrate, sat high above it in the Magistrate's office and planned what would happen tomorrow.

“...Right. If you want to catch flies, you put out some shit first.”

The quiet words carried killing intent that did not belong to an ordinary fifteen-year-old.

Chihuahua looked at him and had a sudden thought.

Perhaps meeting this boy would completely change the rest of his life.
