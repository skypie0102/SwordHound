# Chapter 388: The Rotten Dog of Nouvelle Vague (4)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/388.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 386 is the verified shifted secondary witness and is used only to repair one visibly corrupted mixed-language sentence describing Kirko wondering whether Garam had “awakened” after Sakkuth’s riot. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Garam Nord, Kirko Grimm, Pal Euspear, Sakkuth de Leviathan, and Nouvelle Vague.

“….”

Kirko looked up in a daze.

Normally—

Garam would have looked away the moment she stared at him.

Embarrassed.

Awkward.

Timid.

Today—

he did not care.

Of course—

there was a reason.

The real Garam was dead.

The man standing in his place—

was Vikir.

An escaped prisoner—

who had become a guard overnight.

“It’s dangerous.”

“Step back.”

The words were not truly Vikir’s concern for Kirko.

They were respect for a wish written in Garam’s diary.

**I want to show her the outside world.**

**Just once.**

**The blue sky where I was born.**

**A lake without salt.**

**Animals running across open fields.**

A wish its author could never fulfill.

Garam Nord was dead.

But Vikir had read the small—

worn—

damp notebook.

He knew exactly what Garam had wanted.

*I can at least help the woman you wanted to protect reach the surface.*

Kirko possessed extraordinary talent.

If allowed to grow—

she might become an important force for humanity.

A hidden blade for the Human Alliance.

*In the old future—*

*she may have died here without anyone ever knowing her name.*

*Not this time.*

Protect Kirko.

Develop her potential.

Respect Garam’s final will.

All of those goals aligned.

Vikir swung the three-section baton.

THUD.

CRACK.

SNAP.

Prisoners rushing Kirko were struck down.

Heads split.

Eyes burst.

Teeth flew.

Tongues were torn.

Then—

one face caught Vikir’s attention.

“…Hm?”

He recognized the scar.

*Pal Euspear.*

A bad connection dating back to Colosseo.

They had clashed several times.

Vikir had punished Pal for abusing other students.

Even in dog form—

their paths had crossed.

He had later heard that Pal caused trouble inside the Hell Tree—

then suffered severe retaliation once everyone returned to ordinary society.

Vikir looked almost pleased.

*Still.*

*Seeing an old schoolmate again is somewhat nostalgic.*

He kicked Pal in the stomach.

Hard.

Something inside ruptured.

Vikir moved on.

At first—

he had been too pressed for time to control his force precisely.

Now—

he adjusted.

Moderate suppression.

A few teeth.

A broken joint.

A ruined ankle.

If a prisoner tried to stand again—

Vikir’s boot crushed toes until standing became impossible.

The violence flowed naturally.

Like breathing.

He moved between guards and prisoners like a shadow.

The three-section baton flickered.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Dozens collapsed without understanding who had hit them.

Neither side noticed.

Everyone was too consumed by the riot.

Everyone—

except Kirko.

She had watched him from beginning to end.

*What is this?*

*Was Garam always like this?*

She had never seen him truly participate in suppression.

The Garam she knew—

had been too gentle.

Too passive.

Someone she often thought was fundamentally unsuited to work as a prison guard.

She had never understood why he came here.

Why he endured this place.

Sometimes—

she had even felt contempt.

But what stood before her now—

was entirely different.

*Did something awaken in him after Sakkuth’s riot?*

Expressionless.

Efficient.

Experienced.

Like a mad dog that had spent an entire lifetime hurting others.

Stealthy movement.

Explosive violence.

Overwhelming force.

Exceptional fighting ability.

And through all of it—

almost no emotion.

Kirko shivered.

*If there were such a thing as the ideal Nouvelle Vague guard…*

*wouldn’t he look like this?*

“Ghk!”

She forced herself upright.

Gripped her sword.

Her back hurt.

Her legs hurt.

No time to care.

She was supposed to be the ace of their generation.

She refused to lose to Stupid Garam.

Rotten Dog.

“HYAAAH!”

Kirko charged back into the riot—

following directly behind him.

The bloodbath continued.

◆◆◆

About twenty minutes later—

it was over.

The instigators were locked into heavier restraints.

Dragged toward solitary confinement.

The rest—

bloodied and broken—

were thrown back into their cells.

No treatment.

No meal.

At four the next morning—

forced labor would begin as usual.

If they could not endure it—

they would die.

The major commanding the suppression force nodded.

“Excellent.”

“We were pressed early.”

“Then everything changed.”

“Well done.”

His attention went to the left wing.

“Group B broke through fastest.”

“That let reinforcements spread across both sides.”

“Who was in Group B?”

He walked through exhausted guards.

Blood everywhere.

Breathing hard.

Then saw Kirko.

“I knew it!”

“You were even better than usual!”

“You cleared the left side so quickly that the support groups could redeploy immediately.”

“Excellent work!”

The lower guards looked at Kirko with envy.

Kirko shook her head.

“…It wasn’t me.”

“What?”

“Someone else did most of it.”

“He’s over—”

She turned.

Stopped.

Garam was not standing where he should have been.

Instead—

he was far behind the line.

With the cleanup team.

Wiping blood.

Moving broken stone.

Work normally assigned to guards whose performance was so poor they barely counted during formal evaluation.

*Why is he back there?*

Kirko opened her mouth.

The major smiled.

“Even giving credit to your colleagues.”

“Excellent humility.”

“A model for the rest of them.”

“….”

Kirko stared.

The other junior guards had already accepted the obvious story.

“Of course it was Kirko.”

“She always performs well during riots.”

“This one was worse than usual and she still broke her record.”

“She’s a genius.”

“Not even the same class as us.”

“Captain within three years.”

“Easily.”

Then—

someone looked around.

“Where did that idiot go?”

A familiar target.

Garam.

Again—

cleaning behind everyone else.

The guards laughed.

“Hey.”

“You didn’t do anything again, did you?”

“Not one prisoner?”

“You’re always dragging Kirko down.”

“Imagine how good she has to be to carry someone this useless.”

They hit him.

Back.

Arms.

Back of the head.

One ruffled his hair.

Then—

“….”

The bloodstained three-section baton moved.

THUD.

THUD.

THUD.

THUD.

Four guards folded around their stomachs.

“Ghk!”

“Ugh!”

“Aah!”

“Khk!”

The major turned.

“What’s going on over there?”

Vikir immediately supported the four collapsing guards.

“They seem to have developed sudden stomach pain.”

“Maybe the tension wore off all at once.”

“I’ll take them to the infirmary.”

The major clicked his tongue.

“Tsk.”

“Pathetic.”

“Nervous over a riot this small?”

“Take them away.”

Vikir dragged the four guards toward a quiet corridor.

Then smiled without warmth.

“Come.”

“Friends.”

“Let’s forget the past.”

“And discuss the future.”

It was the same gaze he had used on the prisoners.

No hidden strength.

No mercy.

Kirko watched him go.

Then murmured:

“…Something is definitely wrong.”
