# Chapter 83: Lovesickness (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/083.txt` — `482b93a20ef26c3c9dccceb3589f35cc09d1f12c`. Recovered English Chapter 82 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Aiyen**, **Akwilla**, **Vikir**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Cerberus**, **Madam Eight-Legs**, **Red Death**, and **Baskerville**. Nonsexual nudity and the source-level conception anecdote are retained factually without erotic embellishment.

Aiyen dreamed.

The first image was fire.

Claws.

Pain.

**Cerberus.**

The three-headed hound of hell had burst from an oil pit and attacked the Ballak hunters trespassing on its territory.

Madam Eight-Legs had been expanding her own domain.

That pressure had forced Ballak hunters east.

Eventually they collided with Cerberus.

Ballak drove the monster away.

Aiyen nearly died doing it.

She had been separated from the hunting party with mortal wounds.

She thought that was the end.

Instead, Imperial slave traders found her.

They gave her only enough treatment to keep her alive.

Then put her in a cage.

Aiyen remembered the cold iron.

The smell.

The certainty that freedom was gone.

She had spent her life running through the jungle without walls.

Now she was livestock.

What waited at the end?

A life of sexual exploitation?

An arena where slaves fought monsters until they died?

A butcher's table for some wealthy lunatic?

Her wounds festered.

Her fever rose.

She had gone days without enough water.

The proud Ballak warrior was collapsing.

Then—

light.

A boy appeared.

Pale face.

Black hair.

Red eyes.

A calm expression that did not belong in that place.

He cut through the darkness around her without hesitation.

The slave traders who had abused her were either killed outright or had their limbs cut off before being dragged away.

The cage opened.

And Aiyen went home.

She never forgot his face.

◆◆◆

After that, she crossed the Imperial border again and again.

Sometimes with other Ballak.

Sometimes alone.

Always looking for him.

The Empire was larger than she had imagined.

Too many people.

Too many cities.

But the boy was unusual.

Aiyen believed that if she searched long enough, she would recognize him immediately.

Other Ballak hunters completed their own partner hunts.

Men captured Imperial women.

Women captured Imperial men.

One by one, people her age returned with partners.

Aiyen did not.

No one else interested her.

She kept waiting.

Then fate finally cooperated.

She found him again.

Vikir.

The same boy.

Older.

Stronger.

And holding another woman.

A black flame rose in Aiyen's chest.

Then she forced it down.

The past could not be changed.

The future mattered more.

There was only one problem.

Vikir refused to cooperate with the future she had already imagined.

He fought Ballak warriors.

Defeated them.

Survived.

Then faced Madam Eight-Legs without running.

The more Aiyen saw, the more certain she became.

She wanted him.

Not merely as a trophy.

Not simply because Ballak custom valued strong outsiders.

She wanted *him*.

When Madam Eight-Legs attacked, Aiyen turned back despite her fear.

Her legs shook.

Cold sweat covered her skin.

She still threw the noose.

Still dragged Vikir away from those eight killing limbs.

Then brought his ruined body back to Ballak.

Many elders wanted him executed.

Imperial.

Possible Baskerville connection.

Enemy blood.

Aiyen refused.

“He saved my life.”

And—

“He will be useful.”

She had been right.

Vikir recovered.

Hunted large prey.

Punished merchants who abused the tribes.

Helped stop a plague.

And somewhere between all those events, ordinary days accumulated too.

Aiyen teasing him for wetting himself.

Eating boiled bear testicles.

Hunting together.

Playing in the hot springs.

Arguments.

Silence.

The memories blurred into one long stream.

At some point, possession had become something else.

Something deeper.

◆◆◆

“Aiyen.”

Someone shook her.

“Wake up.”

Her eyes opened.

Straw ceiling.

Heat.

Sweat.

Pain.

Aiyen lay naked on the bed, her body burning with fever.

Her appetite was gone.

She was dehydrated.

Her skin was flushed.

Akwilla sat beside her, wiping her face with a wet cloth.

“You kept calling for Vikir in your sleep.”

Aiyen grimaced.

“Mother.”

Her breath came hard.

“Is this Red Death?”

Akwilla examined her carefully.

Fever.

Sweat.

Redness.

But no red spots.

No familiar plague pattern.

“These symptoms are different.”

Aiyen clutched her chest.

“It hurts.”

“Where?”

“Everywhere.”

She squeezed harder.

“My chest. My whole body is hot. It feels like my heart is drying up.”

Akwilla remained calm.

“Endure a little longer. Vikir will come back.”

Aiyen turned away.

“I don't care whether he comes back.”

Her face said otherwise.

“He isn't coming.”

Akwilla continued wiping her face.

Aiyen's eyes filled.

“Mother.”

“Yes?”

“This is the first time I've hurt this much.”

Her voice cracked.

“It makes me resent being born.”

Akwilla stopped.

“I know I shouldn't think that. But I keep wanting to ask why you gave birth to me.”

Akwilla pulled her daughter close.

Not as chieftain.

As mother.

“My child.”

Aiyen rested against her.

“Long ago, your father and I planted a tiny seed.”

Aiyen blinked.

Akwilla had almost never spoken about her father.

Even through fever, she listened.

“Your father planted it.”

Akwilla stroked Aiyen's face.

“I watered it every day.”

“The seed sprouted.”

“After a few months, it became a healthy flower.”

Aiyen stared at her mother through wet eyes.

Akwilla continued.

“We picked the flower.”

“Crushed it.”

“Squeezed out the juice.”

“Soaked tobacco in it.”

“Smoked it.”

Aiyen's expression began changing.

“And while thoroughly intoxicated, your father and I had sex without contraception.”

Silence.

“That was the night you were conceived.”

Aiyen stared.

Akwilla added one more detail.

“Your grandfather executed your father the next day for committing evil with drugs.”

“……”

Aiyen closed her eyes.

“Mother.”

“Yes?”

“I think I hurt more now.”

Akwilla resumed wiping her face.

“Just wait. If Vikir brings a cure—”

“Stop!”

Aiyen pushed herself upright.

“Don't torture me with hope!”

Her voice cracked.

“He isn't coming back!”

She looked away.

“What slave comes back after someone takes off his leash?”

Then shouting erupted outside.

“He's here!”

“Vikir!”

“Vikir came back!”

“He brought medicine!”

Akwilla's sharp hearing caught every word.

She smiled.

“See, daughter? I told—”

CRASH.

She never finished.

Aiyen was already gone.

She had jumped from the bed, broken straight through the wall, and sprinted away.

Fever.

Pain.

Weakness.

Apparently forgotten.

Akwilla stared at the hole her daughter had left behind.

One corner of her mouth twitched.

“……Was that really Red Death?”
