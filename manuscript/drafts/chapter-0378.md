Christmas ended in an instant.

Then—

hellish labor began again.

THUD.

CLANG.

THUD.

Stone broke.

Earth moved.

Sulfur gas hissed.

Steam burst.

Lava rolled.

Ever since the strange explosive object had been found in Level Nine—

the work formation had changed.

Before—

guards had formed a panopticon-like arrangement around the prisoners.

Now—

under D'Ordume’s order—

they moved toward the outer perimeter.

As far from the suspicious object as possible.

There was also a tactical benefit.

If prisoners rioted—

guards could concentrate their fire inward from outside the labor zone.

But changing a long-established system always created mistakes.

Before—

a lazy prisoner.

A fight.

A dispute.

Guards could intervene immediately.

Now—

while everyone adapted—

responses were slower.

Vikir noticed.

And began watching one person closely.

“…Fuck.”

“Fuck.”

“Fuck.”

“Fuck.”

Sakkuth.

Something had been wrong since Christmas dinner.

Normally—

he looked down at everyone.

Now—

his gaze kept lifting upward.

Bloodshot eyes.

Foam at the corner of his mouth.

Profanity repeated like prayer.

An obsessive fixation on **that person**.

By lunch—

the instability had become obvious.

While everyone else took the ten-minute break—

Sakkuth walked alone into a canyon between red rock walls.

Pulled at his hair.

“How…”

“How did this happen…”

He bit his fingers.

Past the nails.

Into flesh.

“My Queen…”

“Boss…”

“Master…”

“What happened?”

“You said you’d get me out.”

“Two years.”

“Nothing for two years.”

“You told me to enter Nouvelle Vague and serve **Him**.”

“You promised you would take me back out.”

“I couldn’t reach Level Nine.”

“I couldn’t meet Him.”

“But that doesn’t mean you can abandon me here.”

“Why haven’t you come?”

“When can I leave?”

“Please…”

“Get me out of this hell…”

Blood covered his knuckles.

The muttering continued.

Vikir listened from a distance.

*He’s gone insane.*

Sakkuth had always been strange.

Not like this.

Apparently—

he had entered Nouvelle Vague for a purpose too.

Like Vikir.

But there was one decisive difference.

Vikir did not depend on rescue.

He achieved his goals alone.

Sakkuth—

could only wait for someone else.

That difference separated predator from prey.

Vikir leaned against the rock.

Watched.

Sakkuth’s instability worsened.

“No contact this long…”

“I was abandoned.”

“Boss abandoned me.”

“No news from outside…”

“That’s why.”

“Because I failed to reach Level Nine.”

“Because of that—

Boss…”

“Queen…”

“abandoned me…”

BWOOOO—

The horn sounded.

Labor resumed.

Guards shouted.

“Break’s over!”

“Move, trash!”

“Want to die?”

“Get back to work!”

The yelling hit Sakkuth’s already unstable mind.

He closed his mouth.

Eyes red.

A lieutenant walked past.

“Hey!”

“What are you doing?”

“Move!”

That sentence—

became the final push.

“AAAAAAAAAGH!”

Sakkuth screamed.

His eyes rolled.

He bared his teeth.

CRUNCH—

SPURT!

He bit through the tip of his own tongue.

Blood sprayed from his mouth.

The stench was immediate.

A crimson mist—

saturated with plague aura—

spread outward.

“W-what?!”

The guard stumbled back.

Confused.

Terrified.

Vikir finally pushed away from the wall.

*It started.*

The first step of his Nouvelle Vague escape plan.

◆◆◆

A riot by a Level Eight prisoner—

was a major emergency.

Junior guards could not handle it.

Even officers struggled.

And at the moment—

the guard system was badly positioned.

D'Ordume had reversed the usual panopticon deployment.

Everyone was still adapting.

So the guards closest to Sakkuth—

were mostly low-ranking.

One captain.

A dozen or so lieutenants.

The rest—

ensigns.

“Level Eight Sakkuth is rioting!”

“We can’t stop him!”

“Damn it!”

“We need at least Bastille!”

Sakkuth’s physical strength was bad enough.

The plague blood—

worse.

The red mist spread.

Sakkuth roared inside it—

like a diseased beast.

“What do we do?”

The newly promoted captain’s voice shook.

No one outranked him here.

For the first time—

he realized rank could be a burden.

Then—

“We buy time.”

Someone stepped forward.

**Kirko Grimm.**

Now a lieutenant.

Promoted from ensign for exceptional performance.

She looked directly at the senior guards.

“One person stays.”

“Everyone else splits up and calls for senior officers.”

“That is the only workable option.”

“But who stays?”

“That thing is already losing control.”

Sakkuth still wore BDISSEM cuffs and shackles.

Yet the plague aura alone made approaching him terrifying.

Nobody volunteered.

Then—

“I’ll stay.”

Kirko drew her sword.

Rested it across one shoulder.

“I’ll delay him as long as possible.”

“Find someone who can actually suppress him.”

The others exchanged looks.

“Well…”

“If it’s Kirko…”

“She’s only a lieutenant—

but stronger than most captains.”

“She’s almost senior-officer level in pure skill.”

“Good.”

“Kirko stays.”

“We’ll get help.”

“Your brave sacrifice won’t be forgotten!”

“We’ll mention it in the end-of-month evaluation!”

All of them.

Or—

Huff.

Huff.

Turned slowly.

She expected—

Sakkuth.

“…Garam?”

Shaking legs.

Uneven breathing.

“Why didn’t you leave?”

“How could I leave a colleague behind…”

Good words.

Kirko sighed.

Then—

“Kururururu…”

Blood tears ran from his eyes.

His grin reached nearly to his ears.

Drool fell continuously.

“Yes.”

“That’s it.”

“If I kill you—

then I become Level Nine.”
