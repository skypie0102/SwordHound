# Chapter 455: Infiltration of the Water Source (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/455.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 453 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Fandom-backed forms control **Orca** as Warden of Nouvelle Vague; established project terminology controls Tochka, Poison Humans, Red Death, Dolores, Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, Bianca, and Marquis de Sade. The black-robed enemy commander remains only **the Reaper** at this reveal point; later identity is not imported early.

“House Leviathan!”

“The Poison Humans are coming!”

“There are too many!”

The sentries along the wall were all shouting variations of the same warning.

The horizon darkened.

An army of Poison Humans rolled across the ground—

like a curtain descending from the sky.

Red Death miasma spread with them.

The battle to annihilate Tochka had begun.

[AAAAAAAH—!]

The Poison Humans charged the first wall.

No caution.

No fear.

Only animal violence.

THUD!

The first one smashed itself against Tochka’s stone wall hard enough to die.

Another climbed onto the corpse—

then threw itself forward.

THUD!

THUD!

THUD!

Bodies piled over bodies.

A grotesque ramp began to form against the fortress.

And among that mass—

one figure directed them.

Black hood.

Black robe.

A huge scythe whose blade **and** shaft were twisted unnaturally.

“…What is that?”

“Did someone come to harvest us?”

The guards had barely finished speaking when—

FLASH!

The black figure swung the scythe.

A wave of poisonous force struck the wall.

BOOM!

The entire section shook.

The **Reaper** swung again.

Each sweep spread thicker poison around him—

and the Poison Humans nearby became even more violent.

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca gathered together.

“That black bastard is probably the vanguard commander.”

“He’s strong.”

“That scythe work isn’t simple.”

“He’s definitely directing them.”

Bianca stepped forward first.

“Then let’s see what he can do.”

She drew.

The arrow she nocked—

was the same kind of killing shot that had marked Andrealphus’s end.

TWANG!

The arrow screamed through the air.

“…!”

The Reaper sensed the threat.

He did not raise the scythe.

Instead—

SHUFFLE!

Dozens of Poison Humans threw themselves together.

A wall of flesh.

The arrow disappeared inside it.

Bianca fired again.

And again.

Each shot—

blocked by human shields before it could reach the Reaper.

“Tsk.”

“What are you—

some pampered young master?”

Bianca watched him retreat behind bodies.

Meanwhile—

the Poison Humans kept coming.

A tide of Red Death.

The poison leaking from their skin—

and the bloody mist released when they died—

could infect ordinary people and turn them into more Poison Humans.

If Dolores had not been maintaining a huge sacred barrier from the center of Tochka—

the fortress would already have been in danger.

SWISH.

The Reaper stretched one hand to the side.

The Poison Humans changed direction.

He redirected them again—

always toward the weakest sections of the wall.

He was not merely strong.

He understood warfare.

“Damn monsters…”

“A commander who thinks—

leading soldiers who don’t question anything.”

“No wonder they swept through the civil-war fronts.”

“And every soldier is poisonous.”

“They’re frightening our men!”

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca shouted encouragement.

But morale continued slipping.

Then—

“DON’T BACK DOWN, YOU IDIOTS!”

A roar thundered across the walls.

Friend and enemy alike looked toward the central rampart.

A massive old man stood there—

holding a huge club.

**Warden Orca.**

Master of defense.

Master of fortress warfare.

He looked down over the battlefield.

“If you retreat—

everyone behind you dies!”

“If you fight here—

you only risk your own life!”

“Which is the better bargain?”

“To die alone?”

“Or drag your family into death with you?”

The soldiers’ eyes snapped back into focus.

The collapsing mood—

stopped.

Orca seized that instant.

“Spearmen.”

“Long-pole teams.”

“Forward.”

Prepared soldiers stepped up.

They thrust long spears and heavy wooden poles over the walls—

keeping the climbing Poison Humans at a distance.

Blankets.

Thick pads.

Bamboo screens.

Things refugees had spent the night making—

were dropped from above to catch poisonous blood.

Orca looked elsewhere.

Dust rose in the distance.

More Poison Humans moved beneath it.

“Dig holes.”

“Bury jars.”

“Put people with sharp hearing and touch inside.”

“If the enemy begins digging tunnels—

you’ll feel it.”

Then he reorganized the entire defense.

“Five men to each tower section.”

“One captain for every five sections.”

“One commander for every twenty-five.”

“One senior commander for every fifty.”

“Each group defends only its assigned area.”

The troops moved.

Orca kept watching the enemy lines.

“Artillery crews—

keep the charcoal burning.”

“Beat the drums every ten minutes.”

“Make the soldiers think only about surviving until the next drumbeat.”

“Militia inside the fortress—

guard the supplies.”

“Especially oil and gunpowder.”

The moment Orca took command—

the battlefield changed.

Soldiers regained confidence.

The Poison Human offensive slowed.

“As expected.”

From another section of wall—

Vikir watched Orca’s command and nodded.

Before regression—

Orca had been one of humanity’s greatest defensive commanders.

It was not an exaggeration to say—

his arrival late in the Age of Destruction helped keep humanity alive.

The tragedy—

was that he had spent most of that war trapped at the bottom of the sea guarding Nouvelle Vague.

He entered the real war too late.

Too many people had already died.

*This time is different.*

Vikir had brought Orca to the surface **before** the war truly began.

And the old man was already working for humanity.

Actively.

Then—

“Trouble!”

“Leviathan is bringing shield carts!”

“Covered wagons!”

“Ladders!”

More siege equipment appeared.

Large shields against arrows and stones.

Long ladders.

Huge Poison Humans carrying axes—

trying to break the stones and foundations beneath the wall.

Orca’s expression did not change.

“Pour the militia oil over the sloped wall.”

“Artillery crews—

throw down the charcoal.”

“Put away the spears and poles.”

“Use ropes packed with gunpowder and metal fragments.”

“Roll them down.”

Oil streamed over Tochka’s angled stone face.

Poison Humans trying to climb—

lost their grip.

They slipped.

Fell.

Piled up.

Then came gunpowder.

Iron scraps.

Burning charcoal.

BOOM!

BOOM!

BOOM!

The oil ignited.

Explosions chained across the wall.

Metal fragments tore through the Poison Humans below.

Tudor stared.

“There isn’t even some bizarre secret trick.”

Sancho nodded slowly.

“It’s exactly what a textbook would tell you to do.”

Pigi swallowed.

“That might be what makes it impressive.”

“To do the obvious thing perfectly—

you need exact control over people, supplies, and timing.”

Bianca kept watching the old man.

“Being faithful to the basics is the hardest part.”

“That’s experience.”

As time passed—

the assault weakened.

The red mass in the distance began showing gaps.

The soldiers cheered.

“They’re retreating!”

“Warden Orca did it!”

“The old war hero saved us!”

“We’ve almost stopped them!”

“Warden!”

“What do we do next?”

“Give the next order!”

Orca exhaled smoke.

Annoyed.

“How should I know?”

“…What?”

Every nearby soldier doubted their ears.

Orca glared at the weakening enemy line.

“I defend.”

“That’s my job.”

“Attacking is somebody else’s.”

Then—

reluctantly—

he pointed toward another section of the wall.

Everyone followed the gesture.

An old man stood there.

Smiling in a way that made several people uncomfortable.

“Hehehehehe…”

**Marquis de Sade.**

He looked down at the enemy army—

like a starving man looking at dinner.
