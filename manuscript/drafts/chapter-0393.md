# Chapter 393: The Worst Torture (4)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/393.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 391 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Garam Nord, Kirko Grimm, Sakkuth de Leviathan, Lieutenant Colonel Bastille, Madam Eight-Legs, Beelzebub, Poseidon, and Level Ten.

“…You didn’t?”

Nobody understood.

Kirko.

The guards.

Even Sakkuth.

Sakkuth de Leviathan had terrified Nouvelle Vague for years.

A poison-family outcast.

A criminal tied to one of the worst epidemic disasters in memory.

Even BDISSEM restraints could not neutralize what people believed lived in his bodily fluids.

Sweat.

Saliva.

Blood.

Breath.

That reputation alone had made some senior guards argue he belonged on Level Nine.

And Garam—

a mere junior guard—

had put the blood-soaked cigarette into his mouth without knowing whether it was safe.

That was not ordinary courage.

It was indistinguishable from madness.

Sakkuth asked again.

“You really didn’t know?”

“No.”

Vikir smoked.

That part was true.

But he had not gambled his life.

The venom power inside him—

the legacy of Madam Eight-Legs—

had consumed and refined countless toxins.

Poisons entering Vikir’s body did not behave normally.

Disease.

Venom.

Contaminants.

They became material for the monstrous resistance already living inside him.

Whatever Sakkuth truly carried—

Vikir had been prepared to survive it.

Sakkuth did not know that.

He looked genuinely lost.

“I don’t understand what I’m looking at.”

Then—

he released Kirko.

THUD.

She hit the ground hard.

Coughed blood.

Her ribs hurt.

Her collarbone felt fractured.

Her face had gone pale from near strangulation.

And yet—

the instant she touched the floor—

Kirko forced herself up.

Drew her sword.

Reset her stance.

*As expected.*

Vikir glanced back.

*If she doesn’t die in some nameless fight here—

she’ll become extraordinary.*

Then Sakkuth interrupted.

“You like her that much?”

Vikir turned.

Sakkuth looked almost disappointed.

“A man gambling his life for a woman.”

“Do you understand what becoming my hostage means?”

Around them—

guards whispered.

“Garam likes Kirko?”

“You didn’t know?”

“He always stares at her during roll call.”

“But this is too much.”

“He put his life on the line.”

“That’s a man.”

“That’s an idiot.”

“The senior guards are getting ready to attack.”

Sakkuth laughed.

“You’re a sacrifice.”

“Disposable.”

“Do you think those officers care if one junior guard dies?”

“….”

“And you smoked my blood for this woman.”

“Insane.”

Vikir considered it.

*If this were the real Garam—*

*he might actually do it.*

The diaries made that obvious.

Garam might truly have sacrificed himself for Kirko.

Vikir would not.

He had stepped forward because the outcome benefited him.

“Enough.”

“Let’s talk.”

“Lower your voice from here.”

Sakkuth narrowed his eyes.

“You still have something to say?”

“You’re not simply performing for your woman?”

“Shut up and listen.”

Then—

“Garam!”

Kirko shouted.

Both men looked over.

Her sword was half drawn.

“Stop!”

A flash.

Aura exploded from her blade.

Dense.

Heavy.

Nearly liquid.

The experience at death’s edge—

the rage—

the pressure—

had forced something open.

Kirko had advanced.

Sakkuth’s eyes widened.

“This crazy woman—!”

Vikir—

for once—

was caught off guard too.

Other guards moved before Kirko could reach them.

Women who knew her well grabbed her arms and legs.

“Kirko!”

“Are you insane?!”

“You’ll make two hostages!”

“Don’t waste Garam’s sacrifice!”

The difference was striking.

When Garam walked forward—

nobody stopped him.

When Kirko tried—

everyone did.

“Let go!”

Kirko fought against them.

Her eyes fixed on Garam.

The man who had stepped into death—

apparently for her.

Confusion.

Fear.

Desperation.

Then—

“Don’t come.”

Vikir spoke once.

Kirko stopped moving.

“…!”

“You’ll interfere.”

“But—”

“Go.”

“You’ll help me more by leaving.”

A clean boundary.

Cold.

Practical.

Everyone misunderstood it.

Even guards who had criticized Garam moments earlier fell silent.

To them—

this was noble self-sacrifice.

A man refusing to let anyone else die with him.

Kirko’s resistance slowly stopped.

The circle tightened again.

Senior guards whispered too.

“That junior guard has nerve.”

“Reminds me of my early years.”

“Losing someone like that would be a waste.”

“His name is Garam.”

“He’s ranked first among junior guards these last few months.”

“Try to save him.”

“Move the rear team closer.”

“Special unit—

use the ceiling.”

Vikir ignored all of it.

He walked toward Sakkuth.

Close enough now that the two could speak privately.

Sakkuth exhaled.

“…At this point—

I’m actually curious.”

“Good.”

“Then we begin.”

Vikir lowered his voice.

“Level Ten construction must continue.”

Sakkuth stared.

“…That’s it?”

“Yes.”

“Your riot delayed it by a full day.”

“What are you talking about?”

“Construction is your problem.”

“What does it have to do with me?”

Vikir put one finger to his lips.

“Quiet.”

Then leaned closer.

“If Level Ten is delayed—

you suffer too.”

“The moment the blue sphere in the center of the deepest work zone explodes—

that is the timing.”

Sakkuth frowned.

“Timing?”

“For what?”

“What are we waiting for?”

Vikir clicked his tongue.

“Idiot.”

Sakkuth’s eyes went red.

A junior guard—

and **Stupid Garam** of all people—

calling him stupid.

He was about to explode.

Then Vikir spoke.

“The moment Her Majesty the Queen comes to save you.”

Sakkuth’s entire body went rigid.
