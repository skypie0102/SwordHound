# Chapter 442: The Fall of Usher (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/442.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 440 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established project terminology controls Tudor, Bianca, Roderick Usher, Madeline, Gungnir, Red Death, and House Usher. Direct Fandom verification remains robots-blocked.

Masyaf was exactly what House Usher’s reputation suggested.

A fortress.

But unlike Tochka—

which relied on cliffs, altitude, and terrain—

Masyaf was an artificial fortress built from traps.

Everywhere.

Bookshelves that collapsed without warning.

Books and paintings that fell from walls.

Curtains that moved without wind.

Clockwork that shifted slowly inside the walls.

Statues that laughed.

Dead plants in flowerpots.

Taxidermied animals.

Red-brick barriers.

Massive chandeliers.

Nothing could be trusted.

Bianca—

who had grown up here—

moved through the maze without hesitation.

“When a bookshelf falls—

watch which books hit the floor.”

“You can cross only if they’re Gothic stories.”

Tudor stared.

“…Seriously?”

“Yes.”

“If **The Masque of the Red Death**, **The Black Cat**, **The Cask of Amontillado**, **Hop-Frog**, **The Tell-Tale Heart**, or **The Narrative of Arthur Gordon Pym** falls—

the passage is safe.”

“If anything else falls—

don’t move.”

She pointed ahead.

“If the curtains move even slightly—

drop flat.”

“Wind or no wind.”

“When the clock strikes—

a secret door opens somewhere in the corridor.”

“Don’t enter immediately.”

“Wait exactly five minutes.”

“Otherwise the door may lead somewhere completely different.”

They passed a grinning stone figure.

“When that statue laughs—

watch everything.”

“It doesn’t trigger a trap itself.”

“It just laughs when it thinks an intruder is about to die horribly.”

Tudor slowly turned toward her.

Bianca continued.

“If you see a dead plant in a pot—

don’t go near it.”

“House Usher doesn’t keep plants indoors.”

“Stuffed animals are usually safe.”

“If the eyes move—

cover your ears and run.”

“A red-brick wall?”

“Never touch it.”

“If you do—

run.”

“And if someone behind you calls your name—

don’t turn around.”

She pointed upward.

“And never walk under a chandelier.”

“If there’s no other path—

don’t look at the chandelier.”

“Watch its shadow on the floor instead.”

Tudor was silent for several seconds.

Then—

“…I think I understand why your personality became like this.”

Bianca smacked the side of his head.

“What was that?”

“Nothing.”

She turned away.

“Hmph.”

Then—

much quieter—

“…Thanks for coming.”

“What?”

“I said move.”

She pulled him forward.

Eventually—

they reached the deepest part of the mansion.

The bedroom of **Roderick Usher**.

Red Death hung even heavier here.

The poisonous mist crawled along the floor.

Through the walls.

Through the halls.

Like a red-faced clown dancing in an empty corridor.

Even after taking Dolores’s holy protection—

Tudor and Bianca felt sick.

CREEEAK—

The bedroom door opened.

The hinges sounded like something dying.

Then Bianca stopped breathing.

“Father!”

Roderick sat in a chair.

Pale.

Dry.

Mummified.

His body looked almost dead.

His eyes—

were not.

They were open.

Blood-red.

Alive.

Bianca rushed forward.

“Father…”

“What happened to you?”

Roderick tried to move his mouth.

A black tongue shifted between shriveled lips.

His throat produced only a dry—

mechanical rasp.

Tudor’s expression tightened.

“…Same as my father.”

Cervantes had looked almost exactly like this under the Red Death.

The difference—

was that Cervantes had used his final strength to kill himself.

Roderick had endured.

Tudor looked around.

“The swamp.”

“The demon poisoned the swamp itself.”

“The moisture carries Red Death into the mansion.”

“That’s why the entire place is saturated.”

Then he remembered Vikir’s flares.

Red.

Black.

*If Roderick is alive and can escape—red.*

*If dead—or alive but unable to move—black.*

Tudor’s hand shook.

Roderick was alive.

“I’m using the red flare.”

Bianca grabbed his wrist.

Hard.

“…Are you insane?”

She ripped the red flare from his hand—

and threw it out the window.

“Do you want everyone outside killed too?”

“But your father is alive!”

“You heard Vikir.”

Bianca’s voice cracked.

“My father…”

“He can’t move.”

Her head lowered.

Her entire body trembled.

No one in the world wanted the red flare more than Bianca.

Tudor understood.

He lowered his eyes.

Then—

“Hohohoho.”

A woman laughed.

“Giving up awfully quickly.”

Tudor and Bianca turned.

A pale woman stood in the doorway.

**Madeline.**

Roderick’s younger sister.

Acting head of House Usher.

And in practice—

the woman controlling the family.

“Like father, like daughter.”

“Your blood relative is still breathing—

and you’re already treating him as dead.”

Madeline’s corpse-like eyes settled on Bianca.

“Are you going to bury your father alive too?”

“Just like your father buried me?”

The old story.

Madeline had suffered a terrible fever as a child.

Her heartbeat had weakened.

Her skin had gone cold.

Roderick believed she had died.

And buried her.

Yet Madeline later emerged from the grave.

She broke through the coffin.

Clawed through the soil.

Returned alive.

Roderick spent the rest of his life crushed by guilt.

He granted his sister nearly anything she asked.

Bianca gritted her teeth.

“You were a demon from the beginning, weren’t you?”

“You took Aunt Madeline’s body after she died from the fever.”

“And used my father’s guilt against him.”

Madeline smiled.

“Tell me.”

“When did I become a demon?”

“When I was buried?”

“When I was born?”

“Or—”

“was I ever a demon at all?”

She stepped closer.

A spear blade cut the space between them.

“Do not cross this line.”

Tudor.

Gungnir in one hand.

Madeline’s gaze moved to the strip of Chimeries hide hanging beneath the spearhead.

“How grotesque.”

“You killed your uncle in a succession conflict—

then skinned him to decorate your weapon?”

“That taste doesn’t suit a house that boasts about justice.”

Tudor ignored the provocation.

He grabbed Bianca’s hand.

“Run.”

They turned.

Madeline lifted one hand.

CRACK—

Cold exploded.

A wall of ice blocked the window.

Tudor stopped.

Madeline’s voice slid around him.

“You want to leave.”

“You may.”

“If I allow it.”

“….”

“Hohoho.”

“Don’t glare.”

“I’ll give you a chance.”

“One simple test.”

Tudor tightened his grip on Gungnir.

“Don’t bother with tricks.”

“No trick.”

“Prove your courage.”

“House Donquixote is famous for bravery, isn’t it?”

She smiled.

“Say this three times.”

**‘Where is the person who can kill me?’**

“If you can say it—

I’ll acknowledge your courage.”

“And open the way.”

The provocation was obvious.

But there was no visible ambush.

Tudor and Bianca exchanged a look.

Then—

after checking the room again—

Tudor spoke.

“Where is the person who can kill me?”

“Where is the person who can kill me?”

“Where is the person who can kill me?”

His voice was steady.

The only enemy here—

was Madeline.

That seemed obvious.

Madeline’s eyes curved.

Soft.

Almost affectionate.

Cold sweat formed along Tudor’s neck.

Then—

directly behind him—

a voice whispered:

“…Right here.”
