*Right.*

*I still haven’t changed back.*

Just before the Dragon Majin’s fist landed—

Vikir had transformed into dog form.

The smaller body had kept him from being crushed outright.

Sinclaire’s last spell—

buying him a few more seconds—

had mattered too.

Dolores knew none of that.

She was too busy examining every part of Choco’s body.

“Oh?”

“Most of the wounds are already closed.”

“Our Choco really responds well to holy power.”

She spread candy-bought medicine over his injuries.

The remaining external wounds disappeared quickly.

“Mmm…”

“But the internal damage is still bad.”

“You need more rest.”

“Big Sis will buy medicine.”

Dolores patted his head.

Then opened the shop.

**Internal-Injury Medicine — 3 blue candies**

Vikir watched her spend the candy.

*Still terrible with money.*

The internal damage was serious—

but several days of rest would have handled it.

And who spent three whole candies on a stray dog?

Six—

if the earlier wound medicine was included.

Vikir did not swallow the medicine she offered.

He hid it inside his mouth instead.

Later—

he would return it.

Recover the candy.

Give it back to Dolores.

Dolores—

oblivious—

kept talking.

“Choco.”

“You got dragged into this incident too.”

“Poor thing.”

“How did you even get here?”

“Each challenger gets different floors, but still…”

“What are the odds?”

“….”

“They say there’s only a 0.0001% chance of entering this place.”

“We’re lucky, aren’t we?”

“….”

Vikir lowered his head.

The awkwardness was real.

There was another problem.

*Both prisoners have to agree to leave.*

If one person chose to remain in Lost Paradise—

neither could leave.

So Vikir had to convince Dolores.

And Dolores looked prepared to stay.

Soft bed.

Sofa.

Food.

Water.

A fully prepared bathing area.

“Choco~”

“Your outside wounds are healed.”

“Take a bath with Big Sis.”

“My holy water is good for internal injuries too.”

Vikir sighed.

*Why does she like baths so much?*

◆◆◆

He entered the bath.

There was no strength to waste resisting—

and no practical reason to refuse.

Holy water infused with Dolores’s divine power really did help internal injuries.

A large wooden tub sat filled with warm water.

Splash.

Dolores lifted Choco—

then entered with him.

“Ahhh.”

“We bathed together before, didn’t we?”

“What memories.”

Vikir gave a tiny nod.

He remembered.

Back when he was still a Graduator—

after his first brutal fight with Camus—

he had been badly injured.

Dolores had encountered him during the return to the Academy—

and taken him to the baths.

She had said something similar then too.

“Bathing is basically my only hobby.”

“Studying.”

“Work.”

“Volunteer service.”

“There’s always too much to do.”

“The board-game club used to be fun too, but there weren’t enough members.”

“Everyone these days is obsessed with grades and extracurricular activities.”

“The employment market is awful.”

She sighed.

“People hardly even use words like ‘romantic’ or ‘eccentric’ anymore.”

Dolores accepted that the Academy culture had changed.

Still—

she missed what it used to be.

“Haha…”

“Listen to me.”

“Captured by demons.”

“Locked inside a tower.”

“And I’m talking like this.”

“Not very realistic, is it?”

Vikir nodded.

Dolores tapped his nose.

“Tch.”

“You always act like you understand every word I say.”

When Dolores stood up from the water—

Vikir quietly turned his head away.

Silence filled the bath.

He breathed in the warm steam.

Focused on recovery.

Dolores’s body naturally emitted faint holy power.

His internal injuries were healing quickly.

*At this rate—*

*one or two days.*

Then Dolores spoke again.

Much quieter.

“…I know.”

“This isn’t really the time to be relaxing here.”

Her voice trembled.

“I watched classmates die.”

“Juniors too.”

“People I lived with.”

“People I studied with.”

“They killed one another.”

“Used each other.”

“Betrayed each other.”

What could Dolores have done—

as both student and priest—

inside rules created by a demon?

The game itself had been built from malice.

There had been no room for righteousness.

Or love.

Every floor contained threats like Hell Hounds—

or the Daylily of Blood Tree.

The number of survivors was always limited.

Those outside the quota—

died.

Again and again—

Dolores had tried to offer herself instead.

Again and again—

the others stopped her.

The healer had to live.

The group needed her.

Splash.

Dolores dipped her face under the water.

Then came back up.

She wiped the water from her face.

Not the wetness in her voice.

“…I let them protect me.”

“I told myself I had no choice.”

“I said I had to live to save more people.”

“So I ignored the sacrifices of the few.”

“I turned away from the pleas of the weak.”

The weak—

thrown to the Daylily.

The strong—

surviving because of it.

Anyone could see the injustice.

But without sacrifice—

everyone died.

Dolores had wanted to take their place.

That would not have solved the problem.

And by then—

she had become a leader.

The emotional center holding survivors together.

So every choice felt wrong.

Sacrifice herself?

Preserve herself for those who remained?

Search for some third answer?

She never found one in time.

The conflict between weak majority and strong minority spiraled out of control.

The Daylily rampaged.

The remaining students died.

In the end—

Dolores had torn her Return Scroll.

Not for theology.

Not for principle.

Only to survive.

“…I was powerless.”

“I couldn’t do anything.”

“From beginning to end—”

“I only moved inside the demon’s test.”

Dolores lowered her head.

Steam reddened her eyes.

Vikir closed his own.

*The inner demon got her.*

That was Amdusias’s intent.

Break the future heroes early.

Crush faith.

Will.

Self-worth.

Replace them with inferiority.

Self-blame.

Helplessness.

Before regression—

many future heroes had vanished here.

*But some came out stronger.*

Dolores—

the future Ironblood Saintess—

was one of them.

History remembered her awakening on the front lines during the Age of Destruction.

That—

Vikir knew—

had actually been her second awakening.

The first step happened here.

Inside the Tower of Abyss.

*The key is how she gets past this.*

Vikir understood the value of one sentence from someone farther along the road.

Cane Corso’s few words at the Grave of Swords—

had opened the path Vikir needed.

*What would work for Dolores?*

He thought carefully.

This mattered.

Words for a future hero.

He needed to ignore the exhaustion—

the pain—

everything else—

and choose the first sentence correctly.

Then the answer came.

*Use her own words.*

Who could understand Dolores better—

than Dolores?

Vikir remembered something the older Dolores had said before regression—

while treating wounded people in a temporary shelter.

“The more painful reality becomes—

the more deeply you must think about the meaning of life and faith.”

“Only then can that pain lead you toward a greater good.”

Not empty encouragement.

The future Dolores—

after years of suffering—

had spoken those words to a younger saintess crying because she could not save more people.

Experience distilled over decades.

Now—

that experience crossed backward through time.

From the older Dolores—

to the younger Dolores.

The young Saintess lifted her head.

Her expression changed.

As though something had finally connected.

Then—

in disbelief—

she looked at the black dog in front of her.

“…Choco.”

“Did you just talk?”
