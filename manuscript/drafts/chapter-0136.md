“…Huh?”

Tudor came back to himself.

Had he fallen asleep?

Impossible.

He had been sprinting full speed in the middle of a match.

His thoughts returned slowly.

*I had the ball.*

*I passed the Baskerville triplets.*

*I broke Highbro's pursuit.*

*I drove through the defenders.*

*Then…*

Nothing.

He remembered seeing the goal.

Remembered preparing to score.

Then—

a gap.

His vision cleared.

Green field.

Blue sky.

High goalposts.

Stunned classmates.

Everything looked exactly as it had before—

except upside down.

“…What?”

Tudor finally realized his position.

He was sprawled in a corner of the field—

half-inverted—

covered in dirt and grass.

“What the hell?!”

He scrambled upright.

Vikir stood nearby.

Completely calm.

And memory returned.

*Right.*

*Before scoring, I bumped him.*

Tudor had seen a student who appeared good only at theory—

thin.

Unimpressive.

Easy to knock aside.

So on impulse—

he had decided to shoulder-check him.

The moment they touched—

it felt as though Tudor had hit a mountain.

He bounced away.

Rolled.

Lost consciousness for a moment.

Vikir—

the person actually hit—

had barely moved.

Tudor opened his mouth.

Then—

“Goal, Class A!”

The referee's voice carried across the field.

Tudor looked toward the Class B goal.

The ball lay beneath it.

When Tudor had been thrown off balance—

the ball had flown free.

By pure accident—

it had crossed the goal.

Class A led.

But almost no one cheered.

Even Tudor looked confused.

*Why did I fall?*

The point felt meaningless.

The mystery mattered more.

Tudor had never experienced anything like it.

At seventeen—

his natural physique already let him overpower many knights in his own family.

Naphtali suited him perfectly.

Jumping.

Running.

Passing.

Tackling.

Receiving.

Blocking.

Any role.

Any position.

Tudor could do it.

Within his age group—

few could match him physically.

*And I bounced off that bookworm?*

He shook his head.

*No.*

*Impossible.*

If Vikir were truly a powerful defender—

he would have stopped the ball too.

Instead—

Class A had scored.

*Something went wrong.*

*I'll do it again properly.*

Tudor returned to his side.

The next play began.

Class B kicked the ball out.

Tudor took it.

Cleanly.

Again—

his control was perfect.

“Try to stop me!”

He accelerated.

Then frowned.

The Baskerville triplets—

who had been fighting him so aggressively—

were gone.

“…?”

Tudor looked ahead.

They were farther back.

Near Vikir.

Almost—

protecting him.

*What are they doing?*

*Why abandon the line?*

Fear?

No.

Tudor knew the Baskerville triplets by reputation.

Strong.

Cunning.

Aggressive.

Not the kind to retreat after one setback.

So Tudor drove toward them anyway.

Highbro closed first.

“You move like a loach.”

Their bodies collided.

Both carried monstrous physical conditioning.

But Naphtali was not a fistfight.

Tudor moved fluidly—

slipped beneath Highbro's arm—

and broke through.

The goal was ahead.

Yet this time—

Tudor's real target was not the goal.

Vikir stood in the rear.

*No coincidence this time.*

Tudor planted.

Drove power from the ground—

through legs—

hips—

waist—

shoulder—

arm.

A full-body charge.

*Take this.*

Impact.

And—

darkness.

Again.

◆◆◆

Vikir watched Tudor collide with him.

Then disappear backward.

He sighed.

*Why does he keep coming here?*

The first time—

Vikir had barely reacted.

This time—

Tudor hit harder.

Much harder.

Vikir, deciding it would look strange if he stayed perfectly upright again—

sat down.

Deliberately.

Tudor—

on the other hand—

did not get up.

He appeared likely to remain unconscious longer this time.

Roll—

The ball came to Vikir's feet.

He looked down.

No one approached.

Tudor's domination of the field had scattered both allies and opponents.

Then—

Class B began shouting.

“Who's that?”

“He's one of ours, right?”

“Tudor's down!”

“Now's the chance!”

“Why is Tudor down?”

“Who cares?”

“We're losing!”

“Run!”

“Wait, who is he?”

“Doesn't matter!”

“Cheer for him!”

Class B was seconds from losing 1–0.

Every eye turned toward Vikir.

He sighed inwardly.

Doing nothing now—

would attract more attention than acting.

So he picked up the ball.

*What should I do?*

Bad situation.

Teammates were either far away or recovering from Tudor's earlier rush.

Class A players were closing in.

If Vikir simply let them take the ball—

Class B would remember him as the person who gave up.

That would create attention too.

Running the length of the field and becoming a hero—

worse.

So—

a compromise.

Vikir pulled his arm back.

Five seconds remained.

“It's over.”

“We lose on the first day.”

“Wait.”

“What's he doing?”

“Three seconds.”

Class A's defenders slowed.

Everyone assumed the game was finished.

Then—

Vikir threw.

The ball shot upward.

One second.

The horn sounded.

Students began rising from the stands.

Then—

someone stopped.

“…Huh?”

The ball was still climbing.

Far beyond the distance anyone expected.

Higher.

Farther.

Then it curved.

A long parabola across the field.

Players on both teams stared.

The official time was already over.

But the ball had left Vikir's hand before the signal.

It continued its flight.

Then—

clang!

It struck one side of the iron Y-shaped goal.

Deflected inward.

And crossed through.

Silence.

The referee's whistle slipped from his mouth.

He stared.

“…Buzzer beater.”
