# Chapter 441: The Fall of Usher (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/441.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 439 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established project terminology controls Vikir, Dolores L. Quovadis, Tudor, Bianca, Camus Morgue, Marquis de Sade, Orca, Roderick Usher, Madeline, House Usher, Tochka, Red Death, and the Night Walkers. Direct Fandom verification remains robots-blocked.

The Night Walkers traveled through the night.

Their destination—

House Usher.

Desolate wasteland.

Swamp.

Tall black rocks.

And in the deepest part of the marsh—

an island.

On that island stood a mansion large enough to be called a castle.

**Masyaf.**

House Usher’s ancestral residence.

A Gothic fortress wrapped in mist rising from the swamp.

Stone walls.

Clouded windows.

Dead vines clinging to the masonry like cracks.

The windows themselves looked like blind gray eyes—

staring emptily into space.

Cold moisture spread everywhere.

And mixed into that moisture—

something deep red.

Something like a ghost.

Every Night Walker frowned.

“…Red Death.”

Dolores’s expression hardened.

“It’s worse than before.”

The plague—

or poison—

that had driven even Cervantes Donquixote toward death.

Now it hung over the entire Usher estate.

Bianca bit her lip.

Hard enough to draw blood.

“…Father.”

No one answered.

Tudor simply placed one hand on her shoulder.

Cervantes Donquixote and Roderick Usher had been lifelong friends—

and rivals.

Cervantes had already died from the advanced Red Death.

Hoping Roderick had survived untouched—

was almost too optimistic.

Everyone knew it.

No one said it.

Even Camus—

usually the first to make a cutting remark—

kept quiet.

Marquis de Sade did not.

“Puhishishi.”

“What filthy air.”

“At this point, everything inside should already be dead.”

Orca glared at him.

“Close your mouth before I flatten you with this club.”

Sade ignored him.

“Vikir.”

“Is there even a point in recovering House Usher now?”

“With poison that thick—

the mansion is probably full of bones.”

“You don’t know that.”

Vikir’s answer surprised everyone.

Usually—

he was the cynic.

The one who assumed the worst.

This time—

he offered hope.

Bianca turned quickly.

“Then…”

“You think there’s still a chance?”

“Yes.”

Vikir nodded.

“House Usher existed as an elite assassination family even before Imperial unification.”

“Their numbers were always small.”

“The reason they became one of the Seven Great Houses anyway—

was the quality of each individual.”

Small.

Elite.

Secretive.

Deadly.

Few families embodied those words more completely.

Sinclaire added an old story.

“When the Emperor first negotiated with House Usher—

the Usher head demanded a private audience.”

“The Emperor agreed to dismiss everyone except two generals.”

“Two men he trusted completely.”

The rest was famous.

The Usher head asked:

*Are you certain those two must remain?*

The Emperor answered:

*They are my brothers.*

*My other selves.*

*I will not separate from them.*

So the Usher head simply looked at the two generals.

And asked:

*Can you kill the Emperor right now?*

Both generals immediately drew their swords.

Pointed them at the Emperor.

As though they had spent years waiting for the command.

“…That story made House Usher famous.”

Sinclaire finished quietly.

“The Emperor treated them with great respect afterward.”

Bianca nodded.

She had always been proud of the family’s history.

For a moment—

old glory softened the pain on her face.

Then reality returned.

House Usher—

one of the greatest families in the Empire—

was being eaten alive from within.

Vikir continued.

“Usher members are strong individually.”

“That matters.”

“The family is small—

but a higher proportion may have resisted the demon.”

“And Masyaf itself has strategic value.”

“With the right preparation—

it can be fortified almost as strongly as Tochka.”

“Before the great war begins—

we need it back.”

Bianca straightened.

“I know the grounds.”

“And every passage inside the mansion.”

“I’ll guide us.”

Vikir shook his head.

“No.”

“The mansion is a labyrinth.”

“Traps everywhere.”

“And the poison is too concentrated for a large group.”

“We send a small team inside.”

“They lure the demon out.”

“We fight outside.”

That created the obvious problem.

Who entered?

Ordinarily—

Vikir would have gone first.

Not now.

“I can’t enter.”

Everyone looked at him.

“I need to prepare for what happens after the demon takes the bait.”

Dolores spoke next.

“If I concentrate everything…”

“I can make enough protection against the poison for two people.”

“Only two.”

“And only for about an hour.”

Two infiltrators.

One choice was obvious.

Bianca.

She knew the mansion.

And Madeline—

the current power behind House Usher—

wanted her badly.

Bianca lowered her voice.

“Aunt Madeline was always sick when she was young.”

“There was an incident where she collapsed.”

“They thought she had died.”

“She was buried.”

“Then she somehow came back.”

Bianca’s face hardened.

“Maybe she really **did** die then.”

“And the demon took her body underground.”

She raised her head.

“If it wants a younger body now—

mine is the obvious target.”

“If I enter Masyaf—

then run—

it will chase me.”

Several hands rose immediately.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Sinclaire.

“I’ll go.”

“I can help.”

“Take me.”

Then Tudor stepped forward.

“Let me.”

Bianca looked at him.

Tudor raised Gungnir with his remaining arm.

“You saved me two days ago.”

“I owe you.”

Bianca’s eyes reddened.

“You’re still reckless.”

“A knight should know when to be reckless in front of a lady.”

“That’s chivalry.”

The two smiled at each other.

Everyone else sensed the atmosphere—

and quietly yielded.

Vikir did not.

“Can the two of you actually do this?”

“Love won’t solve it.”

“The probability of death is much higher than survival.”

Both exploded.

“L-love?!”

“Who said anything about love?!”

“This is loyalty!”

“Friendship!”

Vikir tilted his head.

Then continued as though nothing had happened.

“Fine.”

“Two tasks.”

“First.”

“Enter the mansion and lure the demon outside.”

“Second.”

“Confirm whether Roderick Usher is alive.”

Simple.

And extremely dangerous.

Dolores handed them the protection she had prepared.

“The Red Death has evolved.”

“There are multiple specialized strains now.”

“My blessing won’t last long.”

“An hour at most.”

Bianca squeezed her hand.

“That’s enough.”

“Thank you.”

Then Vikir called her.

“Bianca.”

He placed something in her palm.

A hairpin.

Pink.

Heart-shaped.

Before she could ask—

he turned to Tudor.

“You take these.”

Two signal flares.

One red.

One black.

Vikir explained.

“If Roderick is alive **and can escape with you**—

fire the red one.”

“If he is dead—

or alive but unable to move—

fire the black.”

Bianca looked from the flares to Vikir.

“Why?”

“What happens depending on the color?”

Vikir’s answer was immediate.

“You’re better off not knowing.”

No one asked again.
