A week passed quickly.

WHOOSH—

Vikir burned every one of Garam’s journals.

He had already extracted all the information he needed.

And he could now reproduce Garam’s handwriting perfectly.

*The handwriting-forgery technique I learned from Chihuahua is useful again.*

Any document requiring Garam’s signature—

or handwriting—

could now be handled without hesitation.

That was fortunate.

*I want to leave this place with her.*

Vikir watched the pages burn.

Garam’s wish to escape Nouvelle Vague—

with Kirko—

would never come true.

His words became black-and-white ash—

then sank toward the dark seabed.

“….”

Vikir watched dozens of notebooks disappear into smoke.

A small ending—

for the record of one young man’s entire life.

*Still.*

*You gave me a great deal of useful information.*

*Thank you.*

Vikir remained silent until the final smoke vanished.

Then—

knock.

Knock.

He opened the door.

Kirko stood outside.

Expressionless as always.

“The execution ceremony begins in three hours.”

“…Why are you here this early?”

“I wanted to notify you in advance.”

“…I see.”

Kirko stayed at the door.

Apparently waiting for Vikir to finish dressing.

Her gaze was unusually direct.

Vikir ignored it and buttoned his uniform.

Then—

“Captain.”

Kirko spoke.

Vikir turned.

She inhaled.

“Before we go to the execution grounds—

may I tell you something?”

The request was unexpected.

There was no reason to refuse.

Vikir nodded.

Kirko’s voice became solemn.

“As you know—

I was born in Nouvelle Vague.”

“….”

“A prisoner once raped a guard.”

“I was born from that incident.”

Vikir’s hand stopped over a button.

He remembered D'Ordume’s earlier warning.

*Insubordination can mean immediate execution.*

*Nineteen years ago, there was a disgraceful incident between a prisoner and a guard.*

*Since then, violations of hierarchy have been punished severely.*

Kirko continued calmly.

“My biological father and mother were both sent before a disciplinary committee.”

“Then they disappeared.”

“People assume they may have escaped.”

“In any case—

I don’t know who either of them was.”

“I’ve never been to the surface either.”

“Why tell me this now?”

Kirko hesitated.

“Because I hated you, Captain.”

“That hatred came from the past I just explained.”

Kirko had disliked Garam.

No.

She had despised him.

Not merely because he had once been clumsy.

Slow.

Too soft.

Too kind.

Mistake-prone.

Garam possessed two things Kirko did not.

“You had a home on the surface.”

“And parents.”

“When we first met—

you said you came here because your parents were struggling.”

“You talked about missing your homeland.”

“The mountains.”

“Fields.”

“Streams.”

“Lakes.”

“….”

“I think I envied you.”

“No.”

“An inferiority complex would be more accurate.”

“Whenever you made a mistake—

I looked down on you.”

“That let me fill some of the emptiness in my own pride.”

The real Garam had probably never known.

*Even if he had—

nothing would have changed.*

Vikir simply watched her.

Kirko understood the look.

She took another breath.

“I wanted to apologize honestly.”

“You are now the superior officer I’m expected to serve.”

“I shouldn’t carry that kind of petty, old resentment toward you.”

“Is that why you volunteered to become my adjutant?”

“…I did not volunteer.”

“I see.”

Vikir nodded.

Kirko hesitated again.

More awkwardly this time.

“Captain.”

“Don’t you have something to say to me?”

“…?”

Vikir looked up.

Kirko—

rarely uncertain—

stumbled over the next words.

“During the Level Eight riot…”

“You saved me.”

“What?”

“I have ears too!”

“…?”

Vikir frowned.

He genuinely did not understand.

Sweat appeared faintly on Kirko’s forehead.

The tips of her ears were bright red.

“The other guards have been talking.”

“About what?”

“About why you offered yourself as Sakkuth’s hostage instead of me!”

Only then did Vikir understand.

Sakkuth’s taunts returned to him.

*You like that woman that much?*

*A man risking his life for a woman.*

*You smoked a cigarette covered in my blood for her.*

And the guards’ gossip.

*Garam likes Kirko?*

*Wasn’t it obvious? He always looked at her during roll call.*

*Still, that was reckless.*

*What a man.*

*Risking his life for the woman he loves.*

Kirko hurried onward.

“I-I became your adjutant because of the normal chain of command.”

“Nothing else.”

“Public and private matters must remain separate.”

“If a superior and subordinate become too personally involved—

it could interfere with duty.”

“And…”

She stopped.

Vikir had already finished dressing—

and walked out of the room.

◆◆◆

Step.

Step.

The underground passage resembled the throat of some enormous beast.

Salty drops fell from the ridged ceiling.

Boots struck stone.

Behind Vikir—

another set of steps came quickly.

“Is it appropriate to walk away while your adjutant is speaking?”

Kirko.

Vikir raised one finger.

Pressed it lightly against her lips.

“Shh.”

“Too many people.”

“….”

They had reached Level Five.

The execution grounds were crowded.

Guards.

Prisoners.

And at the center—

bound in chains—

Sakkuth de Leviathan.

Lieutenant Colonel Bastille stood on the platform.

“This is not a formal execution.”

“On the records—

you will remain alive forever.”

The words were meant for Sakkuth.

And for every prisoner watching.

Sakkuth barely reacted.

His eyes only moved restlessly through the guards.

Then—

“…!”

He spotted Vikir.

Relief brightened his face.

The execution detail stepped forward.

Vikir led it.

He took Sakkuth to the cliff.

A narrow rock jutted from the edge.

Sakkuth was made to stand there.

Vikir spoke softly behind him.

“Do not lose your nerve.”

“The rescue operation begins the moment you fall.”

“Understood?”

“Understood.”

Sakkuth answered calmly.

Being thrown into the abyss—

that was Nouvelle Vague’s **unofficial execution**.

His legs trembled.

Vikir noticed.

“Pathetic.”

“Afraid?”

“H-how could I be?”

“Relax.”

“See the guards and prisoners on both sides?”

“They’ve all been bought.”

Sakkuth looked.

Some people stood stiffly.

Others looked relaxed.

Suspicious—

if one desperately wanted them to be suspicious.

“They’re our people.”

“They’ll move the instant I signal.”

“A-amazing.”

“When did you recruit so many?”

“….”

Vikir did not answer.

None of them had been recruited.

They were ordinary guards.

Ordinary prisoners.

Vikir was only exploiting one simple truth.

A desperate person saw what they wanted to see.

Sakkuth turned toward the abyss.

Hope—

with no basis at all—

still held him upright.

“KURURURU!”

“I have no attachment to life!”

“Do whatever you want, bastards!”

He shouted for everyone to hear.

Then glanced back at Vikir—

and winked.

Sakkuth jumped.

WHOOSH—

He fell into blackness.

The guards did not relax.

Because falling—

was not the end.

A moment later—

GRRRRRRRR—

the **real** execution began.

“Huh?”

Even Sakkuth did not know.

Vikir himself had never seen it directly.

A long line formed in the darkness.

Then opened.

A mouth.

Vast.

Impossible.

Teeth—

misshapen and innumerable—

filled it.

The grin seemed endless.

An enormous face looked upward from the abyss.

“A-AAAH!”

Sakkuth went pale.

Vikir swallowed once.

Of Nouvelle Vague’s Five Wardens—

this was the most mysterious.

*I knew it wasn’t human.*

*I didn’t expect this.*

The true form of **Brigadier General Flubber**.
