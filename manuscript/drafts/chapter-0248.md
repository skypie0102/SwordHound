Basilios.

Hippolite.

Whitebeard Whale.

Banshee Morgue.

The four academy officials—

watched the contest through **Extra Eyes**.

A status display floated over the artifact.

**Survivors**

Colosseo — 23.

Mage Tower — 35.

Varangian — 45.

Temisquira — 46.

Basilios rubbed his chin.

“Hohenheim found another target.”

Hippolite smiled.

“As though eliminating a whole cluster of Colosseo students wasn’t enough.”

Earlier—

Hohenheim had turned the jungle around him into a sea of fire.

Roughly thirty Colosseo contestants—

gone.

Many had been experienced third-year aces.

Dolores herself—

one of the expected contenders—

had nearly been eliminated in the opening stage.

Then Hohenheim kept moving.

More than twenty students from Varangian and Temisquira—

swept away by the same fire.

Again—

experienced upper-years.

The reputation fit.

**Hohenheim of a Thousand Flames.**

He seemed intent on removing every serious rival—

before the contest could mature.

Whitebeard Whale watched with satisfaction.

“Our Snub-nosed is finally showing his true nature.”

Banshee remained silent.

“Oh?”

Whitebeard Whale leaned closer to the mirror.

“Another Colosseo encounter.”

Still—

no answer.

“And Dolores again. She barely escaped him once already.”

Banshee’s expression tightened—

only slightly.

His eyes never left the artifact.

The image before him—

showed Vikir and Dolores together.

Joined—

at least temporarily—

inside the jungle.

◆◆◆

Fire tore across the trees.

Mud dried.

Cracked.

Became sand.

Then—

melted into bubbling lava.

Oraorus Hohenheim walked through it.

“Heh.”

His polite host’s expression was gone.

“Colosseo insects.”

His ambition was simple.

Reverse the academy rankings.

Put Mage Tower above Colosseo.

And place himself—

at Mage Tower’s summit.

The annoying older generation was leaving.

This was his chance.

Then—

“…Hm?”

He smelled food.

A large coconut-shell pot bubbled over a fire.

Red stew.

Rich aroma.

Even a genius—

could be hungry.

Even a fourth-circle mage—

could be thirsty.

Hohenheim narrowed his eyes.

“A trap?”

He inspected the camp.

Deep impressions in the bedding.

Shelters.

Pits.

A carefully managed fire.

Evidence that people had planned to stay.

But—

no ambushers.

Only signs of hurried flight.

Even first-year grade markers—

apparently dropped in the rush.

Hohenheim relaxed.

*Freshmen.*

Not worth worrying about.

At most—

perhaps an archer hiding far away.

He prepared for that possibility—

and stepped toward the stew.

Crash.

A thick log exploded apart.

“Hahahaha!”

A huge figure emerged.

“I followed the smell and found something much better!”

Bakilaga Juragio.

Varangian’s student council president.

Hohenheim’s hands extended.

The mud around them—

boiled.

Steam.

Heat.

Then red light.

The earth became lava.

Bakilaga grinned.

Stomp.

He overturned the surface layer of earth—

sending molten material rolling away—

and exposed intact ground beneath.

Then he charged.

Greatsword—

forward.

Pale blue aura rose around the blade—

like steam.

Hohenheim’s eyes widened.

*Peak Sword Expert—*

*No.*

*Sword Graduator?*

Bakilaga laughed.

“You just reached the fourth circle.”

“How could I let you get ahead of me?”

Mage Tower elites—

often did not reach the fourth circle until their thirties.

Varangian elites—

often did not cross into Sword Graduator until the same age.

These two—

had arrived years early.

Sword.

Magic.

The next generation—

colliding at full force.

Boom.

Bakilaga’s greatsword sheared through rock and trees.

Hohenheim retreated.

“Give a mage time—”

Bakilaga pursued.

“—and you’ll regret it.”

The aura rotating along Bakilaga’s blade—

cut through everything it touched.

A **Low Sword Graduator**.

One clean hit—

and Hohenheim’s HP could vanish.

“Shields!”

Multiple translucent barriers appeared.

Crack.

Crack-crack-crack.

The greatsword tore through them.

Aura burst between the fragments—

like a beast’s claws.

“It’s over, Hohenheim.”

Slash.

Hohenheim rolled.

A lock of hair—

cut away.

But he was not finished.

Both palms hit the mud.

Mana surged underground.

The soil boiled.

Dried.

Cracked.

Fire rose through the fractures.

Bakilaga’s visibility disappeared beneath dust—

smoke—

steam.

Then came the fireballs.

And the lava.

The jungle itself began changing.

Moist air—

dried.

Trees that had only charred before—

caught fire.

Hohenheim forced a forest fire into existence—

inside a humid jungle.

Hot air rose.

Met surrounding moisture.

A huge vortex formed.

Fire—

rode the current.

Across Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain—

students from every academy could see the pillar.

Then the firestorm swallowed Bakilaga.

“Ghh—!”

He cut through flame with aura.

But not—

the missing oxygen.

Not—

the smoke filling its place.

Thud.

Bakilaga dropped to one knee.

Hohenheim advanced.

“No matter how hard an ordinary person tries—”

His smile returned.

“—they cannot defeat a genius.”

“That is why genius is called genius.”

He seized Bakilaga by the neck.

Staff raised.

“So this is the end for you too.”

His voice sharpened.

“For someone of barbarian blood, you did well.”

Bakilaga glared upward.

Then Hohenheim noticed—

the stew.

Still bubbling.

He pulled the pot toward himself with magic.

“What a shame.”

“It looks delicious.”

Bakilaga gritted his teeth.

“Just finish me.”

“Don’t think this settles everything.”

Hohenheim laughed.

“How could I?”

“This is our last League as students.”

“We should make it memorable.”

A victor—

should have composure.

So Hohenheim performed it.

He lifted the stew.

Drank.

“Mmm.”

His eyes widened.

“This really is good—!?”

Grrrrrrr.

The warmth reached his stomach.

Something—

answered.
