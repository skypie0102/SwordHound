# Chapter 492: Running Hounds (3)

Close your eyes—

and you can hear the sea.

Sky.

Water.

Both hidden in darkness.

But the sand between your toes—

rough.

Real.

Every time a wave reaches the shore—

a thin wrist trembles.

A handful of sand slips away.

No matter how desperately one cries—

nothing remains.

Not the wave.

Not the sand.

Not even tears.

A stitched mouth asks:

*God.*

*Can’t I hold on more tightly?*

*Can’t I save a little more from those cruel waves?*

*Everything I have now—*

*or think I have—*

*is all of it only a dream inside another dream?*

A beloved person stands on the shore.

Looking down.

The stitched mouth speaks again.

*My love.*

*If I disappear—*

*do not sing sad songs.*

*Do not plant red flowers or blue flowers beside my grave.*

*Only leave a sword there—*

*something that will endure wind, rain, and time.*

*Remember me when you can.*

*And if you forget—*

*then so be it.*

*I cannot hold the heart twisted by your pain.*

*I cannot hear the whispers from your bruised throat.*

*I can only follow the owl’s sorrowful cry—*

*dream before dawn—*

*and slowly fade.*

*Perhaps one day—*

*by accident—*

*I will remember the face of the one I once loved.*

*Or perhaps—*

*I will forget.*

◆◆◆

“…!”

Vikir returned to himself.

His legs nearly gave way.

People said the heaviest things in the world were eyelids.

Vikir finally understood why.

Mana drained from his body like an outgoing tide.

Cold spread through him.

Toes.

Fingers.

Beelzebub—

normally light as an extension of his arm—

felt impossibly heavy.

THUD.

Something struck the floor.

Not Vikir.

He had not fallen.

Andras’s severed head had.

First Corpse.

Marquis of Discord.

Final enemy.

Final demon.

The owl-headed thing hit the stone—

thick blood spilling.

Vikir’s Ninth Form had torn through so completely—

there was no regeneration.

No recovery.

Sssssss—

The cut surface turned to stone.

Then ash.

Andras was truly dying.

Soul.

Body.

Both approaching annihilation.

“…It’s done.”

The words left Vikir without thought.

Immediately—

Decarabia panicked.

[HUMAN!]

[DON’T SAY SOMETHING THAT CURSED!]

[HE’LL GET BACK UP!]

Vikir laughed dryly.

Every story loved one final reversal.

One last enemy movement.

One last cliché.

Not this time.

Andras had burned everything from within.

Vikir’s Ninth Form had torn even the soul apart.

Still—

Andras did not vanish instantly.

[Hahaha.]

[So this is the end.]

Darkness leaked from his eyes.

Only the head remained.

The tongue still moved.

Barely.

[You won.]

[Demon hunter.]

[The Gate of Destruction will close.]

[You and humanity here will never face the remaining demons.]

“….”

[But—]

[is that truly good?]

[Is that truly right for humanity as a whole?]

[Perhaps you have thrown away humanity’s chance at redemption—]

CRUNCH.

Vikir stepped on the head.

Andras did not finish.

Body.

Head.

Both became warm—

fine—

ash.

The last prophecy lingered in Vikir’s ears.

*A lifetime of running.*

*Loneliness.*

*Those chasing you will find you.*

*You will never escape.*

*Forever.*

*Their furious faces.*

*Your future—bound to them forever.*

And finally—

*Five pieces.*

Then—

nothing.

Andras disappeared.

Even beyond the threshold of death.

Completely.

RUMBLE—

The Imperial Palace shook.

The building was collapsing.

Perhaps the naval battle outside had damaged the foundations.

BOOM!

Roof sections fell.

Pillars cracked.

Then—

a huge charred tentacle crashed through the debris.

Kraken.

Or—

what remained of it.

“…Looks like things outside are ending too.”

Vikir turned.

Beyond the rubble—

one portal remained.

Pigi stood before it.

His comrade—

across two lives.

“You worked hard.”

“Really.”

Pigi smiled sadly.

Vikir asked:

“…What happens now?”

“Simple.”

“I close this last portal.”

“And that’s the end.”

The true Gate of Destruction was already closed.

Only one dimensional passage remained—

the road toward the old world where that Gate had once opened.

“I can close gates.”

“I cannot open them.”

“So once I close this—”

“all of them are gone.”

“Forever.”

Vikir nodded.

The hound had run a very long road.

His body was ruined.

Could he rest now?

No.

There was still one task.

No—

the most important one.

Vikir forced himself upright.

Hands on his knees.

Then looked directly at Pigi.

Pigi understood.

Before closing the final passage—

he asked:

“I’ll give you a choice.”

“….”

“Do you want to go back?”

“To the world you came from?”

Beyond the gate—

the world where Vikir had died.

The old timeline.

And Vikir—

with a new body and a second life—

could cross back.

Pigi clearly did not want him to.

“If you go—”

“you can never return here.”

“The connection breaks.”

“This is goodbye forever.”

This world—

for all its wounds—

was approaching peace.

The Long Rain of Terror had ended almost as soon as it began.

The flood would recede.

The Ten Corpses were gone.

Most demons and monsters would be exterminated.

Many people Vikir loved—

were alive.

“You can stay.”

“Live happily.”

“With the people who followed you.”

“You don’t need to return to that barren world.”

Pigi was right.

The old world now faced sixty-two demons—

each beyond the scale of the Ten Corpses.

A catastrophe worse than the Age of Destruction Vikir remembered.

Hell.

Pigi glanced at the portal.

“Some of them still want this world.”

“I can feel them gathering toward the last passage.”

The dimensional door Andras created before death.

Malice—

thick and overwhelming—

pressed from the other side.

If they waited—

something truly monstrous might cross.

Something worse than all Ten Corpses combined.

Vikir hesitated.

Then—

voices came through.

*Total annihilation!*

*We can’t hold!*

*Retreat!*

*There’s nowhere left to retreat!*

*God—save us…*

Death cries.

The last human survivors of Vikir’s original Age of Destruction.

And among them—

voices he knew.

*Morgue—regroup here!*

*Do not lose your faith!*

*Hahaha… money and gold are useless now anyway.*

Vikir made his decision.

“If I can still hear surviving comrades—”

“I can’t end it here.”

He would cross again.

Return to the original world.

Walk the thorn path again.

No.

A longer road.

A crueler one.

Still—

a hound ran.

Bleeding.

Limping.

It ran.

Pigi closed his eyes.

Not surprised.

“Yeah.”

“I knew you’d choose that.”

Vikir said nothing.

Only nodded.

Then—

he climbed the broken steps.

The shattered throne.

The collapsing palace.

And ahead—

the final dimensional gate.

Beyond it—

the world where Vikir’s severed head had once hung.

A timeline soaked in blood.

Vikir looked back.

Pigi smiled sadly.

The Night Hound—

Vikir Van Baskerville—

was about to return to his original world.

This time—

truly crossing back.

Then—

“Where the hell do you think you’re going?”

A voice grabbed Vikir before he crossed.
