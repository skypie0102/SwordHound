# Chapter 457: Infiltration of the Water Source (4)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/457.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 455 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Fresh Fandom indexing supports **Viscount Giuseppe Baldini**. Established project terminology controls Camus Morgue, Morgue Tzersi, Seere, House Leviathan, Poison Humans, Red Death, Dolores L. Quovadis, and Saintess's Tears. Camus’s “Tzersi” identity is an infiltration alias built from her ancestor’s name, not a new Leviathan character.

**Viscount Giuseppe Baldini.**

A demonized member of House Leviathan—

and one of the men assigned an important role in the Tochka annihilation operation.

His post sat behind the main encirclement.

Far enough from the front that Tochka’s walls were barely visible.

The siege line in front of the fortress was packed so tightly that even a needle would have struggled to pass.

The rear—

by comparison—

was looser.

Only by comparison.

Baldini had established numerous guard posts.

Low-ranking demons stood watch everywhere.

And the viscount himself remained in the central command tent.

Alert.

At least—

in theory.

[…This is fine.]

[The enemy has almost no chance of appearing this far behind us.]

Even before demonization—

Baldini had preferred stability and safety.

Power had not changed him much.

[If trouble starts somewhere else, I reinforce it immediately.]

[When nothing is happening, I relax.]

[When battle begins, I react quickly.]

A perfectly reasonable system.

Which was why—

at that moment—

Baldini was drinking tea and eating biscuits.

Then—

CREAK.

The tent flap opened.

Low-ranking demons protested outside.

[W-wait!]

[You can’t just go in—]

[Ugh!]

Baldini looked up.

A woman stood in the doorway.

Long red hair flowed like a waterfall.

Her eyes burned a deeper red.

And more important—

the demonic presence surrounding her was overwhelming.

Baldini immediately checked the day’s schedule.

No high-ranking visitor.

No inspection.

No superior officer.

He stiffened.

[…Who are you?]

The woman answered without hesitation.

[I am **Tzersi**.]

[Left-wing commander attached to the Tochka Extermination Headquarters.]

[I need to pass through this position into the rear.]

[Write me a travel pass.]

Baldini searched his memory.

*Tzersi?*

He knew no high-ranking Leviathan woman by that name.

No officer of that rank.

But—

House Leviathan had recently absorbed forces from Usher and Donquixote.

The command structure was chaotic.

*I can’t know everyone.*

*She may have come from one of the absorbed forces.*

So Baldini chose courtesy over accusation.

[…My apologies.]

[My memory is poor.]

[May I ask why you need to pass this checkpoint?]

Tzersi snorted.

[Do I have to explain a top-secret order from headquarters to you?]

[Write the pass.]

Baldini swallowed his irritation.

*That arrogance.*

*Definitely one of ours.*

Still—

he was not completely incompetent.

[…Forgive me.]

[We are under wartime military law.]

[Could you show identification?]

Tzersi’s brows drew together.

The demonic aura around her surged.

[…So.]

[You’re refusing an order?]

[No.]

[I’m asking you to follow procedure.]

Baldini tried to stay polite.

[I may only be from a branch line, but I have visited the main family several times.]

[I don’t remember seeing you there.]

[Hmph.]

Tzersi’s eyes narrowed.

[I am an illegitimate daughter.]

[Of course you wouldn’t.]

[Idiot.]

Baldini immediately realized he had stepped into a minefield.

An illegitimate child of the main line—

hidden from public view—

was entirely plausible.

And family shame was not something a cautious branch member wanted to poke.

*Damn it.*

*I brought up the main family in front of a high-ranking bastard child.*

*This is becoming complicated.*

Then—

unexpectedly—

Tzersi backed down.

[Fine.]

[I don’t need the pass.]

[I’ll return to headquarters.]

[I’ll bring identification.]

[Military law is military law, after all.]

Baldini blinked.

[…Oh.]

[Yes.]

[Thank you for understanding.]

Too easy.

Suspiciously easy.

Then Tzersi smiled.

[Since you like military law so much—]

[we’ll follow it properly.]

Baldini’s stomach dropped.

[I will **personally** return to headquarters for identification.]

[My urgent mission will be delayed because you refused to exercise discretion.]

[You will answer for that.]

[…]

[Before I leave—]

[draw the sword and shield at your waist.]

[…What?]

[Now.]

Tzersi’s gaze became merciless.

[Why is your uniform jacket unbuttoned?]

[Why are your boot straps tied that way?]

[Why is your equipment not maintained?]

[If you insist on military regulations—]

[I will inspect exactly how well you follow them.]

Baldini went pale.

[ATTENTION!]

Tzersi roared.

[WHY ARE YOU STILL SITTING WHILE A SUPERIOR IS SPEAKING!?]

[I-I’m sorry!]

Baldini shot upright and saluted.

Tzersi circled him.

Slowly.

Cruelly.

And the more closely she inspected—

the worse things became.

A stain on the sword.

A little rust on the shield.

Dust inside the tent.

Loose buttons.

Improper straps.

Tea.

Biscuits.

Private snacks—

during active wartime duty.

[I was going to let things slide.]

[But after seeing your attitude—]

[I can’t.]

[…]

[Improper dress.]

[Poor equipment maintenance.]

[Unsanitary barracks.]

[Loose discipline.]

[Private tea and biscuits on a battlefield.]

[Is this even an authorized meal period?]

[…]

[You kept repeating “military law.”]

[Yet you follow none of it.]

[…]

[People who are strict with others and lenient with themselves—]

[always cause disasters.]

Tzersi suddenly grabbed Baldini by the hair.

YANK.

She pulled his head backward.

[No.]

[This won’t do.]

[At dawn tomorrow—

you’re going to the front.]

Baldini’s face went white.

[I’ll recommend your transfer the moment I return to headquarters—]

[A PASS!]

He almost screamed it.

[I’LL WRITE THE PASS!]

[I WAS WRONG!]

Tzersi shook her head.

[No need.]

[I’m going back.]

[Your entire unit will join the suicide assault force at dawn.]

[T-the front line!?]

[The suicide assault force!?]

That unit had suffered the worst losses during the day’s battle against Orca and Sade.

The conservative Baldini dropped to his knees.

[I WAS WRONG!]

[I’LL ISSUE THE PASS!]

[I’LL CONTACT EVERY REAR CHECKPOINT!]

[YOU WON’T BE DELAYED AT ALL!]

[PLEASE—]

[JUST NOT THE SUICIDE UNIT!]

The atmosphere had completely reversed.

Tzersi finally sighed.

Annoyed.

[Fine.]

[I remember your face.]

[Your name.]

[Your unit number.]

[If I have to come back because something goes wrong—]

[YOU WON’T!]

Baldini practically shouted.

[I’LL MAKE SURE EVERYTHING GOES SMOOTHLY!]

[…Good.]

[I believe you.]

Tzersi took the pass.

Turned.

Left.

Only after she disappeared—

did Baldini wipe the cold sweat from his face.

A subordinate whispered:

[Viscount.]

[Was it really all right to issue a pass without identification?]

Baldini stared at him.

[Didn’t you feel the demonic energy coming off that woman?]

Then he sighed.

[And look at the way she behaves.]

[She’s vicious to the bone.]

[A demon through and through.]

◆◆◆

[See?]

[I told you this name would work.]

The deep voice vanished.

The woman became herself again.

**Camus Morgue.**

She had borrowed the name of one of her ancestors—

**Tzersi**—

and sold the lie with pure intimidation.

“The name itself already carries an ominous reputation.”

“And I can imitate demonic presence.”

“Mostly the smell.”

Camus glanced at her shoulder.

Seere—

exhausted from helping produce that demonic scent—

looked ready to cry.

Whatever the method—

it had worked.

“Well done.”

Vikir patted Camus’s shoulder.

The first checkpoint toward the water source—

cleared without a fight.

And because Baldini had issued official passage—

the following checkpoints were even easier.

One.

Then another.

Then another.

Soon—

a hot, nauseating wind touched their faces.

Vikir raised his head.

Red shapes churned beyond the horizon.

“…Plague territory.”

“A Poison Human concentration zone.”

Dolores stepped forward.

“From here—

I’ll protect everyone.”

She gathered what remained of her sacred power.

Then—

one by one—

gave the others a drop of a **Saintess’s Tear**.

Vikir and Camus—

both exceptionally resistant to poison—

needed less protection than the rest.

The others accepted Dolores’s blessing.

Red Death miasma drifted across the path.

Poison Human corpses lay scattered through it.

Beyond the fog—

another Leviathan encirclement appeared.

Tents.

Wooden fences.

Barracks.

Vikir measured the distance.

Disguise had carried them this far.

It would not carry them through an army of Poison Humans.

“Fifteen minutes.”

Everyone looked at him.

“We infiltrate then.”

From this point forward—

they would have to break through by force.
