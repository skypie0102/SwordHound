# Chapter 113: The Great Banquet (4)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/113.txt` — `732d48b4d2ed32096c603b30559c42cb6120a082`. Recovered English Chapter 112 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Seven Counts, Boston Terrier Le Baskerville, Great Dane Le Baskerville, John Barrymore, Sword Graduator, and Baskerville Fangs. Vikir's demonstration is intentionally limited to his public Intermediate Sword Graduator level.

“…?”

Vikir's lips parted slightly.

Hugo was still watching his younger brothers fight.

And the expression on his face looked satisfied.

Boston Terrier and Great Dane continued snapping at one another.

Both men were hounds who normally bit first and barked afterward.

Only Hugo's presence kept them from going further.

Vikir studied the Patriarch.

*Why is he so pleased?*

Then an answer occurred to him.

*Of course.*

Baskerville was filled with power struggles.

The Patriarch stood at the top.

Below him were the Seven Counts.

Beneath and around them spread countless factions, alliances, and independent power blocs.

Behind closed doors, their politics were bloody.

Assassinations.

False accusations.

Duels.

Takeovers.

Absorption of rival factions.

One power restraining another was normal.

Even Hugo could not stand outside that system.

A Patriarch had to watch half-brothers whose influence grew too strong.

He had to watch powerful relatives by marriage.

Ironically, the relatives closest by blood could become the most dangerous political enemies.

The Seven Counts were Hugo's greatest internal counterweight.

If Boston Terrier and Great Dane tore at each other—

the Seven Counts became less unified.

Hugo became stronger.

*So he has no reason to stop them.*

Vikir's eyes narrowed.

*He might even encourage it.*

A bitter thought followed.

*As cunning as ever.*

At that exact moment, Hugo looked at him.

Vikir lowered his eyes slightly.

Hugo spoke.

“It makes me happy.”

“…?”

“To see my son recognized.”

Vikir looked up.

The same faint, unnatural smile remained on Hugo's face.

Before Vikir could make sense of it, Barrymore leaned close and whispered.

“All seven Counts wanted to attend tonight, Young Master. Every one of them wished to see you.”

“…Is that so?”

“The other five simply couldn't return in time.”

Barrymore's eyes softened.

“My lord also seems rather pleased to see his usually proud brothers so restless over you.”

He gave Vikir a meaningful look.

“Whose son are you, after all?”

Vikir turned back to Hugo.

So the smile was not pleasure at political division?

Hugo was simply proud because his son had earned recognition?

*Impossible.*

Vikir dismissed the thought.

Not Hugo.

Not the cold-blooded man he remembered.

He pushed the question away and focused on dinner.

The main courses began.

Dish after dish filled the enormous table.

*At least it isn't haggis.*

After two years in the Depths, no longer having to force down that awful but filling food was a small blessing.

Boston and Great Dane had still not stopped.

“Our Count Dane is eating things raw again. Some natures never change.”

“And how many glasses of wine have you had? You're already drunk. Go sleep.”

Eventually, however, the two men realized that fighting each other cost them time they could spend on Vikir.

Both turned toward him.

Boston leaned forward.

“So, Vikir! You lived in Le Rouge et Le Noir's wilds for two whole years? Tell your uncle about it.”

His eyes gleamed.

“How many barbarians did you kill? How many monsters did you tear apart? Ah! Just imagining it makes my blood boil!”

He thumped his chest.

“The blood of the Pit Bull! When I was eight, they threw me into the mountains for a live combat trial. I competed with your father for prey back then! But my nephew has done even better. Magnificent!”

Great Dane cleared his throat.

“My brave and intelligent nephew, I won't bother you with such trivial questions.”

A pause.

“Obviously, I am very curious about how you survived two years in that dangerous place. But I can read the report later.”

His voice softened despite himself.

“Tell me first. After two years away, is there anything you need? Anything at all. Your uncle will arrange it.”

The servants almost forgot to breathe.

Neither Count was known for this kind of warmth.

Vikir gave a faint smile.

Boston openly wanted every bloody detail.

Great Dane pretended not to.

His eyes betrayed him.

Then Hugo cut them both off.

“Let him eat.”

The two Counts stopped.

“Don't crowd him.”

Hugo was protecting Vikir.

Vikir had still not adjusted to that strange fact when Boston muttered—

“Still. As an uncle, I'm naturally curious how much he has grown…”

Great Dane nodded.

“Two years ago he was already extraordinary. I rode all this way because I wanted to see what he had become.”

Hugo's brow lowered.

“So you intend to torment my son at the dinner table?”

“N-no.”

“…Of course not.”

Even the Seven Counts moderated themselves before Hugo.

They looked away.

Then Vikir spoke.

“Proving my progress is not difficult.”

Both Counts brightened.

Hugo, however, sounded almost concerned.

“Son. A proper demonstration would mean going to the training ground.”

His eyes moved over Vikir.

“You've traveled a long way. There is no need to exhaust yourself in the middle of dinner.”

“I'm fine, Father.”

Vikir set down his fork.

“Besides—”

He placed one hand flat on the table.

“Strength can be shown at any time and in any place.”

His gaze remained calm.

“Isn't daily life itself a form of training?”

Hugo smiled.

Boston smiled.

Great Dane smiled.

“Brother,” Boston said, “you really do have a fine son.”

“If I had a son like this,” Great Dane added, “I'd ask for nothing more.”

“Ahem.”

Hugo stroked his beard and turned his face away.

“Enough. It isn't that remarkable…”

Barrymore watched from behind with an openly pleased expression.

Boston could no longer contain himself.

“So how will you show us?”

Great Dane leaned forward too.

Vikir did not answer.

His palm remained on the table.

Silence.

Then—

“Uncle Dane.”

Great Dane looked over.

“Your steak seems a little too raw.”

Vikir turned toward Boston.

“And Uncle Terrier's wineglass is too full.”

The two men glanced down.

Then—

Rrrrrrr—

Vikir's fingers barely moved.

The cutlery trembled.

Both Counts immediately pulled their hands away from the table.

Mana flowed out of Vikir.

Across the stone.

Into selected objects.

Sssssss—

Great Dane's steak began to cook.

Nothing around it changed.

Only the meat.

Blood evaporated.

Steam rose.

Red flesh browned.

Rare became medium.

Medium became well-done.

Great Dane picked up the fork lying beside the plate.

It was cool.

He cut the steak.

Firm flesh.

A faint charred aroma.

At the same time—

“Ha ha ha ha!”

Boston stared at his silver goblet.

Bubble.

Bubble.

Bubble.

Purple wine boiled violently.

Sweet vapor filled the hall.

The level sank.

Then, at exactly half a cup—

the boiling stopped.

Half the wine had vanished.

Nothing else had heated.

Vikir had touched the table once.

Cooked one man's steak.

Boiled exactly half another man's wine.

And left everything surrounding them untouched.

This was more than releasing mana.

It was the ability to send power into an object—

then detonate or release it at a chosen point, in a chosen amount, in a chosen form.

Apply that same control to a sword—

mana entered the blade.

Aura emerged exactly where the swordsman willed it.

That realm had a name.

**Sword Graduator.**

The mature stage immediately below Sword Master.

A realm that symbolized the empire's highest conventional military force.

And Vikir had just displayed the control of an **Intermediate Sword Graduator**.

Even talented Baskervilles normally struggled to reach that level around thirty-five.

Vikir was seventeen.

The youngest Intermediate Sword Graduator in the family's recorded history.

Another old Baskerville record had just shattered.
