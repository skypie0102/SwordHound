The six second-years kept Pigi surrounded.

Three boys.

Three girls.

All of them laughing.

“Call that a draw.”

“Let's see what happens next.”

“If the next round's another draw—”

one of them grinned,

“maybe we'll start pulling teeth.”

Another clapped a rhythm.

“One beat!”

“Two beats!”

“Three!”

“Come on!”

“Do something!”

“We're bored!”

Pigi said nothing.

His eyes were wet.

The girls giggled.

“Is he crying?”

“Aww.”

“Poor thing.”

Then one of them turned theatrically toward the boys.

“You're all terrible.”

“Why are you bullying him?”

The boys laughed harder.

“Bullying?”

“When did we bully anybody?”

“We're just trying to get close.”

“We'll be seeing each other all the time.”

Another leaned toward Pigi.

“Why are you crying?”

“Do we look like bad people?”

A girl patted Pigi on the shoulder.

“Come on.”

“Don't cry.”

“Have a drink.”

A large glass was filled.

Beer and stronger liquor.

Fermented alcohol mixed with distilled spirits.

A brutal combination.

The kind of drink guaranteed to punish anyone the next morning.

One second-year held it in front of Pigi.

“Finish it.”

“Then you don't have to perform.”

The six of them boxed him in.

Their smiles made it clear they had no intention of letting him leave until he obeyed.

Pigi finally took the glass.

He tried.

Barely managed two swallows—

then gagged.

The drink came back up almost immediately.

The second-years erupted.

“Ugh!”

“That's disgusting!”

“You got it on my stockings!”

Another laughed until he bent over.

“He actually threw up!”

“What a first night!”

A nickname followed almost instantly.

“Puke King!”

“Pigi the Puke King!”

“We're calling you that until graduation!”

Heads turned from across the room.

Whispers began.

Pigi seemed to shrink further.

Then—

flutter.

Something covered the mess on the floor.

A cloak.

Vikir's freshman cloak.

The Academy crest was embroidered across it.

For a new student—

the cloak was an important symbol.

A formal keepsake of admission.

Vikir took it off without hesitation and used it to cover what Pigi had vomited.

Then he stepped beside his roommate.

“Go back to the dormitory.”

Pigi looked at him.

His face trembled.

Vikir's voice remained calm.

“I'll handle this.”

Pigi said nothing.

Vikir bent down and began cleaning the floor with the cloak.

Then—

a hand grabbed his shoulder.

“Hey.”

A second-year frowned.

“Are you insane?”

“We're talking to him.”

Another looked down at the cloak.

“You know what that thing means, right?”

“The Academy crest.”

“And you're using it like a rag?”

A third student reached toward Vikir's glasses.

“Are you his classmate?”

“Cold Department B?”

“What was your name?”

“Move those bangs.”

“Let's see your face.”

Vikir did not react.

That only seemed to irritate them more.

“You're pretty confident.”

“Walking in like that.”

One of the second-years lifted the unfinished glass.

“Fine.”

“If you're such a good friend—”

he pushed it forward,

“drink the rest for him.”

Vodka.

Rum.

Absinthe.

Other strong spirits mixed together.

Pigi had barely touched it.

Most of the large glass remained.

“Finish it.”

“Then we'll let both of you go.”

“Come on.”

“Show some loyalty.”

Vikir looked at the glass.

Then took it.

No argument.

No expression.

He drank.

Slowly enough that everyone could see it.

Steadily enough that no one interrupted.

Until the glass was empty.

The second-years stared.

Baskerville children had been conditioned from infancy to tolerate poisons in small amounts.

Field missions also required resistance to alcohol.

A hound could not afford to become useless because someone offered him a drink.

Vikir's body was already unusually resilient.

And in his previous life—

long campaigns had given him more experience with strong alcohol than he cared to remember.

He set the empty glass on the table.

Thunk.

Then—

without saying anything—

walked away.

For several seconds—

the second-years did not move.

By the time they recovered—

someone else spoke.

“What's going on over there?”

**Dolores L. Quovadis** had returned while heading back toward the Hot Department gathering.

Her gaze moved across the group.

“Did someone get sick?”

The second-years immediately straightened.

“A freshman drank too much.”

“We were just cleaning up.”

“Nothing serious.”

Dolores studied them.

Then said,

“Do not mistreat new students.”

The smiles vanished from their faces.

“Upperclassmen are expected to behave with dignity.”

“Understood?”

“Yes!”

“Of course!”

Dolores looked at them one more time.

“…Good.”

Then she continued on.

Only after she disappeared did the group relax.

They looked around.

Vikir and Pigi were already gone.

“Tch.”

“They slipped away.”

One of them scoffed.

“I've never seen someone use an admission cloak to clean up after another person.”

“Commoner thing?”

“Maybe he doesn't understand what the symbol is worth.”

“We'll find them later.”

Another student frowned.

“And then what?”

“You know unsupervised duels are prohibited.”

A grin.

“Who said anything about a duel?”

“There are plenty of ways to crush someone during sports.”

The six left the room—

already discussing how they might make life difficult for the two freshmen later.

◆◆◆

Several hours passed.

The six second-years were drunk enough to want fresh air.

“And ice cream.”

One of them laughed.

“Let's go outside and get some.”

“The shops beyond the wall are better anyway.”

They left through a rear gate.

The Academy's open season had brought a small night festival into the nearby shopping district.

Food stalls lined the streets.

Lanterns hung over alleys.

The second-years headed toward the brighter avenue.

They did not get far.

A hand emerged from the darkness and seized the first student.

“What—?!”

He vanished into a narrow alley.

The others froze.

A tall shadow stood ahead of them.

Black gloves.

A broad black hat.

A beaked mask.

No visible expression.

No readable eyes.

The **Night Hound**.

Before any of them could gather themselves—

the confrontation ended.

Fast.

Overwhelming.

None of the six had time to mount a meaningful defense.

Soon—

all of them were on the ground—

stunned, frightened, and finally silent.

The Night Hound looked down at them.

One of the students managed to speak.

“You…”

His voice shook.

“Do you know who we are?”

The Night Hound nodded.

“Yes.”

A distorted growl came through the mask.

“Very well.”

Then he named them.

One after another.

Their names.

Their houses.

Their family positions.

Even the names and ranks of their parents.

Color drained from every face.

The Night Hound crouched.

In one gloved hand—

a small blade caught the faint light of the alley.

The six students stopped trying to sound brave.

He marked each of them.

A simple **X**.

A target sign.

A scar intended to remain visible long enough that they would remember the night.

Then the Night Hound spoke.

“Cause trouble inside the Academy again.”

His voice was quiet.

“And I will come back.”

The six students shook.

The Night Hound leaned closer.

“Next time, your family name will not protect you.”

No one answered.

No one laughed.

The threat was enough.

The lesson—

delivered.
