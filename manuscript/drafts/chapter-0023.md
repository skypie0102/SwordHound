# Chapter 23: Camus Morgue (4)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/023.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 23 is an aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English forms for Adolf Morgue's Sixth-Circle level and Camus Morgue's **Quadra Casting / Fireball / Ice Sphere / Thunder Ring / Mud Wall** follow current Fandom evidence.

What happened next shocked everyone in the room.

“Hic—!”

Even Hugo Le Baskerville reacted.

Vikir's movement had been too sudden.

“Aaaaaah!”

Camus squeezed her eyes shut and screamed.

From her perspective, her arm was about to be cut off.

For the first time in her life, the pampered young prodigy of the Morgue Clan was facing what felt like an immediate threat of mutilation.

She burst into tears like the eight-year-old child she actually was.

Even Adolf had apparently never seen his fearless niece cry this way.

“Stop!”

Mana surged from him on instinct.

The pressure of a **Sixth-Circle** mage crashed toward Vikir.

But Vikir had already expected it.

Rustle—

He released Camus's arm and retreated before the pressure could pin him down.

“Uwaaah! Uncle! He grabbed my arm…!”

Camus fled to Adolf and buried herself in his cloak.

Adolf stared at Vikir, momentarily too astonished even to be angry.

Then he rounded on Hugo.

“Clan Head! What is the meaning of this?”

Hugo ignored the protest and tilted his head slightly toward Vikir.

“Son.”

Vikir answered at once.

“Yes, Father.”

“That joke went a little too far.”

“My apologies. Among the children of our Clan, this kind of prank is common.”

Vikir took the short blade in both hands.

Then bent it.

Flop.

The blade folded easily.

Rubber.

A cheap toy knife.

Adolf stared.

“What? You have toys like that here?”

“You've never seen a boy playing with a toy blade?” Hugo asked calmly. “Baskerville children start handling such things from the time they can walk.”

Only then did Adolf look closely.

The weapon was plainly fake.

He had been so distracted by the killing pressure Vikir released in that instant that he had failed to judge the object itself.

For a man famous for being careful and composed, it was an embarrassing mistake.

Hugo found it deeply satisfying.

Conveniently, he forgot that he too had been startled a moment earlier.

“Enough. I've already decided how to deal with the ruby-mine issue. Morgue should find the proposal satisfactory.”

“…”

“So let us finish this friendly competition first.”

Hugo made no attempt to hide how eager he was to move on.

Adolf looked ready to object.

“Sniff… h-hic… That brat! I won't forgive him! Just wait!”

Unfortunately, he was occupied with Camus, who was crying into his cloak and wiping her nose on it.

And so a serious meeting between two great clans was temporarily derailed by a quarrel between two eight-year-olds.

◆◆◆

*Magic and the sword oppose one another in ordinary times, but in a crisis they complete one another and together save the nation.*

That belief, attributed to a former emperor, had become the justification for the annual friendly competition between Morgue and Baskerville.

Children from eight to fifteen gathered and compared their ability.

Ordinarily, the younger children drew little attention.

Their events focused more on theory, mana sensitivity, and fundamentals.

The fifteen-year-olds were the real spectacle.

Sword against magic.

Practical combat.

This year was different.

Everyone's attention had shifted toward the arena reserved for the eight-year-olds.

Two children had become the stars of the entire event.

Vikir Van Baskerville of the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan.

Camus Morgue of the great magical house.

At Morgue's suggestion, their match had been moved beside the arena where the fifteen-year-olds were conducting real combat.

The two eight-year-olds would fight under similarly practical rules.

Vikir looked as expressionless as ever.

Camus looked furious.

“Prepare yourself.”

“…”

“Once the match starts, I'm not going easy on you.”

“…”

“Say something!”

“…”

“Argh!”

Vikir yawned.

Camus stamped one foot hard enough to make her shoe click against the ground.

Then the bell rang.

The match began.

“Yaaaaah!”

Camus attacked immediately.

Mana surged around her.

No probing exchange.

No attempt to hide her hand.

She opened at full force.

Tactically, it was immature.

Her talent was anything but.

**Quadra Casting.**

Most young mages struggled to cast two spells in quick succession.

Camus simultaneously maintained four.

**Fireball.**

**Ice Sphere.**

**Thunder Ring.**

**Mud Wall.**

They were only 1st-Circle spells.

Even so, controlling four different spells at once required a degree of parallel calculation that many fifteen-year-old Morgue children would struggle to achieve.

Fire, ice, and lightning hammered the ground around Vikir.

BOOM! CRACK! ZZT!

Each projectile was only around the size of a child's fist.

A direct hit would sting.

That was about all.

Vikir slipped between them with minimal movement.

“Flashy. But at your age you'd be stronger if you concentrated on one spell.”

“Shut up! What would an ignorant swordsman know about magic?”

Camus drove the three offensive spells after him while maintaining Mud Wall for defense.

She was determined to land even one proper hit.

Whoosh—

Vikir avoided the barrage and instead closed the distance.

Then—

CRASH!

His foot smashed through the Mud Wall protecting her.

“Ah!?”

Camus inhaled sharply.

He was already too close for her to comfortably bring the offensive spells around without risking herself.

The broken wall fell away.

Vikir's face appeared directly in front of her.

Camus's heart jumped.

‘I'm done!’

She shut her eyes as his hand came toward her forehead.

Flick!

A sharp sting.

Camus recoiled, eyes watering.

“…Huh?”

That was all.

Vikir had simply flicked her forehead hard enough to leave a small bump.

“Y-you! Are you going easy on me!?”

“…”

“Don't come closer!”

Camus canceled one of her three offensive spells.

Rumble!

Two layers of Mud Wall rose in front of her.

“Hah! Two walls now! Let's see you get through that!”

There was one obvious problem.

Camus could not see through them either.

Thump!

Vikir's hand punched through both layers as though they were paper.

His fingers appeared directly in front of her face.

“Whose hand is this?”

The words deliberately echoed their argument in Hugo's chamber.

Camus stared, too surprised to answer.

“The stronger person's.”

Flick!

The same spot on her forehead was struck again.

A second bump appeared beside the first.

Before she could retaliate, Vikir's hand vanished back through the wall.

It was not a serious attack.

That made it worse.

“You dare make fun of me—aaaaaagh!”

Camus's temper finally broke.

She canceled the remaining offensive spells and threw everything into defense.

Four Mud Walls rose around her at once, sealing her inside a thick earthen enclosure.

“Come on! What about this? Nobody can break through four walls! Not even you!”

Her vision went completely dark.

She did not care.

At least Vikir could not see her rubbing the sore bumps on her forehead.

“…”

Silence.

Camus smiled smugly.

“Ha! Loser! Too short to get through the walls? Come on, idiot!”

Nothing.

More silence.

After a while, Camus's confidence began to fade.

‘Wait. I can't get out either.’

She had sealed herself inside her own defense.

Maintaining all four walls also prevented her from trying much else.

‘Maybe just a tiny hole. Enough to see outside.’

She relaxed one section of the magic.

A small opening appeared.

Camus leaned toward it.

Flick!

Vikir's fingers shot through like a ghost.

The same spot on her forehead was struck a third time.

“Aaaaaah!”

Three hits.

Three little bumps clustered together.

Camus clutched her forehead, trembling with pain and humiliation.

Then her eyes began to burn.

The four walls collapsed at once.

Vikir was already standing well outside arm's reach.

“I'M GOING TO KILL YOU!”

Camus was crying openly now, too furious to care about dignity.

She abandoned defense altogether.

Four offensive spells rose around her simultaneously.

The sheer output was something even the nearby fifteen-year-old arenas rarely saw.

Vikir watched her rage and thought seriously about what to do next.

‘This is troublesome.’

If the problem were simply killing her, it would take less than a heartbeat to break her neck.

That was not remotely what this situation required.

Vikir had spent most of his life on battlefields.

He had almost no idea how much he was supposed to humor an angry child.

And this was not just any child.

She was Morgue's treasured prodigy.

Eventually, Vikir reached a simple conclusion.

‘A Morgue problem should be solved by Morgue.’

The cleanest solution was to let someone else handle it.

BOOM!

An explosion sounded from the neighboring arena.

Vikir glanced sideways.

A fifteen-year-old Baskerville and a fifteen-year-old Morgue mage were locked in an intense sword-and-magic exchange.

Neither had noticed how close the younger children's fight had drifted.

The Morgue combatant appeared to specialize in powerful explosive fire magic.

Perfect.

Vikir retreated toward the edge of that arena.

Camus chased him without hesitation.

‘If anything actually becomes dangerous, the referees will intervene.’

Vikir adjusted his path by a fraction.

Camus followed.

Then—

BOOOOM!

An explosion erupted beside them.

The spectators screamed.

“Camus!”

“Lady Camus!”

“No!”

The scene Vikir had been waiting for was finally taking shape.
