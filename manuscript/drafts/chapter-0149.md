# Chapter 149: Volunteering for the Golden Week (6)

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

Saintess of the Faithful House of Quovadis.

At first—

she had not liked Vikir.

No.

More accurately—

she had disliked him.

Her impression had been poor from the moment they first met at the Academy.

*Vikir.*

*Cold Department, Class B.*

*What did you do to accumulate this many demerits already?*

*…*

*Even if they're only conduct demerits, too many will damage your grades.*

*You may even have to miss lectures to complete community service.*

*Do you understand?*

*I understand.*

*…I'll be watching you.*

A first-year student who had piled up demerits almost immediately after admission.

Late.

Entering restricted areas.

Ignoring rules.

And whenever Dolores confronted him—

he showed neither panic nor visible guilt.

Lazy.

Shameless.

Self-assured.

There had been little to like.

Worse—

the moment Vikir joined the newspaper club—

he had spoken mercilessly about the Night Hound.

*He's definitely a villain.*

The words had made anger rise inside Dolores.

*Who is he to judge someone so confidently?*

A freshman who sat in comfortable lecture halls—

safe beneath the Academy's protection—

and still managed to accumulate demerits through laziness and misconduct.

What right did someone like that have to judge the Night Hound?

Dolores remembered Saint Meka.

The Red Death.

The slums full of suffering people.

And the masked man who had worked beside her.

The Night Hound.

He was now suspected in attacks against Quovadis-affiliated institutions.

Even so—

Dolores could not believe he was evil.

She had watched him treat Red Death patients with her own eyes.

*How could someone who kept treating patients even while handling their filth and waste be called wicked?*

The self-sacrifice she had witnessed in Saint Meka—

could not have been an act.

His soul had been rough.

Scarred.

Worn by suffering.

Yet the purity Dolores sensed from it had been clearer than that of many ordained clergy.

She did not know who the Night Hound was.

She did not know where he came from.

But she could tell—

he had walked through countless thorns.

And to Dolores—

goodness that continued to bloom from those wounds was something sacred.

To suffer abuse.

Oppression.

Persecution.

And still choose to help others.

A martyr.

That was how Dolores saw the Night Hound.

And so—

she disliked people who carelessly insulted such figures.

Words thrown from safe, comfortable places—

could become another weight on the shoulders of someone already walking a path of thorns.

Perhaps that was why—

Dolores had never managed to view Vikir favorably.

Then came the corridor.

When Vikir suddenly covered her mouth and pressed her into the recess—

Dolores had been shocked.

Confused.

Flustered.

But after Quilt passed—

that confusion turned into anger.

She was the Saintess of Quovadis.

Quilt was a lower-ranking cleric—

the head of a collateral family.

By status alone—

Dolores had no reason to shrink from him.

And then Quilt had said—

*Even if someone forced a Saintess into an immoral act, a sufficient offering would purchase absolution.*

A deliberate insult.

Dolores had been furious.

More furious still at Vikir—

because if he had not hidden her away—

she believed she might have stepped out and confronted Quilt directly.

So she stormed off.

Then—

only seconds later—

she regretted it.

As the anger faded—

reflection replaced it.

Vikir had clearly been acting with good intentions.

At least—

that was what Dolores believed.

He had spared her an awkward confrontation.

And if she was completely honest with herself—

seeing Quilt had made her uncomfortable.

Even afraid.

It sounded absurd.

Why fear a cleric lower in rank?

Because Quilt was not an ordinary subordinate.

Some people stood below you on paper—

yet never felt subordinate at all.

To Dolores—

Quilt was one of them.

His oily politeness.

His shameless confidence.

His relaxed smile no matter whom he addressed.

All of it made her deeply uncomfortable.

And she knew where that confidence came from.

Money.

Especially the enormous offerings that flowed upward through his sale of indulgences.

As head of House Indulgentia—

Quilt turned indulgence revenue into donations.

Those donations became influence.

Influence became connections.

Connections became power.

A man with that much money—

and that many ties to the upper clergy—

was not someone even the Saintess of Quovadis could strike at casually.

Even when he insulted her openly—

she could do little beyond become angry.

The internal struggle within Quovadis had already become public news.

Under those circumstances—

Dolores could not honestly blame Vikir for trying to be considerate.

*…No.*

*I should be grateful.*

She finally admitted it.

If she had met Quilt face-to-face in the corridor—

she might have lost control of her expression.

She might have made a mistake.

Vikir had prevented that.

And there was something else.

*“Suffocating.”*

She remembered the expression on Vikir's face as Quilt passed.

He had clearly been disgusted.

That suggested—

at least in Dolores's mind—

that Vikir also hated the sale of indulgences.

On that point—

they agreed.

Her opinion of him improved slightly.

*Still…*

*Shoving a woman you barely know against a wall is excessive.*

*It's not as though we have that kind of relationship.*

Dolores had never been physically close to a man before.

Being pulled into his arms—

pressed against the wall—

had left a stronger impression than she wanted to admit.

Even now—

remembering the moment made her heart pound as though something had struck it.

*He really startled me.*

Heat crept into her face.

Dolores fanned herself with one hand.

*Maybe… aside from being lazy, he's not actually a bad person.*

It was a surprisingly generous judgment from someone whose personal ethic treated diligence almost as a moral virtue.

A little later—

Dolores began looking for Vikir.

She intended to apologize for what had happened in the corridor.

But finding him—

proved strangely difficult.

She spent half the day moving through the orphanage.

During a break—

she asked the first-years.

Tudor answered first.

“Huh? Vikir?”

“He was cleaning the toilets with us a little while ago.”

“Then he heard the dining hall was short on people, so he went to help.”

“We were going to decide who had to go with rock-paper-scissors.”

“He volunteered before we could even play.”

Dolores went from the toilets—

to the dining hall.

There—

Sancho was eating with the children.

“Vikir?”

“He was helping me serve meals a minute ago.”

“Then he heard the dining hall had no hot water, so he went to check the pipes.”

“He was so busy serving food that he didn't even eat.”

From the dining hall—

Dolores went to the plumbing room.

There—

a soaked Pigi pointed toward another building.

“Oh! President!”

“Lunch was great!”

“You're looking for Vikir, right?”

“He should be in the laundry room now.”

“He fixed the pipe, so we finally have hot water again!”

From the plumbing room—

Dolores went to the laundry.

Sinclaire was there washing clothes.

She greeted Dolores brightly.

“Senior Dolores!”

“Vikir?”

“He was doing laundry with me.”

“Then the children begged him to play, so they dragged him off to the playroom.”

“They have so much energy!”

Dolores hesitated.

“And why are you calling him ‘bro’ now?”

Sinclaire laughed.

“He told me not to call him ‘Big Brother,’ so I shortened it!”

“He didn't tell me to stop!”

From the laundry room—

Dolores went to the playroom.

There—

Bianca looked thoroughly exhausted while carrying children around on her back.

“Who?”

“Vikir?”

“Haah…”

“He was helping entertain them until a little while ago.”

“Then some of the children wanted to play outside.”

“So he took them to the playground and went ahead to clean it up.”

“At least he took some of them with him.”

“I couldn't handle all of them.”

“How do children have this much stamina…?”

Toilets.

Dining hall.

Plumbing room.

Laundry.

Playroom.

Finally—

the playground.

As Dolores walked—

one thought kept returning.

*How much work can one person do in a day?*

By her count—

Vikir had already completed the workload of more than ten ordinary volunteers.

Cleaning toilets.

Serving meals.

Repairing pipes.

Doing laundry.

Playing with children.

Maintaining the playground.

Every one of those jobs was tiring.

How many had he finished before noon?

Most Academy students were nearly useless at this kind of labor.

They had grown up too comfortably.

They had never needed to perform chores like these.

Vikir—

by contrast—

worked without complaint.

He handled the unpleasant jobs no one wanted.

Often where no one was watching.

Of course—

Dolores did not know the real reason.

Before his regression—

Vikir had spent years doing exactly this kind of grunt work in the military.

He was extremely good at it.

And now—

moving from one job to another gave him the perfect excuse to gather intelligence throughout the orphanage.

Dolores knew none of that.

Her conclusion was very different.

*I judged him completely wrong.*

*He's incredibly diligent.*

She felt ashamed.

She had thought of Vikir as lazy and careless.

Worse—

she prided herself on treating people without prejudice.

Yet she had judged a junior—

a new member of her own club—

through assumptions she had never properly tested.

Dolores decided that Vikir deserved more than a revised opinion.

He deserved an apology.

At last—

she reached the playground.

Children were playing in the distance.

A little farther away—

Vikir was sitting by himself.

He had been clearing weeds and removing stones—

flattening the ground so the children could use it safely.

Even while doing that—

he was reading papers covered in writing.

*He's studying too?*

Dolores was genuinely impressed.

Hard physical work.

Service to the children.

And still—

he had not neglected his academics.

*A model student.*

Then another thought occurred to her.

*I wonder how good his grades are.*

Dolores did not know Vikir's written-exam scores.

She decided she would check after returning to the Academy.

*If he needs help—*

*I could tutor him.*

Dolores had never fallen below the top three in her third-year written rankings.

She was confident she could be useful.

With that thought—

she walked up behind him.

“Ahem.”

The apology—

which had seemed easy in her head—

refused to come out.

Dolores normally apologized immediately when she was wrong.

For some reason—

doing it in front of Vikir felt difficult.

Perhaps part of her still resented what he had said about the Night Hound.

*That's an excuse.*

*I was wrong.*

*I should apologize properly.*

“Ahem!”

“Ahem!”

She coughed louder.

Finally—

Vikir looked back.

“…?”

The moment he saw Dolores—

he folded the papers and put them away.

Dolores spoke awkwardly.

“Preparing for a written exam?”

“You're studying even while volunteering?”

“…You're diligent.”

“What is it?”

Vikir's voice was cold and short.

Dolores interpreted that as lingering anger from yesterday.

She stumbled over her words.

“Um.”

“Ahem.”

“I just wanted to ask…”

“How is the service work going?”

“I asked the others.”

“They all said you've been working very hard.”

“Do you think the work suits you?”

“Yes.”

“…”

“…”

“…That's the whole answer?”

“Yes.”

“…I see.”

Dolores had no idea how to keep the conversation moving.

*Is talking to someone supposed to be this difficult?*

Everyone she normally dealt with—

talked before she even needed to ask.

People around the Saintess often volunteered ten answers to a single question.

Sometimes they supplied things she had never wanted to know.

Vikir was different.

If something did not need to be said—

he did not say it.

Not even when the person in front of him was a third-year student council president—

and Saintess of one of the Seven Great Families.

Finally—

Dolores decided to say it directly.

“Actually…”

“I came to talk about yesterday.”

“…?”

Vikir frowned.

Dolores interpreted that too.

*He must still be uncomfortable about it.*

She closed her eyes.

Then forced the apology out.

“I think I overreacted because of my family situation.”

“You were only trying to help me.”

“And I shouted at you.”

“So what I did yesterday was really—”

Pop!

Vikir suddenly stood.

Then ran.

“I'm sorry—huh?”

Dolores stared after him.

Forgiveness could not be demanded.

But being abandoned in the middle of a sincere apology still hurt.

Embarrassment.

Shame.

A little indignation.

Then—

“Ah!”

Dolores's eyes widened.

Vikir was not running away from her.

He was running toward the drainage channel—

where several children had gathered dangerously close to the edge.

The channel was deep.

Filthy.

Normally—

the children never approached it.

Dolores ran after him.

When she reached them—

she saw Peri crying.

*The ball…*

The children's ball had fallen into the drain.

Dolores knew how scarce toys were here.

The children repaired the same old balls again and again.

Whenever Dolores visited—

she often brought a new one.

But with so many children playing—

even those wore out quickly.

To lose what they believed was their last ball down the drain—

must have felt devastating.

Then—

“It's deep.”

“Move.”

Vikir's warning was brief.

Dolores turned.

“…?”

Splash!

Vikir jumped into the sewage.

No hesitation.

The wastewater was full of filth.

Vikir moved through it anyway—

caught the black “ball” before the current carried it away—

and climbed back out.

Water and grime poured from him.

The children surrounded him.

Wide-eyed.

Vikir looked at them.

“Don't play near the drain.”

“It's dangerous.”

“I already cleared the playground over there.”

“Play there instead.”

He held up the filthy black egg.

“And this is dirty.”

“Uncle will wash it and give it back.”

Then he turned away—

calmly brushing filth from himself.

Dolores stared at his broad back.

“…”

Suddenly—

her entire body felt hot.

*What is this?*

*Why?*

Her cheeks burned so badly that she pressed cool palms against them.

She did not understand the feeling.

Not even a little.
