# Chapter 82: The Saintess (5)

Quovadis**, **Mozgus Quovadis**, **Nabokov I Quovadis**, **Old Testament Faction**, **New Testament Faction**, **Leviathan Clan**, **Bourgeois Clan**, **Red Death**, and **Hound of the Night**. The Chinese paternal pet-name is rendered nonsexually as “little sprite”; the sexualized English gloss attached to the raw is not reproduced.

Quovadis priests prayed and worked through day and night.

At last, every stage of the third phase succeeded.

They had created a divine purification essence concentrated enough that a single drop could cleanse a wide contaminated area.

Three drops were made.

One for use.

Two as reserve.

**Plany de la Verge.**

**Saintess's Tears.**

Dolores stood before the three tiny vessels and recited a prayer of blessing and gratitude.

An old lament from the first Saintess.

“Look. Here is a woman with only one son.

“Do you know a mother's pain when she sees her son suffer?

“Unfortunate me—I had to raise the son of God. From that night until the next evening, I wept while my heart ached beyond endurance.

“Oh, my beautiful and noble son. This is the most sorrowful, most heart-rending day of my life……”

The ancient lament had comforted generations who endured sacred sacrifice.

As the prayer ended, all three drops began to shine.

Mozgus bowed deeply.

He placed the small glass containers on a tray.

More than a dozen bishops joined the escort.

The holy water would be locked in a fortified vault beneath the Saint Mecca Quovadis residence until deployment.

Priests from across the family gathered to watch.

Dolores felt the tension immediately.

Quovadis was already divided internally.

**Old Testament Faction.**

**New Testament Faction.**

The Red Death was serious enough that both sides had assembled in the same place.

Then a dignified middle-aged man looked down at her.

“Come closer, my little sprite. My daughter.”

Dolores hesitated.

Then bowed.

“……Yes, Father.”

She approached in small, careful steps.

**Humbert L. Quovadis.**

Cardinal.

The practical leader of the Old Testament Faction.

Within that hierarchy, only the Pope stood above him.

He touched Dolores's head lightly.

“You have contributed greatly.”

“You praise me too much.”

Dolores was always careful around him.

Their formality seemed unusual for father and daughter.

In a family as large and political as Quovadis, no one commented on it.

“Report.”

“I prepared the written version—”

“Tell me directly. Writing is too stiff.”

Dolores nodded.

“We will use one drop of Saintess's Tears to cleanse the groundwater beneath Saint Mecca.”

“Did you find the source?”

“Yes.”

She looked up.

“An unregistered well on the high side of the slums.”

Dolores had always been an excellent investigator.

In her first academy year, she had joined a detective club.

She questioned patients.

Especially children.

Again and again she heard the same strange story.

A ghost at the well.

A ghost that said the water was cursed.

That testimony led her directly to the contaminated source.

Near it, Quovadis found a glass vial believed to have carried the plague.

“And there was something else.”

This part was not in the written report.

Dolores unwrapped a clean cloth.

Humbert's eyes widened.

A black dagger.

A great serpent engraved across it.

**Leviathan.**

Beside it lay a gold button.

A nail-and-hammer emblem.

**Bourgeois.**

“Where?”

“Different places around the same well.”

Humbert's expression changed.

Dolores had already reported that Red Death looked artificial.

Creating something so complex required two things.

Deep knowledge of poison.

Enormous research money.

Leviathan.

Bourgeois.

BANG.

Humbert stamped once.

White marble cracked.

Two nearby stone pillars collapsed.

“Prepare for holy war.”

His voice shook with fury.

“Poisoners and profiteers dare bring this filth into Quovadis territory?”

The will of Quovadis carried enough political weight that even the Emperor yielded ground to it. For poisoners and profiteers to provoke the holy house so openly was an outrage.

The Seven Great Clans had always fought through politics.

Now that conflict was close to becoming something else.

Dolores remained silent.

Then Humbert asked another question.

“The man called the Hound of the Night.”

She looked up.

“He is suspicious too.”

Humbert's face hardened.

“Arrest him. Put him before the Inquisition. Make him tell us everything he knows.”

Dolores panicked.

“He isn't guilty!”

Silence.

Humbert looked down at her.

“……He?”

Dolores had almost never contradicted him.

Now her fists were clenched.

Her body trembled.

But she did not lower her eyes.

“A man who worked that hard to save the sick cannot be evil.”

Patients had told her about him.

He had stayed through the night.

Used what little divine power he possessed.

Given away every coin in his pocket.

And repeatedly apologized.

Dolores understood that last part more than anyone.

She too felt guilty before suffering people.

Others told her she should accept gratitude.

She still felt sorry.

The Hound of the Night seemed to understand that feeling.

She remembered a conversation from the relief work.

“I was simply born with divine power.”

Dolores had looked down.

“My body is weak. My mind is weak. My faith is weak.”

There were people with stronger faith who had no miraculous gift at all.

“Why was I chosen?”

The masked man had answered quietly.

“Perhaps the fact that suffering hurts you is the reason.”

Dolores had looked up.

“Only when someone reaches the lowest and most painful places can they understand the love your faith speaks of.”

The words had felt strangely mature.

As though a far older Saintess had reached backward through time to comfort her younger self.

Not romance in that moment.

Guidance.

Something like the reassurance of a trusted elder sister.

Dolores had asked him one more thing.

“When I grow older, will I be able to say something like that?”

A pause.

“……Perhaps.”

That tiny expression of confidence had stayed with her.

So now she defended him.

The more strongly she did, the colder Humbert became.

“Prepare an emergency arrest party.”

“Father—”

“I will question him myself.”

Dolores followed in alarm.

She was ready to stand between Humbert and the Hound if necessary.

They reached the guest room.

Everyone stopped.

The room was empty.

Cold wind entered through a broken window.

Curtains rose and fell like ghosts.

The Hound of the Night was gone.

Then a priest shouted from below.

“Emergency!”

Footsteps thundered through the hall.

“One drop of Saintess's Tears is missing!”

Dolores's mouth opened slightly.

Understanding came all at once.

Why he had appeared.

Why he had endured the slum.

Why he had waited for the holy water.

And why, now that he had obtained what he needed, he had disappeared.

Humbert began shouting orders.

Paladins formed pursuit groups.

Dolores barely heard them.

She stood at the broken window.

Looked into the darkness.

The certainty slowly settled over her.

She might never see the Hound of the Night again.

THUMP.

Her heart dropped.

This heartbeat felt nothing like the one before.
