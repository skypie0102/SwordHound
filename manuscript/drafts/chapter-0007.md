# Chapter 7: The Baskerville Dog (4)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/007.txt`. Canonical English forms for Hugo Le Baskerville, John Barrymore, Morgue Clan, and Bloody Beans checked against the user-designated Fandom wiki where applicable. Recovered English Chapter 7 is an aligned secondary reference only.

Vikir understood Hugo’s personality very well.

‘… A lizard.’

His blood was so cold that one could almost mistake him for a reptile.

At his core, Hugo cared about only two things: the future of the clan and his own achievements.

Everyone else was merely a tool in his eyes.

Especially those he regarded as weapons.

A weapon existed to hurt others.

A weapon did not grant mercy on its own.

A weapon did not hesitate.

Naturally, the more poisonous and vicious a weapon was, the better the weapon became.

So there was nothing strange about Hugo’s gaze toward Vikir gradually softening.

“Hm. You really believe you did nothing wrong?”

“Yes. My brothers were the ones who were wrong.”

“What did they do wrong?”

“They were weak.”

The strong stood above.

Weakness itself was a sin.

Was that not the Baskerville way?

Vikir’s answer struck directly at the core doctrine of the clan.

A lion did not sin by devouring a deer.

The strong crushing the weak was simply the law of nature.

Trying to divide that law into crime and punishment was nothing more than the whining of the powerless.

Those were the lessons Vikir’s tutors had hammered into him throughout his childhood, all of them rooted in Hugo’s teachings.

‘… Back then, I tried telling him that my brothers had bullied me first.’

In his previous life, Vikir had reacted like an ordinary child, explaining his innocence and listing the wrongs committed by his brothers.

Hugo had only frowned at him with even greater contempt.

That contempt had never disappeared.

Not even in the final moment when Vikir knelt beneath the guillotine.

Across from him now, Hugo Le Baskerville interlaced his fingers and covered the lower half of his face.

Then he spoke in a low voice.

“Your brothers came here before you.”

“…”

“They said that, as your elders, they would forgive you.”

Vikir did not answer.

He had spent far too many years at Hugo’s side not to understand what had happened.

The triplets had almost certainly failed to give Hugo an answer he liked.

More likely, they had only irritated him further.

‘They must have been terrified. And all that talk about forgiveness… they probably complained about being attacked by someone younger and begged to be kept away from me.’

Vikir answered without emotion.

“Age is something anyone can gain with time.”

“…”

Hugo paused.

Then a faint smile appeared on his lips.

“Heh. True enough. This father of yours also had to work very hard to become the eldest son before taking the patriarch’s seat.”

Hugo referring to himself as Vikir’s father was strange enough.

But the rest of the statement was stranger still.

‘Worked hard to become the eldest son? Is that something effort can change?’

Vikir had never heard that story before.

He thought for a moment.

Then he understood.

Hugo had become the eldest by killing the brothers who had been born before him.

Only after that fratricide had he risen to become patriarch.

‘I see. I always assumed the eldest inherited the clan. I suppose, in Baskerville, even “eldest” can be acquired after the fact.’

Once again, Vikir was reminded what sort of family the Baskervilles truly were.

Hugo asked another question.

“Regardless, your brothers reached out first and offered forgiveness. Do you still feel no guilt?”

“…”

Vikir stared at him in silence for a moment.

There was an unfamiliar warmth in his father’s eyes—something Vikir had never received in his previous life.

But a heart frozen solid over decades could not be melted by warmth this faint.

Long before his regression, there had once been a woman who was the final survivor of a family destroyed by the Baskervilles.

Years later, she became a nun and came to Hugo personally to hold a mass in which she declared, ‘I forgive you.’

When Hugo heard those words, he had said,

“Isn’t forgiveness nothing more than an excuse used by the weak who lack the power to take revenge?”

Vikir now returned essentially the same answer to him.

Hugo’s eyes widened.

“Hahahahahaha—!”

His laughter thundered through the room hard enough to make the windows tremble.

Hugo leaned back in his chair, utterly satisfied.

“Now that sounds like my child. It’s almost like looking at my eldest.”

It was the first time Vikir had ever seen Hugo express such strong emotion toward one of his children.

Their conversation ended there.

“Barrymore.”

The instant Hugo summoned his butler, his expression returned to its usual impassive state.

Yet some warmth still lingered in his voice.

“Give Vikir the key to the pantry.”

John Barrymore’s eyes widened.

Baskerville children ate the same food until the age of fifteen.

Water and haggis.

Various meats and offal were mixed with a small amount of vegetables, mashed together, and made into dense patties.

The result was extremely high in calories and nutrients.

It was also salty, unpleasant, and nearly devoid of pleasure.

The food itself was unlimited and prepared under strict hygienic standards, but taste was not considered important.

That was why Baskerville children became so excited over the occasional candy or piece of chocolate awarded for excellent results in their basic education.

It was an extremely cheap way to motivate them, intensify competition, and shape them into useful members of the clan.

Knowing all that, Hugo asked Vikir,

“Is there any snack you want?”

Vikir immediately put on the innocent smile of an ordinary eight-year-old.

“Chocolate!”

Hugo nodded.

‘At least his childishness is appropriate for his age.’

Barrymore also smiled with obvious satisfaction, as though thinking, ‘A child is still a child after all.’

Hugo gestured to him.

“Take him to the pantry and let him choose as much chocolate as he wants. Don’t let him become absurdly greedy, though. Only as much as he can carry himself.”

“Yes, my lord.”

Barrymore took Vikir by the hand and led him toward the door.

Just before they left, Hugo turned his back to them and spoke again.

“Prepare well for this midterm examination.”

Encouragement from Hugo was rare enough.

What followed was even more extraordinary.

“… Don’t lose to the direct line.”

At those words, Vikir’s eyes seemed to glow blood-red.

Like two suns.

◆◆◆

“We’re here, Young Master.”

Barrymore led Vikir to the kitchen outside the nursery castle.

Several cooks stood respectfully behind them.

The pantry lay deep underground and remained cool at all times.

Cold air seeping through cracks in the stone met the warmer air rushing in through the opened door, leaving a thin crust of frost along the entrance.

Barrymore raised a lantern and illuminated the interior.

Magic could have provided light more easily, but Vikir had no reason to slight an old butler who had deliberately chosen to guide him this way.

He stepped inside.

Ingredients eaten by the clan’s guardian knights, family members over fifteen, and other servants had been arranged neatly across the storeroom.

“Candy and jelly are kept here, Young Master. If there is a particular dish you would like, I can have the cooks prepare it for you.”

Vikir shook his head at the kind offer.

“Chocolate is enough.”

Barrymore looked down at him with faint pity.

The poor child must have been desperate for chocolate.

“Once you are older than fifteen, you will be able to eat whatever you wish.”

He meant it sincerely as consolation.

At a glance from the head butler, the cooks began taking boxes of premium chocolate from the shelves.

“This is top-grade chocolate favored by the gourmands of the Morgue Clan. We obtained several boxes ourselves this time. They say its flavor becomes even richer when combined with nuts from the south and honey from the west.”

Vikir shook his head again.

“I don’t need anything processed.”

“… Pardon?”

Barrymore and the cooks looked confused.

Vikir clarified.

“I want raw cocoa beans. The strongest-flavored variety you have.”

Barrymore frowned.

Cocoa beans were the raw material used to make chocolate.

Unprocessed beans were not sweet in the slightest.

They were intensely bitter.

A particularly strong variety would be almost nothing but bitterness.

After listening to the cooks, Barrymore finally said,

“Hm. Something with an especially powerful flavor… yes, we do have one. Years ago, the Patriarch personally led clan officials in subjugating barbarians on the western frontier, then turned a vast stretch of jungle there into farmland. One of that region’s specialties is called the ‘Bloody Bean.’ They say a single bean is concentrated enough to flavor nearly a hundred liters of chocolate.”

“Good. Bring them.”

“How many?”

“All of them.”

At Vikir’s order, the cooks hurried away.

Before long, one returned carrying a small leather pouch.

The bag was barely large enough to hold two clenched fists, yet it was packed with vivid red beans.

There were more than a hundred of them.

Bloody Beans.

One bean was said to have enough concentrated flavor for roughly a hundred liters of chocolate.

Vikir bit into one.

Crunch!

The reaction was immediate.

An overpowering bitterness flooded his mouth and coated his entire tongue.

Vikir spat the bean out and nodded in satisfaction.

Barrymore nodded as well.

‘He really does love chocolate.’

Hugo had said Vikir could take as much food as he could personally carry, so there was no rule preventing him from taking the entire pouch.

Still, this one little bag held enough cocoa to flavor roughly ten thousand liters of chocolate.

Barrymore could not help admiring both the greed and ingenuity of the eight-year-old.

At this rate, Vikir could enjoy his beloved chocolate for the rest of his life.

“Young Master. Shall I have these processed and delivered to your room?”

But Vikir gave an answer that confused everyone around him.

“No processing. They’re fine exactly like this.”

Apparently, he had not asked for the beans because he wanted to eat them.
