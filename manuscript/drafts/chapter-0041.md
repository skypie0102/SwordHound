# Chapter 41: Morgue's United Front (2)

“Have a safe journey! I'll take care of the administration here!”

With Chihuahua's salute behind him, Vikir rode away from Underdog City Hall.

He headed toward the edge of the territory on a black horse.

Before long, another black horse drew up behind him.

“Good to see you again, Young Master.”

The rider was Sir Staffordshire Baskerville of the Pit Bull Knights.

He had once been responsible for Vikir's training, and he had also taken part in the operation against the illegal slave auction.

“How is Uncle Boston Terrier?”

“Quite well. The Count has been insisting ever since the slave-auction incident that you must join the Pit Bull Knights one day. He made certain I understood that too.”

The Morgue Clan was aware of what was really happening here.

For some time, Morgue's armed forces had also been gathering in the region under the guise of ordinary miners.

Now Morgue and Baskerville intended to cooperate against the tribes beyond the frontier.

Those tribes, unaware of the force being assembled, would eventually come raiding as they always did—seeking crops and captives.

The moment they did, the counterattack would begin.

Staffordshire said,

“I hear the Morgue Clan has sent one Grand Senator and twelve Middle Senators from the Light Faction, one Grand Senator and nineteen Middle Senators from the Dark Faction, and roughly a hundred additional mages who hold no senatorial rank.”

“They have committed quite a force.”

By comparison, the visible Baskerville contingent consisted of Vikir and only a few dozen ordinary knights.

But that was merely what could be seen.

Baskerville's true elites were lying in ambush on the far side of the mountains.

That arrangement had been part of the agreement with Morgue from the beginning.

Vikir himself really had come to inspect the territory and maintain the appearance of friendly cooperation.

*Still, if Hugo is right, it would be useful to observe the tribes' movements while I'm here.*

Before long, the Baskerville party entered the mining district.

Unfinished mine shafts lay everywhere.

Wooden palisades, crude fortifications, and tall watchtowers dotted the landscape.

Then Vikir noticed something.

“Hm?”

He slowed his horse.

A burnt smell reached his nose.

It was the smell of meat cooked far past done, until nothing remained but blackened flesh.

The horses sensed it too.

They grew uneasy and began resisting the reins.

Something stood upright in the distance.

Long.

Sharp.

Rising straight from the earth toward the sky.

And something had been threaded through the middle of it.

Vikir nudged his reluctant horse forward.

Soon the strange structures came into focus.

They were enormous iron spikes.

The metal had been drawn together from trace iron scattered throughout the soil.

Only one kind of person could create something like that.

“A mage.”

They were on land controlled by the Morgue Clan, one of the great magical families.

That part was hardly surprising.

What had been impaled on the spikes was another matter.

Skeletons.

Charred bodies.

Everything that had died on those spikes had been burned black.

Monsters and humans alike had been skewered together.

Some had burned so completely that even the bones had crumbled away, leaving only empty iron spikes behind.

Whoooosh—

Whenever the wind passed through, the impaled corpses shed black powder into the air.

Thud.

A lump of charcoal that might once have been a tribesman—or perhaps a monster—fell from one spike and collapsed into ash.

“That's one hell of a warning.”

Staffordshire looked up at the burned bodies.

It was clearly a warning from Morgue to the monsters and tribes beyond the frontier.

And Vikir already knew someone who favored exactly this kind of warning.

*...So you've grown quite a bit too.*

As an old memory stirred in his mind—

“Who goes there!”

“Stop!”

“Identify yourselves!”

Sharp voices rang out from the watchtower ahead.

Vikir looked up.

Three girls were descending from the top of the tower.

They stepped lightly through empty air as though walking down an invisible staircase.

Their hair burned red like flame.

Elegant dresses looked strangely out of place against the cold, brutal fortress.

Vikir already knew who they were from his memories before regression.

**Highsis Morgue. Middlesis Morgue. Lowsis Morgue.**

Triplets of the Morgue Clan.

They were sixteen this year.

Between the three of them, they specialized in plant, earth, and water magic, and their power multiplied dramatically when they worked together.

Born on the same day at the same hour, the three were known as the **Three Flowers of Morgue**.

The wider world had another name for them.

**Samhwa.**

The **Three Plagues**.

Each possessed a thoroughly troublesome personality.

Together, they were said to be capable of turning any gathering into a disaster.

Their pride bordered on arrogance, and they were notorious even among the Baskervilles.

At annual family events, they routinely ruined what were supposed to be friendly matches.

What made matters worse was that their magical talent was strong enough to support their behavior.

Now the three sisters were guarding the fortress controlling entry into Morgue's leased territory.

Vikir rode forward.

“We are an envoy from House Baskerville.”

Highsis, standing in front, gave a derisive snort.

“And?”

“And we are here to inspect the territory and conduct a friendly exchange. Open the fortress gate.”

“Not now. We've reported your arrival to the person in charge. Wait.”

She said *wait*, but nothing about her manner suggested the wait would be reasonable.

Vikir asked,

“How long?”

“Hm? Maybe we'll get an answer tomorrow? Hohoho.”

That was not procedure.

It was provocation.

Vikir's eyes narrowed.

“We are Baskervilles entering Baskerville land. On what authority are you blocking us? You're remarkably arrogant for tenants.”

“What? Tenants? Haven't you heard of the Tenant Protection Act? Your own family made that law. Don't you even know your own laws?”

“I do. I already revised that one. Troublesome tenants can now be removed.”

“...”

Highsis was briefly struck speechless.

Vikir urged his horse another step forward.

“I am the Deputy Magistrate of Underdog City. This visit was arranged in advance. This is the last warning.”

“...”

“Open the gate.”

The three sisters exchanged glances.

Then Highsis smiled.

“I heard some young upstart took over Underdog City.”

“Oh my. What should we do?”

“We have plenty of experience dealing with little upstarts!”

Mana gathered over their palms.

A moment later—

RUMBLE!

Water, plants, and earth magic erupted across the ground in front of the Baskerville party.

Vikir frowned and drew his horse back a step.

A cloud of dust and debris rose between the two groups.

The sisters' laughter rang sharply through it.

“Then wait like you're told, you ignorant swordsmen!”

“Even Baskervilles don't get to march freely through Morgue territory!”

“Let's see just how impressive Baskerville's little rising star really is!”

Staffordshire came alongside Vikir.

“It seems words aren't going to solve this.”

Vikir had reached the same conclusion.

Then three angry voices exploded from behind them.

“You insolent little brats! Do you have any idea who you're mouthing off to!?”

“Who do you think you're talking to!?”

“Who do you think you're talking to!?”

Three familiar figures burst through the settling dust.

Highbro.

Middlebro.

Lowbro.

The Baskerville triplets had been riding near the rear of the delegation.

Now they stepped forward together and glared at the three Morgue sisters.

The sisters sneered back.

“Insolent?”

“You think you're above us?”

“Know your place!”

The brothers shook their heads.

“Not us.”

“Not us.”

“Not us.”

“Then who?”

“Who?”

“Who?”

At the sisters' questions, all three brothers turned their heads at once.

“This guy is the face of Baskerville!”

Their gazes—filled with an odd mixture of respect and fear—landed in the same direction.

On Vikir.
