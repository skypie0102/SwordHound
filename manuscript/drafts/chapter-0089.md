# Chapter 89: The Illiad (5)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/089.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 88 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Vikir**, **Ah'Heman**, **Aiyen**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain**, **Beelzebub**, **High Sword Graduator**, **Sixth Fang**, **Silent Heel — Mushuhushu (A+)**, **Incinerate — Cerberus (A+)**, **Rokoko**, and **Oxbear**. Chinese **spider** and **inner thigh** control over conflicting recovered-English wording.

The sword forests of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain were extraordinarily dangerous.

Every leaf on every tree was as sharp as a razor.

From bark to roots, there was almost nothing in the forest that could not cut, pierce, poison, or kill.

And now, in the middle of that hellish jungle, two men were fighting for their lives.

Vikir.

Ah'Heman.

Each stood facing the other with a knife at his waist and a bow across his back.

Ballak warriors formed a huge circle around them.

Dense leaves and tangled vines blocked ordinary sight, but Ballak hunters possessed extraordinary eyes and ears. Even from hundreds of meters away, they could follow the duel as clearly as if it were taking place directly in front of them.

Ah'Heman clenched his teeth.

“…No matter how much you've learned, you've only been here for two years.”

Vikir had spent only two years adapting to this jungle.

Ah'Heman had lived here for nearly seventy.

He knew the terrain.

The plants.

The hiding places.

The dangers.

Most young hunters could not compare with that depth of experience.

“I'll make you regret choosing the jungle as the battlefield for the Illiad.”

Ah'Heman slipped quickly into the dense forest.

He hid among roots and vines and nocked an arrow.

“Oh? Here's something useful.”

Something beneath the vines caught his eye.

A large spider.

Red body.

Black legs.

Ah'Heman took one of his arrows and drove the tip through the spider's head.

The skull burst.

Highly toxic fluid from the crushed brain coated the arrowhead.

Then he prepared the ground.

He stretched several almost transparent vines across likely approaches. They were difficult to see, difficult to snap, and sharp enough to cut flesh deeply.

Anyone who caught a foot in one while jumping could be thrown to the ground.

With luck, the vine might even sever an ankle.

Next, Ah'Heman disguised patches of soft mud beneath dry sand and fallen leaves.

He also positioned several beehives so they could be knocked down and awaken the aggressive insects sleeping inside.

“Come on.”

He crouched behind thick foliage with the poisoned arrow ready.

“Let's see how you get through all this.”

He waited for Vikir to appear.

Then—

“…Who are you waiting for?”

A cold voice touched his ear.

Ah'Heman's entire body seized.

His heart nearly leapt out of his chest.

He forced himself to turn.

Vikir was standing behind him.

Expressionless.

Close.

And somehow—

completely silent.

“H-how!?”

How had he approached without making a sound?

Ah'Heman did not have time to ask.

Vikir flicked Beelzebub to one side.

Slice.

Every cutting vine Ah'Heman had stretched across the ground snapped apart.

Ah'Heman hurried backward to avoid the blade.

His foot came down on the mud pit he had hidden himself.

He sank into it.

In the process, he knocked loose several of the beehives he had prepared.

BZZZZZZZZ!

The furious insects swarmed the man who had just destroyed their nests.

Vikir silently stepped away.

Ah'Heman thrashed in the mud, waving his arms and trying to drive the swarm off.

In the end, he escaped by submerging himself in the pit for a long time until the insects lost interest.

By then, however, his body had swollen all over from the stings.

When Ah'Heman finally crawled out of the mud—

Vikir was already sitting on a tree trunk above him.

Yawning.

“Y-you bastard!?”

Ah'Heman snatched up his bow.

Falling into the mud had washed the spider venom from the arrowhead, but a sharp arrow from a powerful bow was dangerous even without poison.

Twang!

Mud sprayed from the string.

The arrow shot straight toward Vikir.

But—

Crack!

A crimson aura slash crossed the air and split the arrow cleanly in two.

Before Ah'Heman could react, Vikir disappeared like a ghost.

Then he was behind him.

Ah'Heman's eyes widened until the skin around them tightened.

Vikir's speed was astonishing.

But that was not what truly terrified him.

*There's no sound!?*

Vikir was moving at extreme speed directly in front of him—

yet Ah'Heman could hear almost nothing.

No grass crushed underfoot.

No splash through mud.

No branches snapping.

No stones shifting.

No logs kicked aside.

If any sound existed at all, it was so faint that the chirping insects of the jungle swallowed it completely.

“You—what trick are you using, you bastard!?”

Ah'Heman fired arrow after arrow.

They struck only several unfortunate orangutans in the trees.

Then Vikir drew Beelzebub fully.

A dark-red aura flowed from the blade.

Thick as honey.

Viscous as blood.

There was no mistaking the level of power.

**High Sword Graduator.**

Even among Ballak's experienced veteran warriors, very few could display aura of that density.

Ah'Heman's face went pale.

*This brat was this strong!?*

The power did not match Vikir's apparent age at all.

Ah'Heman retreated.

But once the six fangs of a hound sank into prey—

they did not let go.

**Baskerville Sixth Fang — Carnivorous.**

Six hidden fangs burst forward.

They ripped across Ah'Heman's body again and again.

And wherever Beelzebub's edge passed—

burning pain followed.

The infernal flames of **Incinerate — Cerberus** clung to the wounds.

Only Vikir could properly see them.

To Ah'Heman, it simply felt as if his flesh were being sliced apart with red-hot blades while fire burned beneath his skin and cooked the fat and muscle underneath.

“Aaaaaagh!”

Ah'Heman shook from pain he could not understand.

He had lived almost seventy years.

He had been cut by knives.

Pierced by spears.

Shot by arrows.

He knew pain.

But he had never experienced anything like this.

The Ballak warriors watching from afar did not know about the invisible hellfire.

All they saw was an old shaman screaming over wounds that did not look severe enough to justify such agony.

Mockery erupted from the forest.

“Ugh…!”

Ah'Heman finally abandoned all pretense of dignity.

Honor.

Pride.

Tradition.

None of it mattered anymore.

The old man who had challenged a younger warrior was now fleeing in disgrace, and the Ballak warriors jeered him mercilessly.

Booing and curses rolled through the dense foliage until it felt as though the entire jungle were condemning him.

Ah'Heman spun around in panic and tried to shoot Vikir again.

What he had forgotten was that Vikir had spent the last two years learning archery from Aiyen.

He was no novice anymore.

Twang!

An arrow rose in a clean arc.

Thud!

It buried itself in the inside of Ah'Heman's thigh.

“Ghh—!?”

His eyes opened so wide that the skin at their edges split.

Bloody tears ran down his face.

Ah'Heman clutched his thigh and collapsed.

The leaves in front of him parted.

Rustle.

Vikir walked through them without expression.

“You were so interested in Oxbear genitals before.”

His gaze dropped briefly to the arrow wound.

“Now look at the situation you're in.”

Two years earlier, the subject of eating the genitals of hunted prey had been one of the humiliating threats Vikir had used against Ah'Heman.

Remembering it, Ah'Heman bit through his own lip.

Blood ran down his chin.

“You're mocking me!”

“I wasn't trying to.”

Vikir's voice stayed flat.

“You aren't important enough to be my plaything.”

“Ugh… Ughhh!”

Ah'Heman staggered upright.

Then he threw away both knife and bow.

“…!”

Vikir immediately sensed the change.

The flow of the air shifted.

Dark mana began gathering.

It crawled inward from every direction and converged between Ah'Heman's bloodied palms.

*…So this is the shaman's real power.*

Vikir had expected Ah'Heman to keep something hidden.

The old man traced sigils with bloody hands and began chanting an unfamiliar incantation.

Black currents spilled outward.

Crack. Crack!

Vikir felt several hands seize for his ankles and stepped back.

Corpses were rising between him and Ah'Heman.

The orangutans Ah'Heman had accidentally shot earlier.

Some still carried rotting flesh.

Others showed exposed bone.

Now they moved again.

Ah'Heman had reanimated them through witchcraft.

Vikir's eyes narrowed.

*I see.*

*Ah'Heman was born among the Rokoko.*

He remembered Aiyen's words before the Illiad.

Ah'Heman had originally come from another tribe.

Now the missing name became obvious.

The Rokoko.

Among the peoples of the jungle, this art was called witchcraft.

In the Empire, it would be classified as a form of black magic.

And corpse reanimation was one of the specialties of Rokoko shamans.

Ah'Heman had turned the freshly dead orangutans into zombies and skeletons to defend him.

“Heh… heh heh heh!”

The old man laughed desperately.

“The Illiad has no rules!”

His eyes shone with relief at finally having found a weapon Vikir had not yet broken.

“No one said we had to fight only with knives and bows!”

Many Ballak warriors frowned.

Their society valued physical ability above nearly everything else, and most warriors had little interest in sorcery.

Ah'Heman, however, had spent years quietly studying and mastering it on his own.

“Go!”

He pointed at Vikir.

“Stop him! Buy me time to treat my wounds!”

The undead orangutans lurched forward.

They stood about as tall as humans.

The largest could weigh close to a hundred kilograms.

Ah'Heman believed they would be more than enough as disposable shields.

Vikir merely took one quiet step backward.

“Hm.”

His voice remained emotionless.

“Weaker than I expected.”

“…?”

Ah'Heman opened his mouth.

Then—

BOOM!

Something enormous struck him from behind.

The impact smashed into his back with tremendous force.

Skin tore.

His spine cracked.

His organs felt as though they had burst inside him.

Ah'Heman was hurled forward and rolled across the ground.

The undead orangutans beside him were crushed at the same instant, reduced to mangled heaps of blood and flesh.

Only Vikir had moved back without making a sound.

“???”

Saliva hanging from one corner of his mouth, Ah'Heman painfully lifted his head.

He had no idea what had just happened.

His tilted vision slowly found the huge shadow looming above him.

[Grrrrrr…]

A giant beast with poor eyesight raised its ears, listening.

An old female **Oxbear** stood over Ah'Heman.

One enormous forepaw was reaching toward him.
