do not boast of the pyramids.

**Colosseo**—

**Colosseo Academy.**

The foremost educational institution in the world—

east, west, north, south, and across the Central Continent.

It stood on the most valuable land in Venetior, the Imperial Capital at the heart of the Central Continent.

Unlike the **Magic Tower**, which specialized in magic—

or **Varangian**, which specialized in martial discipline—

Colosseo demanded excellence in both scholarship and combat.

Its standards were infamous.

Fail to meet them—

and one could neither enter nor graduate.

With the new semester approaching, enormous crowds poured into **Venetior**, capital of the Rok Empire.

Freshmen.

Their families.

The escorts accompanying them.

Merchants hoping to sell them everything imaginable.

No other day of the year drew as many people into the city as the Academy's opening season.

New students arriving from every continent found themselves astonished twice.

First—

by the beauty of the capital.

Then—

by the scale of the Academy.

**Venetior, the City of Water.**

The Imperial Capital lay in a gently descending basin where rivers from across the continent converged.

From the roads leading into the city, one could look down over the entire lower district.

A clear freshwater lake embraced the capital.

One hundred and twelve islands dotted its surface.

Nearly four hundred bridges connected them.

Warm rivers flowed slowly through countless canals.

Bridges crossed waterways at every turn.

Crowded streets ran over and between them while beautiful buildings rose high above.

Aerial bridges connected one tall structure to another.

Evergreen plants covered many of those elevated walkways.

The canals were not merely decorative.

They served as major transportation routes.

Small boats threaded through narrow alleys.

Floating vendors appeared everywhere.

As the capital of a great empire, Venetior possessed nearly every modern cultural institution imaginable—

theaters.

Museums.

Clock towers.

Religious sites.

Opera houses.

The Imperial Library.

Huge public baths.

And yet—

the city's most famous institution remained Colosseo.

The place where the continent's finest talents gathered.

The Academy itself formed an enormous circular complex.

Students, professors, and staff together numbered roughly twenty thousand.

A vast training ground sat at its center, surrounded by monumental buildings.

Walking the diameter of the Academy grounds took an ordinary person more than four hours.

Colosseo was broadly divided into dormitories and academic buildings.

Dormitories were separated by year and gender.

Classroom buildings were divided by year and class.

Because the program lasted four years, students were organized into four grades.

First year.

Second year.

Third year.

Fourth year.

Repeating a year after failing examinations was rare but possible.

So were leaves of absence and returns following failed graduation requirements.

The Academy's main practical curriculum was divided into two broad departments.

The **Cold Department**.

And the **Hot Department**.

Students in the Cold Department specialized in weapons such as swords, bows, and spears.

Courses tied to their chosen weapon were major requirements.

Other cold-weapon disciplines became major electives.

Hot Department subjects formed part of their general education requirements.

The Hot Department centered on magic.

Students specialized in elemental disciplines—

fire.

ice.

earth.

plants.

And more.

Their primary element formed the core of their major.

Other magical fields became electives.

Cold-weapon courses still remained part of their required general education.

In other words—

whether a student specialized in swordsmanship, archery, spearmanship, fire magic, ice magic, earth magic, or plant magic—

the Academy expected them to graduate with basic knowledge across all of those fields.

That was only the practical side.

Students could also study languages.

Theology.

Law.

Astronomy.

Geography.

Military science.

And many other general subjects.

Graduation required a sufficient number of credits across major requirements, major electives, general requirements, general electives, double majors, and minors.

Some schedules were chosen freely.

Others were imposed.

Naturally—

grades created a hierarchy among students.

That hierarchy influenced Academy life far beyond the classroom.

Club eligibility.

Volunteer work.

Career-experience programs.

Double-major opportunities.

Other internal and external activities.

Many came with performance restrictions.

That was why freshmen cared so much about their first **placement examination**.

And why upperclassmen cared just as much about the results.

Talented juniors were valuable recruits for clubs, organizations, and social circles.

The placement tests had ended in February.

From that moment—

the Academy began buzzing.

Everyone wanted first access to the strongest newcomers.

“The freshman placement exam is over, right?”

“Who stood out?”

“Probably the students who scored highest on the practical assessment.”

Freshman records were officially restricted to professors.

Unofficially—

scores and rankings were already circulating among influential students.

Accurate information commanded high prices.

It was traded almost like stock-market rumors.

Most students admitted to Colosseo would eventually enter the upper ranks of the Empire.

Making connections early had obvious value.

“Oho—look at this.”

One senior whistled.

“This year's freshmen are ridiculous.”

“Huh?”

Another leaned closer.

“First place in the Cold Department is tied?”

“Tudor Donquixote and Bianca Fo Usher.”

“Both first place.”

“Those two are top-tier.”

A third student grinned.

“Highest recruitment priority.”

The Cold Department's upperclassmen were already excited.

Colosseo traditionally valued practical ability more heavily than written scores.

That made the incoming spearman and archer especially attractive.

Two geniuses—

immediately drawing the attention of professors and seniors alike.

eldest son of Donquixote, one of the Seven Great Families and a house famed for spearmanship.

Bianca—

Highbro.

Middlebro.

Lowbro.

a scholarship student selected by the Northern Mercenary Guild Alliance.

The Cold Department had no shortage of exceptional freshmen.

The Hot Department was no calmer.

“What?”

“A perfect score on the practical exam?”

“And second place on the written exam too.”

“That's absurd.”

“Sinclaire, right?”

“A genius among geniuses.”

One student lowered their voice.

“I heard this directly from someone in admissions…”

“Apparently she's incredibly cute too.”

Every eye in the Hot Department had turned toward **Sinclaire**.

First in the practical assessment.

Second in the written examination.

A record-setting combination.

> **Hot Department — Practical Assessment Ranking**
>
> **1 — Sinclaire**

She had not disclosed a family name or affiliation.

Even so—

magic was expensive to study.

Ranking first in the Academy's magical practical assessment strongly suggested that some powerful background supported her.

And Sinclaire was hardly the only notable entrant.

The Bourgeois family.

Leviathan.

Quovadis.

Other great houses had also sent talented students.

Ordinarily—

the Seven Great Families staggered the enrollment of their best heirs.

No house wanted its brightest talent fighting another great family's heir for the same year's top rank.

This year—

by coincidence—

many exceptional descendants entered at once.

The result was a cohort already being called the **Glorious Generation**.

In another year—

many of these freshmen might have become unquestioned top students.

Potential student council presidents.

Future leaders.

But because so many had arrived together—

some remarkable students were receiving less attention than they otherwise would have.

And then—

there was one person receiving almost none at all.

The Academy had not seen so many exceptional practical-test results in decades.

And because the school's culture valued practical performance far above written theory—

one ranking was nearly ignored.

Commoner.
