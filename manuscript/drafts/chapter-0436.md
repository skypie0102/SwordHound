# Chapter 436: The Returned Hound (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/436.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 434 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Quovadis, Chimeries, Passamonte, Beelzebub, and the Hell Tree. Pigi remains male per established project canon despite local source pronoun drift. Direct Fandom verification remains robots-blocked.

**Doppelganger leeches.**

Black Tongue’s copy-form parasites.

Vikir’s secret weapon from Nouvelle Vague.

Hunger.

Gluttony.

Tiny vampires driven by endless appetite.

They drank the blood spreading across the ground—

swelled—

then immediately wanted more.

Black Tongue had bred this strain to pursue only Vikir’s blood.

Their bodies were small.

Their appetite was not.

Even Vikir’s monstrous regeneration could not support them indefinitely.

But now—

another source of blood had appeared.

The same composition.

The same body.

The perfect feeding ground.

“Eat.”

Vikir spoke as though handing out a reward.

The leeches obeyed.

They burrowed between torn flesh—

exposed bone—

twisted tendons—

fat—

and fed.

Even the Ninth-Form Death Knight could not escape that relentless hunger.

[…Demon…]

[…Die…]

Meanwhile—

the parasites began changing.

CRACK.

CRACK.

CRACK.

Bodies the size of beans thickened to the size of fingers.

Then more.

Heads.

Torsos.

Tiny limbs.

Faces.

Features—

slowly taking the shape of Vikir.

They still looked rough.

Like crude dolls made by children.

But the more blood they consumed—

the more closely they resembled him.

“…Cute.”

Sinclaire crouched.

“They look exactly like Bro.”

She reached toward one that had lost the feeding competition.

KYAAG!

The tiny creature snapped its eyes open and bared teeth.

Sinclaire recoiled.

“What the—”

“I was trying to help you.”

The little copy staggered away toward the blood again.

At least—

they seemed interested in nothing except Vikir’s blood.

Meanwhile—

the Death Knight was collapsing.

Its Ninth Form had already been broken.

It had then taken two consecutive Eighth-Form Black Suns.

Catastrophic outer wounds.

Severe internal damage from aura overload.

And now—

parasites designed specifically to consume Vikir.

Even a Ninth-Form undead remnant could not endure all of that.

[…Demons…]

[…Must die…]

Still—

the remnant tried to stand.

Its body failed.

Its identity failed.

Its hatred did not.

Vikir looked down at his past self.

Then reached out.

Covered the remnant’s eyes.

“Don’t worry.”

“The Age of Destruction will not happen again.”

For the first time—

the Death Knight’s expression eased.

Only slightly.

But enough.

SSSSSS—

Its body became ash.

Blackened dust scattered across the ground.

The wind carried it away.

Nothing remained.

Vikir watched the direction it vanished.

Dry-eyed.

The Age of Destruction.

Even someone who had lived through death twice—

could not say where hatred like that truly ended.

Then—

scritch.

Something climbed onto his boot.

Vikir looked down.

The doppelganger leeches had lost their food source.

Finger-sized miniature Vikirs wandered across the floor in confusion.

“Back.”

Vikir dropped one bead of his blood into the leather pouch.

Then shook it near the ground.

The tiny copies smelled it.

Immediately—

they drooled and leapt inside.

Vikir tied the pouch.

“Hatred for demons.”

“A copy of the original body’s appearance and part of its power.”

“Useful.”

“If I can control the appetite.”

Whatever emotion the remnant had stirred—

was already gone from Vikir’s face.

A normal person might have spent hours—

days—

dwelling on meeting a broken version of his old self.

Vikir did not consider that affordable.

Pain.

Burden.

Trauma.

If any of it could become useful material—

he used it.

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca could only stare.

“Before, I would’ve just said he was stiff and boring.”

“But after feeling all that…”

“He carried too much alone.”

“I can’t even casually tell him to rely on us anymore.”

Dolores and Sinclaire said nothing.

Their eyes were wet.

Vikir finally turned back.

“You asked something earlier.”

Tudor blinked.

“Huh?”

“Oh!”

They had wanted to know how Vikir got here.

“As you know—

I was imprisoned in Nouvelle Vague four years ago.”

“Then I escaped.”

Tudor stared.

“…You say that like escaping Nouvelle Vague is normal.”

“It wasn’t easy.”

“I almost died once.”

Vikir summarized.

Forced labor.

Solitary confinement.

Volcanic eruption.

Being blasted upward inside a column of fire.

Everyone stared.

If anyone else told the story—

they would have called it absurd bragging.

“When I went up—

I thought I would die.”

“When I came down—

I thought I actually had.”

“I lost my memory after landing.”

“For a while I wandered around.”

“I lived in a fishing village.”

“Caught fish.”

“Dried them.”

Vikir frowned.

“Fortunately—

two people who escaped with me found me.”

“Seeing familiar faces restored my memory quickly.”

“Then I moved to assassinate Passamonte.”

“He was focused on you.”

“That created openings around the castle.”

Tudor exploded.

“You bastard!”

“You should’ve told us!”

“I deliberately didn’t.”

“You were already being influenced by the demon.”

“Sometimes—

to deceive the enemy—

you deceive allies first.”

Everyone went quiet.

Then nodded.

They had—

without realizing it—

fallen deep into Passamonte’s manipulation.

Tudor grimaced.

“…That courage-inducing ability is disgusting.”

“You don’t realize what it’s doing until you’re already acting like an idiot.”

Vikir nodded.

Bianca watched Tudor and Vikir talking.

Then laughed.

“You two got really similar while I wasn’t looking.”

Tudor glared.

But she was not talking only about him.

Over the last four years—

every Night Walker had become a little more like Vikir.

Speech.

Temperament.

Decision-making.

Partly because of how much he remained in their memories.

Partly because brutal eras sanded people down into similar shapes.

“I guess people become alike as destruction gets closer.”

“The real beginning hasn’t happened yet.”

Tudor forced a laugh.

“So in a few years—

I’ll talk exactly like you?”

Vikir smiled faintly.

Before regression—

many of his comrades had indeed become similar.

Extreme environments—

war—

loss—

all of them slowly erased the softer edges of individuality.

Sinclaire suddenly asked:

“Then…”

“What were you like before all that?”

Everyone looked at Vikir.

He considered it.

“According to the nanny who raised me—

I was shy.”

“Introverted.”

“My favorite hobbies were playing with dolls and flower arranging.”

“Of course—

the family banned both quickly.”

Silence.

Tudor blinked.

“…That really doesn’t suit you.”

Everyone nodded.

Vikir ignored them.

“It’s time to leave.”

Sancho looked around.

“How?”

“The same method we used to escape the Hell Tree before.”

Vikir turned to Pigi.

Pigi’s blood carried a strange power.

Poisonous to demons.

Capable of distorting and collapsing demonic space.

The moment Vikir looked at him—

a voice brushed across Pigi’s thoughts.

*…What a good person.*

Pigi froze.

“Huh?”

He looked around.

No one had spoken.

*What was that?*

He had experienced strange ringing before.

Even back at the Academy.

But never words this clear.

Before Pigi could think further—

Vikir spoke.

“Pigi.”

“Can I borrow some blood?”

“Of course!”

“If it helps—

take as much as you need.”

Vikir took only a few drops.

He spread them across Beelzebub.

Then slashed.

A long red line appeared in the air.

CRACKLE—

The space inside the Hell Tree twisted.

Tore.

The same phenomenon they had seen years earlier.

Vikir looked toward the opening.

“The moment we get out—

we attack the demon.”

“He won’t expect us to escape.”

Everyone’s expression tightened.

They had already lost once.

Launching another immediate attack—

was frightening.

But this time—

something had changed.

They had an ally they did not have before.

Vikir ended with one sentence.

“Let’s go out and hit him.”

Four years later—

his voice was still just as easy to believe.
