CLANG! CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!

The watchman shouted over the alarm bell.

“Outsiders!”

Something unusual was happening in the village.

Vikir rose immediately.

“……War?”

Aiyen looked at him as though he had said something absurd.

“Are you serious?”

She ruffled his hair roughly.

“Who would be crazy enough to attack Ballak first in this swamp?”

She had a point.

Apart from exceptionally dangerous monsters, few forces in the region would deliberately provoke Ballak.

Baskerville or Morgue might be capable of opposing them from outside the swamp.

Even the cannibalistic Rokoko kept their distance.

A tribe of only a few hundred people had been willing to stand against the Empire itself.

Their neighbors had good reason to fear them.

Locally, the one existence Ballak treated as an overwhelming terror was Madam Eight-Legs.

“Then who are the outsiders?”

“Look.”

Aiyen pulled back the entrance curtain.

Vikir saw pale-skinned Imperials in clean clothing.

Blond.

Red-haired.

Blue-haired.

They spoke Imperial loudly among themselves.

*Bourgeois Clan.*

Vikir frowned.

One of the Seven Great Families.

Baskerville represented the sword.

Morgue represented magic.

Quovadis represented faith.

Leviathan represented poison.

And Bourgeois had amassed enormous power through commerce and capital.

“It has been a while since merchants came here,” Aiyen said.

She did not sound pleased.

Bourgeois merchants were famous for pursuing profit wherever it could be found.

These people had brought goods deep into the swamp to trade with indigenous tribes.

“They bring things that aren't necessary—but once you've seen them, you miss them when they're gone. I don't like merchants like that.”

Vikir understood the feeling.

Before the regression, Hugo had once dismissed the Bourgeois as fools who confused money with true strength.

Baskerville bled on the frontier.

Bourgeois prospered near the center of the Empire.

The fact that some of their merchants secretly crossed frontier territory to trade with Baskerville's enemies only deepened the hostility.

And yet every great family still had to deal with them.

Trade.

Banking.

Real estate.

Diplomacy.

The Empire's monetary economy was saturated with Bourgeois influence.

*But how did they reach this place?*

From the Imperial capital, a caravan coming here would normally have to cross Baskerville or Morgue territory.

Entering the depths of the swamp also required large quantities of supplies and local assistance.

One answer came to mind.

*Smuggling.*

They had probably slipped through Baskerville territory by way of **Underdog City**, the closest major settlement.

◆◆◆

The merchants reached the center of Ballak village and began unloading.

“Come, come! The five-day market is open again! We reached you before word spread to the other tribes!”

Their leader was a middle-aged man with a long mustache.

**Sir Smuggler.**

He presented himself pleasantly.

His eyes were greedier than his smile.

The merchants began by handing out cheap gifts.

Glass beads.

Brooches.

Whistles.

Perfume.

Cosmetics.

“Free! Take them!”

The strange colors and fragrances were rare in the jungle.

Villagers gathered quickly.

Once enough people had been drawn in, the real trade began.

The merchants had grain and vegetables that Ballak could not easily grow.

Ballak did not use Imperial currency, so payment came in other forms.

Hides.

Bones.

Teeth.

Bear gall.

Antlers.

Rare mushrooms.

Spices.

Medicinal herbs.

Raw gemstones.

Gold dust.

Vikir watched.

*This is robbery.*

A Ballak villager offered a diamond for an ear of corn.

To the villager, an inedible stone for food seemed reasonable.

Vikir knew the Imperial value of both.

The disparity was infuriating.

“Wait!”

Aiyen stepped in.

She stared between the diamond and the corn.

“This trade is unfair.”

Vikir almost nodded in relief.

“One diamond for one ear of corn is ridiculous.”

Exactly.

“At least give her two.”

Aiyen turned toward Vikir and flashed a triumphant two-finger gesture.

Vikir sighed.

Aiyen was an excellent warrior.

Imperial market prices were another discipline entirely.

The rest of Ballak negotiated just as proudly and just as badly.

“This sweet potato isn't even sweet. One pearl shell is too much. Give me two potatoes.”

“This cabbage is wilted and full of insect bites. I'm not giving you a pair of antlers. One antler.”

“Do you know how hard it was to dry this otter hide? Add two more potatoes.”

The merchants groaned theatrically.

“You Ballak drive such hard bargains.”

“How are we supposed to make any money?”

“Fine, fine. We'll accept your terms.”

Their wagons filled with valuables.

Antlers.

Bear gall.

Sea cucumbers.

Ivory.

Bones and hides.

Medicines.

Lingzhi.

Pine mushrooms.

Gemstones.

Gold.

Rare monster cubs and eggs.

In exchange, the villagers received ordinary produce and cheap trinkets.

The merchants were making profits thousands upon thousands of times greater than what they had paid.

Once their cheap stock had mostly sold, their friendly manner disappeared.

Then a young Ballak girl approached.

Black hair.

Dark eyes.

She carried several fat larvae roasted carefully on a stick.

Among Ballak, they were considered a valuable delicacy.

“Could I trade these for one glass bead?”

The merchants looked at the skewer.

“What is that?”

“Sun dung beetle larvae.”

Other children watched enviously.

The larvae were said to have a rich taste reminiscent of milk and egg yolk.

The merchants sneered.

They mocked the food, then the girl's skin.

One of them made a lewd remark about her clothing.

The girl did not understand every Imperial word.

She understood the contempt.

A merchant slapped the skewer from her hand.

The larvae fell into the dirt.

Then—

CRUNCH.

Someone picked them up and ate them.

Vikir.

Soot from the cooking fires covered his face and body.

The girl looked up at him.

Vikir patted her head once.

Then he turned to the merchants and spoke fluent Imperial.

“This trade is void.”
