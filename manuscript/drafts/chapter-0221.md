# Chapter 221: That Day, Him and Me (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/221.txt` — `5c67124d92986ba285a9ad4066f6fd377ca1a7df`. Recovered English Chapter 219 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Camus Morgue, Snake Morgue, Raspane Morgue, Adolf Morgue, Professor Banshee Morgue, Light Faction, Dark Faction, Vikir Van Baskerville, Rosie Morgue, Art of Complete Resurrection, and Colosseo Academy. Camus is explicitly seventeen here. Her desperate sexual provocation toward Snake is retained only as a brief non-erotic act of leverage; Snake immediately rejects it.

Time—

flowed like water.

And for someone—

who could recover nothing—

it seemed to move even faster.

◆◆◆

**Morgue Dark Faction.**

A place built in shadow.

Sharp towers—

under a sky filled with crows.

At the highest point—

on the council seat—

sat a man.

Tall.

Thin.

Dark-red hair.

Dark-red eyes.

An ominous presence—

kept at a distance from almost everyone.

**Snake Morgue.**

Leader of Morgue's Dark Faction.

Third in practical family authority—

after **Raspane Morgue**—

and **Adolf Morgue** of the Light Faction.

Snake was—

a distant relative.

And unlike Raspane and Adolf—

who were close siblings—

he lived apart.

The Dark Faction matched him.

Secret research.

Hidden experiments.

Dangerous development.

The weapons and shields born there—

made Morgue stronger.

Snake—

therefore—

was indispensable.

He looked down from the tower.

At a girl below.

**Camus Morgue.**

Light Faction.

Future clan head.

Recently—

seventeen.

Snake spoke.

“Young Lady.”

“What brings a member of the Light Faction—

here?”

There was almost no reason—

for Light Faction members to enter Dark Faction headquarters.

Even quarterly technical exchange—

was reserved for established practitioners.

Camus answered—

“I left the Light Faction.”

Snake's eyes widened.

Then—

“I want to join the Dark Faction.”

Silence.

Changing factions—

was not ordinary.

For the heir apparent—

it was almost unthinkable.

Snake finally asked—

“…Why?”

“I'm tired of effort—

that produces nothing.”

He knew.

For years—

Camus had searched Le Rouge et Le Noir.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Vikir's remains—

never found.

“You gave up?”

“Yes.”

Camus did not evade it.

She had searched—

until hope itself became exhausting.

A bond at eight.

Reunion at fifteen.

Those days at Red Salt Castle—

the happiest of her life.

She had once imagined—

marriage.

a honeymoon.

a future.

That happiness—

lasting forever.

Instead—

Vikir disappeared into the mountains.

No body.

No bones.

Nothing.

“I want to learn black magic.”

Snake's gaze sharpened.

“Why?”

“To bring him back.”

“…You do not even have a corpse.”

“His soul may still exist.”

“Wandering.”

“Undead.”

“Fragmented.”

“I will summon it.”

“If I find remains later—

even better.”

Snake pressed a hand to his forehead.

“Young Lady.”

“Do you understand what changing factions means?”

“Leaving the Light Faction.”

“Joining us.”

“Walking the black path.”

“It means—”

“Abandoning my mother.”

“My uncle.”

“My authority as future clan head.”

“Living in shadow.”

“For the rest of my life.”

Snake stopped.

Even his younger relative—

**Professor Banshee Morgue**—

famous for almost never changing expression—

might have reacted to that.

Snake adjusted his posture.

“What exactly—

do you want from me?”

“Instruction.”

“Black magic capable of true resurrection.”

“At best—

ordinary black magic animates a ghost or corpse.”

“I know there is something beyond that.”

Snake's expression—

shifted.

“The **Art of Complete Resurrection**.”

“Ancient.”

“Forbidden.”

“Life for life.”

“Nearly impossible.”

“Even if you succeed—

the revived person may corrupt.”

“Lose control.”

“Become something else.”

Camus did not hesitate.

“His soul is strong.”

“If I succeed—

I trust him.”

Snake stared.

Then—

“No.”

A black folding screen—

rose between them.

Dismissal.

Camus stayed.

“Marquis Snake.”

“I know you love my mother.”

The screen—

stopped.

Camus continued.

“You hid in Morgue's shadows—

because that feeling could never be accepted.”

“And even now—

you still love her.”

The screen vanished.

Snake had already descended.

Standing—

directly in front of her.

His face—

hard.

“Did Adolf tell you?”

“That I'm filthy?”

“That you should mock me?”

“No.”

“Mother and Uncle never said anything.”

“I heard old rumors.”

“From elders.”

“They're dead.”

Snake's voice—

flat.

Camus raised mana.

Dark figures—

appeared behind her.

White-bearded spirits.

Dead Morgue elders.

“The dead talk.”

Snake's eyes widened.

“Who taught you that?”

“No one.”

“I taught myself.”

“…Self-taught?”

Black magic—

alone?

Opening a branch of knowledge—

without a teacher?

Snake's surprise—

was genuine.

“So the rumors were true.”

“Once in a century.”

“Maybe more.”

Still—

he shook his head.

“Talent changes nothing.”

“You are not joining my faction.”

Camus asked—

“Because Mother would be angry?”

“Or because you still love her?”

Snake—

said nothing.

Camus—

desperate enough to use anything—

tried one final provocation.

She stripped—

and offered herself as a substitute—

arguing that she resembled Raspane when young.

Snake immediately turned his head.

“Get dressed.”

Silence.

“Get dressed—

Young Lady.”

One gesture.

Her clothes rose—

like snakes—

and wrapped around her again.

Snake kept looking away.

“Never insult my feelings for her—

like that again.”

Camus stared at him.

Then—

bowed.

“Please.”

“Teacher.”

Snake—

did not dismiss her again.
