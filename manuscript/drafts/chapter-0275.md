# Chapter 275: Money War (3)

A new rumor swept through the business world.

“Did you hear?”

“Colosseo Academy has a group of extraordinary young investors.”

“The students from the Bourgeois mock-investment competition?”

“Apparently their returns weren’t mock at all.”

“They used real money.”

“What?”

“Those absurd returns were genuine?”

“Exactly.”

“That’s why everyone accepted tonight’s invitation.”

**Oracle.**

The investment club that had produced shocking real-world gains while participating in House Bourgeois’s mock competition.

Little was known about its members.

Only that they were legitimate Colosseo students.

That alone made the financial world curious.

Then Oracle sent out party invitations.

The curiosity became obsession.

The most magnificent banquet hall in the Imperial Capital had changed owners.

The buyer paid the entire building price in cash.

Then left a ten-percent tip.

Immediately afterward—

construction crews began renovating and expanding both exterior and interior.

That made no sense.

The hall was already one of the grandest buildings in the Capital.

What could possibly still need improving?

Now—

even wealthy merchants who usually ignored gossip—

were watching Oracle.

Then the appointed night arrived.

Nine bells rang.

The invited rich came from the Capital—

and from across the Empire.

Only around one hundred principal guests.

But with attendants—

more than three thousand people gathered.

Then murmuring spread.

“Who is that?”

“He actually came?”

“I’ve never seen him in person.”

“That’s a real heavyweight.”

Old financiers and noblewomen stepped aside.

A carriage rolled through the center.

Black field.

White triangular crest.

The mark of the wealthy northern **Sinan** family.

Inside sat a famous merchant.

**Viscount Sinan Saltsail.**

The Salt Merchant.

Head of one of Donquixote’s maternal-side families.

One of the wealthiest men in the north.

A magnate who had built his fortune transporting tens of thousands of tons of salt across the Empire.

“Hehehe.”

“Let’s see what kind of party justified dragging me all the way to the Capital.”

Saltsail stroked his beard.

Then his carriage stopped.

“…?”

Someone stood in the road.

A boyish figure.

Cute cat mask.

Tall boots.

Like a fairy-tale character made real.

Saltsail frowned.

“Who are you?”

“Why are you blocking the carriage?”

The cat-masked boy answered.

“My name is **Puss in Boots**.”

“I am your guide for tonight’s party.”

Dry voice.

No emotion.

Perfectly clear.

He showed the invitation proving his role.

Then climbed into Saltsail’s carriage.

“We have roughly twenty-three kilometers left.”

Saltsail stared ahead.

Then noticed something blue-green stretching along the road.

“What is that?”

A giant blue silk barrier.

Fabric wrapped in a huge circle—

hiding whatever stood within.

Puss in Boots explained:

“My master invested considerable effort into tonight.”

“A gathering of distinguished guests should not be exposed casually.”

“So fine silk was purchased to surround the venue.”

The carriage moved alongside the enormous curtain.

Saltsail—

who knew luxury goods—

recognized the quality immediately.

“Blue silk lined with premium purple satin.”

“Hm.”

“Not bad.”

“I have something similar surrounding my own estate.”

“Sixteen kilometers.”

Puss in Boots replied evenly.

“My master used twenty-one.”

“…What?”

Saltsail turned.

His pride had been hit.

The cat mask showed no reaction.

Eventually—

the carriage reached the venue.

The curtains concealed the building.

Only its enormous height could be guessed.

“Before you enter—

I need to explain tonight’s dress code.”

“Dress code?”

“Red cloaks.”

Saltsail frowned.

“No one told me.”

“Then there was an error in the invitation process.”

“Please don’t worry.”

“We prepared spares.”

Puss in Boots presented a red cloak.

Saltsail’s eyes narrowed.

“This fabric…”

A rare luxury textile.

Fire-resistant.

Enchanted.

Decorated with handwork made from the teeth and claws of fierce western-jungle monsters.

“Very expensive.”

“You know fabric well.”

“Of course.”

“I own two cloaks of this quality.”

Saltsail had been ready to reject some cheap party costume.

Instead—

the replacement cloak was astonishing.

It even carried mild enchantments against spilled food and alcohol—

and retained heat.

He accepted it.

“Would you like a cocktail before entering?”

A clear glass.

Red liquor.

Transparent cubes.

Saltsail took it.

“I don’t care much for alcohol.”

“It only makes me need the restroom.”

“Is that so?”

Despite complaining—

he drank because he was thirsty.

They passed through a dark hall.

Then—

into a brightly lit chamber.

Saltsail stopped.

“Oh.”

The floor was marble from the south.

A ruby chandelier hung overhead.

Rare mineral pigments covered the walls.

Coral statues.

Feather-soft rugs.

Purple curtains.

Perfumed attendants.

And—

oddly—

a golden washbasin in the center.

Even Saltsail had to nod.

“…Impressive.”

He looked toward Puss in Boots.

“A beautiful main hall.”

“Someone in the Capital actually has taste.”

“It nearly rivals the main hall of my estate.”

Puss in Boots tilted his head.

“What do you mean?”

Saltsail frowned.

“I said the main hall is well decorated.”

Then he looked around.

No other guests.

Only attendants.

Only himself.

“Where is everyone?”

“Why is the main hall empty?”

“Don’t tell me they all went to the restroom.”

Puss in Boots answered calmly.

“Of course not.”

“The other guests are already in the main hall.”

“…What?”

Saltsail stared.

If this was not the main hall—

then what was it?

Puss in Boots gave him the answer.

“This is the restroom.”
