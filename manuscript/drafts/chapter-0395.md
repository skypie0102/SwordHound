Four months later.

“What a wonderful day!”

“To welcome such capable new subordinates!”

Lieutenant Colonel Bastille was almost crying.

A small ceremony had been arranged inside Nouvelle Vague.

Promotion.

It was not the full formal ceremony held on the normal schedule.

But it contained everything necessary.

Applause filled the room.

The promoted guards walked forward.

Bastille embraced each of them.

But one—

longer than the rest.

Harder than the rest.

“Congratulations—

**Captain Garam Nord!**”

Garam had been a Second Lieutenant.

Now—

he had jumped two grades to Captain.

An almost unprecedented rise for a junior guard.

Even more surprising—

nobody considered it unfair.

“He always exceeded his work quota.”

“Promotion was inevitable.”

“And four months ago—

he ended a Level Eight riot.”

“He proved himself.”

“Captain is reasonable.”

“Bastille really pushed this through.”

“You don’t understand.”

“A double promotion normally triggers a complete background investigation.”

“Birth.”

“Past assignments.”

“Every detail.”

“That can take months.”

“Bastille thinks highly enough of him that he waived most of it.”

Nobody objected.

Many guards had avoided punishment—

demotion—

or worse—

because Garam ended the Sakkuth incident.

Bastille personally pinned the Captain insignia onto Vikir’s uniform.

Three diamonds.

Then lowered his voice.

“After your performance—

that prisoner spent three months in solitary.”

“Since he came out—

he’s worked seriously.”

“That’s good.”

Vikir answered.

“Everyone’s courage helped.”

“Still humble.”

Bastille smiled.

“But you were the bravest one there.”

“Though I remain curious…”

He leaned closer.

“What exactly did you say to make that bastard submit?”

“Nothing special.”

“I explained logically that continuing the riot would not benefit him.”

“Is that so?”

Bastille laughed.

“All right.”

“This isn’t the place for a long interrogation.”

“We’ll have plenty of time later.”

Bastille was often described as kind.

Or—

if one preferred—

conservative.

Vikir’s bland answer seemed to satisfy him.

Soon—

Vikir received the equipment available to Captain-rank guards.

An improved weapon.

Formal appointment papers.

The weapon—

was still a three-section baton.

Vikir held it.

Swung once.

CLACK.

CLACK.

The linked sections extended.

The weight felt good.

*Interesting.*

The real Garam had used this weapon.

Vikir—

who had spent most of his life as assassin and swordsman—

had rarely used a tool designed primarily for striking and suppression.

But the longer he handled it—

the more natural it felt.

Almost like discovering a second specialty.

Decarabia spoke from his chest.

[Human.]

[Have you awakened to something?]

“Non-killing?”

“If you mean a path other than killing—

perhaps.”

[No.]

[That is not what I meant.]

“…?”

Vikir ignored him.

Captain was enough.

For now—

the objective had been achieved.

◆◆◆

Click.

Vikir closed the door.

His new private room—

assigned after promotion—

was luxurious by Nouvelle Vague standards.

Fifteen square meters.

Clean stone.

Still humid.

Still salty.

But compared with the filthy cubicle where junior guards lived—

a palace.

A window.

A private shower.

Vikir walked to the glass.

Beyond the thin Flubber membrane—

dark deep sea.

Below—

faintly glowing coral.

Clusters of luminous jellyfish.

Enough light to see.

Far away—

a massive unknown fish crossed the darkness.

Vikir watched it for a while.

Knock.

Knock.

“Captain.”

“May I enter?”

Cool.

Firm.

Kirko.

Vikir did not answer quickly enough.

The door opened.

Kirko stood there in uniform.

Since Garam’s promotion—

she had become his adjutant.

Kirko insisted it was simply the natural personnel assignment.

Bastille had described it differently.

*Kirko rejected every previous adjutant posting.*

*Strangely—

she did not reject yours.*

*Hehehe.*

Vikir turned away from the window.

“What is it?”

The superior-subordinate formality came naturally.

Kirko accepted it just as naturally.

“Level Eight prisoner Sakkuth de Leviathan is acting up again.”

“First incident since his solitary confinement?”

“Yes.”

Vikir nodded.

Then picked up his three-section baton.

Started for the door.

Stopped.

“…Ah.”

Kirko tilted her head.

Vikir returned to the room.

When he came back—

he carried a thick **Rune Bible**.

Kirko’s expression became strange.

“Captain.”

“What?”

“Are you religious?”

“To some degree.”

“I’ve never been to the surface.”

“I don’t know much about surface religion.”

“But I’ve heard corruption is common.”

“Money exchanged for forgiveness.”

Vikir looked at her.

“What are you asking?”

“I wondered whether that book would help calm the prisoner.”

Vikir smiled dryly.

“No.”

Short answer.

Then he descended the spiral stairs toward Level Eight.

Before reaching the cell—

the stone corridor was already vibrating.

“EXECUTE ME?!”

“WHAT NONSENSE!”

“I WORKED AFTER SOLITARY!”

“I WORKED HARD!”

“THE BLUE THING IN LEVEL TEN IS HUGE NOW!”

“THAT’S BECAUSE OF MY PICKAXE!”

“AAAAAAH!”

Sakkuth’s voice echoed through the corridor.

He had learned about the death sentence.

Bastille—

kind if one wanted to be generous—

risk-averse if one did not—

wanted as few unpredictable variables as possible before his own promotion.

Sakkuth was the definition of a variable.

*Quiet unofficial execution.*

*Reasonable from Bastille’s point of view.*

Vikir clicked his tongue inwardly.

Sakkuth had accelerated his own end.

Meanwhile—

the prisoner kept screaming.

He threw together foul-looking substances from the cell.

“I FINALLY MET **THAT PERSON** IN SOLITARY!”

“YOU THINK I’LL STAY HERE FOREVER?!”

“I’M LEAVING SOON!”

“EXECUTION?!”

“CANCEL IT!”

“I WORKED!”

“WHAT MORE DO YOU WANT?!”

Foam gathered at his mouth.

Then—

“…!”

Sakkuth suddenly stopped.

The guards holding the BDISSEM chains looked around in confusion.

Vikir stood at the end of the corridor.

“Senior guards.”

“Please give us some space.”

They obeyed.

One person did not.

Kirko remained beside him.

Vikir looked over.

“You too.”

“No.”

“What do you mean, no?”

“I am Captain Garam’s adjutant.”

“I have a duty to assist you.”

Flat answer.

But stubborn.

Vikir shook his head.

Then walked to the bars.

“One week.”

“That is when you die.”

“….”

“This is the result of your stupidity.”

“Accept it.”

Sakkuth said nothing.

The other guards remained ready in case he exploded again.

Vikir passed something through the bars.

The Rune Bible.

“Read.”

“Repent before execution.”

“….”

Sakkuth accepted it with shaking hands.

Bloodshot eyes moved across the pages.

Then—

“…!”

His pupils widened.

Hidden in the middle of the book—

small writing.

**There is almost no time left. D-7.**

A secret message.

Sakkuth slowly sat down.

Tears began to fall.

“I acknowledge my sins.”

“I will accept everything.”

The guards stared.

“My god…”

“He calmed down instantly.”

“I can’t believe this.”

“Garam—”

“what did you do?”

Those who would have been responsible for controlling Sakkuth during his final week—

looked as though ten years had been removed from their shoulders.

They crowded around Vikir.

He answered dryly.

“Nothing.”

“I gave him hope.”

False hope.

There would be no rescue.

Seven days later—

Sakkuth would be executed.

No exception.

*The torture of hope.*

*Perhaps the worst torture there is.*

Vikir understood it.

The Age of Destruction had taught him thoroughly.

Salvation from someone else—

did not exist.

In the end—

only you could save yourself.

Vikir left the quiet Sakkuth behind.

And returned to preparing the **real** escape.

Not the false escape he had promised.

*Breaking out of Nouvelle Vague will not be a simple jailbreak.*
