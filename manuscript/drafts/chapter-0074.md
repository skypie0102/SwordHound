# Chapter 74: The Red Death (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/074.txt` — `cc0e1e6b22601d136440825972a08cebcd429e8b`. Recovered English Chapter 73 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Aheul**, **Ahun**, **Ah'Heman**, **Aiyen**, **Akwilla**, **Pomeranian La Baskerville**, **Red Death**, and **Ballak Tribe**. Medical symptoms and infection-control measures remain clinical; the chapter ends before target 75's Saint Mecca / Hound of the Night opening.

The storm battered the jungle.

Hide walls shook under the wind.

Inside one shelter, firelight burned red across a bed.

Aheul lay there.

Fourteen years old.

Breathing too fast.

Pale red spots had begun spreading across her skin.

Red Death.

Ahun stood beside her, face drained of color.

“Please, Vikir.”

His voice broke.

“Save my sister.”

Vikir approached the bed.

Red spots.

Uncontrolled vomiting and diarrhea.

High fever.

Joint pain.

Swelling at the neck, armpits, and groin.

The pattern was unmistakable.

“How?”

Ahun answered.

“She went into the swamp to cut timber for the construction work.”

Aheul had likely been exposed while working deep in flooded ground.

Aiyen looked at Vikir.

“What can we do?”

Vikir remained silent for a moment.

Prevention had been simple enough.

Treatment was different.

That required specialized medicine.

Then the door opened.

Ah'Heman entered.

The shaman took one look at the siblings and erupted.

“I told you not to wander wherever you wanted!”

He spat at Aheul's face as she lay groaning, then shouted over her sickbed.

“You listened to that Imperial spy, and now look at you. You brought this on yourself.”

Ahun stood.

“Grandfather! That's too far.”

SLAP.

Ahun dropped to one knee.

His cheek reddened immediately.

Ah'Heman stared down at him.

“Useless.”

His mouth twisted.

“Just like your parents.”

Ahun's eyes filled.

Aiyen sighed as though she had seen this scene too many times.

Vikir spoke.

“There is a treatment.”

Ahun looked up.

Ah'Heman snorted.

He shoved past Vikir.

“Another trick?”

The shaman turned toward the door.

“This is divine punishment. Sacrifice is the only answer. Now even Akwilla will have to admit it.”

He left.

Wind and rain blew through the opening before the curtain dropped back into place.

Aiyen.

Vikir.

Ahun.

Aheul.

Vikir addressed Ahun first.

“You handle her waste yourself.”

Ahun nodded immediately.

“Do not touch it with bare skin.”

Another nod.

“Boil every tool or container after use. Burn wormwood near the fire. Keep mosquitoes, fleas, bats, and other pests away.”

“Will that save her?”

“No.”

Ahun's expression collapsed.

“It may stop other people from getting sick.”

Vikir turned to Aiyen.

Then made the request he had intended to make sooner or later.

“Take me outside the jungle.”

Aiyen went still.

Outsiders who became Ballak were not treated as lesser members.

They could hunt freely.

Move around the village.

Enter almost anywhere except the chieftain's private space and the shaman's altar.

But leaving Ballak territory entirely was different.

For an outsider to gain unrestricted permission to leave, tradition demanded two things.

More than two years living with Ballak.

And a family bond with a native Ballak, including at least three children.

Vikir met neither condition as Ballak law defined it.

Yet he asked anyway.

“If you let me go, I can bring back medicine.”

Aiyen bit her lip.

Akwilla was strict about rules.

If the choice were one sick girl against tribal security—

she might refuse.

Aiyen knew that.

She also knew Aheul.

The girl who sang while working.

Who washed clothes.

Who cleaned without complaint.

Aiyen did not want to watch her die.

But another fear mattered just as much.

Would Vikir return?

He had originally entered Ballak as a captive.

His freedom inside the jungle had always existed within a boundary the tribe could enforce.

If he disappeared overnight, wolves and hunters could track him.

If Aiyen gave him permission to cross beyond Ballak reach, that restraint vanished.

He could leave forever.

Aiyen hesitated.

Hesitation was unlike her.

That made the answer obvious.

She was afraid of losing him.

Her eyes reddened.

“……Go.”

The decision was hers.

◆◆◆

Aiyen did not ask Akwilla.

She already knew what her mother would say.

So Aiyen accepted responsibility herself.

Vikir crossed the flooded jungle at night with Pomeranian secured on his back.

*Flood control alone won't stop this.*

Even Ballak's raised village remained exposed.

Other tribes had no engineering at all.

Stopping the Red Death across the region required something more fundamental.

Vikir stepped from one flood-borne log to another.

The disaster had covered old paths—

but debris floating downstream created new ones.

He moved quickly.

Then stopped.

Rain hissed across the water.

Someone was following.

Vikir turned.

Aiyen stood behind him.

Soaked.

Breathing hard.

“Why did you follow me?”

Her mouth opened.

Closed.

Then—

“I'm following my slave.”

“Go back.”

“No.”

“Don't follow me.”

“I said no.”

Vikir's expression hardened.

Aiyen tried to push back.

“I'm your master.”

He did not soften.

“I rarely say the same thing three times.”

She froze.

“You know that.”

Rain ran down both of them.

“Go back.”

Aiyen stared at him as though he had struck her.

“How can you look at me like that?”

Vikir did not answer.

She forced herself to ask something else.

“Where are you going?”

Silence.

“Just tell me where.”

“No.”

“Why are you taking Pomeranian?”

Vikir said nothing.

“Can't you leave her with me?”

The old language of master and slave had become meaningless a long time ago.

Aiyen knew it.

Perhaps she had known from the day she first saw Vikir from inside a slave cage.

Whatever name she gave their bond, she had long since decided that she would stand on his side.

Now the possibility of him disappearing made that truth impossible to ignore.

Her voice shook.

“Then answer one thing.”

Vikir waited.

“You're coming back, right?”

This time he answered without hesitation.

“Of course.”

Aiyen exhaled.

The tension left her shoulders.

“Promise.”

Vikir nodded.

“I promise.”

Then he turned away.

The hound crossed the flood toward the dark horizon.

Clouds swallowed the dawn.

Aiyen remained where she was, watching until his figure disappeared.
