# Chapter 147: Volunteering for the Golden Week (4)

Sinclaire's “big brother” address and Vikir's preference for “uncle” are retained as the source joke leading into the next title family.

The next morning arrived.

The volunteer students enjoyed a rare luxury—

two extra hours of sleep.

They woke at eight.

At the Academy—

the wake-up call came at six and the day's routine began at eight.

At the orphanage—

the children woke at eight and regular work began at ten.

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Vikir were sharing a room.

All four woke early anyway.

They washed.

Got dressed.

And prepared to leave.

Vikir had just finished putting on his shoes when Pigi called to him.

“Vikir.”

“Where did you go last night?”

“…?”

Vikir looked up.

Pigi rubbed his sleepy eyes.

“I woke up in the middle of the night to use the bathroom.”

“You weren't in bed.”

“I was in a toilet stall.”

“Oh, really?”

Pigi yawned.

“Whenever I sleep somewhere new, I can't even take a proper dump for at least a week.”

“You must have adjusted already.”

It was a lie.

Vikir had spent almost the entire night investigating the orphanage.

He had followed Quilt—

then lost the trail halfway through.

*He's more careful than I expected.*

*Even in ordinary life, he changes his routes to make pursuit difficult.*

A hound tracked prey by scent.

But once the scent vanished—

the trail ended.

Vikir, however—

was not merely a hound.

He was human.

Humans used tools.

In this case—

maps.

Construction records.

And the orphanage's original architectural plans.

Vikir had pressed Cindywendy for the documents from when Indulgentia was first built.

Those plans revealed what scent alone could not.

*His lair is hidden deep in the center of the orphanage.*

*Now the target is clear.*

Quilt's quarters lay at the center of the complex.

Four buildings housing the children formed a ring around it.

The volunteers slept outside that ring.

Anyone trying to reach Quilt at night—

had to pass through the children's residential buildings.

That meant Vikir needed to learn those buildings during the day.

Every corridor.

Every staircase.

Every entrance and exit.

So today—

he intended to volunteer in as many places as possible.

“Seriously.”

Vikir stood.

“With complete dedication.”

That was his plan.

Tudor, Sancho, and Pigi had no idea why.

To them—

Vikir's enthusiasm looked like pure public service.

Tudor's expression became solemn.

“Vikir.”

“I'll be honest.”

“Seeing you here has changed the way I think about you.”

“You're working this hard for the children.”

“Meanwhile, I came here thinking mostly about filling my required service hours.”

“I'm ashamed.”

He clenched a fist.

“I should learn from you and become a true hero.”

Sancho nodded.

“I used to think you were insane because of how you behaved around professors and upperclassmen.”

“But then I see how hard you work for people weaker than you.”

“I respect that.”

“We're the same age, but there's something to learn from you.”

Pigi joined in immediately.

“Vikir! I'll work harder too!”

Vikir barely heard them.

Recognition did not matter.

Praise did not matter.

The only thing that mattered—

was finding and killing the demon hidden inside this orphanage.

Then—

“Hi! Good morning!”

A cheerful voice called out.

Sinclaire waved as she approached.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

The three looked at one another.

None of them was close to her.

Their faces all said the same thing.

*You're here too?*

Sinclaire walked straight past them—

smiling—

and tapped Vikir on the shoulder.

“Big Brother!”

“Why are you pretending you don't know me?”

“I didn't realize you were greeting me.”

Vikir paused.

“And…”

*Big Brother?*

His brow furrowed.

Being called that by a girl this young felt strangely uncomfortable.

*I'd rather she call me uncle.*

So he corrected her.

“We're classmates.”

“Don't call me Big Brother.”

“Why not?”

Sinclaire looked genuinely confused.

“I'm a year younger than you.”

“You told me I could speak casually, but you're still older than me.”

“It's uncomfortable to hear—”

Vikir never finished.

Six hands clamped over his mouth.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

All three spoke at once.

“W-We like being called Big Brother!”

“…?”

Sinclaire blinked like a rabbit.

Then smiled.

“Really?”

She looked back at Vikir.

“If you don't like that name, I'll think of something else.”

“I'll have one before lunch.”

“Ah—”

“Well then!”

Sinclaire raised both fists encouragingly.

“Tudor, Sancho, Pigi—you work hard too!”

Then she ran toward the children.

“Kids! Big Sister's back!”

“Whoa! Sinclaire! Pass it over here!”

Within seconds—

she had joined their game.

Tudor, Sancho, and Pigi stared.

“She really is a good person.”

“She's good at studying.”

“Good at magic.”

“And she's great with children.”

Then all three spoke together.

“And most importantly—”

“The prettiest freshman in our year!”

A cold voice came from behind them.

“You three are unbelievable.”

Everyone turned.

Bianca stood with her arms crossed.

“If you're here to volunteer, then volunteer.”

“Don't stand around judging people behind their backs.”

Tudor, Sancho, and Pigi all flinched.

Vikir did not.

Bianca looked at him.

“At least you're better than those idiots.”

“You don't waste time saying stupid things.”

Tudor exploded.

“Who are you calling an idiot!?”

“You.”

“You know our written-exam scores are almost the same!”

“What does that prove?”

Bianca shot back.

“You spent months receiving absurdly expensive private tutoring just to reach that score.”

“I got mine while taking it easy.”

“I heard your father practically spent the price of a castle on your tutors.”

“All that money for *that* result?”

“What an embarrassment to the nobility.”

Tudor's face twisted.

“Oh, really?”

“What about the noble lady who got caught putting on makeup during first year?”

“Then argued with the second-year girls who reprimanded her?”

“Then invoked her family name—”

“only for the supervising professor to catch the entire thing?”

“And now she's here doing mandatory volunteer service?”

Bianca's eyes narrowed.

Tudor and Bianca had been rivals since before entering the Academy.

They knew exactly where to strike.

“You idiot!”

Bianca snapped.

“What are you so proud of, standing around rating a commoner girl's face?”

“I said she's pretty because she's pretty!”

Tudor fired back.

“And the Academy forbids discrimination between commoners and nobles!”

“Someone that obsessed with status has no right to call herself heroic!”

“Can you stop talking about heroes for one minute?”

“You really are a Donquixote!”

“And gloom and cynicism are practically House Usher's family traditions!”

“There's no talking to you.”

“Hmph!”

“I'd have more success arguing with a rock.”

“Hmph!”

Their usual argument over who deserved to be considered the true top first-year of the Cold Department rapidly became impossible to follow.

Sancho stepped between them with an exhausted expression.

Vikir used the opportunity to leave.

He had no intention of wasting time on children's quarrels.

Pigi followed.

“Oh, right.”

“Vikir.”

“I have some information for you.”

“Information?”

Vikir glanced over.

“What is it?”

“Nothing major.”

Pigi scratched his head.

“It's about Sinclaire.”

“…?”

Vikir waited.

“Seriously, it's nothing.”

“But the other day I saw Sinclaire talking to the person in charge of volunteer placements.”

“She was asking for something.”

“What?”

“Your assignment.”

Pigi grinned.

“She asked where *you* were going to volunteer.”

“Then she asked to be assigned to the same place.”

“She looked really serious about it.”

Pigi nudged Vikir in the ribs.

“You know what I mean, right?”

“Good luck.”

Vikir's expression hardened.

Because—

he believed he understood exactly what Pigi meant.

*She deliberately changed her volunteer assignment to follow me?*

*At a time like this?*

*Suspicious.*

The smell of conspiracy entered his mind immediately.

He remembered Sinclaire's words from the day before.

*It's such a coincidence that we were assigned to the same place.*

*Is it?*

*I saw you submit your volunteer form last week, but I never imagined we'd end up together.*

*I see.*

She had called it coincidence.

Now—

it was clear that it had been deliberate.

Vikir looked across the grounds toward Sinclaire.

His gaze grew cold.

*Come to think of it—*

*her heartbeat sped up when she said that.*

*Her breathing was unstable too.*

*Signs of someone hiding something.*

A survivor of the age of destruction did not ignore suspicious behavior.

Demons were treacherous.

Give one the smallest opening—

and it would drive a fatal sting through that weakness.

The faces of dead comrades passed through Vikir's mind.

Grief.

Emptiness.

Warning.

He could sense no impurity in Sinclaire's soul.

That did not mean he should lower his guard.

*There's no harm in being careful.*

*I'll keep my distance.*

*A very large distance.*

Pigi's meaningful warning only strengthened his concern.

Perhaps Pigi had noticed some sinister sign—

and was trying to alert him indirectly.

*…If it becomes necessary—*

*I'll deal with the problem quietly.*

The dead did not speak.

Vikir walked away—

grateful to Pigi for the “warning.”

From now on—

he would watch Sinclaire's every movement.

And keep as far away from her as possible.
