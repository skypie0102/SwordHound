# Chapter 20: Camus Morgue (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/020.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 20 is an aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English forms for Camus Morgue, Adolf Morgue, Raspane Morgue, Osiris Le Baskerville, Morgue Clan, Beelzebub abilities, sword ranks, and Baskerville Fangs follow current project/Fandom evidence.

Ten people lived ten different lives.

A hundred people lived a hundred different lives.

No single formula could measure the course of every human life.

But House Baskerville was different.

Within the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan, a person's growth was measured, sorted, and compared according to brutally simple formulas.

One of the most basic looked like this.

That was the rough equivalence between the realms of swordsmanship and magic.

Age could be reduced to a formula as well.

The years other people passed without much thought were life-or-death milestones for Baskerville's young hounds.

Every stage of childhood was filled with training severe enough to kill those who could not keep up.

As a result, their growth tended to follow another familiar pattern.

<div class="info-window">
<p class="info-window-row">Low Sword Expert = about 15 years old</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Mid Sword Expert = about 18 years old</p>
<p class="info-window-row">High Sword Expert = about 20 years old</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Low Sword Graduator = about 30 years old</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Mid Sword Graduator = about 35 years old</p>
<p class="info-window-row">High Sword Graduator = about 40 years old</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Sword Master = ?</p>
</div>

Most Baskerville children first sensed mana around the age of seven or eight.

At that stage, all they could usually do was gather a little mana in their hands and feel its warmth.

It was normally around fifteen that they became capable of properly channeling mana into a blade and producing gaseous aura.

That was the threshold of Low Sword Expert.

Swordsmanship supported the mana.

Most Baskervilles learned the Clan's Fang Sword Style, progressing from one Fang to the next.

A single Fang traced by the sword.

Then two.

Then three, four, five, and beyond.

Among ordinary Baskerville prodigies, rank and Fang count usually advanced together.

<div class="info-window">
<p class="info-window-row">Low Sword Expert = 1st Fang</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Mid Sword Expert = 2nd Fang</p>
<p class="info-window-row">High Sword Expert = 3rd Fang</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Low Sword Graduator = 4th Fang</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Mid Sword Graduator = 5th Fang</p>
<p class="info-window-row">High Sword Graduator = 6th Fang</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Sword Master = 7th Fang</p>
</div>

Anyone capable of learning the Fifth Fang and above belonged among the elite of the elite.

In practice, such teachings were reserved for the acknowledged direct line and its heirs.

Everyone knew that Hugo Le Baskerville, the current Clan Head, could manifest seven Fangs.

Everyone also knew that his eldest son, Osiris Le Baskerville—the Young Patriarch stationed far away on the frontier—could manifest five, while his second son, currently secluded in training, could manifest four.

By contrast, children of the Van line—illegitimate or otherwise unrecognized by the direct lineage—were not granted the higher teachings no matter how much mana they accumulated or how old they became.

Even a powerful Graduator of the Van line would normally hit a hard ceiling in the number of Fangs he could display.

And almost no one complained.

No one.

No one.

No one.

No one.

The Baskerville Fang Sword Style was so formidable that mastering even four Fangs was enough to make ordinary warriors tremble.

And yet—

There were always exceptions.

Some hounds broke from the expected formula by falling behind.

Those cases were not particularly remarkable.

The Clan disposed of failures quickly.

Some died in training and became practical experience for their siblings.

Some died or disappeared on missions and survived only as a line in a casualty report.

Some were assassinated because of interests inside or outside the Clan.

A few simply died of illness.

Baskerville casualties were enormous among the young and gradually decreased only as the survivors grew older, harder, and more experienced.

But there was another kind of exception.

The hound who broke the formula by advancing too quickly.

Even among a family famous for producing geniuses, a handful were acknowledged as geniuses by the other geniuses.

Until recently, the greatest example had been Osiris Le Baskerville.

Hugo's eldest son.

The Young Patriarch who would one day lead the Clan.

A little sun beneath the greater sun that was Hugo.

But another rumor had begun spreading through the estate.

A new prodigy had appeared.

**Vikir Van Baskerville. Eight years old.**

According to the level personally confirmed by Hugo, Vikir had already reached **Mid Sword Expert**.

Two Fangs.

The equivalent of a 2nd Circle Mage.

At eight, he had reached a milestone most of his siblings would not attain until around eighteen.

Even Baskervilles who usually cared little about their brothers and sisters found themselves talking about him whenever they gathered.

“Is that really true? There's an eight-year-old like that?”

“Whose child is he? Van? Then no one even knows what line he came from.”

“He may be worth raising. If he isn't, then it would be better to deal with him early…”

Some spoke with interest.

Some with indifference.

Some weighed Vikir against their own interests.

Whatever their motives, everyone agreed on one thing.

Vikir had already shown his fangs.

◆◆◆

‘Idiots.’

The subject of all those rumors could not have cared less.

Vikir already knew where the Clan's rivalries, factions, and ugly struggles for advantage would eventually lead.

How could he not?

He had spent decades in his first life being sent from one great family and battlefield to another.

Plop.

Another serving of haggis landed on his plate.

Vikir looked down at the sour, gamey meat porridge and continued eating.

‘Two Fangs were exactly what I intended to show. That's more than enough.’

Now that he had publicly demonstrated a respectable level of talent, he could begin making use of the Clan's resources more freely.

Recognition in moderation was useful.

Enough to justify opportunities away from Hugo's immediate surveillance.

Not enough to reveal what Vikir truly possessed.

He sat alone in a corner of the dining hall, spooning down the haggis while considering that hidden strength.

‘My official level is Mid Sword Expert and the 2nd Fang.’

His actual level was already **Low Sword Graduator**.

His swordsmanship was sufficient to produce a stable **4th Fang**.

And after the enlightenment he had gained in the 10,000 Book Library, the path toward the **5th Fang**—and with it Mid Sword Graduator—was no longer an impossible wall.

‘Before I turn seventeen, I need to reach High Sword Graduator.’

By then, his target was the **6th Fang**.

That alone would place this life far beyond the previous one, where he had remained trapped at four Fangs for years.

And there was more.

Twitch.

The spoon in Vikir's hand trembled slightly.

Something hidden along the artery of his right wrist had moved.

Beelzebub.

<p class="info-window-row">Slot 1: Incinerate — Cerberus (A+)</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Slot 2: Hemorrhage — Hellhound (B+)</p>
<p class="info-window-row">Slot 3: Superspeed Regeneration — Troll (C+)</p>
</div>

The relic had discarded the Brown Rat Norvegicus ability that previously occupied its third slot.

In its place, it had absorbed the far stronger regenerative trait of the Troll Vikir had just killed.

Make the opponent bleed without end.

Regenerate his own wounds at terrifying speed.

The combination was vicious.

With Beelzebub included, Vikir suspected he could now fight above what his nominal Low Sword Graduator rank suggested.

He continued eating.

This was already his sixth serving.

Ever since Beelzebub had entered his body, his appetite had become strangely voracious.

“…H-hey.”

A hesitant voice came from behind him.

Vikir stopped chewing and turned.

“?”

Three unexpected figures were standing there.

Highbro Le Baskerville.

Middlebro Le Baskerville.

Lowbro Le Baskerville.

The triplets who would one day become known as the Baskerville Trident.

Vikir's brow furrowed.

“What do you three little bastards want?”

No greeting.

No attempt at politeness.

The triplets visibly flinched when his eyes narrowed.

There were few things easier to handle than frightened dogs.

Vikir tightened his grip around the spoon in his hand.

All three immediately raised their hands.

“N-no! It's not like that!”

“We only wanted to—”

“Y-you were really cool when you killed Cerberus and that Troll!”

“…?”

For once, Vikir did not immediately understand what was happening.

‘A two-faced tactic? Some kind of trap?’

He searched their faces for signs of calculation.

There were none.

The three simply looked at him with an awkward mixture of admiration and fear.

The silence became so uncomfortable that Vikir felt goosebumps rise along his arms.

His fingers shifted around the spoon.

He was just considering whether to send it flying into one of their foreheads when another voice interrupted.

“Young Master.”

John Barrymore stood nearby.

As always, the Head Butler's posture was immaculate.

“The Master is asking for you.”

◆◆◆

Hugo Le Baskerville sat on a sofa beside the window, visibly irritated.

“Is the Butler not back yet?”

A maid bowed quickly.

“Looking down toward the entrance, it appears he has just entered the first-floor hall. Young Master Vikir is with him.”

“They should be here shortly.”

Hugo nodded.

Then his gaze shifted toward the neighboring sofa.

A middle-aged man with a carefully groomed mustache sat there, holding a teacup.

**Adolf Morgue.**

A senior figure of the Morgue Clan, the great house of magic long treated as Baskerville's rival.

He was the younger brother of **Raspane Morgue**, the current head of the Morgue Clan, and frequently represented the family in outside affairs.

Adolf took a sip of tea and smiled at Hugo.

“I hope this year's annual event passes without incident.”

He was referring to the annual friendly joint training between Baskerville and Morgue.

The custom had begun under a former emperor who believed sword and magic should complement one another in times of national crisis.

Children between eight and fifteen took part.

Officially, it was a friendly exercise designed in part to satisfy the imperial court.

In reality, relations between the two houses were especially tense this year.

A newly disputed ruby mine lay near the border between their territories.

Hugo and Raspane Morgue had been at odds over its ownership.

And now Raspane's younger brother had come in person.

Whether this visit was truly about friendship remained to be seen.

Hugo looked unconcerned.

“It's only joint training. It has always ended well enough.”

Adolf chuckled.

“Last year one child from Baskerville and one from Morgue were both seriously wounded, were they not?”

“They lived. They were treated in time. What of it?”

A vein pulsed faintly on Adolf's forehead.

He coughed, smoothed his expression, and changed the subject.

“Ahem. Then let us discuss this year's exercise. Ah—before that.”

He seemed to remember something.

“I hear Baskerville has produced a new supernova. I confess I'm eager to see him.”

“…He's already being summoned.”

Hugo answered as though it meant nothing.

Adolf was observant enough to notice the almost invisible lift at the corner of Hugo's mouth.

‘That lizard of a man reacts when someone praises his child? That's new.’

It was interesting enough to remember for his report to Raspane.

“A once-in-a-century talent appearing in the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan is a blessing for the empire.”

“There is no need for such praise.”

Hugo's modesty was purely formal.

Adolf smiled more broadly.

“Then perhaps this year brings two blessings. Morgue has produced a once-in-a-century talent of its own.”

Hugo's eyebrow shifted.

Adolf gestured toward a side door.

“Come in, Camus. Greet the head of House Baskerville.”

The door opened as though moved by magic.

Servants entered first.

Behind them walked a little girl.

Hair the color of flame.

Eyes shining like rubies.

A fine nose, small lips, and neat white teeth.

**Camus Morgue.**

Eight years old.

She walked to her uncle's side without the slightest hesitation.

Even among the prodigies of the Morgue Clan, Camus was spoken of as a genius born perhaps once in a century.

She held a corner of Adolf's cloak and looked around with bright, curious eyes.

Even Hugo's expression softened by a fraction.

The girl was charming.

Adolf's intent was not.

The message behind bringing an eight-year-old Morgue prodigy before Baskerville's eight-year-old prodigy was obvious.

*We have one too. Let us see which child is greater.*

Hugo nearly turned away in contempt.

Then Camus spoke.

“Uncle, are you the thief who stole our ruby mine?”
