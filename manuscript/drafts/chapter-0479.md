Rain poured without end.

The world—

already transformed into an ocean—

grew rougher by the day.

Inside Tochka, unease spread among the refugees.

“Are we going to get trapped here and starve?”

“What are you talking about?”

“There’s still a mountain of food.”

“But what if the flood keeps going?”

“It’s supposed to last one hundred fifty days.”

“We have enough food.”

“And more than enough drinking water now.”

“But the outside world is already an ocean.”

“How do we know it’ll stop exactly after one hundred fifty days?”

“Donquixote’s young head went to bring back the fleet.”

“So everyone’s waiting.”

The rumors were not unfounded.

Tochka—

high above the flooded world—

was now both the safest fortress on the continent—

and a completely isolated island.

Perhaps that was why Vikir spent so much time looking out over the distant sea.

CRASH!

Waves struck even the high plateau’s stone walls.

White foam bloomed like flowers.

Vikir sat on the battlement—

letting the cold rain strike him.

As though cooling iron that still held too much heat.

Then—

“Getting soaked again?”

“Aren’t you noble.”

Camus approached from behind.

She made no attempt to stay dry either.

“This rain is ridiculous.”

She watched sheets of water pour from the walls like waterfalls.

“If the demons hadn’t already turned the world into a sea of fire, this flood would have been a disaster all by itself.”

“Two catastrophes collided.”

“And somehow the damage became smaller.”

“You calculated that much in Nouvelle Vague before the eruption, didn’t you?”

“Mostly.”

Vikir nodded.

His calculation had not been perfect.

But it had been close enough.

If the error had been only slightly larger—

things could have gone horribly wrong.

“….”

“….”

They looked toward the same horizon.

For a while—

neither spoke.

Camus finally broke the silence.

“…I’m sorry about your father.”

Vikir reacted.

Only slightly.

A faint tremor in one shoulder.

The memory remained painfully clear.

*…Why?*

*I don’t know either.*

A vague final exchange.

White ash.

Now scattered by rain.

To everyone at Tochka—

Hugo had died a hero.

To Vikir—

the feelings were more complicated.

“…Him.”

Vikir spoke after a long silence.

“I still don’t understand how he crossed into the Ninth Form.”

It was completely different from the future Vikir remembered.

Camus hesitated.

“Actually…”

“?”

“He came to see me once.”

Vikir looked at her.

“It was not long after your trial at Nakajaniye.”

“He wanted to discuss how to get you out of prison.”

“And—”

Camus paused.

“Sadi was cooperating with him too.”

That surprised Vikir.

Hugo had apparently prepared much more than Vikir realized.

The search for the vanished Sadi.

Aiyen being able to reach Nouvelle Vague instead of being summarily executed.

Sadi obtaining a false identity capable of passing Nouvelle Vague’s checkpoints.

There had been hidden assistance behind all of it.

Vikir remembered Hugo at the trial.

*House Baskerville’s position is as follows.*

*The accused has committed grave crimes—rebellion, attempted kinslaying, and poisoning the Head of House.*

*However, he is of Baskerville blood and therefore entitled to noble treatment.*

*We request that this be considered during sentencing.*

Hugo had made no further objection.

Sitting in his wheelchair—

back turned.

“….”

Vikir said nothing.

Camus continued.

“When we discussed getting you out—

the Wraith Tree came up.”

“And naturally—

so did the Grave of Swords.”

She lifted one hand.

Sssssss—

The Wraith Tree’s dry branches swayed.

“Hugo’s reaction surprised me.”

“I think he read something from the Wraith Tree.”

Vikir nodded slowly.

He already had an idea what Hugo might have seen.

Cane Corso.

The elder brother who had crossed death.

The words of the Grave of Swords returned to Vikir.

The Sixth Form—

reached by transcending emotion.

The Seventh—

reached by reclaiming what had been abandoned.

The Eighth—

reached by gripping the sword as though for the first time and surviving countless brutal battles.

The Ninth—

the incomprehensible core of the higher realm.

A place accessible only to one who had experienced death.

*If you are a true Baskerville—*

*you will come here at the end of your life.*

*We will meet again someday.*

Had Hugo seen Cane Corso through the Wraith Tree?

What had he felt—

seeing his elder brother?

How had he crossed that final threshold?

“….”

Vikir thought again.

Then—

pat.

Camus placed a hand on his shoulder.

“Stop trying to carry everything alone.”

“You’ve come this far.”

“Think about the people who care about you.”

“…You’re right.”

Another voice agreed.

Dolores appeared with an umbrella.

“All the people here followed you.”

“Wherever you go—

they’re ready to follow.”

Vikir looked at the umbrella.

“…That may be true.”

“But don’t I get one?”

“It’s a two-person umbrella.”

Camus’s complaint was ignored.

Dolores kept speaking.

“Don’t worry about Tudor.”

“He’s strong.”

“He’ll bring the fleet back.”

Vikir turned again toward the sea.

The storm was vicious.

The waves enormous.

It was difficult to imagine any fleet crossing them.

Victory had put the final battle within reach—

which only made the waiting more frustrating.

“I’ll watch from the tower.”

“You should go inside.”

“You’ll get sick standing here.”

Aiyen had appeared too.

Her eyesight could reach several kilometers.

If Tudor returned—

she would see him first.

Camus glared.

“What are you doing, barbarian?”

“Why are you always hovering around someone else’s man?”

“Barbarian?”

Aiyen’s eyes narrowed.

“Want me to strip you naked again?”

“Oh?”

“You remember that?”

“You think you could still do it now?”

Morgue’s Camus.

Ballak’s Aiyen.

They had never gotten along.

Then—

“Stop fighting.”

“Bro already has enough to think about.”

Sinclaire arrived after finishing repairs on the wall.

Camus and Aiyen both stared at her.

Sinclaire deliberately ignored them.

She handed Vikir a cup of hot tea.

“The next battle is the last one.”

“Drink something warm and calm down.”

Beside her stood Kirko—

currently handling security.

“…The Imperial Capital.”

“I’ve only read about it.”

Then Kirko asked:

“If the surface is flooded like this—

does that mean everyone in the lowlands died?”

Sinclaire answered.

“We gathered as many people into Tochka as possible.”

“Anyone who couldn’t reach us in time was moved to other high ground beforehand.”

“We did everything we reasonably could.”

Apparently the two had already discussed it.

Then—

“…!”

Aiyen suddenly stood.

She stared through darkness and rain.

Far beyond the violent waves.

Then shouted:

“They’re here!”

“The fleet!”

Everyone turned.

Through storm and churning sea—

enormous warships approached.

Massive hulls ignored ordinary waves and drove directly toward Tochka.

There were more ships than anyone could count quickly.

Dolores shouted in delight.

“Tudor!”

“Tudor’s back!”

At the head of the fleet—

House Donquixote’s banner flew.

And the decks—

were packed with people.

“Tochka really survived!”

“There’s actually food there!”

“We’re saved!”

“There really was land above the flood!”

“We should’ve trusted the Night Walkers sooner!”

The ships carried refugees too.

At the stern of the flagship—

two familiar figures appeared.

Tudor.

Bianca.

“Vikir!”

“I’m sorry we’re late!”

“I’m ashamed!”

Bianca immediately pointed at Tudor.

“We’re late because this idiot insisted on rescuing everyone trapped on high ground!”

Even now—

they argued.

Camus, Aiyen, Dolores, Sinclaire, and Kirko watched the arriving refugees with mixed expressions.

“That’s more mouths to feed.”

“And potentially more security problems.”

“We have food.”

“Nouvelle Vague personnel can help with security.”

“About five months’ worth of provisions.”

“Our family spent everything gathering it.”

“Helping people is right, but we can’t let it interfere with the last battle.”

Then Vikir stood.

“It doesn’t matter.”

“We’ll be leaving soon anyway.”

Everyone looked at him.

“It’s time for the final battle.”

Their expressions hardened.

They understood.

The decisive fight—

would take place in the Imperial Capital.

Carvi—

thirteen pairs of oars and twenty-six rowers.

Sneka—

twenty pairs and forty rowers.

Skade—

large enough for a hundred warriors.

Draka—

veteran sailors capable of crossing open sea.

Navigation skill.

Discipline.

Courage.

Enough to charge straight toward the Imperial Capital—

even now that the heart of the Empire had become ocean.

CRASH!

gathered.

The Tochka Alliance boarded Donquixote ships.

The old city map of the Imperial Capital—

now a sea chart—

guided them inward.

Deeper.

Toward the center.

Vikir stood aboard the lead ship.

Looking across the horizon.

Clank.

Every roll of the hull dragged an iron chain across the deck.

The chain—

wrapped around Vikir’s hand.

Chihuahua suddenly pointed over the side.

“Deputy!”

“Over there!”

A huge shadow passed beneath the water beside them.

An enormous sea monster.

A species that should never have existed here.

*The Imperial Capital has changed.*

The city where the Emperor had lived.

No—

where the Crown Prince now resided.

Vikir could already imagine what the flooded capital had become.

The center of the Empire.

The final battlefield.

The First Corpse.

The being who had stood at the beginning of the Age of Destruction.

And—

the end point of Vikir’s many lives.

The end of his fallen comrades’ revenge.

WOOOOOSH—

The wind filled the sails.

Ships accelerated over the waves.

Clank.

The chain scraped again.

Vikir tightened his grip.

MiniPin looked toward its far end.

“Deputy…”

“I’ve been wondering.”

“What exactly is that?”

He knew what the object itself was.

A coffin.

Large.

Heavy.

Dragged behind Vikir by chains.

But who—

or what—

was inside?

No one knew.
