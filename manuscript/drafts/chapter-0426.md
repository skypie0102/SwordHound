Dolores, Tudor, Sancho, and Pigi entered the command tent.

Cindywendy sat over a large map.

Serious.

Focused.

She was one of Vikir’s appointed aides—

and Osiris Le Baskerville’s lover.

For years—

she had poured enormous amounts of wealth into one task.

Relief supplies.

Preserved food.

Drinking water.

Medicine.

Equipment.

Wagons continued arriving from all across the Empire.

And the truly strange part—

was how long she had been doing it.

The Tochka project had begun years ago.

Long before Vikir publicly appeared in the Imperial Capital as Night Hound.

The fortress now contained enough stockpiled supplies to support an enormous population for months.

Even Cindywendy—

who executed the plan—

still did not fully understand why.

Neither did Chihuahua.

Neither did MiniPin.

Vikir rarely explained more than necessary.

He gave the objective.

They accomplished it.

That was the arrangement.

Cindywendy opened her eyes.

“Status of the myth?”

Dolores nodded.

“We created the concept of the **Ark**.”

“We tell people that anyone who reaches Tochka can be saved.”

Her expression grew slightly uncomfortable.

Because the Ark—

at least in that form—

was not an ancient prophecy.

Not a hidden verse from Revelation.

Not a rediscovered sacred text.

It was deliberately created.

Dolores.

Martin Luther.

Several senior Quovadis clergy.

They had constructed the myth because desperate refugees were much easier to move when hope had a name.

Dolores had expected punishment when she proposed it to Pope Nabokov I.

Instead—

Nabokov I smiled.

**If the myth does not exist—

then create it.**

**No myth existed from the beginning.**

**Human beings create them.**

**God respects human free will.**

Then Nabokov I patted Dolores on the head.

**Your footsteps will become myth.**

**They will be written into scripture.**

Nabokov I placed almost everything Quovadis could spare into Dolores’s hands.

Only forces absolutely necessary for the civil war remained elsewhere.

Because of that—

Quovadis temples across the Empire quietly supported Night Walker movements.

Shelter.

Information.

Food.

Priests.

Safe roads.

And Quovadis was not alone.

Morgue helped secretly.

Bourgeois helped secretly.

Even Baskerville helped.

Important supporters included—

Banshee Morgue.

Lovebad Bourgeois.

Mozgus Quovadis.

Isabella Le Baskerville.

Some provided materials.

Some protected supply routes.

Some passed information about battles—

monster movements—

or unsafe regions.

Others who had crossed paths with Vikir had joined more directly.

Gordon.

Underdog.

Lovegood.

People from different backgrounds—

working under one network.

The Night Walkers belonged to neither side of the civil war.

Their concern was ordinary people.

Refugees.

Civilians.

Anyone abandoned by the great powers.

They were informal.

Radical when necessary.

And they did not hesitate to use force when force was required.

Dolores looked at the map.

“What about the western continent?”

“Stable.”

Cindywendy tapped the region.

“Baskerville is cooperating fully.”

“And Ballak has appeared again.”

“Trade has resumed.”

Then she remembered something.

“Actually—

there’s a strange rumor from Ballak.”

“A new guardian.”

“Apparently they call it a **reliable companion**.”

Everyone looked confused.

Cindywendy shrugged.

“I don’t know what it means either.”

Then her expression hardened again.

“The real reason I called this meeting is supply.”

Food.

Water.

Vikir had ordered Tochka fortified before he ever entered Colosseo Academy.

Cindywendy had spent years purchasing supplies.

The current stockpile—

under normal calculations—

could support huge numbers of refugees for more than three months.

But Vikir had been precise.

**At least one hundred fifty days.**

Five months.

“We’re still short.”

Dolores frowned.

“With famine and drought everywhere—

prices have exploded.”

“And even your fortune has limits.”

Cindywendy stared at her.

Then burst out laughing.

“Hahahaha!”

“Saintess.”

“You’re underestimating me.”

“I’m Cindywendy.”

“Money isn’t the problem.”

“I haven’t even spent a tenth.”

Everyone stared.

“Then what is?”

“Transportation.”

Cindywendy pointed at several lines across the map.

“Food still exists.”

“Water still exists.”

“If the price doubles—

I pay double.”

“If it triples—

I pay triple.”

“The problem is getting it **here**.”

Tochka was remote.

High in the north.

Supply routes passed through wilderness already full of monsters.

Wildfires.

Bandits.

Civil-war patrols.

Cindywendy had compensated with mercenaries.

Baskerville escorts.

Morgue support.

But recently—

fewer convoys had arrived.

“Some of my people are sitting on warehouses full of supplies.”

“They can’t move.”

“Someone is blocking every secret route leading specifically to Tochka.”

She tapped several X marks.

“This isn’t random.”

“They know something is here.”

Tudor’s expression changed.

“Donquixote?”

Cindywendy did not answer immediately.

She only looked at him.

That was answer enough.

“…Passamonte.”

Cindywendy nodded.

**Donquixote La Mancha Passamonte.**

Cervantes’s younger brother.

Tudor’s uncle.

The man who had seized control of the family and wanted Tudor removed completely.

“He knows you left Donquixote.”

“And he seems to know you joined the Night Walkers.”

“Donquixote’s **Invincible Cavalry** is searching this region.”

Tudor’s face darkened.

“If they trace the refugee caravans—

or the supply wagons—

they’ll find Tochka.”

“Yes.”

“The Ark myth has spread far enough that it’s a miracle we remained hidden this long.”

For the demons—

Tochka had not looked important.

A dry fortress filled with refugees.

No natural water source.

No obvious long-term value.

What they did not know—

was how much Cindywendy had already hidden inside it.

That secret was now in danger.

Tudor lowered his head.

“…So I’m the reason.”

“I should leave.”

“Draw them away before they find the fortress.”

“No.”

Dolores cut him off.

“This isn’t why Cindywendy called you.”

Cindywendy nodded and let Dolores continue.

Dolores’s voice became firm.

“It means the decisive battle has arrived.”

Tudor looked up.

“You’re one of us.”

“You care about us.”

“We care about you.”

His eyes reddened.

“And regardless—

the demons will not leave this place alone forever.”

“If they’ve shown their teeth—

we answer.”

“Whether preparation is perfect or not.”

Sancho and Pigi agreed.

“If Donquixote sent a search force—

their home defenses are thinner.”

“That can become an opportunity.”

“We hit the center.”

Dolores nodded.

“This time—

we do not wait for Vikir to solve everything.”

“We do it ourselves.”

“We hunt Passamonte.”

Then she corrected the name.

“No.”

“We hunt the **Fourth Corpse — Chimeries**.”

The Night Walkers did not object.

If they inherited Vikir’s will—

then they inherited the fight too.

Cindywendy raised one warning.

“Remember the Leviathan attempt.”

“Even Aiyen and Ms. Ouroboros—

people who can stand beside Night Hound—

failed to finish that operation.”

“Assassination is never simple.”

The room quieted.

“Demons grow stronger with time.”

“Years have passed since Vikir began hunting them.”

“Cimeries will be stronger now.”

“We cannot be careless.”

Then the tent flap burst open.

“Emergency!”

A sentry rushed in.

Face white.

“Donquixote cavalry!”

“Several kilometers out!”

Everyone stood.

But the report continued.

“And—”

“**Sniper of the Night** has been surrounded!”

Only one person carried that title.

Bianca.
