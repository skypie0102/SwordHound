Dolores L. Quovadis left the dormitory before noon.

Her destination—

an Old Testament temple.

“…This should keep me from being recognized.”

A black cloak covered her body.

A veil hid her face.

She intended to investigate as an ordinary believer—

not as the Saintess.

Dolores moved suspiciously through the crowd—

then hid in what she believed was the darkest corner of the temple.

*Perfect.*

It was not.

The entire temple—

floor, walls, pillars—

was white.

Her all-black disguise stood out immediately.

Only Dolores failed to notice.

Her reason for coming was serious.

Quovadis was in chaos.

The Old Testament and New Testament factions were locked in conflict.

That internal struggle had weakened the Church’s ability to respond across the Empire.

Heresies and cults were spreading.

Dolores had come to investigate the root of the problem herself.

*Come to think of it—*

*this was part of the University League assignment too.*

She remembered the task.

**Faithful Holy House Quovadis / Difficulty: ★★★★**

**The Empire is suffering from the rapid spread of heresies and cults.**

**From the standpoint of the Quovadis Inquisition, propose a method to eliminate them while raising the standing of the Rune faith.**

Dolores had assumed Mozgus Quovadis prepared it.

Apparently—

the order had come from higher up.

And the student who answered it—

was Sinclaire.

*The Pope personally reviewed her proposal.*

Dolores had later asked Sinclaire what she wrote.

Sinclaire only laughed.

*Oh, it wasn’t anything special.*

*It was rejected for being too radical.*

*They only passed me because the idea was original. Hehehe.*

Dolores had swallowed hard.

To eliminate heresies and cults—

either the Old and New Testament factions needed to reconcile—

or one side had to disappear.

Otherwise—

someone would need to eradicate every heresy and cult in one sweep.

The last option sounded impossible.

So Dolores came here—

to investigate the Old Testament Faction itself.

If she appeared openly as the New Testament Saintess—

everyone would immediately put on a performance.

Disguise was necessary.

“…There are so many people.”

The Old Testament temple was much busier than New Testament temples.

The donations were larger too.

*I thought most of their funding came from Indulgentia.*

*Apparently not.*

House Indulgentia had collapsed.

Quilt—

Dantalian—

was dead.

Yet Old Testament money continued flowing.

Was it only indulgence sales?

Could that really explain all of it?

The people standing in line were mostly small buyers.

Ordinary sinners.

Not the sort of criminals capable of paying enormous sums.

That made sense.

Anyone who needed a truly expensive indulgence would not walk into a public temple in broad daylight.

They would use representatives.

Documents.

Private transactions.

*Then where do the senior Old Testament priests find their VVIP clients?*

That funding source—

was exactly what Dolores wanted to know.

Then—

a huge, lavish carriage stopped nearby.

The Quovadis white-shield emblem gleamed on its side.

A tall middle-aged man stepped down.

The crowd cheered.

“…!”

Dolores froze.

The person she feared most had arrived.

**Humbert L. Quovadis.**

The effective leader of the Old Testament Faction.

A cardinal.

One of the highest-ranking men in Quovadis beneath the Pope.

And Dolores’s father by adoption.

Dolores was afraid of him.

She had been adopted long ago because she was born with extraordinary divine power.

From then on—

every interaction with Humbert had left her tense.

Especially at night—

when she went to offer formal greetings before bed—

his gaze moving over her made her feel as though a snake were coiling around her body.

“…!”

Dolores pressed herself against a pillar.

*Stay still.*

*Hold your breath.*

*He won’t notice.*

Humbert stopped.

“Hmm?”

He was perceptive.

He had felt the instant of attention.

“Wait here.”

He spoke to his adjutant.

“Something caught my attention.”

“I’ll check before entering.”

Step.

Step.

Step.

The crowd opened a path for him.

Believers removed their hats and bowed.

Dolores’s heart hammered.

Each approaching footstep felt like another coil tightening around her chest.

It reminded her of the corridor incident with Quilt.

Back then—

a kind younger male student had helped her.

That luck would not repeat itself.

Dolores swallowed.

Then—

“Oh?”

“What a surprise.”

Two white-robed figures stepped in front of Humbert.

Their hoods concealed their faces.

Humbert frowned.

“…Who are you?”

“Oh, my memory.”

The first man laughed.

“I covered my face so completely.”

He removed the hood.

An elderly man appeared.

Weathered.

Kind-looking.

Short.

Somewhere between a grandfather and an uncle in appearance.

Humbert’s expression tightened immediately.

“…It has been a long time, Cardinal Luther.”

There were only two cardinals at the absolute top of Quovadis beneath the Pope.

Humbert—

and the man before him.

**Martin Luther.**

A clergyman who had risen from a small, impoverished frontier temple.

Leader of the New Testament Faction.

Humbert’s greatest political rival.

Behind him stood Mozgus Quovadis—

large, rigid, and equally New Testament.

Humbert forced a polite smile.

“What brings Cardinal Luther here?”

“Hehehe.”

“Espionage.”

“…What?”

Humbert’s face hardened.

Luther laughed.

“Our temples have nothing but flies.”

“Every temple Cardinal Humbert visits is packed with believers.”

“I was jealous.”

“So I came shamelessly to learn your secret.”

Humbert barely maintained his smile.

“There is no secret.”

“There are simply more people in this district.”

“Hahaha.”

“Is that so?”

“Another revelation.”

“Clearly my virtue is lacking.”

Martin Luther’s ministry was unlike Humbert’s.

He rarely preached doctrine.

Rarely gave stern lectures.

Instead—

he sought out the old.

The sick.

The poor.

And said things like:

*If you’re hungry or tired, come to me.*

*If you become sick or weak, come to me.*

*If you need someone to sincerely celebrate your marriage, come to me.*

*If you need someone to sincerely mourn at a funeral, come to me.*

*If you are so sad you need to cry or talk, come to me.*

*If your home is too cold or damp to sleep in, come to me.*

*If you need help lifting or carrying something heavy, come to me.*

*Whatever it is—come.*

He did not ask how many times they had read scripture.

Whether they memorized hymns.

Whether they evangelized.

Whether they attended temple regularly.

Whether they donated.

He simply visited lonely old people to check whether their floors were warm.

Visited sick children to see whether their fever had broken.

Asked whether they had eaten.

Asked whether the weak needed help.

Martin Luther and his New Testament priests were always short of something.

Food.

Clothing.

Money.

They lived narrowly.

Nothing like the elegant and well-dressed Old Testament clergy.

And the largest difference between them—

was the amount of money they collected.

Humbert bowed.

“Then I’ll excuse myself.”

“I have a meeting.”

“Oh my.”

“This old man has taken too much of your valuable time.”

Luther bowed as well.

He made the sign of the cross over his forehead.

Humbert turned.

Then Luther recited a verse.

“Demons boil upon this earth—

seeking to devour us.”

Humbert stopped.

Luther continued toward his back.

“Do not fear.”

“Stand firm.”

“Win through truth.”

The crowd buying indulgences had turned to watch.

Luther’s voice remained gentle.

But firm.

“Even if you lose family.”

“Property.”

“Honor.”

“Life.”

Humbert did not turn around.

Luther finished.

“Truth will make the Empire endure.”

“Lumen.”

◆◆◆

Two of Quovadis’s highest powers appearing in the same temple was rare enough to draw a crowd.

Dolores used the commotion to slip away.

She hid in another corner—

out of Humbert’s sight—

and finally exhaled.

*Why is Cardinal Luther here?*

Martin Luther had rarely appeared publicly lately.

The Pope was aging.

His dementia symptoms were growing worse.

Dolores worried about that constantly.

Humbert had become increasingly active—

while Luther appeared less and less.

*Then I need to be more active myself.*

The New Testament Faction needed a visible presence.

But reality was harsher.

Dolores was still a student.

She carried the prestigious title of Saintess—

yet held little practical power.

Sometimes—

the helplessness was suffocating.

*…If only he were here.*

A face came to mind.

Someone whose presence made her feel safe.

The first person she had ever wanted to rely on.

And the first person she had wanted to become reliable **for**.

*Night Hound.*

When he was there—

even impossible things felt possible.

Had they not fought a terrifying Demon King together?

*If he were beside me—*

*the Old Testament.*

*the heresies.*

*the cults.*

*Humbert.*

*None of them would feel frightening.*

*…I want to see him.*

Dolores admitted it to herself.

Perhaps this was the first time she had felt something like this so clearly.

But the chance of meeting him again seemed impossibly distant.

Maybe he had already forgotten her.

One night at the orphanage.

Perhaps that was all their connection had ever been.

“…Haa.”

A sigh escaped.

Then—

“Was it this way?”

A voice came from behind the pillar.

Dolores stiffened.

Humbert’s adjutant.

Humbert had not forgotten the gaze he sensed earlier.

Before entering the temple—

he had ordered the man to search.

*What do I do?*

Dolores looked around.

Dead end.

No escape.

The adjutant approached.

He leaned around the final pillar.

Then frowned.

“…No one.”

He shook his head.

“The Cardinal really has become too sensitive lately.”

He turned away.

Dolores did not move.

Because she was no longer standing on the floor.

A strong arm held her tight against the pillar several meters above the ground.

Another hand covered her mouth.

A nearly invisible thread supported the two of them.

The rough hand over her mouth triggered an old memory—

a school corridor.

Quilt approaching.

A strong hand pulling her out of sight.

*This happened before…*

Without meaning to—

Dolores whispered into the hand.

“…Vikir?”

The answer came in a low, roughened voice.

“…Shh.”

Dolores’s eyes widened.

The mask was different.

The presence was not.

**Night Hound.**

He had appeared again.
