# Chapter 389: The Rotten Dog of Nouvelle Vague (5)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/389.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 387 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Garam Nord, Kirko Grimm, D'Ordume D'Orcdile, Poseidon, Nouvelle Vague, and guard-rank conventions. Kirko’s remarks about D'Ordume’s past are preserved as her attributed account rather than narrator-confirmed institutional history.

Another page came off Nouvelle Vague’s calendar.

At the end of every month—

the guards were evaluated.

The labor system was unusual.

One guard controlled twelve prisoners.

For that month—

the guard was effectively a god to those twelve.

Punishment.

How severe.

Workload.

Rest.

Rewards.

Food.

All of it—

at the guard’s discretion.

Then the month ended.

The groups were reassigned.

New prisoners.

New guard.

New cycle.

That did **not** mean guards could simply be kind if they wished.

They were evaluated too.

Their scores depended heavily on how much work their twelve prisoners completed.

Those scores affected promotion.

So every guard had one incentive—

extract as much labor as possible.

How they did it—

was up to them.

Some used knives.

Whips.

Beatings.

Food restriction.

Sleep restriction.

Solitary confinement.

Others bribed prisoners with extra bread.

Better tools.

Small privileges.

The prisoners—

in turn—

had reason to work.

A guard who received a bad score might decide someone had to pay for it.

And the person controlling food—

rest—

punishment—

and life itself—

was not someone a prisoner wanted angry.

◆◆◆

At month’s end—

the new evaluation list appeared.

The lower guards gathered around it.

Competition over these rankings was constant.

Cheating during assignment draws.

Bribing people to transfer useful prisoners.

Stealing other teams’ food.

Stealing their tools.

Every resource inside Nouvelle Vague was limited.

One group receiving more—

meant another received less.

And for years—

one name had always been easy to ignore.

**Garam Nord.**

His team had been miserable.

No drive.

No results.

No future.

Exactly what people expected from—

Stupid Garam.

Rotten Dog.

Lazy Dog.

Other guards took the useful prisoners before he could.

They stole his bread.

His work equipment.

Garam sometimes surrendered his own food just to persuade prisoners to keep working.

That generosity did not inspire them.

It taught them he was weak.

Once someone landed in Garam’s group—

they assumed the month was already lost.

He almost never used physical punishment.

Even prisoners ignored him.

His fellow guards—

worse.

Construction progress.

Solitary-confinement referrals.

Riot suppression.

Overall work results.

Garam had always been at the bottom.

Until now.

**<Monthly Evaluation>**

**1. Garam Nord / ◆ / 108%**  
**2. Kirko Grimm / ◆◆ / 96%**  
**3. … / … / 89%**

**※ Name / Rank / Task Completion Rate**

Silence.

Then—

“What?”

“Did you see this?”

“Garam is insane.”

“I didn’t even know completion could go above one hundred percent.”

“They set the official target deliberately high.”

“Nobody is actually supposed to reach it.”

“Exactly.”

“Even Kirko only gets around ninety-five or ninety-six.”

“And look at the gap between her and third.”

“Seven points.”

“But Garam beat **her** by twelve.”

“How?”

“Was he ever good at this?”

“Of course not.”

“He was always last.”

“Usually around forty percent.”

“…Then has he been hiding his ability this whole time?”

“What an irritating development.”

The room quieted.

Kirko had entered.

The former permanent first-place guard—

now ranked second.

She set down her tray.

Grilled fish.

Fermented sea eel.

Boiled peas.

Sea-cow milk.

She began eating alone.

Whispers continued behind her.

“Kirko looks angry.”

“Obviously.”

“She lost first place.”

“To Stupid Garam, of all people.”

“…At this rate—

won’t Garam get promoted before her?”

Kirko stopped chewing.

Turned.

Clearly displeased.

Then—

“…!”

The room went quiet again.

The subject of every conversation had entered.

Garam Nord.

◆◆◆

Garam—

Vikir—

chewed boiled peas while reviewing the next stage of his plan.

*To activate Poseidon—*

*I need to deliver as much mana or physical force as possible to the detonation point.*

That made guard status far more useful than prisoner status.

He could check Poseidon’s condition.

Watch the construction.

Move through work areas.

But his current rank was too low.

Poseidon sat deep within Level Nine’s labor zone.

A junior guard could not wander there freely.

*At minimum—

Captain.*

*Major would be safer.*

Three-diamond insignia—

Captain.

Hibiscus insignia—

Major.

At those ranks—

his movement would become much easier.

*Mid-level guards, Captain and above, can inspect the labor zones without filing reports every time.*

*That would even let me approach Poseidon secretly at night.*

So he needed achievements.

Quickly.

Fortunately—

Nouvelle Vague promoted differently from ordinary military organizations.

Surface armies might take months or years to move someone upward.

Nouvelle Vague rewarded performance aggressively.

It had to.

Guards died.

Guards were crippled.

Vacancies appeared constantly.

Vikir was still calculating when—

THUD.

A small paper bag landed in front of him.

“Garam Nord.”

“It’s your birthday today, right?”

“Congratulations.”

A kitchen guard slapped his shoulder and walked away.

Vikir stared at the bag.

Only then did he realize—

today was Garam’s birthday.

“…?”

White powder.

He touched a fingertip to it.

Tasted.

Sweet.

“Sugar?”

He remembered Garam’s diary.

Nouvelle Vague gave each guard a small bag of sugar on their birthday.

But this bag carried something unpleasant too.

Sharp.

Salty.

The powder looked identical—

but much of the bag seemed to be ordinary salt.

“What is this?”

Vikir tilted the bag.

Then—

“Are you actually stupid?”

Kirko’s voice.

She had approached without him noticing.

Arms folded.

“Every year you react exactly the same way.”

“No wonder everyone calls you Stupid Garam.”

She peered at the bag.

“Colonel D'Ordume probably ate most of the sugar.”

“I heard he secretly loves sweets.”

“He’s embarrassed about it—

so he filled the missing amount with regular salt.”

“I see.”

Vikir nodded.

“Covering his ears while stealing the bell.”

Kirko snorted.

“You don’t know why D'Ordume was sent down to Nouvelle Vague?”

According to Kirko—

while serving on the surface—

D'Ordume had been caught mixing sand and rice bran into rice meant for his own subordinates.

D'Ordume insisted the punishment was discrimination against his beastfolk blood.

Kirko’s version was simpler.

“Embezzlement of military supplies.”

She clicked her tongue.

“A dog that eats filth never changes.”

Vikir looked at her.

Speaking about a superior that openly—

Kirko truly had an unusual personality.

Then—

“…Anyway.”

She hesitated.

“Happy birthday.”

A pause.

“And…”

“Thank you.”

“For helping me last time.”

The final words were so quiet—

even Vikir’s superhuman hearing almost missed them.

*Birthday.*

Vikir thought about his own.

A date with little meaning.

Something he barely remembered—

and never celebrated.

Across his lives—

that had remained consistent.

In the Age of Destruction.

Inside the Hell Tree.

Within Baskerville.

And here.

A birthday was simply another marker—

inside the passage of time.

Humidity made the sugar clump.

The boiled peas smelled aggressively fishy.

Vikir picked up his empty tray.

Walked toward the washing area.

Kirko watched his back.

“…Strange.”

◆◆◆

For the next several days—

Kirko watched Garam constantly.

*Was he always like this?*

They had entered service around the same time.

So she had observed him occasionally.

Garam had always seemed pathetic.

Slow.

Weak.

But unlike some others—

not hateful.

Just—

wrong for Nouvelle Vague.

Sometimes Kirko wondered if the reason was simple.

Garam had lived on the surface as a child.

Kirko—

born and raised here—

had never seen it.

To her—

Garam had represented the surface itself.

Relaxed.

Timid.

Not sharp enough.

Not competitive enough.

Someone without the hunger Nouvelle Vague demanded.

That image irritated her.

Now—

everything had changed.

Impossible labor quotas.

Exceptional physical tests.

Strong written scores.

The other guards had begun changing too.

Contempt became interest.

Interest became friendliness.

Some now tried to approach him first.

And Kirko—

for reasons she did not entirely understand—

did not want to accept it.

Inferiority.

Perhaps.

If Garam represented the surface—

then acknowledging him meant acknowledging how small Nouvelle Vague had made her world.

A frog in a well—

no matter how exceptional inside the well.

For the first time in her life—

Kirko felt genuine competition toward another person.

*How is he managing the prisoners?*

*How did he reach one hundred eight percent?*

So she followed him.

Quietly.

Garam.

His twelve prisoners.

The work site.

She wanted the answer.

Then she saw how Vikir placed the prisoners into labor.

And froze.

“…!?”

What happened in front of her—

was nothing she had imagined.
