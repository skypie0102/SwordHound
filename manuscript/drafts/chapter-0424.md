CRACK!

The Poison Gnoll’s skull burst.

Poisonous brain matter scattered across the ground.

The rest of the pack died soon after.

At first—

Tudor’s spear left clean wounds through heads and hearts.

By the end—

the battlefield had become a ruin of shattered bodies.

The one-armed **Knight of the Night** sat on a corpse and breathed heavily.

“…Still not used to one arm.”

No sleep.

Constant marching.

Continuous combat.

The strain had slowly stripped expression from his face.

Then Tudor noticed something.

His own dry voice.

Short phrasing.

Cold manner.

He laughed.

“So this is how he lived all the time.”

“No wonder he was so boring.”

Vikir.

Night Hound.

Years had passed.

But Tudor still remembered his friend clearly.

The friend expelled from the Academy.

The friend sent to a prison at the edge of the world.

Pain pulsed through Tudor’s missing left arm.

He had once been Donquixote’s eldest son.

A future family head.

Now—

he had abandoned the surname.

And left his arm behind with it.

The rumors he heard in the tavern had reopened old wounds.

Cervantes—

his father—

supposedly bedridden.

Donquixote ruled by the younger brother instead.

Usher similarly controlled by a regent.

Tudor knew the truth was worse.

He remembered **Passamonte**.

His uncle.

The man who helped escort Vikir to Nouvelle Vague.

The man who had demanded Vikir’s execution in the Nakajaniye court.

For years—

Tudor had doubted the accusation against him.

Passamonte had been kind when Tudor was young.

He had taught him chivalry.

Romance.

Impossible dreams.

The duties—

no—

the privileges—

of a true knight.

Tudor could still hear him reciting the old verses.

To dream the impossible dream.

To defeat the unbeatable enemy.

To endure unbearable pain.

To die for a noble ideal.

To correct injustice.

To love with purity and kindness.

To reach for a star through faith.

But Tudor never doubted Vikir either.

Vikir did not make accusations carelessly.

And Professor Banshee Morgue—

however difficult his personality—

never asserted what he could not support.

Tudor intended to confront Passamonte directly.

He never got the chance.

After Cervantes suddenly became ill—

Passamonte took control as regent.

Then isolated Tudor.

Shortly before Tudor’s Academy graduation—

a secret civil war erupted within House Donquixote.

Passamonte’s faction attempted to seize everything.

Assassins came for Tudor.

Only when a blade reached his throat—

did he accept the truth.

Donquixote had already been consumed from within.

His father’s illness was not ordinary.

And Vikir—

their missing friend—

had been right.

Tudor escaped.

His left arm was cut off during the pursuit.

At one point—

furious and unwilling to abandon his father—

he prepared to fight to the death.

**Bianca** stopped him.

She repeated the words Vikir had spoken during his trial.

**Retreat is not surrender.**

**When danger outweighs hope, waiting is not wisdom.**

**The wise preserve tomorrow rather than gambling everything today.**

Bianca had suffered the same kind of family takeover.

So Tudor left with her.

The legitimate heads of Donquixote and Usher—

who had attempted to remain neutral in the widening civil war—

vanished from public life.

In practice—

Passamonte became ruler of Donquixote.

And another regent seized Usher.

Under the pretext of searching for the missing young heirs—

their forces hunted Tudor and Bianca across the continent.

The escape had been brutal.

Encirclements.

Checkpoints.

Pursuit teams.

The people who finally sheltered them—

were the followers who carried on Night Hound’s legacy.

The **Night Walkers**.

◆◆◆

Now Tudor stood over the dead gnolls.

The world had changed.

Famine.

Drought.

Fire.

Monster hordes.

Nothing in Academy textbooks had prepared him for this.

Nearby—

a large man shoved aside a starving girl and tore meat from a gnoll corpse.

The strong exploited the weak.

But Tudor had also learned something else.

Weakness did not automatically make someone kind.

Reality was uglier than chivalric stories.

He remembered an old line attributed to **Donquixote La Mancha Alonso Quijano**—

the distant ancestor of his House.

**The truly strong stand firm before the strong and gentle before the weak.**

**Those with strength need not torment others.**

**Those without it sometimes torment others to hide their weakness.**

The romantic knight Tudor once wanted to become—

had slowly turned into a battlefield veteran.

Each march made him colder.

Each fight made him sound more like Vikir.

“Hahaha.”

“I shouldn’t copy the way he talks too.”

“That’s too stiff.”

Someone approached nervously.

Shammua.

She held out clear water—

and herb-covered bread.

“Please take this!”

Tudor stared.

In this age—

clean water and herbs were precious.

“Can you really give this away?”

“Yes!”

The girl smiled brightly.

Tudor ate.

He still had a long distance to travel before reaching the Night Walkers’ gathering point.

When he finished—

more villagers had assembled.

“Knight of the Night!”

“Please take us with you!”

“We heard the Night Walkers know a place with food and water!”

“A place blessed by God!”

“Please!”

These were people who had lost homes to famine—

wildfire—

and monsters.

Roughly one in ten villagers had chosen to leave.

Tudor stood.

Then nodded.

“Follow me.”

That—

was the real reason he had come.

The Night Walkers had a destination.

A hidden refuge.

A place described in the new Revelation.

A place said to be beyond fire—

famine—

and war.

The **Ark**.

Built by the **Saint of the Night**.

Their mission was simple.

Bring as many people there as possible.
