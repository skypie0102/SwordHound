# Chapter 141: Identity of the Villain (1)

Back at the Academy, Vikir thought over what had happened the night before.

*That man in the black mask.*

*He definitely used one of Quovadis's secret techniques.*

The strange creature with **Ephebo** carved into his chest had possessed strength at the upper end of the Sword Graduator realm.

He had been troublesome to put down.

Worse—

Vikir had failed to stop him from escaping.

*Starting tomorrow night, I'll have to move more aggressively.*

*If I tear apart every place that reeks of demonic energy, sooner or later we'll meet again.*

The demons could not stay hidden forever.

They craved human blood.

Especially children's blood.

Orphanages.

Hospitals.

Monasteries.

Places where the weak gathered in large numbers were natural hunting grounds for creatures like them.

*If I want to hunt them more efficiently, I need to become a Sword Master as soon as possible.*

Vikir was presently a **Peak Sword Graduator**.

He had already recovered all the strength he had possessed before his regression.

And that was no longer all he had.

The blessing of the River Styx.

The demonic sword Beelzebub.

The Baskerville swordsmanship he had refined beyond what he once knew.

The experience accumulated through killing countless monsters and demons.

Taken together—

he could now stand against a Sword Master.

And Vikir had only just turned eighteen.

His room for growth was enormous.

*First—the Seventh Fang.*

*I need to complete it.*

While Vikir's thoughts continued to turn—

“Okay, everyone! Attention!”

A bright voice pulled him back to the present.

A second-year girl stood before the gathered first-years and clapped for their attention.

The nameplate beside her displayed the club's name.

> **Newspaper Club — Media Society**
>
> **Ryukeion (Λύκειον)**

A club.

A student-run organization—

also called a society, circle, or association depending on the school and the activity.

Ryukeion was a relatively small newspaper club with only about thirty members.

Its influence, however, was anything but small.

The paper it produced every day carried news from both inside and outside Colosseo Academy.

Every issue sold out.

Demand was high even beyond the Academy walls.

Students wanted to know what was happening around campus as quickly as possible, no matter how trivial the story.

Parents, sponsors, and other interested parties outside the Academy were even more eager.

Colosseo gathered together young people who would one day become giants capable of changing the world.

Naturally, the outside world watched them closely.

Vikir needed information more than most.

By day—

he hid his identity and lived the ordinary life of an Academy student.

By night—

he left the campus and hunted demons.

That made fast information essential.

If he wanted to know what had happened overnight before the morning paper reached everyone else—

and what had happened during the day before the evening edition appeared—

the simplest answer was obvious.

Join the people who made the paper.

Ryukeion.

*Still, there really aren't many members.*

Vikir looked around the clubroom.

Magical printing presses filled one side of the room, machines capable of transferring images from mana screens onto paper.

Stacks of photographs and reference material covered the tables.

Yet only a small number of students occupied the room.

Several second-years were organizing the newcomers.

Most of the others were first-years like Vikir.

The remaining first-year members were:

**Tudor Donquixote — Cold Department, Class A.**

**Sinclaire — Hot Department, Class A.**

**Sancho Barataria — Cold Department, Class A.**

**Bianca Fo Usher — Cold Department, Class B.**

**Pigi — Cold Department, Class B.**

**Vikir — Cold Department, Class B.**

There had been more freshmen at the beginning of the semester.

Most had already quit.

Ryukeion was notoriously demanding.

Its workload was far heavier than that of an ordinary hobby club.

And Colosseo itself was brutal enough that neglecting one's studies even slightly could lead to failure.

Only unusually capable students could keep up with both coursework and club duties.

There was another reason the weak-willed did not last.

The current head of the newspaper club—

**Saintess Dolores L. Quovadis**—

was a famous workaholic.

There was even a rumor that working under Dolores during midterms and finals was harder than the examinations themselves.

The freshmen still standing were therefore an unusually capable group.

One of the second-year members looked them over.

“So why did each of you stay? You know Ryukeion is famous for being strict.”

Tudor answered first.

“Hahaha! I'm an alpha male. Something this minor doesn't bother me! Studies, clubs—I'll handle all of it!”

It was exactly the kind of confidence expected from a top Cold Department student born into one of the Seven Great Families.

Sancho spoke next.

“Hmm. I didn't want people thinking I'm just a strong idiot, so I stayed. Newspaper work sounds intelligent. I'd like to become an intelligent man.”

The unexpectedly earnest answer made several students blink.

Then came Pigi.

“I-I like collecting and analyzing information. I think… I think this might actually be my calling!”

He stumbled over the words—

but the ambition behind them was sincere.

Next was Sinclaire.

“Aren't money, information, and power inseparable? Of the three, I think information is the most important.”

A precise answer.

She clearly understood the forces that moved the world.

Finally, everyone turned to Bianca.

She sighed.

“I was tricked into joining by Senior Dolores.”

“Tricked?”

Every eye turned toward her.

Bianca hesitated.

Then admitted the truth.

“She told me this was a ‘sniping’ club.”

A silence.

Technically—

that was not entirely false.

Newspapers did sometimes “snipe” their targets with reporting.

The room burst into laughter.

One of the second-year girls chuckled and patted Bianca on the shoulder.

“Then I suppose we really are a sniping club. Look at this!”

She unfolded page two of the latest morning edition.

A sports article occupied most of the page.

The magical photograph attached to it showed both view and comment counts.

> **[EXCLUSIVE] Who Is the ‘Genius Face’ Who Appeared in the First-Year Naphtali Match!?**
>

Nearly fifty thousand views.

More than thirty thousand comments.

Absurd numbers.

Tudor and Sancho immediately burst out laughing.

“Wahahahaha! Hey, Vikir! That's from that day, isn't it? Our Naphtali match!”

“…”

Vikir pressed a hand to his forehead and let out a quiet sigh.

Bianca turned her face away with a disappointed look and said nothing.

Sinclaire stared intently at the article carrying Vikir's photograph.

Pigi—

who seemed to have grown much closer to Tudor and Sancho lately—

looked at Vikir with open admiration.

Then—

the clubroom door swung open.

“Hello, everyone!”

The newspaper club's president entered with her usual energy.

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

She gathered the first-, second-, and third-year members around the central table.

“Last night, our second- and third-year reporters went out to cover a story. The article itself is mostly finished. We only need to decide on a headline.”

Dolores placed the draft on the table.

The article concerned the incident from the previous night.

> **[UNPUBLISHED ARTICLE]**
>
> **Views: 0**
>
> Late last night, near dawn, a mysterious individual appeared and reduced an orphanage on the outskirts of the Imperial Capital to ruins…
>
> Strangely, the attacker did not touch the valuables stored in the orphanage vault…
>
> Authorities presently believe robbery was not the motive…
>
> The individual's identity, objective, and combat strength remain unknown…
>
> Damage to the institution was severe…
>
> The confirmed dead include children estimated to be between ten and thirteen years old…
>
> Neither the Imperial Family nor the Quovadis faith has yet issued an official statement…
>
> **Comments: 0**

The rough narrative beneath it was even more direct.

An unknown assailant had broken into an orphanage on the outskirts of the Imperial Capital.

The director had been killed.

The grounds had been devastated.

The bodies of children had been found.

Gold and jewels in the vault had gone untouched.

The article therefore suggested that the attack had been committed for slaughter rather than profit.

Vikir read it.

*There seems to be a misunderstanding.*

The children had been killed by demons—

not by him.

But the Imperial Guards who arrived later apparently believed the deaths were part of Vikir's attack.

*It doesn't matter.*

*They won't identify me either way.*

*More importantly, I need Cindywendy to investigate everything connected to this orphanage.*

*It may be linked to Viscount Gambino's slave auction.*

While the other newspaper members struggled over a headline—

Vikir was thinking about something else entirely.

Around the table, the debate grew increasingly serious.

Everyone wanted the prestige of seeing a headline they had written printed across the front page of a paper read throughout the Empire.

“A terrifying new villain is born!?”

“A monster beyond the Academy—terror in the Imperial Capital!?”

“What are the Imperial Guards doing!?”

“An unprecedented villain challenges Quovadis!?”

“The mystery behind the dead children!”

Sensational headlines came one after another.

None felt right.

The room stalled.

Then one of the sharper students realized what was missing.

“Ah! The villain doesn't have a name yet!”

“Right! Just saying ‘villain’ or ‘criminal’ doesn't give the story any identity.”

“Then let's give him a nickname first!”

Notorious criminals often acquired ominous names.

King of the Cave.

Son of the Fountain.

Murder King.

The Human-Flesh Butcher.

Bluebeard.

A memorable criminal needed a memorable name.

This one had appeared without warning—

then spread fear through the nights of the Imperial Capital.

Prominent figures.

Infamous mercenaries.

Corrupt officials.

He struck them down brutally—

then vanished like a ghost without leaving a trail.

What should such a figure be called?

The students enthusiastically joined the naming contest.

“The first reports came from the informant district, so what about ‘Wolf of the Informant District’?”

“But the attacks happened outside that district too. How about ‘Crow of the Unfortunate Night’?”

“Too long. ‘Orphan Predator’ has more impact.”

“Using ‘orphan’ like that is discriminatory. If you're going in that direction, call him ‘Orphanage Raider’ instead.”

“But orphanages aren't the only places that were attacked. There were monasteries, hospitals, chapels, even municipal residences. What about ‘Mr. Terror’?”

“Hmm… this is harder than I thought.”

Suggestions came and went.

Nothing stuck.

“…”

“…”

“…”

At last—

the room fell quiet.

And in the silence—

Dolores murmured unconsciously.

“…The Night Hound.”
