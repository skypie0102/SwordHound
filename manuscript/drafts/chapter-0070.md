# Chapter 70: Blood Relatives (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/070.txt` — `f06195bc01febbb1caa2989c2719cd04bd674703`. Recovered English Chapter 69 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project-established terminology controls **Rokoko**, **Aiyen**, **Red Death**, **Pomeranian La Baskerville**, and **Baskerville**. Pomeranian's canonical English name replaces Chinese/transliteration drift; later family history is not imported before the pendant reveal.

Every Rokoko hunter was dead.

Vikir and Aiyen approached through heat and the stench of decomposition.

Aiyen's eyes narrowed.

The corpses explained themselves.

Vomit.

Diarrhea.

Fire pits and piles of leaves where feverish hunters had tried to fight chills.

And above all—

red spots across the skin.

Aiyen swallowed.

“These are the ones healthy enough to leave the village and hunt.”

That meant the people who had stayed behind were probably worse.

“Let's see.”

Vikir moved first.

They crossed into Rokoko territory.

Under normal circumstances the borders were impossible to miss.

Skulls.

Bodies.

Warnings placed deliberately for intruders.

Now the old markers hung untouched.

Moss and mold covered them.

No one had refreshed the boundary in a long time.

“Something happened.”

They reached the low hills where Rokoko lived.

No light.

No smoke.

No sentries.

The real problem was not missing guards.

It was the absence of movement anywhere.

Weeds grew through abandoned shelters.

Crude tools lay where people had dropped them.

Aiyen pulled aside one shelter's curtain.

BZZZZZZ.

Flies burst outward.

The smell of rot and waste followed them.

Inside lay one woman and three children.

Dead recently enough that their condition was still recognizable.

Vikir checked another shelter.

“The same.”

And another.

The village had not been slaughtered by weapons.

Disease had killed people where they lay.

On less-decayed corpses, the red marks were unmistakable.

Aiyen shivered.

“A plague?”

Vikir stared.

A memory from before regression surfaced.

*The Red Death.*

Yes.

This was the time.

A catastrophic epidemic that devastated tribes and nonhuman peoples throughout the jungle.

It eventually reached the Imperial frontier as well.

The symptoms matched.

Red eruptions across the body.

Vomiting.

Diarrhea.

Weakness.

Pain.

The infected person's metabolism slowed to an extreme crawl, dragging the dying process out for a very long time.

And the contagion spread at an abnormal speed. Among the jungle tribes, even making eye contact with a sick person could transmit it.

*How was it stopped?*

The memory was old.

He needed time to pull the answer free.

“Slave!”

Aiyen grabbed his clothes.

“Leave. Now.”

“Why?”

“It's a curse!”

For the first time in a long while, Vikir saw genuine fear in her face.

He caught her wrist before she could pull him away.

“It's disease.”

Aiyen looked even more horrified.

“That's worse!”

“It doesn't spread as easily as you think.”

She trembled but did not abandon him.

Instead she stayed close enough to keep hold of his clothing.

“If you're scared, go back.”

“And leave you here to die?”

“If I die, I die.”

“Who says that?”

Vikir blinked.

*I do.*

He chose not to answer.

They searched the settlement.

Rokoko had not vanished completely.

The tribe had apparently abandoned the dead and severely ill, then migrated elsewhere with whoever could still move.

A brutal choice.

Possibly a useless one if carriers traveled with the survivors.

Vikir collected books and ritual objects that appeared important.

Rokoko culture and magic were poorly understood in the Empire.

Even if the tribe was an enemy, preserving knowledge had value.

Then Aiyen screamed.

“AAAH!”

Vikir left the shelter immediately.

Aiyen stood rigid, pointing toward the village edge.

“A cursed child!”

Beside the poorest shelter stood a small girl.

About five.

Barefoot.

Black hair.

Red eyes.

Skin pale as snow.

Behind her was a crude stone grave.

Fresh purple flowers lay in front of it.

Aiyen looked ready to flee despite legs that no longer seemed willing to cooperate.

“Ghost. Definitely a ghost. Vikir, you go first. I'll follow.”

Vikir almost laughed.

After two years beside her, this was the first time he had seen her this frightened.

He approached the girl slowly and crouched until their eyes were level.

She tensed.

But when he placed a hand gently on her head, she did not run.

“Who are you?”

No answer.

He tried again.

The girl eventually produced a few broken words.

“Rokoko. Slave. Kitchen.”

Her speech mixed Rokoko and Imperial.

Vikir reconstructed the meaning.

“You were kept here as a slave?”

A small nod.

“Your name?”

“……Pomeranian.”

The name was Imperial.

Unusual, but unmistakably not Rokoko.

Then she added one more fragment.

“La Baskerville.”

Vikir froze.

Baskerville.

And **La**.

A direct-line female name.

Very few members of the family bore the direct-line middle names **Le** or **La**.

Vikir's reaction frightened the child.

She hid behind a shelter post.

He wiped a hand over his face and forced himself to calm down.

“It's all right. Come here.”

Pomeranian did not move.

Aiyen emerged from another shelter and tossed something toward him.

“Found this. It's not cursed, is it?”

Vikir caught it.

A small gold pendant.

The front carried Baskerville's fang crest.

Aiyen recognized the symbol from the potion bottle Vikir had once given her.

“That's yours, isn't it?”

Vikir ran a thumb over the crest.

The pendant was old.

Roughly the style worn three decades earlier.

CLICK.

He opened it.

Inside was a miniature portrait.

A young blond woman with blue eyes.

A younger Hugo Le Baskerville, black-haired and red-eyed.

And between them, a black-haired, red-eyed girl in her early teens.

Vikir stared at the man.

There was no possibility of mistake.

*Hugo Le Baskerville.*

A much younger Hugo—

but Hugo.
