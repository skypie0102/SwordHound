# Chapter 54: Slaves of the Savage Tribe (3)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/054.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 54 is an aligned secondary witness only. Aiyen was Ballak's youngest hunting captain.

She led the tribe's younger generation through the jungle without fear.

Yet even she had finally encountered someone she did not know how to handle.

His name was Vikir.

The slave she had personally captured during the recent raid into imperial territory.

“……”

Aiyen stared at him.

Despite the state of his body, Vikir had built himself a shelter with startling speed and without asking anyone for help.

CLACK. CLACK. CLACK.

More than twenty wooden poles, each over two meters long, stood in careful order.

Mud mixed with stone powder and lime sealed the gaps.

Leaves lined the walls, animal hides covered them, and sawdust and dry leaves insulated the floor.

A door and window were added almost immediately.

The scraps of material normally allotted to a slave had been enough.

In less than half an hour, Vikir had produced a perfectly livable tent by himself.

“Task complete.”

He turned toward Aiyen with his usual flat voice.

Her first command had been simple:

*Build your own place to sleep.*

Aiyen looked inside in disbelief.

A small opening in the roof could be shut during rain.

A fire pit sat inside, with a smoke channel running beneath the floor before venting outdoors.

When a fire burned for cooking, the exhaust passed beneath the shelter and warmed the ground before escaping.

The smoke left.

The remaining heat stayed.

Ventilation and light were both excellent.

“……How are you so good at building tents?”

Aiyen had secretly expected to watch the badly injured Vikir struggle.

She quietly hid the extra supplies she had intended to offer if he failed—sturdier poles, brick material, fuel.

He had needed none of them.

Instead, Vikir found lime-rich soil, mixed and hardened it with water, and used broad leaves to reinforce the shelter against rain and wind.

*Nothing unusual about that.*

Swordsmen who survived the Age of Destruction could not afford to know only how to swing a blade.

They needed enough practical skill to camp and survive through extreme heat, bitter cold, and ruined supply lines.

Vikir had learned all kinds of domestic work along the way.

After finishing his own shelter, he processed the pile of hides near Aiyen's tent and mended torn cloth.

He cleared a drainage trench.

He split enough wood for the day.

Before his master could order anything, Vikir found inconvenient work and finished it himself.

An A-rank slave, if there were such a thing.

“The bricks beneath the chieftain's tent look unstable.”

“……What? Mother's tent?”

“Yes. If they stay like that, the central foundation may shift during the rainy season. Water will get inside. If you order it, I can mix the lime-rich soil here with crushed stone from the hill, make bricks, bake them, coat them with hyena oil, and reinforce the base.”

“……Do that. I was just about to suggest it myself.”

Aiyen scratched her head.

Vikir did exactly what he had described.

Still limping, he dug white soil from the rear slope, hauled stone from the opposite hill, crushed it, and mixed it into the earth.

He added water, formed rectangular bricks, fired them, then treated them with hyena oil.

The new foundation made Akwilla's tent substantially sturdier.

Even Akwilla, who had initially been annoyed by the disturbance, approved once the work was finished.

“So *that's* why the roof always leaked no matter how many leaves and hides I added. The foundation was shifting.”

Akwilla still showed little personal interest in Vikir.

She simply ordered him to keep an eye on the chieftain's tent and repair whatever looked neglected.

Aiyen disliked seeing *her* slave spend so much time maintaining her mother's home, but did not disobey.

Instead she hovered behind Vikir while he dug drainage channels and fitted simple screens to stop leaves and mud from clogging them.

“Hey. You're fine doing all that alone?”

“……”

“Don't overdo anything.”

“……”

“Do you actually *like* working? You've been at it all day.”

“……”

“Why clean drainage before the rainy season is even here?”

“……”

“Where did you get that food? Eat some.”

“……”

“Your master is talking to you. Why won't you answer?”

Vikir considered the conversation a distraction and mostly ignored her.

Aiyen disappeared.

Then reappeared.

Then disappeared again.

Then returned to hover nearby.

At this point, it was increasingly difficult to tell who was attending whom.

THUMP. THUMP. THUMP.

Vikir also finished the laundry with lye water and a wooden beater.

Scout.

Herbalist.

Healer.

Cook.

Assassin.

Cleaner.

Laundry worker.

From disposable recruit to veteran survivor, he had done almost everything during the Age of Destruction.

*I use that era as an explanation for everything… but it really was that kind of time.*

He drifted into old memories as he worked.

“……Ahem. Hm.”

Nearby, Aiyen—who had originally stepped in to demonstrate the laundry—quietly hid a torn leather skirt behind her back while watching him.

◆◆◆

For Vikir, life as a slave in Ballak was strangely manageable.

Compared with Baskerville training or his workload in Underdog City, the daily chores were almost restful.

There was only one person he found difficult to understand.

“Ahh. Much better.”

Aiyen.

Princess of Ballak.

She had chosen the drainage channel Vikir had just dug as a place to relieve herself.

“I've been holding that for ages.”

“……”

Vikir turned away.

“What exactly are you doing?”

“Marking territory. This is my slave's home, so it's also mine.”

“Do you have to do that *here*?”

“Why be embarrassed by something as ordinary as relieving yourself? I don't care about that.”

Aiyen treated bodily exposure with none of the shame expected in imperial society.

Vikir remembered an old anthropological paper on the tribes of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Sexual relationships among the mountain tribes were comparatively unrestricted, and prospective partners openly considered fertility and disease status. As a result, nudity carried far less taboo.

*Right. I did read something like that.*

It was a real custom with its own history.

Vikir decided there was no point judging it by imperial standards.

“Fine. Just don't turn the area around my shelter into a latrine.”

“Why? A few centuries ago, didn't Imperials throw waste straight from their windows? I heard the streets were so filthy people started wearing high heels to stay above it, then drowned themselves in perfume.”

Vikir had no immediate answer to that.

The chores eventually ended.

When Ballak was neither fighting nor hunting, life in the village was remarkably relaxed.

For someone as relentlessly industrious as Vikir, slavery here was far less demanding than life under Baskerville or the endless work and training of his previous assignments.

Aiyen watched him with a strange expression.

“Hey.”

“……?”

“Don't you want to become a proper member of our tribe sooner?”

Vikir frowned.

What was that supposed to mean?

Aiyen sat beside him.

“Slave work is hard, isn't it?”

“Not particularly.”

“You've learned how to answer well. A slave wouldn't dare complain.”

“No. I mean it. This really isn't that bad.”

“How could it not be? Cleaning, laundry, all this tedious work…”

Aiyen waved dismissively.

Clearly she considered those tasks far more painful than Vikir did.

“Come on. Come with me.”

“……?”

“I'll help you fit into the tribe.”

As a slave, refusing was not much of an option.

Aiyen touched the collar still fastened around Vikir's neck.

“If you do well, I'll take this off too.”

That part was tempting.

The rope had been rubbing his neck raw.

“What do you want me to do?”

Aiyen's eyes lit up.

“Hunt.”

“……Hunt?”

“Something big.”

Ballak lived primarily by hunting.

To feed nearly three hundred people evenly, the tribe needed the equivalent of a boar weighing more than two hundred kilograms every day.

That assumed they could supplement the meat with mushrooms, berries, and edible roots.

Recently, game had been scarce.

More warriors were going days at a time eating little but gathered plants.

“If we bring back enough meat now, freeing you isn't impossible.”

Vikir gave her an odd look.

Hearing the word *freedom* from the person holding his leash was ironic.

Still, the offer was useful.

“You only need to assist me. You seem capable of moving around and carrying light loads.”

It was impossible to tell whether Aiyen's only goal was a successful hunt.

For now, Vikir chose to cooperate.

“Fine. Let's go.”

◆◆◆

Young and old hunters gathered in small groups.

Many sat astride wolves with bows and spears ready.

Aiyen alone had no partner.

Only one wolf—

and her slave, Vikir.

“Hunters, gather. Time for the rite.”

An oddly dressed old man approached with a bowl of black charcoal paste.

Colorful tattoos showed beneath his dark robe.

A shaman.

“Receive my blessing before you leave, or I'll curse the lot of you.”

He smeared black ash across the hunters' faces.

Ballak hunters felt guilt when they killed prey.

According to tradition, they blackened their faces before a hunt so the god of death would not recognize them.

The younger warriors, Aiyen included, seemed unimpressed.

“Here we go again.”

“Leave him alone. A shaman needs to look important.”

“Hasn't his fortune-telling gotten even worse?”

“He'd probably predict an earring goes on an ear and a nose ring goes on a nose.”

Young people were young people, regardless of tribe or century.

Aiyen grudgingly held out her face.

The old shaman smeared a few careless strokes across it and moved on.

The dislike between them was obvious.

Aiyen then put on a spiked collar and handed one to Vikir.

“This keeps things from getting at your neck.”

Vikir nodded.

The hunters began leaving the village one group at a time.

Blades sheathed.

Spears raised.

Bows in hand.

Wolves moving beneath them.

Vikir prepared as well.

*If I'm staying here, I need to blend in.*

He had to adjust his assumptions to Ballak life.

A basic bodily need gave him what seemed like an easy place to start.

He stepped aside and relieved himself openly.

The reaction was immediate.

Ballak women covered their eyes.

Several still peeked through their fingers.

The men stared at him in open disbelief.

Vikir frowned.

“Why is everyone looking at me? Don't you all do this?”

Replies came from every direction.

“Do what? Who relieves themselves in the open like that?”

“Is that how Imperials live? Barbarians.”

“We have toilets.”

“Men over there. Women over here.”

“Have some manners and cover yourself.”

“How uncivilized!”

“……”

Vikir slowly turned toward Aiyen.

She was smiling.

The instant he caught her eye, she looked away.


◆◆◆

Aiyen mounted her wolf.

“Hold tight.”

Vikir climbed on behind her.

Uncertain where he was supposed to brace himself, he grabbed the wolf's fur.

Aiyen reached back, caught his arm, and pulled it around her waist.

“Here. Hold on properly. If you don't, you'll fall and break your neck.”

Vikir caught hold of her at the hips, but not firmly enough.

BOOM!

The wolf leapt.

Vikir immediately tumbled to the ground.

Pain exploded through him.

Bones that Tough Life had barely begun to knit twisted again.

Riding a wolf was nothing like riding a horse.

There was no saddle.

No stirrups.

A wolf's body was narrower and far more agile.

Aiyen stopped and jumped down.

“Tch. I told you to wrap both arms around my waist. If you hold on that lightly, of course you'll fall.”

She tapped her side impatiently.

Vikir had just started pushing himself up when—

THUD!

Someone stopped between them.

Ahun.

He sat on another wolf, staring down at Vikir.

The atmosphere chilled.

Aiyen crossed her arms.

Ballak hunters competed over everything.

Ahun, who constantly tried to outdo Aiyen, had apparently decided this hunt would be no different.

“This time I'll bring back the larger prey. Then I'll become captain of the next joint hunt. I swear it on my grandfather's honor.”

“Why stake the honor of a man who only knows shamanism on a hunt?” Aiyen replied. “But gamble whatever you like.”

She dismissed him and motioned Vikir over.

Vikir rose.

THUMP!

His head snapped sideways.

Ahun had kicked him in the face from atop his wolf.

Vikir was still too injured to dodge even though he saw the blow coming.

Fortunately, Ahun had not used much force.

Aiyen exploded.

“You bastard, what do you think you're—”

Then she stopped.

Her gaze moved from Vikir on the ground to Ahun above him.

Ahun mistook the pause for restraint and sneered.

“You plan to use this weakling as a hunting assistant? Wake up, Captain. He'll only drag you down.”

Aiyen's eyes narrowed.

“Before you say that, look at your own waist.”

Ahun looked down.

Nothing.

The quiver that should have been full of arrows was gone.

“……!?”

He spun around.

Vikir was already standing again—

holding Ahun's quiver.

He had taken it during the fall.

*Another useful skill from the Age of Destruction.*

Before the regression, there had been very few kinds of work Vikir had never done.

That included theft.

“A weapon is like a lover,” Vikir said flatly. “You notice its importance when someone takes it away.”

Aiyen burst out laughing.

Ahun went red.

“You insolent slave!”

He swung his bow into Vikir's shoulder.

CRACK!

Vikir went down again.

Ahun dismounted and raised the bow a second time.

WHOOSH—

CLACK!

The blow stopped in midair.

Aiyen had drawn an arrow and used it to block his bow.

“Enough. Whose slave do you think you're punishing?”

Her voice was hard as ice.

“He's a slave!” Ahun protested. “He insulted a warrior!”

“You embarrassed yourself first.”

“Even so! A slave has no right to insult a warrior!”

Ahun appealed to tribal rules.

Aiyen thought for a moment.

Then nodded.

“Fine. A slave is a slave. If he breaks the rules, he must be punished.”

“Exactly, Captain.”

“But *you* don't have the right to do it. He's my slave.”

“……?”

Ballak had little concern for private property in most areas.

Slave ownership was one of the exceptions.

“If he needs punishment, I'll handle it.”

Aiyen stepped down from her wolf and stood before Vikir.

SLAP!

Her palm struck his cheek.

When he fell, she continued.

Blows struck his abdomen, sides, and legs.

Vikir curled in on himself and endured them without making a sound.

After several minutes, Aiyen finally stepped away, breathing hard.

Then she looked at Ahun.

“Satisfied?”

“……”

Ahun nodded, unsettled.

Aiyen met his eyes.

“I know you've got personal grudges against my slave. He pulled you off a wolf. You failed to catch your target. Fine.”

“……Yes.”

“But after today, if you touch my slave behind my back again, *you'll* be the one I beat like this. Understand?”

Blood dripped from Aiyen's knuckles.

Her expression carried no humor at all.

Ahun nodded several times, then gave Vikir one last exhausted glare.

He mounted his wolf and left.

Only Aiyen and Vikir remained.

Sigh…

Aiyen approached and crouched beside him.

Vikir slowly sat up.

Her expression had become difficult to read.

She gently checked the swollen side of his face.

“……Did it hurt?”

Her tone sounded different.

Perhaps Vikir imagined it.
