“Oh my.”

“I’m thirsty.”

“Where’s the well, old man?”

The old woman looked perfectly ordinary.

Vikir knew she was not.

*Nabokov I Quovadis.*

Pope of the Lun faith.

Head of House Quovadis.

The oldest surviving member of the classical Saintess line.

A living legend—

more than two centuries old.

She had lived from before Imperial unification—

through the old Warring States era—

all the way to the present.

*Before regression—

I only knew her from history books.*

In Vikir’s previous life—

Nabokov had died before he entered the war against the demons.

The commonly accepted story—

was that Humbert—

corrupted by ambition—

had poisoned her.

But Humbert had disappeared.

And Nabokov was still alive.

*The future changed.*

That made her one of the largest variables in this parent-visitation event.

How much could someone like her change humanity’s future?

Vikir had no answer.

This future had never existed before.

Still—

looking at her now—

he was not impressed.

Small body.

Poor eyesight.

Unsteady behavior.

Hints of dementia.

“Water!”

“Where’s the water?”

“You old man!”

Nabokov slapped Vikir on the head.

The blow had almost no strength.

Vikir stood in silence.

He remembered priestly comrades from the Age of Destruction.

They had often said—

*If Pope Nabokov had still been alive—

the Human Alliance would never have been pushed this far.*

Vikir had never believed things he had not seen himself.

Now that he finally stood in front of her—

he still could not see the overwhelming power people had described.

This was completely different from meeting Cane Corso.

Vikir sighed.

“The drinking fountain is over there.”

“I’ll get you water.”

“I’m thirsty!”

“Hurry up!”

Vikir led her toward the fountain.

A giant ceramic water jar.

Clear water.

Several ladles hung beside it.

Vikir picked up one.

Scooped water.

Drip.

Drip.

Drip.

The bottom leaked.

A crack.

A small hole.

“This one is broken.”

“I’ll use another.”

Before Vikir could reach for the next ladle—

splash.

The broken one disappeared into the jar.

“…?”

Nabokov had thrown it in.

She smiled.

“There.”

“Now it doesn’t leak.”

Vikir stared.

Technically—

she was correct.

The cracked ladle was completely underwater.

No water leaked **out** of it.

Nabokov laughed to herself.

“Old man.”

“Natural is best.”

“…Natural?”

“What does that mean?”

“Natural means natural.”

“Why should I explain something obvious?”

She studied Vikir’s face.

Her expression became unexpectedly gentle.

“If something is cracked—

or has holes—

leave it that way.”

“If you wrap it in something larger—

accept it inside something larger—

then the cracks stop mattering.”

“Hehehe.”

Vikir looked at the ladle resting at the bottom.

“But then you can’t drink from it.”

Nabokov blinked.

“What?”

“It sank.”

“You can’t use it anymore.”

“…Ah.”

“And everyone drinks from this jar.”

“Throwing dirty utensils directly inside is unhygienic.”

Nabokov’s mouth remained open.

For once—

no answer.

Then—

“Pope!”

Dolores came running from far away.

Mozgus followed.

His face had gone pale.

“Pope!”

“There you are!”

“We’ve been searching everywhere!”

“Hehehe…”

Mozgus hurried to Nabokov’s side.

“Where is Luther?”

“Unfortunately—

he had a performance scheduled for elderly people living alone.”

“He couldn’t come.”

“Tch.”

“That sounds exactly like him.”

Mozgus looked toward the fountain.

“Were you trying to get water?”

“Should I pour some?”

“No.”

“I’m not drinking.”

“Then why did you come here…?”

Nabokov stopped looking at him.

Her eyes returned to Vikir.

Then—

to Dolores.

The two stood side by side.

Nabokov’s expression softened again.

“Don’t force the cracks closed.”

“Accept what is there.”

“Let it be natural.”

“?”

“Thank you, young man.”

“Now I can leave with peace of mind.”

She patted Mozgus on the shoulder.

Then turned away.

The statement made no obvious sense.

Behind her—

the broken ladle still rested at the bottom of the jar.

Filled completely with water.

◆◆◆

“….”

“….”

Vikir and Dolores were left alone.

“Bye, Uncle!”

Pomeranian had left holding Nabokov’s hand.

After a short silence—

Dolores spoke first.

“Um…”

“You heard the Pope talking about the cracked ladle.”

“She’s been somewhat confused lately.”

“Don’t take everything she says too seriously.”

“She says strange things often.”

But—

even as she said that—

Dolores was thinking deeply.

*I need to control the resonance I felt when I strengthened Night Hound.*

*For that—

I need the Pope’s advice.*

Nabokov.

The oldest classical Saintess.

The highest-ranking living authority on that ancient sacred tradition.

*The classical Saintesses used to talk about awakening all the time.*

*I thought they were just rambling.*

Dolores regretted that now.

Her fight against Dantalian had changed her thinking.

Ever since—

she wanted to understand the phenomenon that had let her resonate with Night Hound.

To help him again.

To become more useful.

Nabokov’s worsening mind—

made clear advice difficult.

Dolores touched her chin.

She remembered the recent fight against Belial.

*Don’t try to carry everything alone.*

*We’re comrades.*

Night Hound had called her a comrade.

That single acknowledgment—

had made her laugh and cry at the same time.

But the resonance during the Dantalian battle had been much stronger.

So Dolores had eventually asked:

*Night Hound.*

*Tell me your name.*

She wanted to shorten the distance between them.

Understand him.

Know him.

And—

if she was honest—

there had been a selfish reason too.

*It doesn’t have to be your full name.*

*A nickname.*

*Anything.*

*Even one small part.*

That was when she finally heard it.

*…Van.*

A fragment.

Only one syllable.

Yet even that tiny piece of his identity—

had dramatically strengthened the resonance between them.

Dolores’s ears turned red just remembering it.

Names carried power.

Distance mattered.

The closer she felt to Night Hound—

the stronger her blessing became.

*Then how do I become even closer?*

*How do I create stronger resonance?*

The emotional distance still remained.

As long as it did—

Dolores’s divine power could not completely resonate with Night Hound’s soul.

Soulmate.

That—

according to the old classical tradition—

was one key to a Saintess’s awakening.

True resonance required emotional synchronization.

And that required understanding.

Dolores wanted to know more.

His fate.

His pain.

The path he carried alone.

She wanted to be ready to sacrifice alongside him.

That desire—

made her impatient.

And impatience made the path ahead harder to see.

*Night Hound.*

*I want to know you better.*

*I want to see you.*

The same determined expression that reporters had once misread as a declaration of holy war—

now looked almost lonely.

Then—

[And that concludes the second-year Ranking Battle!]

[Next is the third-year final!]

[The match that will decide the third-year leader!]

The announcement echoed from the arena.

Dolores jolted.

“Ah!”

“V-Vikir.”

“I actually came looking for you because I wanted to ask something.”

“But I’m almost out of time.”

“I was heading down anyway.”

“Tell me while we walk.”

“Okay.”

“Thank you.”

“It’s about Sinclaire.”

“I want to talk to her.”

“I thought maybe you could help arrange a place…”

Vikir nodded.

*It probably won’t change much.*

Sinclaire’s decision already seemed firm.

A short conversation with Dolores—

would not easily undo it.

So Vikir listened only half-attentively.

Something else had caught his attention.

*The trees.*

*And the magic stones.*

The path toward the arena was packed with newly planted trees.

Along the outer wall—

new barrier stones had been installed.

Vikir’s gaze sharpened.

*The root layout and the magic-stone placement…*

Individually—

nothing looked wrong.

Together—

the arrangement felt subtly dangerous.

Too coordinated.

Too deliberate.

And worse—

the new stones carried something else.

Extremely faint.

But unmistakable.

A smell.

Only Night Hound would recognize it.

*Demonic scent.*

Vikir stopped for half a second.

Then continued walking.

*It’s time to leave the Academy.*

He had spent a surprisingly long time here.

Long enough to become attached.

“….”

Even so—

Vikir turned away without hesitation.

Cutting the attachment cleanly.

A battle was approaching.

Brutal enough that—

once he entered it—

he might never return.
