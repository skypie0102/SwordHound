THUD.

The massive head of the Sixth Demon King—

Belial, the **Worthless One**—

hit the ground.

It was a sight countless heroes of the old future would have given their lives to witness.

Vikir watched the demon’s body dissolve.

Then leaned against the wall.

Barely managing not to collapse.

*…With this, the brains of the Demon Coalition are gone.*

The long war between the demon army and Human Alliance.

The Age of Destruction—

when corpses became mountains and blood gathered into seas—

had ended only in a hollow human victory.

And throughout that war—

the demon that caused the greatest tactical damage to the Human Alliance—

and the most civilian deaths—

had been Belial.

Now both Dantalian and Belial were gone.

The two schemers who had thrown the Human Alliance into chaos.

Vikir felt an enormous weight leave his shoulders.

“Y-you’re about to fall!”

Dolores rushed over and caught him.

Vikir answered shortly.

“More importantly—

it’s almost time.”

“What?”

“Time for what… ah!”

Dolores remembered.

They were still inside the enormous Bourgeois vault.

And the vault had a time limit.

She pulled a pocket watch from beneath a pile of coins.

Her breath caught.

“The doors are about to open!”

The instant they did—

Bourgeois retainers and private soldiers waiting outside would enter.

“As soon as the door opens—

we run.”

“Be ready.”

Dolores nodded.

Damien’s arrangements should have weakened the immediate guard deployment.

But this was still an operation in which they had infiltrated one of the Seven Great Houses—

killed its head—

and now needed to escape.

Vikir looked at Dolores.

“It’ll be better if I take you as a hostage.”

“Understood.”

“If you appear to have kidnapped me—

escaping should be easier.”

The moment the vault opened—

they would need to move at full speed.

But something still bothered Dolores.

“….”

She looked across the gold piles.

At the white-haired girl standing in one corner.

Sinclaire.

She stared blankly into space.

Sssssss—

Belial’s corpse melted away with a foul stench.

As though the demon had never existed.

When the demonic remains vanished completely—

only Bartolomeo’s twisted human corpse remained.

His face—

frozen in fear and pain.

Sinclaire trembled as she looked at it.

“Sinclaire!”

Dolores grabbed her shoulders.

“Get a hold of yourself!”

“That wasn’t the real head of House Bourgeois!”

“You saw it yourself!”

“He was a puppet possessed by a demon!”

“…!”

Focus returned to Sinclaire’s eyes.

Then—

SMACK!

She slapped Dolores’s hands away.

“Don’t come near me!”

“Sinclaire!”

“Believe me!”

“I swear—it wasn’t human!”

“It was a demon!”

Dolores spoke urgently.

“The vault is about to open.”

“A lot of people are going to come in.”

“….”

“Come with me first.”

“I’ll explain everything afterward.”

Sinclaire said nothing.

Her mouth stayed tightly shut.

Dolores sensed something wrong.

Normally—

Sinclaire was polite.

Obedient toward seniors.

Sharp enough never to cause unnecessary trouble.

But now—

she was completely different.

She had just seen a demon manifest.

The head of House Bourgeois had died in front of her.

The rational move—

surely—

was to leave the scene.

Yet Sinclaire refused to go with Night Hound.

Dolores tried one last time.

“Sinclaire.”

“If you stay here alone—

you’ll be in danger.”

“The ones in danger are **you**.”

Sinclaire’s voice shook.

“If I stay and tell them what I saw…”

Still—

she refused.

Her anxiety was obvious.

But so was her resolve.

Then—

SHING.

Vikir drew Beelzebub and stepped forward.

Sinclaire looked at him.

His voice was ice.

“Do whatever you want.”

“I’m the one who killed the head of House Bourgeois.”

“….”

“The Saintess knew nothing.”

“I took her hostage.”

“That’s what the knights will report.”

Sinclaire swallowed.

“Are you going to kill me?”

“If necessary.”

Not a trace of warmth.

Sinclaire shivered.

That voice.

She had heard it before—

during the Academy festival.

*Back away.*

*If you don’t want to be caught up in this and die.*

Cold enough to crawl down the spine.

Vikir approached.

Sinclaire stood frozen.

“….”

He looked down at her pale face.

And thought.

In the future Age of Destruction—

Sinclaire’s name did not appear on any Human Alliance roster.

Yet countless great heroes remembered her.

A genius.

An elite.

An extraordinary person.

A mysterious female student who disappeared from the world after graduation—

and whose name could never be found again.

Should she live?

Or should he eliminate a future uncertainty here?

Vikir’s judgment came quickly.

“You decide.”

His voice broke into short fragments.

“What you saw.”

“What you heard.”

“What you believe.”

Sinclaire’s chest tightened.

What she chose to believe—

was hers.

Vikir turned away.

Then—

CLUNK.

BOOM.

The vault doors opened.

Shouts poured in immediately.

“What happened?!”

“We’ve been attacked!”

“Find the Patriarch!”

“All forces to the vault!”

◆◆◆

BOOM!

Night Hound burst out of the vault.

CLATTER!

CLANG!

Gold and jewels spilled everywhere.

The pursuing soldiers carried swords, bows, and staffs.

Their eyes—

for one crucial instant—

fell toward the treasure scattered across the floor.

That was enough.

Night Hound crossed the outer wall.

Dolores—

apparently unconscious—

was tucked under one arm.

“The Patriarch is dead!”

“The Saintess has been kidnapped too!”

“We’re finished if this continues!”

“Catch him!”

“All forces to the outer wall!”

“At least rescue the Saintess!”

“Don’t touch the treasure!”

“Anyone who pockets even one coin will be punished!”

Despite the chaos—

House Bourgeois responded quickly.

Among those arriving—

was Damien.

He had just returned from the Imperial Money Manufacturing Bureau.

Dolores cracked one eye open as Vikir carried her away.

“Is it really all right to leave Sinclaire behind?”

Vikir shrugged.

“She’s intelligent.”

“She’ll find a way to survive.”

“And if she talks—

we can say the sleeping medicine caused hallucinations.”

“What if she tells them about us?”

“Who would believe her?”

As far as the public knew—

Sinclaire was a first-year student.

A commoner.

No backing.

No family name.

Even if she told everyone exactly what happened inside the vault—

people would probably call her insane.

Sinclaire herself was smart enough to understand that.

Decarabia murmured from Vikir’s chest.

[Hehehe.]

[I don’t think that white-haired girl will talk.]

“When problems come—

she prefers solving them herself rather than relying on adults.”

[Have you considered that personality may cause even more trouble later?]

“I have plans.”

Vikir ran while reorganizing everything he knew about Sinclaire.

*She still has a lot of potential use.*

A cold calculation.

From the standpoint of benefit—

keeping her alive remained the better choice.

Vikir looked down from the wall.

House Bourgeois security truly was formidable.

People flooded in from every direction.

Breaking through them openly would be difficult.

“This is where we separate.”

Using Baby Madam’s thread—

Vikir lowered Dolores to the ground.

“Night Hound…”

Dolores hesitated.

“No.”

“Mr. **Van**.”

“What are you going to do?”

“Call me what you used to.”

“And I have another way out.”

Dolores watched him worriedly.

Vikir jumped down.

The Ring of Andromalius would have made escape simple—

but its dimensional ability was still on cooldown.

So he used the next-best option.

The **Picaresque Mask**.

“Hack, hack—”

In the confusion—

no one noticed the small black dog carefully crawling along the outer wall.

◆◆◆

Inside the vault—

only Damien remained.

He stood over Bartolomeo’s corpse.

Then lowered his head.

“…Brother.”

“What happened to you?”

The demon’s remains were gone.

Only Bartolomeo’s ruined body remained.

A man who had possessed almost unimaginable money and power.

And this—

was his end.

In a place like this—

what meaning did the surrounding mountains of treasure have?

Damien sighed heavily.

After his daughter’s soul wedding—

he had thought nothing could surprise him anymore.

Apparently—

he had been wrong.

“What are demons?”

“What is this Gate?”

“What exactly is happening…”

Then—

“It was you.”

A cold voice behind him.

Sharp as a dagger at the back of his neck.

“You were the one who brought Night Hound and the Saintess into the vault.”

Damien turned slowly.

The tendons stood out on the hand gripping his stiletto.

Then—

“…!”

His eyes widened.

His grip loosened.

His voice shook.

“…Juliet?”

His daughter—

whom he had sent away in tears—

seemed to be standing behind him.

Cold-faced.

Alive.
