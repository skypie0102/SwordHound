Dark clouds withdrew from the horizon.

The sun rose.

Rain weakened.

A double rainbow appeared.

The monsters—

as though some invisible source of strength had vanished—

began fleeing into the sea.

BOOM!

SPLASH!

RUMBLE!

Kraken—

the great enemy that had tormented the Tochka Alliance—

lost its remaining tentacles—

then collapsed beneath the water.

Far away—

the Imperial Palace was falling apart.

“The black fog is gone!”

“Vikir did it!”

“…Of course he did!”

Tudor.

Sancho.

Bianca.

All three shouted with relief.

Then—

Camus frowned.

“Hm.”

“I have a bad feeling.”

She stood atop the corpse of a giant monster.

Mana felt wrong.

A colossal demonic presence had appeared.

Stopped.

Then something weaker—

different—

continued.

And Vikir’s presence—

was fading.

Not life force.

Distance.

Like he was moving somewhere impossibly far away.

“Camus?”

Adolf Morgue stood across the water.

“What is it?”

Camus nodded once.

“…A woman’s intuition.”

She moved.

BOOM!

Camus launched across the water.

Four figures followed.

Aiyen.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Kirko.

Camus looked back—

irritated.

“What the hell?”

“Why are all of you following me?”

Aiyen answered first.

“I’ve been watching the palace.”

“Something feels wrong.”

Dolores followed.

“Vikir may be injured.”

“He needs treatment.”

Sinclaire raised her chin.

“I’m worried about Bro.”

“Even a cat’s paw helps in a crisis.”

Kirko’s answer was flat.

“I came here following him.”

“Is there a problem?”

Then—

SPLASH.

The sea opened.

A vast dark surface rose beneath them.

**Baby Madam.**

She presented her back—

inviting the five women aboard.

Camus grinned.

“Good.”

They climbed on.

Then raced toward the palace.

And soon—

saw something unbelievable.

Pigi.

Alive again.

And Vikir—

covered in blood—

standing before a dimensional gate.

Camus screamed first.

“WHERE DO YOU THINK YOU’RE GOING—!”

A curse nearly escaped.

She swallowed it.

Pressed both hands against her chest.

Breathed.

“No.”

“I fixed my speech.”

“I’m not going back.”

A beat.

“Damn it!”

She pointed at Vikir with a shaking finger.

“WHERE ARE YOU GOING!?”

Vikir stared.

For once—

speechless.

Finally—

“I’m going back.”

“To where I came from.”

“To where I was originally supposed to be.”

“BULLSHIT!”

Camus’s eyes turned as red as her irises.

“Where you belong is beside **me**!”

“Do you still not understand?”

“….”

“Do you always think only about yourself?”

“….”

“Do you ever think about what everyone around you wants?”

Her voice grew hoarse.

“Who do you think I rejected every marriage proposal for?”

“….”

“Who do you think I skipped romance for?”

“Normal school life?”

“My whole youth?”

“I spent it in the jungle because of you!”

“Black magic changed my life because of you!”

“It even made things awkward with Uncle!”

“….”

“I became a Dark Hall senator because of you!”

“I transferred schools looking for you!”

“Then you fell into the Hell Tree!”

“You came back and got arrested!”

“You escaped prison!”

“You started a siege in Tochka!”

“Then you ran into the Imperial Palace!”

She shook with rage.

“You bastard!”

“And you say you aren’t a Baskerville hound!?”

Mana gathered.

Vikir—

in his current condition—

quietly lifted both hands.

Then stepped away from the portal.

Camus’s aura eased.

A little.

Vikir looked at her.

Really looked.

Time had passed.

The little girl he remembered—

was gone.

She had grown.

Completely.

He once assumed her childhood affection would fade.

It had not.

Then Aiyen stepped beside Camus.

“You haven’t forgotten the jungle.”

“Or Nouvelle Vague.”

“….”

“The first time I saw you—

I was inside a slave trader’s cage.”

“When you saved me—

I knew.”

“We would spend our lives together.”

“No fate—

no matter how cruel—

would separate us.”

Aiyen’s face reddened.

“I didn’t forget you during Red Death.”

“I didn’t forget you inside the Hell Tree.”

“I even went into Nouvelle Vague myself to save you.”

Then—

even more red—

“And I’m already married to you.”

“You’ve seen everything.”

“Even me peeing.”

Every face nearby went red.

Dolores’s fingers twitched.

“…I…”

She hesitated.

Then found courage.

“I worked hard too.”

“To become useful to you.”

“I studied soul resonance because I wanted to understand the connection between us.”

“I followed you while hunting demons.”

“I created the Night Walkers to fulfill the mission you left me.”

Her eyes filled with tears.

“I worked so hard…”

“And now you’re leaving without even saying anything.”

“That isn’t fair.”

Beside her—

Sinclaire looked directly at Vikir.

“Bro.”

“Do you remember what you told me?”

“….”

“You said what someone chooses to see—

what they believe—

and what choice they make—

belongs to them.”

“….”

“You were right.”

“So now—

I’m making my own choice.”

She inhaled.

Then said it.

“Date me.”

“Seriously.”

“With marriage in mind.”

“We promised during late-night snacks in the dorm, didn’t we?”

“When you finally accomplished everything you wanted—”

“you’d take me as your bride.”

Camus.

Aiyen.

Dolores.

Their eyes sharpened instantly.

Before the situation exploded—

a fifth voice entered.

Kirko.

“Garam.”

She paused.

“No.”

“Vikir.”

“….”

“I’m still confused.”

“Why you saved me.”

“What Garam was thinking.”

“What all of it meant.”

“….”

“So I want to keep following you.”

“For a while.”

“Until I understand why I’m alive.”

“….”

“I already prepared myself to die once.”

“Without you—

I don’t have much tying me to this world.”

She looked straight at him.

“This may sound shameless.”

“But you saved me.”

“You brought me away from a home that was disappearing.”

“So I think you should take responsibility.”

“Take me with you.”

“To your original world.”

Camus.

Aiyen.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Kirko.

Five women—

all looking at Vikir with the same stubborn resolve.

Vikir spoke hoarsely.

“The world I came from is ruined.”

All five nodded.

“Obviously.”

“Your personality already told us that.”

“What kind of place makes a man this difficult?”

“Vikir, you really should reflect.”

“Bro, do you have character problems?”

“Worse than Nouvelle Vague…”

“Interesting.”

Vikir tried again.

“I’m not a good person.”

“I carry heavy burdens.”

“And…”

“I’m old.”

Again—

all five nodded.

Camus shrugged.

“We know you’re awful.”

“I understand.”

“That’s part of why I love you.”

Aiyen nodded.

“You always did seem old.”

“Your manner.”

“Your speech.”

Dolores looked thoughtful.

“Our Pope said your soul seemed ancient from the beginning.”

“That’s why she called you an old man the first time.”

Sinclaire smiled.

“Speaking of carrying things—”

“Bro.”

“Remember when you kicked me away inside the Hell Tree because I was carrying that giant fist?”

“I remember.”

“I hold grudges.”

“I’ll make you regret kicking me.”

Kirko tilted her head.

“How large an age gap is normal between a man and woman?”

“I’ve never dated.”

“Is fifty years strange?”

They continued upward.

Talking.

Arguing.

Then—

Camus.

Aiyen.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Kirko.

All five stood behind Vikir.

Just as they always had.

They looked through the portal.

At the ruined world.

None stepped back.

Camus spoke.

“I’m not letting you go somewhere like that alone.”

“You remember?”

“We said we’d face even the other side of death together.”

Aiyen nodded.

“I’m going.”

“Maybe people from my tribe are still alive over there.”

Dolores followed.

“I saw your heart inside Chimeries’s Hell Tree.”

“You said we were comrades.”

“So let us share the burden.”

Sinclaire folded her arms.

“Wherever Bro goes—

I’m going too.”

“Don’t even try to ditch me.”

Kirko looked into the old world.

“Interesting.”

“Did Nouvelle Vague collapse over there too?”

“I’d like to see.”

Vikir stared at them.

Then—

sighed.

“…Do whatever you want.”

He turned toward the portal.

The five followed.

Pigi watched all of them.

A faint smile appeared.

“…Good people.”

Then—

the portal began to close.

Vikir jumped into the burning distortion.

Camus.

Aiyen.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Kirko.

After him.

The Baskerville hound.

The hound that walked at night.

He had finished every task placed upon him in this world—

and returned to where he came from.

Sssssssss—

Vision twisted.

Every sense warped.

Clock hands spun backward.

And in that brief, impossible instant—

Pigi’s final words—

from a friend who had once been left behind—

and now was the one seeing Vikir off—

reached him.

*Have a good trip, my friend.*

That—

was the final scene of this world.
