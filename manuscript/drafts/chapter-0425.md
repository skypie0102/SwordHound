# Chapter 425: The Night Walkers (1)

**Tochka.**

A fortress on a high plateau in the northern continent’s Rocks Mountains.

Towering walls.

A deep moat.

Cliffs surrounding it from every direction.

A natural stronghold.

There was a saying that even a little girl could hold Tochka against a hundred soldiers.

And yet—

for generations—

nobody wanted it.

Tochka had another name.

**The Crying Castle.**

The name came from an old story from the Warring States Era.

A young general—

talented and confident in his own military theory—

chose Tochka for a defensive stand.

The walls were almost impossible to breach.

But the fortress had one fatal flaw.

Water.

The northern highlands were dry.

The plateau was almost entirely stone.

There was no reliable drinking source nearby.

The defenders grew thirsty.

Then desperate.

Eventually—

the general opened the gates and fled.

His defeat led his country toward ruin.

The king—

forced by military law to execute a beloved commander—

was said to have wept as he swung the blade.

Thus—

the Crying Castle.

A magnificent fortress.

A terrible place to live.

Dry.

Barren.

Haunted by an old story of failure.

So the world forgot it.

Until now.

◆◆◆

Tochka was no longer empty.

Voices filled the plateau.

Walls were being repaired.

Watchtowers raised.

Cracks filled.

Goats pulled wagons loaded with grain and drinking water.

Tents covered broad stretches inside the fortress.

Refugees had come from every part of the continent.

People driven from their homes by famine.

Drought.

Wildfire.

Monsters.

They had followed the **Night Walkers** to the place called the **Ark**.

The wounded received treatment.

The hungry received food.

There was never enough to waste—

but children no longer curled around empty stomachs.

The people looked exhausted.

They also looked hopeful.

They had left behind villages where neighbors mocked the rumors.

Endured ridicule.

Walked great distances on nothing more than a fragile belief.

At Tochka—

at least—

priests blessed them.

Food was rationed.

Water existed.

Compared with homes where every day meant starvation, bandits, or monsters—

it felt almost like paradise.

Inside one of the medical tents—

dozens of Quovadis priests moved between patients.

A young nun knelt beside a refugee injured while repairing the wall.

Her hands trembled.

“Calm down, Lolita.”

“It’s only a simple healing rite.”

“There’s nothing to fear.”

“So stop shaking.”

The patient blinked.

“Sister…”

“My name isn’t Lolita.”

“I know.”

“Lolita is **my** name.”

“…?”

The novice nun took another breath.

Then a warm hand covered hers.

“Our Lord Rune gladly extends His hand to a lamb crying out in pain.”

Lolita turned.

Then nearly froze.

**Dolores.**

Leader of the Night Walkers.

The **Saint of the Night**.

Her expression was gentle.

Beautiful.

Almost painfully dignified.

Lolita shrank under the pressure.

*Too bright.*

Dolores noticed.

For one instant—

the solemn Saintess disappeared.

A mischievous senior took her place.

She leaned closer and whispered so only Lolita could hear.

“Don’t be stingy with divine power.”

“Use everything you can.”

“That person will pay it all back for us.”

“In a crisis like this—

think of it as a low-interest loan.”

Lolita stared.

“Saintess…”

“Are you allowed to say things like that?”

“Of course.”

Dolores smiled and raised both fists in encouragement.

Lolita finally relaxed.

She prayed.

Holy power gathered.

The injured refugee’s wounds began to close.

Dolores watched with satisfaction.

The young priests who had followed her out of Quovadis were becoming capable healers.

Faith—

she thought—

often bloomed most beautifully at the harshest front.

Then a voice called from outside.

“**Saint of the Night!**”

“Long time no see!”

Dolores looked across the plateau.

Past a herd of grazing goats—

a tall man approached.

Handsome.

Blond.

Black armor.

A ragged traveler’s cloak.

The perfect image of a wandering knight.

Except—

his left arm was gone.

Dolores smiled.

“**Knight of the Night!**”

Tudor had returned.

They had once been senior and junior at Colosseo Academy.

Both had graduated early.

Their shared school life had been brief—

but Dolores still remembered the Tudor from those days.

Bright.

Romantic.

Naturally charismatic.

After Vikir was imprisoned in Nouvelle Vague—

that Tudor began disappearing.

He spoke less.

Trained more.

Studied more.

Then the disaster inside House Donquixote changed him further.

Now his smiles were rare.

And Dolores knew the same hidden grief existed among the others.

**Sancho — Warrior of the Night.**

**Pigi — Gatekeeper of the Night.**

**Bianca — Sniper of the Night.**

All of them had grown up too quickly.

*If I hadn’t met Vikir when I did…*

Dolores sometimes wondered whether she would have ended up like Tudor.

Her own House—

Quovadis—

had been rotting from within.

If Quilt and Humbert had remained untouched—

the civil war might have become even worse.

Or perhaps—

it never would have become a simple human civil war at all.

Seth—

Andromalius—

inside Baskerville.

Seere inside Morgue.

Bartolomeo—

Belial—

inside Bourgeois.

If each demon had seized the family it infiltrated—

the human world would already have become hell.

Thinking of that—

Dolores felt again the impossible weight Vikir had carried.

And the path of thorns he had walked almost alone.

*Then we have to carry his will properly.*

That mattered more than reckless attempts to storm Nouvelle Vague.

A badly timed rescue could sabotage everything Vikir had prepared.

They had to complete the task he left behind first.

“Hey!”

“Knight of the Night!”

Two more men appeared from behind a water wagon.

Sancho.

Pigi.

“Been a while!”

Tudor embraced both.

It had been months since the three had last met.

That was how the Night Walkers operated.

They crossed the continent.

Spread the story of the Ark.

Located displaced people.

Then guided them to Tochka.

**Only here can you escape flood and fire.**

**Only here can true salvation be found.**

That message—

that promise—

came from Vikir’s instructions.

Sancho looked over the now-crowded fortress.

“This place really changed.”

“When we first arrived—

it felt like a ghost town.”

Tudor nodded.

“It’s high.”

“Dry.”

“Barren.”

“And people still trusted us enough to come.”

Pigi nodded too.

But none of them had solved the obvious question.

“Why here?”

“Why did Vikir insist everyone gather at Tochka?”

Even Dolores did not know.

Vikir had been rushed.

There had been no time for a complete explanation.

Still—

everyone knew one thing.

Vikir did not say things without reason.

“I’ve never lost by listening to him.”

“There must be a reason.”

“Then we keep going.”

“Until we find a way to bring him back—

this is what we can do.”

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

All three hardened their resolve again.

Dolores watched them.

Then a curtain lifted from a large command tent.

“Executives.”

“Come inside.”

“We need to discuss something.”

Everyone turned.

A woman stood there.

The **Wealthy of the Night**.

The financial foundation of the Night Walkers.

To a very small circle—

also known simply as Vikir’s **rich friend**.

She had paid to transform an abandoned fortress into a defensible settlement.

Paid to purchase food.

Water.

Medicine.

Construction materials.

Mercenaries.

Transport.

Enough wealth to rival the Empire’s great financial nobles.

She had also served as adviser to Colosseo’s old investment club Oracle.

**Messinadnaro Cindywendy.**

Now—

**Countess Cindywendy Baskerville**.

She looked down at the group.

Her expression—

unusually grave.

“…It’s urgent.”
