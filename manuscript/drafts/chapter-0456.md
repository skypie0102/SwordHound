# Chapter 456: Infiltration of the Water Source (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/456.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 454 is the verified shifted secondary witness. The Chinese raw visibly drops the detailed **Eightfold Formation** passage after Sade opens the gate; aligned E454 securely restores only that localized missing material. Established project terminology controls Marquis de Sade, Warden Orca, Camus Morgue, Sinclaire, Tochka, Poison Humans, Red Death, Night Walkers, MiniPin, Chihuahua, and the Warring States Era.

With Warden Orca taking command—

House Leviathan’s siege lost momentum.

That created the opening for someone who specialized in the exact opposite kind of warfare.

“Hehehehehe…”

**Marquis de Sade.**

His eyes shone.

Almost feverishly.

“So.”

“Is it my turn now?”

He looked back at Orca.

“About forty years ago—

my attacks gave you a terrible time, didn’t they?”

“I’d like to use that strategy again.”

Orca snorted.

“And my defense stopped you.”

“That’s why you ended up captured.”

“We were massively outnumbered, old man.”

The two men had spent much of their lives fighting each other.

Now—

they were on the same side.

Marquis de Sade possessed a near-genius instinct for causing suffering.

Forty years earlier—

with an alliance of only forty-seven fallen families—

he had challenged the entire Empire.

And nearly taken the Emperor’s head.

He was also the heir to the old Marquis de Sade line.

A symbol for survivors and descendants of kingdoms that had been defeated—

absorbed—

or erased during Imperial unification.

“Marquis de Sade!”

“We follow you!”

“Command us!”

“We’ll die with you!”

“The old glory returns!”

Even inside the same alliance—

troops descended from the defeated states had never mixed comfortably with the others.

Under Sade’s name—

they united immediately.

Vikir watched.

*Saving him really was worth it.*

Before regression—

during one of the Human Alliance’s worst defensive campaigns—

Orca had once muttered:

*If that bastard Sade were still alive—*

*we wouldn’t be this helpless.*

Sade possessed a military mind comparable—

in its own way—

to the strategic cunning of Dantalian and Belial.

If he had survived into the Age of Destruction—

he would have perfectly covered Orca’s greatest weakness.

Orca knew defense.

Sade knew attack.

If even Orca—

Sade’s lifelong enemy—

had regretted his absence—

then Sade was worth extracting from Nouvelle Vague.

“Hehehehehe!”

“I thought the Warring States Era was over!”

“I thought I’d never see such a delightful war again!”

“But here we are!”

“Live long enough—

and good things really do happen!”

The old spirit of that era—

**the strong survive**—

seemed embodied in Sade himself.

He raised his whip.

“Come!”

“Brave young ones!”

“Follow me!”

A fanatical roar answered.

The gates of Tochka opened.

Sade led his soldiers out.

CRACK!

His whip moved like a giant serpent.

Poison Humans within dozens of meters were torn apart.

“Hehehe!”

“Kill them all!”

Sade divided his followers into eight units.

The **Eightfold Formation**—

once used against House Usher—

had been altered again.

The **Life Gate**.

The **Scenery Gate**.

The **Open Gate**.

Routes that normally preserved life.

The **Injury Gate**.

The **Shock Gate**.

The **Rest Gate**.

Routes of severe injury.

The **Obstruction Gate**.

The **Death Gate**.

Routes from which survival was nearly impossible.

But under Sade’s laughing redesign—

there was no longer any path of life.

Every gate—

was a death gate.

A sealed jar of malice.

No exit.

Spearmen advanced according to the formation.

Shield-bearers followed.

Archers shifted behind them.

All changing position—

whenever Sade’s whip moved.

Tudor, Pigi, Sancho, and Bianca watched from the walls.

“People can command soldiers like that?”

“That’s nothing like ordinary tactics.”

“He’s forcing them into positions where they have no room to retreat.”

“…Why is he more poisonous than the Poison Humans?”

Traditional local wars had rules.

First—

arguments over legitimacy and cause.

Then declaration.

A fixed time.

A fixed battlefield.

Fighting during daylight.

Withdrawal at night.

Warriors challenged one another.

Armies often divided into offensive right wings and defensive left wings.

Break the opponent’s left—

win.

Casualties were kept relatively low.

Prisoners had value.

Slavery.

Ransom.

That was the unspoken ethic of ordinary war.

Sade rejected all of it.

“Kill everything!”

“And you lot—

die too if you have to!”

“Hehehehehe!”

“If anyone fights timidly—

I’ll cut off his head myself!”

His mercenary tactics drove soldiers to lethal efficiency.

CRACK!

SLASH!

On the front line—

his whip reduced Poison Humans to chunks of meat.

Even demons—

it seemed—

might have fought with more restraint.

The younger Night Walkers understood something while watching him.

Against House Leviathan—

against demons—

the old rules of war did not apply.

The enemy was not human.

That realization—

simple but psychologically difficult—

settled into them.

Orca clicked his tongue.

“…Still insane.”

“No.”

“You got worse in solitary confinement.”

He gripped his club.

“Wouldn’t mind if the Poison Humans killed you.”

“But unfortunately—

you’re still more human than they are.”

“Rear guard!”

“Support him!”

Orca charged out as well.

Troops from the allied houses—

and refugee vigilantes—

followed through the gates.

RUMBLE!

Iron spikes erupted beside Sade.

Camus smiled over the impaled Poison Humans.

“Old man.”

“I like the way you fight.”

“Want to teach me?”

Elsewhere—

BOOM!

An entire vein of gold ripped out of the earth.

A massive golden hand smashed down.

Sinclaire stood opposite Camus.

“Keep pressing while they’re off balance!”

Flaming arrows burned through Red Death mist.

Spears.

Arrows.

Magic.

The allied forces pushed.

Then—

“Hehehe!”

“That’s enough.”

“They’re retreating.”

“If we chase farther—

their reinforcements will counterattack.”

“Back!”

Sade called the retreat at exactly the right moment.

Camus reluctantly withdrew her magic.

Sinclaire stopped just as cleanly.

More Poison Humans arrived—

but every allied soldier was already back inside Tochka.

◆◆◆

The opening battle ended in Tochka’s favor.

Orca’s preparation.

Decades of defensive experience.

Sade’s horrifying counterattack.

And—

just as importantly—

his timing in withdrawing.

But the real war had not begun.

The priests maintaining sacred protection against the Red Death—

were exhausted.

Tomorrow—

they might not be able to repel the plague at all.

The soldiers had also consumed much more water than expected.

By the next day—

water and holy power would both be dangerously low.

Dolores returned from inspecting the walls.

“They’re trying to dry us out.”

“The siege lines are layered.”

“And I think they’ll choose clear weather for the real attack.”

At this rate—

Tochka might not last three days.

So Vikir made the decision.

“We go to the water source.”

He would leave the fortress defense to Orca and Sade—

and personally create the channel from the supposed volcanic lake.

MiniPin handed over a map marked with several Xs.

“These are the places I think should be blasted.”

“If the terrain estimate is right—

water should run down near Tochka’s moat.”

Chihuahua still looked worried.

“Leviathan’s Poison Humans are everywhere.”

“Breaking through the encirclement is practically impossible.”

“The situation has changed this much.”

“Maybe we should abandon the water-source plan.”

“No.”

Vikir answered immediately.

“We might endure.”

“The ordinary refugees cannot.”

“We need that water.”

There was no alternative.

An expedition was assembled.

Orca and Sade had to remain with the fortress.

The rest—

Vikir’s trusted fighters—

gathered.

**Camus Morgue.**

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

**Sinclaire Bourgeois.**

**Tudor Donquixote.**

**Bianca Usher.**

**Sancho Barataria.**

**Pigi.**

With **Vikir Van Baskerville**—

eight people.

Their objective—

reach the volcanic lake on the opposing peak.

Blast open the rock.

Bring water to Tochka.

“Moving with an army would be suicide.”

“We can’t empty the fortress either.”

“So we use a small team.”

“People I trust.”

Everyone nodded.

Night—

would cover the infiltration.

Tudor adjusted the explosives he was carrying.

“So.”

“When do we leave?”

Everyone looked at Vikir.

Vikir looked up at the dark sky.

“Now.”

“Immediately.”
