# Chapter 39: Sponsored (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/039.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 39 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Current indexed Fandom independently confirms **Cindywendy** as first appearing in Chapter 39 and as the sole surviving member of the **Messinadnaro Family**. The Chinese/recovered-English reveal sequence controls the alias and family identity used here; no later-life information is imported.

Late at night, before the moon had risen, Vikir descended into the dungeon beneath City Hall.

The nearly empty prison reeked of decay.

A dungeon was unpleasant even when crowded.

Now, with darkness filling rows of empty cells, it felt even more unsettling.

The guards had all been sent home early.

No one had been assigned to the night shift.

Every prisoner had either been executed, pardoned, or transferred.

Almost every cell stood empty.

Vikir walked to the deepest part of the prison.

The darkest place.

The place where the stench was strongest.

Solitary confinement.

Only one door at the farthest end remained locked.

One prisoner had deliberately been left behind.

She was the woman who had served as secretary to Baron Gambino, one of the largest figures in Underdog City’s underground economy.

Her short hair was blond with a greenish tint.

She wore a prison uniform and heavy restraints and sat crouched in the corner of the cell.

Vikir dragged over a chair and sat before the iron bars.

Screech—

The iron legs scraped over stone, the sound carrying through the empty prison.

“Your name is... Sen Rose Cindywendy. Correct?”

She did not answer.

Her empty eyes remained fixed on the floor.

“Why did you keep me alive?”

Her voice was hollow.

The Freak Show.

The Pit Bull Knights.

On the night when anyone who moved was cut down, Cindywendy had desperately tried to escape.

She could not die there.

Nor could she afford to survive with her wrists and ankles severed.

There was something she still had to accomplish.

She had believed herself more desperate than anyone else in that hall.

But desperation meant little before a Pit Bull Knight’s blade.

One of the knights had blocked her path.

The red edge had flashed toward her neck.

Clang!

It never reached her.

Vikir had caught the strike on a bundle of chains.

The knight stared at him in surprise, then immediately bowed.

“This woman is an exception. Do not injure her. Take her alive.”

That was how Cindywendy had ended up alone in the underground prison.

Vikir watched her in silence for a while.

Then he spoke.

“If you answer my questions honestly, I will let you out.”

Her ears twitched.

But a moment later she lowered her head again.

“I lost the desire to escape after I was locked in here. There’s no reason anymore.”

Vikir began questioning her anyway.

“Baron Gambino used to be an unimpressive libertine. Was his sudden rise in wealth and influence your doing?”

“...”

“I investigated you. You have an exceptional talent for making money. Had you used it outside the black market, you could have become an outstanding merchant or financier.”

Cindywendy gave a faint, bitter laugh.

What use was that now?

Vikir looked directly into her eyes.

“I guarantee one thing.”

“...?”

“Answer my questions, and I will release you. But—”

He tilted his head, red eyes meeting green through the bars.

“If you refuse to answer, you will regret it later.”

There was something strange in his quiet voice.

Not merely fear.

A pressure she could not define.

“If I can answer, I will.”

“Good.”

Vikir went straight to the point.

“What do you think?”

“About what?”

“Do you think the way I have enforced the law is wrong?”

Cindywendy answered calmly.

“Why would the opinion of an insignificant female criminal like me matter?”

“I am asking that insignificant female criminal.”

“Then I’ll be frank. I think you are wrong.”

Vikir nodded.

“Why?”

“In every theory of rulership, the central virtue of a great administrator is virtue itself. Harsh rule by law may work in the beginning, but in the long term it has limits.”

“Virtue? What do you mean by that?”

“A virtuous ruler does not ride simply because he is tired. He does not hide beneath a canopy merely because the weather is hot. He does not surround himself with armed processions wherever he goes. When such a ruler dies, the citizens weep. Children stop singing. Even laborers at their work fall silent. That is virtue.”

“So lacking virtue is a serious flaw?”

“A very serious one. It means you may not live long. Severe laws can crush petty villains and establish authority for a time, but resentment accumulates.”

“You think I should fear the resentment of commoners?”

“Not only commoners. House Baskerville may dislike what you are becoming as well. This is only Underdog City, yet its citizens now fear the Deputy Magistrate more than they fear the Baskervilles themselves.”

“True.”

“Your superior or successor will face the same problem. You changed laws rapidly, asserted your authority, and accumulated achievements. Local powers resent you for it. Criminal organizations in other cities will resent you too. If you continue this way, I doubt you will live very long.”

“Then what should I do?”

“You are walking on thin ice. If you want to survive, step down as Deputy Magistrate. Return the authority you were given to House Baskerville. Give the credit for what happened here to the Patriarch and the Imperial Family. Then go somewhere appropriate for your age—the frontier, perhaps, or an academy in the Imperial Capital—and prepare for your future.”

“And?”

“Keep a low profile. Hide your strength. Study. Find talented people no one has noticed and raise them up. Respect the elderly. Care for orphans. Recognize those whose achievements were ignored. Encourage your superiors to honor people of virtue.”

“What do I gain from that?”

“Peace of mind, for one thing. House Baskerville’s Patriarch will absorb the blame that otherwise belongs to you. Even if you surrender your immediate achievements, you are only fifteen. Your name has already spread far enough.”

A rare smile appeared on Vikir’s face.

Her advice matched his own future plans remarkably well.

“Good.”

He nodded.

Clang!

The cell door opened.

Vikir tore the lock apart with his bare hands and removed Cindywendy’s restraints.

“As promised, you are free.”

“...”

She stared at the open door.

Then at Vikir.

“You’re really letting me go?”

“Yes. I keep my promises.”

“You’ll regret it.”

A little life returned to her eyes.

Vikir smiled.

“I hope so.”

“...?”

Cindywendy tilted her head.

“You gave me useful advice,” Vikir said. “So I’ll give you some in return.”

She had just stepped out of the cell when she stopped.

Vikir spoke behind her.

“There were seven famous local families in Underdog City. Montblanc, Pierre, Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Ferragamo, Hermes, and Prada. They are the families that were destroyed this time.”

“...”

“But a few years ago, there were eight.”

Her body trembled almost imperceptibly.

“The eighth was the Messinadnaro Family. They were the wealthiest merchant family in the city.”

“...”

“They were annihilated overnight. Every member of the household died horribly. Do you know why?”

Silence.

“They were accused of stealing House Baskerville’s swordsmanship manual. Their eight-year-old son had somehow learned excellent swordsmanship, and the family was proud of him.”

“...”

“On the boy’s birthday, the Messinadnaro Family held a feast and asked him to demonstrate what he had learned.”

“...Enough.”

Cindywendy raised a hand.

Vikir continued.

“In front of everyone, the boy performed the technique. The guests were horrified. What he used was Baskerville swordsmanship—military knowledge taught only inside the clan.”

“Enough.”

“Word reached the Baskervilles immediately. The clan treats its swordsmanship as a top-level military secret. The hounds were unleashed, and that very day the Messinadnaro Family vanished from the world.”

“Shut up, you bastard!”

Her scream echoed through the dungeon.

Vikir did not stop.

“Later, the truth came out. The Messinadnaros had done nothing wrong. The children of the other seven families had engineered the entire incident. They manipulated a Baskerville child into stealing a swordsmanship manual, taught its contents to the Messinadnaro boy, then arranged for the innocent child to display it before adults.”

“...Stop. Please.”

“But there was one survivor.”

Cindywendy froze.

“A girl younger than the boy. The seven perpetrators secretly removed her from the massacre.”

She staggered into the wall.

Her eyes turned red.

“Because she was clever and beautiful, they subjected her to all kinds of cruel, degrading, and humiliating abuse. The details are not something I care to repeat.”

“I don’t want to hear any more.”

A bloody tear slid from one of Cindywendy’s eyes.

Vikir’s voice remained steady.

“The girl now calls herself Sen Rose Cindywendy.”

Cindywendy stopped breathing.

“Her real identity is Messinadnaro Sen Cindywendy—the sole survivor of that family.”

Her face went blank.

Then she doubled over and vomited.

The darkness spun around her.

The rotten stench filling the isolated cell seemed stronger than before.

She braced herself against the wall.

“How am I supposed to believe you?”

Vikir said nothing.

“You expect me to believe you captured them, tortured them, learned the truth, and even forced them to apologize?”

Still no answer.

Fwoosh!

Vikir lit the torch in his hand.

The cell suddenly filled with light.

Cindywendy’s eyes widened.

Seven headless corpses knelt in one corner.

Their fingers had been worn down until almost nothing remained.

Blood covered the floor, the walls, even the ceiling.

At first the entire chamber looked simply red.

Then she realized the red surface was made of words layered over words.

- Please forgive us. Kill us if you must, but do not hurt our families...

- We were the ones who destroyed the Messinadnaro Family. Please forgive our crime...

- We sincerely apologize to Lady Cindywendy. We committed a mortal sin...

- We were wrong. We were wrong. We were wrong...

- Spare us. Spare us. Spare us...

- I don’t want to die...

- I’m scared. I’m scared. I’m scared...

The corpses reeked of decay.

The apologies they had written with their own blood covered the room.

Cindywendy stood in the middle of that red chamber and stared at Vikir.

“But the Messinadnaro Family’s revenge is not complete,” he said.

“...”

“One family remains.”

His meaning was obvious.

House Baskerville.

“Blame the Baskervilles. You have every right to.”

“...”

“And separately from that, I will say this on behalf of every Baskerville.”

Vikir slowly bowed before her.

“I’m sorry.”

Drop.

Drop.

Tears struck the stone floor.

Neither of them moved for a while.

At last Cindywendy spoke.

“You’re a Baskerville too. Why are you helping me?”

Vikir did not answer.

Cindywendy was clever enough to understand his silence.

His hatred of the Baskervilles was no less real simply because he bore their name.

In that one respect, the two understood each other perfectly.

Cindywendy left the dungeon.

At the doorway she looked back at Vikir and spoke in a voice calmer than any she had used that night.

“No matter what you do with your life, I’ll make sure you never run out of money.”

At that moment, Vikir gained a powerful sponsor.
