# Chapter 112: The Great Banquet (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/112.txt` — `47db9853101379f74f5e62e85fad4f4ef3e65498`. Recovered English Chapter 111 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Hugo Le Baskerville, Boston Terrier Le Baskerville, Great Dane Le Baskerville, Pit Bull Knights, Mastiff Knights, Seven Counts, Sword Graduator, and Club Burning Suspension. The family keeps Vikir's public realm at Intermediate Sword Graduator even though his hidden strength is higher.

The setting sun burned across the tips of Baskerville's spires.

Night fell.

The Great Banquet began.

Vikir entered the deep inner castle, an area reserved for those who had rendered major service to the main family—especially direct descendants entrusted with important positions.

The first floor held the banquet hall.

A vast chamber carved from black stone.

It was not luxurious.

It was not glittering.

But it was magnificent.

Severe Gothic lines climbed the walls and ceiling, filling the room with a cold, almost funereal atmosphere.

It did not feel like a place where a family gathered to eat.

The servants only reinforced that impression.

Men and women alike wore pale, expressionless faces.

They moved like dolls.

Food arrived.

Utensils were arranged.

Then, whenever there was nothing to do, the servants returned to the walls and stood perfectly still until called.

At the center sat an enormous circular table.

Several people were already eating.

Hard angles in the black stone cast long shadows across the chamber.

At the far end sat Hugo.

Hugo Le Baskerville.

Patriarch of the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan.

He cut his meat.

Chewed.

Swallowed.

Again.

Again.

Not once did he speak.

Two men seated far apart on either side of him were far less quiet.

Boston Terrier Le Baskerville.

Great Dane Le Baskerville.

Commanders of the **Pit Bull Knights** and **Mastiff Knights**.

They had been glaring at each other since they entered the room.

“Ahem.”

Boston Terrier twirled his fork.

Then frowned.

“Isn't this meat a little undercooked?”

Every fork and knife at the table stopped.

Boston stabbed the steak in front of him.

Blood dripped onto the plate.

The servants went pale.

The head chef turned white as paper.

Hugo looked over.

“Boston. Is the meat not to your taste?”

Boston Terrier shook his head so quickly that the scars across his face shifted.

“N-no, Brother. Mine is fine. I meant the meat across from me.”

Everyone turned.

A huge man sat opposite him.

Great Dane.

He silently cut a piece from a bloody steak, chewed it, and swallowed.

“…What is your complaint?”

Boston smiled.

“Nothing. I simply thought the meat was too raw. I recommend cooking it longer.”

“…This is exactly how I like it.”

“Ah. Of course.”

Boston's smile showed teeth.

“You always like things raw. Meat. New recruits.”

Great Dane's knife stopped.

“…What did you say I eat raw?”

“Oh? You heard that? Good ears.”

“Say it again.”

“…Perhaps not such good ears after all.”

Boston laughed.

Great Dane's mouth became a thin line.

The cold in the hall deepened.

Boston leaned forward.

“Eat your raw meat. Get sick and die if you want. Just don't snatch new recruits before they're ready.”

“I don't know what you're talking about. Must you behave this crudely at the Great Banquet?”

“I know exactly why you're here.”

Boston finally stopped pretending.

“You want to drag my adorable nephew into that rotten Mastiff order.”

Great Dane calmly wiped his mouth.

“I'll accept *adorable nephew*. The problem is *my*.”

His eyes narrowed.

“Vikir is your nephew and mine. As his uncle, I have every right to guide him toward the proper path.”

“Throwing him into that rotten Mastiff kennel is the proper path?”

“Watch your words. It is certainly better than sending him to the dull-witted Pit Bulls.”

“Careful. Keep talking and you'll get bitten.”

“I can only pity puppies that bark without knowing friend from foe.”

“And I pity animals with big bodies and empty heads.”

The two Counts bared their teeth across the table.

All because both wanted Vikir.

“…”

Vikir ate quietly between them.

He had once commanded the Pit Bull Knights for a short period, so he knew exactly what that order could do.

One hundred Sword Graduators.

**Extermination specialists.**

Among the most vicious knight orders in Baskerville—and perhaps in the entire Empire.

When Vikir served as Deputy Magistrate of Underdog City, the underground slave auction had gathered wanted criminals from across the empire.

The Pit Bull Knights had destroyed the organization in half a day.

Its leaders died.

Its criminal network collapsed.

And Club Burning Suspension, a den of luxury, vice, and crime, burned to the ground.

Vikir had directed the operation.

That was one reason Boston Terrier now looked at him with such affection.

“Nephew. Come with me.”

Boston chewed a piece of dripping meat and tried to smile.

The servants around him visibly trembled.

“I'll make you the strongest Pit Bull this family has ever seen.”

Great Dane immediately cut in.

“Ahem. The Mastiff Knights are also an excellent choice, nephew.”

His expression remained solemn.

“Not because I command them. I recommend them because they are genuinely excellent.”

The **Mastiff Knights** were every bit as elite as their rivals.

They also numbered one hundred Sword Graduators.

But where the Pit Bulls were extermination specialists who spread terror through overwhelming slaughter and were willing to use assassination—

the Mastiffs were **war specialists**.

They moved after formal declarations or duels.

They met an enemy openly.

Then broke that enemy until it submitted.

Both orders spilled blood whenever they deployed.

Their methods were simply different.

In overall combat reputation, they stood close enough for their commanders' rivalry to burn constantly.

“Look at them,” Boston growled. “They need paperwork before they're allowed to show their teeth. And now they want to ruin my nephew's future. Do you people have consciences?”

“Perhaps yours was exiled to the northern sea,” Great Dane replied. “Killing only through filthy tricks is not skill. Don't stain my nephew's chivalry.”

“Filthy tricks? Says the man dressed like a polished display suit.”

“At least we do not strut around like rabid dogs and call it virtue.”

Again—

all for Vikir.

Recruiting the family's newest rising star would bring prestige.

His existing achievements would become part of the order's legend.

His future would strengthen theirs.

And publicly, Vikir was a seventeen-year-old **Intermediate Sword Graduator**.

Baskervilles worshiped strength.

The two Counts were no exception.

Neither were the five Counts unable to attend.

…But Vikir had no intention of joining either order.

*I'm planning to swallow them all.*

Not two.

All seven great knight orders of Baskerville.

Bring them beneath his feet.

Then the clan itself.

It was a plan he had refined through all seventeen years since regression.

For now, though, the bickering was tedious.

Boston and Great Dane only became louder.

Vikir finally looked toward Hugo.

The host.

The Patriarch.

Surely he would end it.

…Instead—

“?”

Vikir stared.

Hugo was watching his two half-brothers argue.

And smiling.
