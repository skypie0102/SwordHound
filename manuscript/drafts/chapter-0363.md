# Chapter 363: Ghost Castle of the Ultra-Deep Sea (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/363.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 361 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Decarabia, Nouvelle Vague, BDISSEM, and the Seven Great Families. Garam Nord / Sakkuth de Leviathan / Gate of Good and Evil are source-local aligned-witness forms pending direct Fandom access.

It was the coldest place.

The hottest.

The roughest.

The filthiest.

The cruelest.

A place cut off forever from the sunlit world.

A hell that words and pictures could never properly describe.

To understand Nouvelle Vague—

one had to breathe its air.

People of the world.

Do not sin.

And if you already have—

find a way to repent.

Because at the bottom of the abyss—

the Great Prison of the Ultra-Deep Sea waited with its mouth open.

◆◆◆

At the bottom of a trench beyond ordinary depth—

black seaweed swayed.

Above it—

an ancient castle rose from the summit of a dormant volcano.

Dim yellow light glowed like the lure of a deep-sea creature.

Huge cubic stones formed walls that looked older than the Empire itself.

**Nouvelle Vague.**

THUD.

Vikir’s Iron Maiden landed on the castle’s highest point.

A gigantic circular gate.

Through Decarabia’s shared vision—

Vikir examined it.

*The Gate of Good and Evil.*

The circular door was tens of meters across.

Its material resembled the adamantium-orichalcon alloy used in Bourgeois vault construction.

Left half—

white.

Right half—

black.

The last boundary separating good from evil.

Nouvelle Vague’s front gate.

And its symbol.

CREAK.

The huge circle rotated one hundred eighty degrees.

Like an enormous manhole cover turning beneath the sea.

Vikir’s coffin—

along with the surrounding water—

was pulled inward.

Below the gate—

a transparent, viscous membrane prevented the sea from flooding the castle.

Similar membranes covered the windows.

BOOM.

SPLASH.

The coffin struck solid ground.

By now—

the Iron Maiden had deformed until it hugged Vikir’s body like armor.

Rough hands twisted the lid open.

“…Arrival.”

Vikir lifted his head.

Nouvelle Vague’s interior spread before him.

Dark blue floor.

Walls.

Ceiling.

Cold.

Wet.

Oppressive.

Black-uniformed guards watched from beneath low-brimmed hats.

The first guard was bald.

His face carried a large burn scar.

A nameplate hung on his chest.

**Garam Nord.**

One diamond on the shoulder.

Lieutenant.

“…Night Hound.”

“You’re the final arrival?”

Garam looked down at Vikir.

“Admission ceremony begins shortly.”

“Change into prison clothes.”

Vikir looked beyond him.

Several prisoners waited in striped black-and-white uniforms.

Every wrist and ankle—

BDISSEM.

Garam addressed them.

“You’ve probably heard rumors.”

“I’ll explain the structure simply.”

Nouvelle Vague looked like a castle built on a dormant volcano.

In reality—

most of it extended downward.

A vast vertical prison—

excavated through the mountain’s interior.

“There are nine official zones.”

**Level One.**

Guard living quarters.

Admission and promotion ceremonies.

And prisoners serving less than three years.

**Level Two.**

Sentences under five years.

One year in Nouvelle Vague—

was treated like ten years in an ordinary surface prison.

So inmates here would normally deserve thirty to fifty years elsewhere.

**Level Three.**

Sentences under ten years.

Equivalent to roughly a century of ordinary imprisonment.

**Level Four.**

Sentences under fifteen years.

From here upward—

ordinary criminals disappeared.

City-scale terror.

Mass killing.

War crimes.

**Level Five.**

Sentences under twenty years.

People who planned national rebellion or large-scale attacks.

Senior guard quarters.

Special facilities.

The slaughterhouse.

Execution grounds.

**Level Six.**

Life imprisonment.

People considered dangerous enough to threaten a nation.

From this point—

the prison no longer treated inmates as people.

Only monsters.

**Level Seven.**

Multiple life sentences.

At least one hundred.

**Level Eight.**

Up to one thousand life sentences.

And finally—

**Level Nine.**

Garam stopped there.

The other prisoners swallowed.

Then—

began comparing crimes.

“Eight years.”

“Thirteen.”

“A whole life sentence.”

“Hah.”

“You think that’s impressive?”

“I got three life sentences.”

They measured themselves by brutality.

By sentence.

By reputation.

Then one prisoner drew every eye.

Huge.

Covered in dark red blotches.

A face almost demonic.

**Sakkuth de Leviathan.**

Known on the surface as the **Plague Leper**.

Even House Leviathan—

famous for poison—

had expelled him for excessive human experimentation.

His bounty had once reached eleven digits.

He grinned.

“Children.”

“Ever hear of the Red Death?”

“I made it.”

His danger had earned him—

**666 life sentences.**

For now—

he waited at Level One for admission processing.

He did not belong there.

Sakkuth continued.

“I even completed clinical trials on western indigenous people.”

“Then those hymn-singing Quovadis insects interfered.”

“What a waste.”

“It could have become the greatest epidemic in history.”

He coughed.

Other inmates moved away.

So did guards.

Not only because of his reputation.

Because of disease.

Then Sakkuth revealed something stranger.

“I surrendered voluntarily.”

“I came here to meet **that person**.”

“So don’t compare me to trash dragged here in chains.”

Even the guards avoided challenging him.

Garam ignored the boasting.

He explained the admission procedure.

“Three stages.”

“One.”

“Belongings inspection.”

“Two.”

“Health examination.”

“Three.”

“Bath.”

The prisoners visibly relaxed.

“A search?”

“We can’t bring weapons here anyway.”

“A health exam?”

“This place has medical care?”

“And a bath?”

“Nouvelle Vague sounds better than the rumors.”

“Good.”

“I still have salt crusted all over me from the trip.”

Vikir said nothing.

*Naive.*

He already knew.

In Nouvelle Vague—

ordinary words rarely meant ordinary things.

Belongings inspection.

Health examination.

Bath.

The admission ceremony—

had begun.
