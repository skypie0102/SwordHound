# Chapter 108: The Returned Hero (4)

Vikir rode toward Underdog City.

He had once been appointed Deputy Magistrate and worked there.

As his horse carried him through the streets, he studied the city he had left behind.

Public order had improved considerably.

There were far fewer vagrants and drunks.

The roads and alleys were cleaner.

One thing, however, was strange.

He could barely see any children.

Ordinarily they would have been running through the alleys and playing in the street.

Now the alleys were empty.

The few children Vikir did see appeared to be running errands, and every one of them had an adult beside them.

Otherwise, they hurried home as though something frightening waited outside.

“…?”

Vikir tilted his head and continued toward City Hall.

Soon the familiar main entrance came into view.

This was his first time back since leaving temporarily to seek a cure for the Red Death.

A plaque still hung prominently outside.

**Trust Won by Moving the Stake**

Chihuahua’s handiwork.

It commemorated the old incident in which Vikir had burned ten billion gold over a single wooden stake.

Inside City Hall, employees hurried in every direction.

Vikir dismounted without making a fuss and passed through them.

He had come to see Chihuahua Baskerville.

In front of the Deputy Magistrate’s office, a young woman was buried in paperwork.

Vikir did not recognize her.

A new employee, apparently.

Tap, tap.

He knocked on her desk.

She was so busy that she only frowned.

Vikir knocked again.

Tap, tap.

Still nothing.

“Take a number and wait.”

Her voice was perfectly bureaucratic.

“…”

Vikir considered the situation, then nodded.

He was officially listed as missing.

Strictly speaking, he was not currently the Deputy Magistrate.

Following procedure was reasonable.

It was also the best way to avoid creating a disturbance.

He took a ticket.

**Waiting Number 3021**

Then he sat down.

“…”

No one noticed him.

Every passing employee had their face buried in documents.

Hours passed.

Eventually his turn approached.

Customer 3020 finished and left.

Vikir had just risen when—

Bang!

A nearby desk shook.

A male employee had leaped to his feet.

“Message from Red Salt Castle! Lord Vikir is alive!”

“What!? Really!?”

“It’s true! The fortress commanders of Morgue sent it themselves! He has already left Red Salt Castle and is coming this way!”

Chaos erupted.

The entire City Hall seemed to boil.

“Lord Vikir is alive!”

“The hero has returned! Contact Baskerville’s main house!”

“He’s coming here! Roll out the red carpet!”

“We only have a white carpet! What do we do!?”

“Spill coffee on it and turn it red! We have to show him everything we’ve got!”

Employees immediately abandoned whatever important work they had been doing.

The clerk in front of Vikir was no exception.

“What do we do? We have to prepare for Lord Vikir right now!”

She tossed aside ticket 3021 and tried to rush away.

Vikir stepped in front of her.

“Wait. Handle my business first.”

“If it’s urgent, submit the paperwork! I’ll process it first thing tomorrow morning!”

“That will be too late.”

The clerk stared at him as though he were insane.

“I’m sorry, but I can’t receive anyone right now! An honored guest is coming. A *very* important guest. Whatever your business is, under the circumstances it will have to wait—”

“I need to see the Director.”

She paused at Chihuahua Baskerville’s title.

Someone who could ask directly for the Director could not be dismissed quite so casually.

Grumbling, she gathered a form.

“Honestly. Lord Vikir is on his way, and now there’s another appointment… The Director should tell us these things in advance. Then we could cancel them faster.”

She glanced enviously at coworkers preparing for Vikir’s arrival and shoved the document across the desk.

“Write your name and purpose of visit here. Quickly, please!”

Vikir obeyed.

**Name: Vikir Van Baskerville**

**Purpose of visit: Reporting survival**

The clerk snatched the paper from his hand.

After dealing with complaints all day, nobles no longer intimidated her.

She did not even look at the form before knocking on Chihuahua’s door.

“Director Chihuahua! You have a visitor!”

His voice came from inside.

“What? I don’t have any appointments today.”

“I know, but the visitor is already here!”

“I said I don’t have any appointments! Besides, we’re busy. Send them home. They’ll understand.”

The clerk had clearly expected that answer.

Still without reading the form, she turned to Vikir and sighed.

“The Director has no meetings scheduled and cannot take anyone because of the emergency. Next time, please make an appointment. Your name was… right, Mr. Vikir. Please contact us in advance from now on—”

She stopped.

Her entire body went rigid.

Slowly, she adjusted her glasses and looked down.

The letters on customer 3021’s form were perfectly clear.

**Name: Vikir Van Baskerville**

**Purpose of visit: Reporting survival**

A coworker noticed her frozen expression and peered over her shoulder.

“Hey, why aren’t you helping with the carpet— Ah!”

A supervisor came over.

“Don’t block the way— Ah!”

Another employee joined them.

“What are you staring at— Huh!”

One by one, people gathered around the clerk.

One by one, they froze.

Soon nearly every employee in City Hall had formed a circle around Vikir.

The office fell silent.

After a long, long pause—

“Customer number 3021… may enter.”

The clerk’s tiny voice drifted across the room.

◆◆◆

Chihuahua Baskerville looked up as Vikir entered.

“Oh. You used the door this time. Last time you came through the window.”

Apparently, he had grown used to Vikir’s habits.

Vikir dropped onto the sofa.

A small child immediately came running.

“Daddy! Daddy!”

Pomeranian.

Pomeranian La Baskerville.

Vikir caught her beneath the arms and lifted her.

“Not Daddy. Uncle.”

“Uncle! Uncle!”

Pomeranian laughed and clung to him.

“Uncle! Hee hee. Rat! I caught a rat!”

She had learned to speak much better.

Vikir suspected Chihuahua’s care had helped.

Pomeranian proudly held out a rat.

It was clearly not a pet.

The creature was little more than skin and bone.

Vikir nodded.

“A cute little rodent. Be careful not to catch plague from it.”

“Hee hee. Yes, Uncle!”

Vikir patted her head and turned to Chihuahua.

Chihuahua summarized what had happened while Vikir was away.

Vikir, in turn, told him about the Depths.

Chihuahua listened with growing amazement.

“Good heavens. So you killed the unidentified monster in the end?”

“Not completely. It escaped with mortal wounds into a cold, barren region. It probably froze or starved.”

“Ha! That’s our Deputy Magistrate. Once you owe a debt, you collect it to the end.”

Then Vikir asked his own question.

“Were Underdog City’s problems resolved?”

“Most of them. Your old plan worked. Cindywendy did an excellent job.”

Chihuahua’s bright expression dimmed.

Vikir noticed.

“What is it?”

“…There is one case even Cindywendy hasn’t been able to solve.”

Chihuahua rose and pulled a file from the shelf.

**Unsolved Serial Child Abduction Case**

It concerned disappearances in Underdog City.

“Children from the slums have been vanishing one after another. It’s clearly a serial pattern, but it doesn’t look like ransom kidnapping. We can barely find a trail.”

“One perpetrator?”

“That’s my guess. I have no evidence.”

“Cindywendy is capable. She’ll find a lead eventually.”

“I hope so.”

Chihuahua rubbed his head.

Then—

“Daddy! Uncle! Look!”

Pomeranian grabbed Vikir’s arm again.

She hung from it with her whole body until he turned.

The brief separation had apparently made her even more clingy.

“All right, Pomeranian. What is it?”

“Rat! Rat!”

Again, she offered him the animal.

Vikir nodded automatically.

Then paused.

“…?”

He looked more closely.

Something was wrong.

The rat reeked of death.

It was not merely thin.

It was already dead.

Only skin and bone remained.

Yet it squirmed and danced on Pomeranian’s palm.

“…!”

Vikir’s mouth opened slightly.

Corpse revival.

Dead flesh being moved through negative mana.

“Pomeranian.”

“Yes, Uncle!”

“Did you bring this rat back?”

She beamed and nodded.

“Yes!”

“When did you learn to do that?”

“Um… one night, two nights, three nights…”

She counted carefully on her fingers.

Then held up five.

“Five days ago?”

“Yes.”

“Who taught you to play with rats like this?”

“The tribe people!”

Vikir fell silent.

Pomeranian had been found in a Rokoko village.

Perhaps she had picked up some fragment of their dark magic and spirit arts there.

That part was uncertain.

What mattered was her aptitude.

*She can already sense mana at this age? And negative mana, which is much harder to perceive?*

Even gifted children in Baskerville and Morgue often did not properly sense mana until around eight.

Pomeranian was five.

And she was already playing with it freely.

“…Hmm.”

Vikir considered the child clinging to him.

A magical prodigy had appeared in the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan.

And she was the patriarch’s granddaughter.

*How would Hugo react if he knew?*

Vikir pictured the Hugo he knew.

*…A granddaughter? And mixed with the blood of some unknown bastard? Useless.*

Something like that seemed most likely.

Hugo evaluated even his children by utility.

He valued swordsmanship above almost everything.

Magic—especially dark magic—was even less likely to please him.

There was also the question of what he thought about having a granddaughter at all.

There were extraordinarily few women in Baskerville’s direct line.

Hugo had no daughter living beside him.

Pomeranian was unique.

Pomeranian La Baskerville.

The only daughter of Penelope La Baskerville, Hugo’s firstborn daughter by his first wife.

Direct blood of the direct line.

*Would introducing her to Hugo really be wise?*

Vikir remembered the man’s frozen expression and shook his head.

*I know what that cold-blooded man would say.*

Useless.

A burden.

A mongrel.

Or perhaps Hugo would simply look through her as if she were an object.

*If that’s what will happen, it may be better for them never to meet.*

Vikir was still considering Pomeranian’s future when—

Bang!

Something struck the window.

Chihuahua jumped and opened it.

An owl fluttered inside with a letter tied to its leg.

He pulled the letter free and read it.

Then his face went white.

“Lord Vikir! This is serious!”

“What?”

“The main house! The Patriarch himself! The moment he heard you were alive, he immediately—”

A thunderous sound cut him off.

Bang! Bang! Bang!

Someone was pounding on City Hall’s front doors.

Outside came the heavy breathing of horses driven hard and fast.

Chihuahua swallowed.

Then finished his sentence.

“He’s here.”
