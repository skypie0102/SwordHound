# Chapter 29: Special Laws of Vikir (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/029.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 29 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Underdog City, Vikir Van Baskerville, Chihuahua Baskerville, Deputy Magistrate, and established Baskerville terminology follow current project/Fandom evidence.

Dortmeyer Street lay at the heart of the great metropolis of Underdog City.

A massive notice had been posted in the central square of its bustling market.

It was unusual for the Magistrate's office to post such a proclamation personally, but there was nothing new about the contents.

<div class="info-window">
<p>Constitution of Baskerville Territory, Article 00, Section 0.</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Human Trafficking — Penalty: Death</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Gambling — Penalty: Amputation of the wrist</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Usury — Penalty: Removal of the eyes</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Entertainment Establishments — Penalty: Facial tattooing</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Lobbying — Penalty: Exile from the territory</p>
</div>

These were all laws that had existed for years.

The laws governing territory controlled by the Iron-Blooded Sword Clan were notoriously severe.

Almost no citizen believed they would actually be enforced fairly.

“Laws always favor people with power.”

“The people at the top line their pockets first, then lecture us about the law.”

“In this world, the people who follow the law are the ones who lose.”

“The law is far away. A fist is close.”

Underdog City suffered from an extraordinarily high crime rate, and its citizens openly scoffed at laws that existed only on paper.

They had already seen officials sent by the Baskerville Clan collude with local magnates and the children of influential families for private gain.

People with money, connections, and power interpreted and enforced the law in whatever way benefited them.

People without money, backing, or strength had no way to resist even when they were wronged.

They could only cry, drink, curse their own bad luck, or vent their anger on someone poorer and weaker than themselves.

People who had lived under those conditions for years naturally distrusted the law.

“Still, all the previous Deputy Magistrates were arrested over bribery. Maybe things will change this time.”

“Not a chance. I heard the new Deputy Magistrate is only fifteen.”

“What? Fifteen? That's the same age as my eldest daughter. What can a kid like that possibly do?”

“Exactly. Give it a little time and the local power brokers will have him completely bought off too.”

Public opinion of the newly appointed Deputy Magistrate was anything but favorable.

Some doubted him because of his age.

Some doubted the laws because they had never meant anything before.

Others pointed to the countless illegal gambling organizations or the temptations waiting for any inexperienced official.

Every argument reached the same conclusion.

There was no reason to trust the new Deputy Magistrate.

Then—

“Hey! Everyone, get to the central square! Something big is happening!”

The already crowded marketplace became even more chaotic.

Curious people poured toward the square from every direction.

There were countless spectators.

Yet in the middle of all those bodies was a perfectly empty circle, as though a meteor had fallen there.

No one dared step inside it.

At its center stood a cold-faced boy holding a wooden stake.

Vikir Van Baskerville.

The newly appointed fifteen-year-old Deputy Magistrate.

◆◆◆

Vikir held a wooden stake in one hand.

It was seventy centimeters long and weighed roughly three kilograms.

People crowded in to see the new Deputy Magistrate, but no one came too close.

The pressure rolling from the boy was like a mountain.

Behind that untouchable presence stood several nervous officials from the Magistrate's office, including General Secretary Chihuahua Baskerville.

“What in the world is the Deputy Magistrate trying to do?”

“He's been standing there since early morning. Who knows?”

“Look at how many people came.”

Neither the citizens nor the officials understood Vikir's purpose.

Then—

“Listen.”

Once enough people had gathered, Vikir spoke.

“I am your new Deputy Magistrate.”

The murmuring grew louder.

“So young.”

“He looks even younger in person.”

“He's actually kind of cute.”

“What can a kid like that do?”

Most of the reactions were unserious.

Vikir ignored every stare and every voice.

“You've all seen the notices posted on the north side of the square.”

Every head turned north.

A huge banner displayed another collection of laws.

<div class="info-window">
<p>Constitution of Baskerville Territory, Article 00, Section 0.</p>
<p>Prohibition of Tax Evasion — Penalty: Removal of the kneecaps</p>
<p>Prohibition of Smuggling — Penalty: Death by drowning</p>
<p>Prohibition of Illegal Drugs — Penalty: Removal of the tongue</p>
<p>Prohibition of Embezzling Military Supplies — Penalty: Extermination of three clans</p>
<p>Prohibition of Adultery — Penalty: 600 strokes of the rod</p>
</div>

Everyone knew those laws.

Everyone also knew they were rarely obeyed.

In practice, such punishments seemed to exist only for weak people without money, influence, or force of their own.

Since the laws had first been enacted, countless commoners had been punished under them.

The number of nobles and wealthy men punished was effectively zero.

That fact exposed the reality more brutally than any speech could.

The citizens looked away from the banner and back at Vikir.

“These are solemn and unyielding laws that were established long ago and have sustained Baskerville rule ever since.”

Quiet laughter rippled through the crowd.

It was open mockery of laws that were never enforced—or, more precisely, laws that somehow ceased to exist whenever the offender had enough money or power.

Vikir made no effort to identify the people laughing.

He simply swept the crowd with an unreadable gaze.

It was an expression suspended somewhere between tolerance and indifference.

“Changing the Deputy Magistrate does not change the laws of Baskerville Territory. They remain in force, and they will be obeyed.”

When Vikir still did not react to the jeers, the crowd grew bolder.

“Yeah, sure!”

“We know!”

“Of course, of course!”

Mocking voices rose from all sides.

Unable to endure it any longer, Chihuahua shouted from behind him.

“You people! How dare you speak so carelessly! This man comes from the main Baskerville family! Do you need your throats cut before you remember where you are?”

Chihuahua was a local-born official with considerable standing among the citizens.

The hecklers immediately quieted and drew back.

“Tch. If Mr. Chihuahua says so, fine.”

“Why does the General Secretary have more authority than the Deputy Magistrate?”

“I'd rather have Mr. Chihuahua as Deputy Magistrate.”

“Look at the kid. Without Chihuahua, he can't even answer a few jeers.”

“The law's only going to get worse.”

Ordinarily, no one would have dared speak that way in front of a Baskerville official.

But Vikir's young face and complete indifference encouraged them.

“...”

Even when the citizens compared him unfavorably to his own subordinate, Vikir did nothing.

The people turning red with embarrassment were the City Hall officials, Chihuahua foremost among them.

The hecklers themselves eventually began to lose momentum.

“Maybe he's too young to know how to react when people insult him.”

“How is a kid like that supposed to enforce the law?”

“...The criminals are going to run wild for a while.”

Resignation and mockery.

Anger and pity.

Contempt and helplessness.

Every kind of gaze converged on Vikir.

Then he raised the wooden stake high.

Thump!

He drove it straight into the ground.

Only about ten centimeters disappeared beneath the surface.

The stake was somewhat heavy, but still nothing more than a small piece of wood.

Even a child could pull it out.

The crowd stared in confusion.

Vikir stepped back several paces.

“Now, under the legislative authority vested in me as Deputy Magistrate, I hereby proclaim a new special law.”

Vikir's Special Law, Article 1, Section 1.

<div class="info-window">
<p>Whoever pulls this wooden stake from the ground shall immediately receive a cash reward of 100,000,000 gold from Deputy Magistrate Vikir Van Baskerville.</p>
</div>

It could hardly have been simpler.
