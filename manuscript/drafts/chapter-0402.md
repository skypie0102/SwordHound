Vikir carried Kirko back to her room.

CREEEAK—

He pulled the handle.

The door shrieked—

but did not open.

Salt had eaten deeply into the hinges.

Rust locked them.

*Bad living conditions.*

Vikir clicked his tongue.

Then remembered—

all junior guards lived like this.

Until recently—

he had too.

The windowless stone room contained almost nothing.

A desk.

A bed.

Kirko groaned as Vikir laid her down.

The backlash of forcing too much mana through herself—

combined with Black Tongue’s strike—

had wrecked her body.

“….”

Vikir looked down at her.

He remembered Bastille’s words during Kirko’s promotion.

*Strange fate.*

*Children do resemble their parents.*

Perhaps Bastille had known.

Kirko’s parents—

used as tools to deepen the hatred between guards and prisoners.

And Black Tongue—

the man who had created the false story.

*Now I understand why Kirko’s name never became famous.*

Her talent was obvious.

Potentially—

she could have grown into a hero known throughout the Empire.

Someone on the level of Camus.

Dolores.

Yet in Vikir’s previous life—

he had never heard the name Kirko Grimm.

The likely reason was simple.

*She died before she could become one.*

*Killed by Black Tongue.*

Born in Nouvelle Vague.

Raised in Nouvelle Vague.

And destined to make an enemy of Black Tongue.

Those circumstances alone could erase even extraordinary talent before it reached the world.

Vikir looked down again.

“….”

Even unconscious—

Kirko bit her lip to suppress pain.

Sweat soaked the bed.

Vikir sighed.

Then placed a hand over her abdomen.

Sssssss—

Mana flowed from his body into hers.

Slowly.

Carefully.

The wounds inside her channels began to close.

Kirko’s expression eased.

*She forced herself into a higher realm too suddenly.*

*An anger-driven breakthrough like that is unhealthy.*

Her fury at Black Tongue had pushed her into the Sword Graduator realm.

But the method was dangerous.

The body had limits.

So did mana channels.

Trying to force power beyond those limits—

was like lifting weight until muscle tore—

or falling from a height until bones shattered.

A rage-fueled awakening expanded the mana pathways by force.

Sometimes by tearing them open.

The symptoms afterward resembled mana rampage.

Kirko had been lucky.

Instead of becoming permanently crippled—

she had actually advanced.

Almost impossibly lucky.

*Still.*

*Bad luck became opportunity.*

*What happens next depends on her.*

The Kirko who should have died to Black Tongue—

was alive because Vikir interfered.

What kind of future that created—

even Vikir could not know.

He continued stabilizing her body.

Restoring circulation.

Ordering the damaged mana channels.

A level of treatment few people could perform.

Then—

“…!”

Something felt wrong.

Mana flowed from Vikir into Kirko.

Filled wounds.

Closed gaps.

Raised her depleted reserves.

But one place—

would not fill.

A hole.

No matter how much mana entered—

power leaked away through it.

*What is this?*

Vikir followed the leak.

Kirko’s face tightened.

Sweat returned.

Her breathing became ragged.

She gripped the sheets.

Curled around the pain.

Vikir continued.

He found it.

RIP—

Vikir sat Kirko upright.

Opened the front of the uniform enough to reach the injury.

The lower abdomen—

exactly where Black Tongue’s palm had struck—

was turning black.

At the center—

a black sphere.

Round.

Egg-like.

Attached to her skin.

Rooting into her.

*A parasite.*

Vikir recognized the type immediately.

It resembled the black leeches inside Black Tongue’s chamber.

But this one was a larva.

And every time it consumed Kirko’s blood and mana—

it grew.

Squirm.

Hands formed.

Feet.

A head.

Then a face.

A miniature Kirko.

The more it fed—

the more closely it copied her.

Soon—

it looked like Kirko as a child.

Human in shape.

Except completely black.

If allowed to continue—

it might become a full black duplicate.

“…A parasite with a strange ability.”

Vikir grabbed it.

Pulled.

RIP!

He tore it from Kirko’s abdomen.

Then used the power of **Starvation Drought** to seal the damaged area.

“SKREE!”

The larva hit the floor.

Vikir crushed it beneath one boot.

Kirko finally relaxed.

Sweat stopped.

Breathing steadied.

Vikir looked at Decarabia.

“Have you ever heard of a parasite that consumes someone’s blood and mana—

then copies their form?”

[No.]

[First time.]

Even Vikir—

who had lived through countless disasters—

did not recognize it.

Neither did Decarabia—

an ancient being with knowledge stretching across ages.

*Then Black Tongue developed it here.*

Strange parasites existed on the surface.

Bone-sucking insects.

Blood parasites.

But something that stole both power and appearance—

this was different.

Decarabia chuckled.

[Hehehe.]

[If the surface learned about such a creature—

people would fight over it.]

[Its applications are nearly endless.]

[Even demons would want it.]

A new monster species unknown even to Decarabia.

Vikir’s eyes sharpened.

*I want it.*

Ever since learning the strange parasites at Nouvelle Vague’s admission ceremony belonged to Black Tongue—

he had been curious.

Now—

for the first time since entering Nouvelle Vague—

there was something inside the prison he actively wanted to take.

*I should design the jailbreak route so I can collect parasite eggs.*

That created a second conclusion.

Black Tongue himself—

might interfere.

Then Black Tongue would need to be dealt with.

His true identity was unknown.

His affiliations unclear.

His danger obvious.

There was no reason to preserve him.

And if removing him improved Kirko’s future—

so much the better.

A small debt.

After all—

Kirko had been the first and final love of the real Garam—

whose identity Vikir had borrowed.

Vikir started to rise.

Then—

grip.

Kirko caught his clothes.

Her eyes were barely open.

“…Garam.”

The way she spoke made it clear she was not fully conscious.

Half dreaming.

Half awake.

“Thank you.”

Dry lips moved.

“…No need.”

Vikir answered coldly.

Turned away.

Then—

“Do you have to go?”

Kirko’s voice came weakly.

Like something buried for years—

finally escaping.

A child of the deep sea.

Born inside the ghost fortress.

A woman with no home beyond it.

“…I don’t want to be alone.”

Vikir looked down at her.

A sentence from Garam’s journals came back.

Always written near the ends of the pages.

*I want to leave this place.*

And then—

*With her.*

But Vikir was not Garam.

Nouvelle Vague was only a temporary stop.

His real destination—

far beyond it.

“You won’t be alone.”

That was all he could say.

“…Maybe.”

Kirko’s fingers slipped from his collar.

Fell beside the bed.

Her eyes closed.

Deep sleep.

Vikir checked her breathing.

Her complexion.

Stable.

Only then did he leave.

Click.

The door closed.

The hinge—

slightly looser than before.
