# Chapter 52: Slaves of the Savage Tribe (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/052.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 52 is an aligned secondary witness only. Established terminology controls **Vikir Van Baskerville**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Aiyen**, **Ahun**, **Madam Eight-Legs**, **River Styx**, **Beelzebub**, **Tough Life**, **Infernal Buffalo ‘Murcielago’**, **Mid Sword Graduator**, **Adolf Morgue**, **Camus Morgue**, and **Staffordshire Baskerville**. Recovered-English `Gradient`, `camel`, gender flips, and corrupted ability wording are rejected.

Chirp, chirp—

The sound of small birds.

Vikir opened his eyes beneath a shaft of morning sunlight.

The light itself seemed to stab into his body.

He tried to rise, only to grimace as pain tore through him from head to toe.

By sheer force of will he managed to prop up his upper body.

Standing was another matter entirely.

*Where am I?*

His thoughts returned to the previous night—or perhaps several nights ago. He had no idea how long he had been unconscious.

He remembered emptying everything he had into the darkness of the flooded jungle.

Mana squeezed dry.

Aura burned to nothing.

The full force of a Mid Sword Graduator, combined with Beelzebub, had produced one miserable result.

*Defeat.*

Madam Eight-Legs had been far beyond his estimate.

At the decisive moment, the enormous burst of light from Vikir's attack had startled the monster just enough to keep it from finishing him.

Even so, the backlash from producing that much power had nearly destroyed his body on its own.

Muscles and blood vessels had ruptured throughout his body.

The bones supporting them were worse.

The fingers and arm that had held the sword were shattered. Several ribs had suffered spiral fractures, their sharp ends piercing into his internal organs.

His legs had suffered multiple fractures of different kinds.

Bone, muscle, blood vessels, skin—

There was scarcely a part of him that had escaped injury.

*Still, surviving is something to celebrate.*

Vikir let out a slow breath.

The blessing of the River Styx and **Tough Life**, the ability Beelzebub had taken from the Infernal Buffalo Murcielago, were the only reasons he had endured that punishment.

Misjudging Madam Eight-Legs had been costly.

But at least the outcome had removed him from Hugo's surveillance.

Before long, Baskerville would receive word of his death.

There were plenty of witnesses: Adolf Morgue, Camus, Staffordshire, and the Baskerville triplets.

No one would question the report.

More importantly, Vikir had not supposedly died through recklessness or disgrace.

He had sacrificed himself while saving Morgue's heir from barbarians and a monster.

*Baskerville will profit enormously from this in its future dealings with Morgue.*

For a martial clan such as Baskerville, the story would also strengthen the family's reputation both inside and outside its territory.

A deed like this might even draw recognition from the imperial court.

Even dead, Vikir would have brought his family honor and political benefit.

No one would remember him as a disgrace.

And if he returned one day and revealed that he had survived?

His treatment would only improve.

*First I need to recover—and regain the strength I had before the regression.*

Vikir drew on Tough Life.

It was not an instant cure, but the broken bones and torn tissue were already knitting together at an astonishing rate.

Then he noticed something around his neck.

A rope.

More precisely, a noose hanging from his throat like a dog's collar.

Aside from the River Styx and Tough Life, this was the third reason he was still alive.

Just before Madam Eight-Legs' attack could crush him completely, a noose had flown in and caught him by the neck, wrenching him away.

The timing had nearly snapped his neck.

It had also saved his life.

Vikir took hold of the rope.

Its other end ran through the tent and out through the entrance.

Only then did he properly examine his surroundings.

A conical tent made from animal hides.

A few wooden poles supported the frame, while dry leaves covered the ground.

Rustle—

A glass bottle rolled beside him.

The Baskerville crest was stamped on it.

A potion bottle.

The tent smelled of dry earth, fallen leaves, animal fur, and the lingering trace of medicine.

Vikir crawled outside on all fours.

Every movement set his body on fire, but he needed to know where he was.

Outside stood tents of different sizes, all made from hides.

Drums sounded from different parts of the settlement.

Fires burned atop tall stacks of wood.

Barbarians moved everywhere between them.

Black or silver hair.

Brown skin.

Powerful physiques regardless of sex.

Some wore deer hide, others jaguar skin, with lower garments made from cloth dyed in oak sap.

Tattooed and scarred adults butchered monster carcasses while children chased birds nearby.

The place was cheerful and energetic, but raw and untouched by imperial refinement.

Vikir understood at once.

This was a **Ballak** village.

The center of a people who had been enemies of Baskerville for generations.

He should have been on guard.

In his present condition, that was easier said than done.

Vikir was still deciding what to do when Ballak warriors simply walked past him without reacting.

Women and children glanced at him with some caution, but nothing more.

Then—

“Hurry up! Move!”

A sharp voice rang out.

Vikir turned.

Several Ballak warriors were dragging a line of prisoners bound with rope.

The captives were of several different peoples.

Most were black-haired, brown-skinned members of other mountain tribes.

Nearly all bore signs of harsh torture.

Among them Vikir recognized a few familiar faces.

He did not know their names, but some were Baskerville soldiers and some were Morgue warriors.

A few had been at the fortress.

Others had entered the flooded jungle with him.

Their bodies were trussed in rope like fish on a line.

For a brief moment their eyes met Vikir's.

He wore only a single collar around his neck.

“……”

“……”

Then they passed each other.

Vikir remained where he was and watched.

The Ballak warriors jabbed the captives forward with spearpoints.

“Climb. Unless you'd rather be torn apart by wolves.”

They were driven toward an enormous tree.

Its dense branches jutted outward like blades.

The Ballak stripped the prisoners and forced them to climb.

Blood ran down the captives as the sharpened branches cut and pierced them.

Anyone who refused to keep climbing faced fire at the base of the trunk.

Oil had been spread across the bark, so the flames raced upward faster than most prisoners could climb.

One way or another, the punishment killed them.

Some bled out from the branches.

Some burned.

Some fell to their deaths.

WOOF! WOOF! WOOF!

Wolves barked below, driving the captives higher.

Those who refused the tree entirely were met with spears or teeth.

It was a hideous Ballak execution devised by an old shaman:

the **Thorn-Tree Punishment**.

Vikir watched the hellish scene in silence.

“Heh. Be grateful you didn't end up like that.”

Someone addressed him.

Vikir recognized the warrior who had fired the paralytic dart at Camus.

Ahun.

He stared at Vikir as though he would gladly kill him on the spot.

“If you weren't the captain's pet, I'd have thrown you in a pot already.”

“……”

“Stop standing around. Go find your master.”

Ahun kicked Vikir hard in the backside.

Vikir hit the ground.

Then he looked up.

Ahun flinched.

*Those eyes…*

There was no agitation in them.

Not from watching prisoners die.

Not from the pain of his own ruined body.

Ahun realized that none of it had so much as scratched Vikir's composure.

For reasons he could not explain, that realization unsettled him.

Vikir slowly stood.

Then he followed the rope hanging from his neck.

Ahun stayed behind and watched him go.

◆◆◆

*That was Ahun, wasn't it?*

He probably still resented being yanked off his wolf and injuring his back.

Vikir had not forgotten the face of the man who had just kicked him.

For now, however, his condition left him no choice but to endure it.

The River Styx and Tough Life were steadily repairing his body.

His immediate goal was to find the owner of the noose that had saved him.

Following the rope led him away from the village.

Eventually he reached a secluded pool between rocks, fed by a small waterfall and a narrow stream.

Crayfish crawled beneath the clear water.

The rope continued toward the pool.

“……?”

Vikir stopped.

It ran all the way to the center and disappeared beneath the surface.

Then—

SPLASH!

Someone rose from the water.

Silver-and-black hair scattered droplets.

Pointed triangular ears.

Brown skin.

A young woman stepped out of the pool without showing any concern for her lack of clothing.

The far end of Vikir's rope was tied around her wrist.

“……”

Vikir stared at her face.

Arched brows.

Foxlike eyes.

Clear irises.

A straight nose and red lips.

Without the charcoal smeared across her face, she looked different.

But Vikir remembered those eyes perfectly.

This was the Ballak captive he had once found caged at the illegal slave auction in Underdog City.

Back then, their positions had been reversed.

Now Vikir stood at the end of *her* leash.

“……”

“……”

Silence lingered between them.

Aiyen broke it first.

She took hold of the collar around Vikir's neck and gave him a bright smile.

“Get down.”
