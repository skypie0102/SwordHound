# Chapter 26: The Graduate (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/026.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 26 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English forms for Staffordshire Baskerville, Oxbear, Colosseo Academy, knight orders, sword ranks, and numbered Fangs follow current project/Fandom evidence.

Baskervilles continued to be born in great numbers.

And just as many continued to die.

On the River Styx, one wave drove the next onward.

Seven years had passed since that day.

◆◆◆

“I'm telling you now—you can become angels, or you can become demons!”

A Guide Dog with a short beard shouted at the trainees before him.

His name was Staffordshire Baskerville.

Thirty-five years old. A veteran knight at the Mid Sword Graduator level.

He had been born a commoner outside the clan and had never received even the middle name Van, much less Le. But through exceptional talent and an upright character, he had earned the Baskerville surname and risen beyond his birth.

His hound swordsmanship, known as the Stabbing Fang, had reached the level of the Baskerville Fourth Fang.

Four perfect Fangs.

That level of strength was more than enough to qualify him to lead and train the younger Baskervilles gathered here.

“All right! This is your final exercise! Treat training like real combat! And when it's real combat, treat it as real combat! You all know that by now!”

Staffordshire swept his gaze across the fifteen- and sixteen-year-old Baskervilles before him.

They looked exhausted, but the fighting spirit—and the vicious edge—in their eyes had only grown sharper.

Every young Baskerville here had reached Low Sword Expert and mastered the First Fang.

Once Staffordshire had checked the trainees' condition, he turned toward the center of the training ground.

A gigantic bear stood there on its hind legs.

The beast weighed roughly eight hundred kilograms and rose about three meters when upright. Its enormous size was not the only unusual thing about it.

Two sharp, curved horns jutted from its head.

Among carnivorous bears, it stood at the pinnacle of terrestrial predators—a near-perfect killing machine and one of the dominant monsters of the 7th Ridge ecosystem.

Aside from its own kind, it had almost no natural enemies.

Its head bore a pair of massive curved horns like ivory crescents, while the rest of its body was wrapped in dense muscle, fat, and coarse fur.

[Grrrrrrrr...]

The Oxbear placed in the training ground was not yet fully grown.

Worse, it had spent so long confined in a cage that its condition was poor from inadequate nutrition.

That only made it more irritable and aggressive.

Staffordshire brandished his sword to keep the Oxbear under pressure as he called to the young Baskervilles behind him.

“Do exactly what you've practiced on monster corpses. Last time, you dissected an Oxbear. You already know how tough its hide is, how dense its muscles are, and what organs lie beneath each part of the body.”

Staffordshire, normally mild-mannered, became a completely different man before the Oxbear.

Killing intent filled his eyes, and the aura rolling from the point of his sword twisted like an enormous hand.

That pressure was enough to overwhelm the Oxbear.

But Staffordshire did not attack.

That was not his role today.

A moment later—

[GRAAAAH!]

The Oxbear charged.

It came on like a buffalo, both horns thrust forward.

The young Baskervilles responded according to their own instincts.

Some scattered left and right.

Others raised their swords and met it head-on.

BOOM!

Those who dodged survived the charge cleanly.

Those who confronted it directly were hurled backward.

Staffordshire continued his instruction without missing a beat.

“When an opponent stronger than you charges straight at you, the best choice is to evade. But don't flee so far that you surrender the initiative. Slip to the side and take a small piece out of them.”

Advice poured from the Guide Dog one lesson after another.

The young hounds absorbed every word greedily.

“Going straight for a vital point at the start is foolish. It makes you easier to counter. First attack the part that controls the enemy's balance. Here, that's the right hind leg. Cripple it, and you lower both its movement and its reaction speed.”

The trainees followed his direction and repeatedly struck at the standing bear's legs.

After so many attacks to the same rear limb, one of the Oxbear's legs was nearly useless.

Its terrifying horn charge had been sealed away.

Only then did the young Baskervilles truly bare their fangs.

They no longer needed Staffordshire's moment-to-moment commands.

They had become hounds capable of hunting on their own.

The Baskerville First Fang.

More than thirty Fangs stabbed in from every direction and tore into the Oxbear's body.

The most conspicuous among them were the triplets—Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro.

Slash! Slash! Slash!

They fought as though they were the leaders of the pack.

Any arrogant sibling or companion who tried to push past them was shoved aside without mercy.

So was anyone cowardly enough to fall behind.

Before long—

THUD!

The Oxbear collapsed.

“Well done.”

Staffordshire stepped toward the carcass with his sword in hand.

Slash—!

The bear's abdomen split open, exposing steaming organs and slabs of flesh.

The fourth training period was over.

Naturally, it was time for lunch.

Right there on the training ground, Staffordshire distributed the Oxbear's organs and meat among the young hounds.

Hot red flesh still steamed in their hands.

What would have turned an ordinary person's stomach was swallowed raw by the young Baskervilles without hesitation.

Staffordshire spoke while they ate.

“Listen. As of today, three and a half years of basic training and three and a half years of practical training are over. Congratulations. You've graduated.”

Seven years had turned the little pups into real warriors.

Every one of them could now channel mana into a sword and draw a proper Fang.

But Staffordshire immediately told them that this was only the beginning.

“Training against captured monsters is nothing more than an introductory lesson. It barely resembles real combat. From now on, you'll have to build your own experience.”

The advice of a veteran Guide Dog was always worth remembering.

“The wild monsters of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain are nothing like beasts that have spent years rotting in our cages. They're healthy, cunning, and calm. Sometimes an old specimen appears that even I can't handle safely.”

The Oxbear they had just killed was a perfect example.

A fully grown wild Oxbear could drive in with its horns, follow with crushing forepaw strikes, then rip with its jaws.

Even a Sword Graduator could be in serious danger against one.

Controlled variables.

A safe environment.

Fixed rules.

Training under those conditions alone could never prepare someone to survive in an untamed wilderness.

And that wilderness was exactly where these young hounds were headed next.

They already knew it.

The countless scars on Staffordshire's face shifted as he spoke.

“From here on, you'll experience the hell ahead with your own bodies. Overcome it with the blood you were born with, or overcome it with the experience you earn yourselves. I have nothing else to tell you. Dismissed!”

He ended his time as their Guide Dog with a short salute.

For a moment, the young Baskervilles lowered the meat and organs in their hands.

Ahead of them lay roughly twenty-one months in undeveloped territory.

Once Staffordshire had left, the trainees gathered in groups of twos and threes and began to talk.

“Hey. Which knight order did you apply for?”

“My first choice is the Doberman Knights. Second is Pit Bull. Third is Rottweiler.”

“Huh. None of those overlap with mine. I want the Wolfhound Knights first, then Shepherd, then Mastiff.”

The Baskerville Clan maintained seven knight orders.

Each had been established for a different purpose—border defense, search, protection, assassination, suppression, full-scale warfare, and other specialized missions.

Among them, the Wolfhound Knights stood at the summit, symbolizing the clan's military might.

“I want to join the Wolfhound Knights too.”

“As if they'll take you just because you want in. You have to be selected.”

“I'll be realistic and apply to the Doberman Knights. Aside from Wolfhound, they're basically the first-rate choice.”

“Joining the Doberman Knights means spending your life guarding the walls. Sounds boring to me. I'd rather join the Pit Bull Knights and stay on the battlefield.”

The youths talked noisily among themselves.

Then, naturally, their attention began drifting toward a single place.

‘...Where is that guy going to end up?’

It was the question everyone there shared.

Even the triplets, who had been the most aggressive during the Oxbear hunt, set down their meat and quietly looked over.

Vikir Van Baskerville.

Hugo's illegitimate son, said to have been brought in from outside the clan.

He had not participated in the group hunt.

He sat alone some distance away.

At his feet lay the corpse of another enormous beast, one far larger than the Oxbear that more than thirty trainees had just brought down together.

The young Baskervilles looked at him with open awe.

“Is that guy really fifteen, same as us?”

“How did he kill something that big by himself?”

“I heard he's already reached High Sword Expert.”

“And he can draw three Fangs.”

“That's insane! He can use the Third Fang? That's practically Guide Dog level!”

Admiration for the strong existed in any group.

In the Baskerville Clan, it was practically instinct.

Vikir himself paid little attention to those rumors.

‘High Sword Expert.’

At High Sword Expert, the aura gathering along a blade had become so dense that it hovered between gas and liquid.

Most Baskervilles only reached that stage somewhere between twenty and twenty-five.

Yet the clan believed Vikir had reached it at fifteen.

...But that was only what they believed.

Vikir's true hidden level was already Mid Sword Graduator.

That placed him two full stages above High Sword Expert and roughly in the same realm as Staffordshire.

And once the blessing of the River Styx and the power of Beelzebub were added, his actual combat ability rose even higher.

If the clan knew the truth, the reaction would be enormous.

‘...I've mastered five Baskerville Fangs now. In swordsmanship alone, I've already surpassed my previous life.’

In his first life, Vikir had never been permitted to master anything beyond the Fourth Fang.

Learning higher swordsmanship while simultaneously raising his aura had taken time.

That was why reaching a complete Mid Sword Graduator state had taken somewhat longer than he had originally planned.

Vikir closed his eyes and thought back to the enormous beast he had killed earlier.

There had been a few dangerous moments.

Still, years of captivity had weakened it enough that he had been able to bring it down without exposing too much.

His thoughts were occupied with measuring his present strength against the man he had been before regression.

The chatter of Baskerville pups who had only just begun to grow their fangs meant very little to him.

Then one subject caught even Vikir's attention.

A young Baskerville mentioned it casually.

“...Then someone from our group is probably going to the Academy, right?”

Colosseo Academy.

An imperial institution where the Seven Great Clans sent their young heirs to be educated.

Its enrollment was limited, so each clan could send only a small number of students.

Naturally, the best youths from each family gathered there.

That made it interesting to Vikir as well.

Not because he wanted honor.

Not because he had any particular hunger for education.

‘It's a chance to get away from Hugo and raise my strength.’

There were other reasons too.

Debts from his previous life still needed to be repaid.

Hidden relics still needed to be claimed before anyone else could reach them.

As Vikir considered the future—

“Young master.”

A voice called from behind him.

It belonged to an old man, but it was still strong, warm, and gentle.

Head Butler John Barrymore approached Vikir's side.

He had been one of the few people who had shown Vikir sympathy in his previous life.

For that reason, Vikir trusted him more than most.

Barrymore leaned closer and spoke quietly.

“The Patriarch is looking for you.”

Hugo Le Baskerville?

It was always unusual for Hugo to summon one of his sons personally.

Vikir's expression showed a trace of puzzlement.

Barrymore gave a faint smile.

“Your position has been decided, young master.”
