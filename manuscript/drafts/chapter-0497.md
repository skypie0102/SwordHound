“…The Full Revival Magic.”

The Camus of this world remained skeptical.

“To even attempt it—

you need enough mastery to form at least ten mana wheels.”

“In practical terms—

that’s nearly impossible.”

“Nearly impossible.”

The other Camus smiled.

“Not completely impossible.”

“Don’t play word games.”

“I spent years trying to complete that spell.”

“No matter what I did—

there was one wall I could never cross alone.”

“Yes.”

The other Camus nodded.

“I had the same problem.”

“…?”

“**Alone.**”

That word stopped her.

Camus Morgue.

How many people had betrayed her?

How many failed to meet her expectations?

Unmatched talent.

An arrogant temperament.

Years of scars.

All of it had taught her one lesson:

**Do not trust others.**

…But what about herself?

The Camus from the other timeline smiled with absolute confidence.

“I am a magical genius.”

“The strongest Morgue.”

“….”

“You are a magical genius.”

“The strongest Morgue.”

“….”

“So if we join hands—

and cover each other’s blind spots—”

“what happens?”

Silence.

Even while inspecting her own mana—

the Camus of this world always worked alone.

Eventually—

she asked quietly:

“…Can we really bring everyone back?”

“I cannot promise that.”

“But if there is even the smallest possibility—

shouldn’t we try?”

The Camus of this world nodded.

Then—

GRIP.

Two of the greatest magical geniuses ever born—

took each other’s hands.

Their target:

the forbidden spell no one had ever completed.

The **Full Revival Magic**.

“Now?”

“Scared?”

“Of course not.”

They sat facing each other within the giant magic circle.

The other Camus inhaled.

Then spoke.

“Do you know House Morgue began as a morgue?”

“…!”

“A family that handled nameless corpses.”

Long before nations and noble Houses took their present form—

the bloodline that became **Morgue** survived by gathering unidentified bodies.

They cleaned them.

Searched for relatives.

Returned the dead to families—

and received payment.

Because they lived alongside death—

some among them gradually developed strange abilities.

Voices of the dead.

Necromancy.

Communication across the boundary.

Even when House Morgue once held influence almost equal to a nation—

even during periods when the line nearly died out—

people with those talents kept appearing.

The other Camus remembered an old voice.

Her teacher.

A man to whom she owed a debt she could never repay.

“So strictly speaking—

Morgue’s origin is inseparable from death.”

“The ancestors were people who spoke with—

and controlled—

the dead.”

“So we were connected to black magic from birth.”

“Exactly.”

The Camus of this world listened.

“As head of House Morgue—

I’ve never heard it explained that way.”

“Some Dark Hall elders said similar things long ago.”

“But war left no time to listen.”

“I only know because Master taught me.”

The “Master” meant **Snake Morgue**.

The other Camus noticed the scowl immediately.

“So why does any of this matter?”

“Because true black mages understand one thing.”

“The truth a human can grasp in one lifetime—”

“is a handful of sand.”

“Then where is the rest?”

The other Camus smiled.

“You asked exactly what I did.”

Then answered.

“Beyond death.”

“Past the door.”

“…!”

“Only after crossing death—

can a human become truly free and eternal.”

“Only then can we study the infinite truths beyond it.”

The Camus of this world frowned.

“So you embraced black magic to become closer to death.”

“No.”

“Exactly the opposite.”

“…?”

“To become wary of death first.”

“Why?”

The other Camus gave a bitter smile.

“True black mages hate death more than anyone.”

“That makes no sense.”

“Before understanding death—

you must understand life.”

Silence.

“Life.”

“Feeling for others.”

“Love.”

“Friendship.”

“Trust.”

“Connection to everything around you.”

“Gratitude for being alive.”

“The preciousness of life.”

“Only after understanding those things—

can you understand death.”

“Two sides of the same thing.”

The Camus of this world slowly nodded.

“So a true black mage—

might resemble what ordinary people call a sage.”

“Someone who loves living things—

and pities everything dying.”

“Extremes meet.”

The other Camus grinned.

“As expected of me.”

“Fast learner.”

They talked for a long time.

Vikir—

nearby—

silently watched the two women debate with themselves.

At last—

they sat together at the center of the magic circle.

“To complete the Full Revival Magic—

we need the missing theory and formulae.”

“They exist in the **Magic Abyss**.”

“The foundation beneath this world.”

“A place where truth itself is stored.”

“It may take a long time.”

The other Camus had attempted the spell once before.

And failed.

“With the previous failure as experience—

I won’t fail again.”

Both crossed their legs.

Then—

WHOOSH.

Mana flooded the circle.

Complex patterns lit one after another.

At the center—

Water.

Carbon.

Ammonia.

Lime.

Phosphorus.

Salt.

Potassium nitrate.

Sulfur.

Fluorine.

Iron.

Silicon.

Trace elements.

Heat.

*Failure.*

“THIS ISN’T FAILURE!”

The other Camus’s shout snapped her focus back.

At the center of the circle—

something unnatural began forming.

The same thing—

the other Camus remembered from the previous attempt.

“Again.”

“Good.”

Last time—

she had tried to keep **it** trapped inside the magic circle.

Now she understood.

The goal was not to keep the thing **out**.

They needed to go **inside it**.

Sssssssss—

The other Camus shaped it with terrifying precision.

“You suppressed this with brute force before?”

The Camus of this world laughed.

“You’re a monster.”

“Don’t compliment yourself.”

“You’re helping too.”

Wind.

Returning to nothing.

“CAMUS!”

*Master.*

*Lend me strength.*

*Master.*
