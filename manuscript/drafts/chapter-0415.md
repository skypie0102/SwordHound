Water was entering Nouvelle Vague everywhere.

On Level One—

the guards who had been suppressing the riot finally understood how bad things had become.

“The outer wall isn’t repairing!”

“Where is Brigadier General Flubber?!”

“Why is the water still coming in?!”

“The collapsed battlement section is flooding!”

“Half the inner wall is underwater!”

As more of the prison fell outside Flubber’s coverage—

the inflow increased.

“If this continues—

we all die!”

Prisoners and guards stopped fighting.

This was different from BDISSEM’s disappearance.

Without Flubber—

Nouvelle Vague itself could not remain sealed against ten thousand meters of sea.

Everyone inside would die.

“Stop fighting!”

“We need to block the water!”

“With what?!”

“Run!”

“Where?!”

“We’re already at the bottom of the ocean!”

Bastille shouted over the panic.

“Warden Orca!”

“Where is Warden Orca?!”

“I-I don’t know!”

“He disappeared!”

The old Warden—

who had been fighting on the front line only moments earlier—

was gone.

Bastille finally made the only decision left.

“Everyone to Level Five!”

“Head for the Gulper Eel kennel!”

Then—

the command that would later appear in human history as a tiny line beneath a much larger catastrophe.

“ABANDON SUPPRESSION!”

“RETREAT!”

The first formal declaration—

by human authority—

that Nouvelle Vague was being abandoned.

◆◆◆

Meanwhile—

Level Ten gained another monster.

Professor Sadi.

She looked exactly as she had on the surface.

“Hello, friends~”

“Been a while.”

She waved toward D’Ordume and Souaré.

Vikir stared.

“…You actually made it here.”

“Oh my.”

“Night Hound himself sounds impressed.”

“What an honor~”

Sadi walked over—

then shook Aiyen’s hand.

“Night Fox.”

“Long time no see.”

Aiyen nodded.

“Good to see you again, Ms. Ouroboros.”

“You kept your promise.”

“Of course.”

“I said I’d follow.”

“And I have business here too.”

The connection had started with Cindywendy.

After escaping the prototype Hell Tree—

Aiyen had gone to the Imperial Capital.

Cindywendy introduced her to Sadi.

Night Fox—

a sniper operating against demon-linked targets.

Ms. Ouroboros—

an Imperial terrorist.

Both deeply hostile to the current Imperial order.

They got along remarkably well.

Vikir thought back.

*Good thing I told Cindywendy to find Sadi before I entered Nouvelle Vague.*

Before his sentence—

Vikir had given Cindywendy a list of future instructions.

One had been simple:

**Find Professor Sadi.**

Volatile.

Unpredictable.

Dangerous.

But their goals overlapped enough that she might become useful.

She had.

Aiyen deliberately allowed herself to be captured—

following Vikir into Nouvelle Vague through the prisoner route.

Sadi used a different path.

Her former position in the Imperial arrest apparatus—

plus old contacts—

allowed her to disguise herself as a guard and infiltrate the enormous returning escort force.

“Hehehe.”

“Orca and the Wardens happened to be away from the surface.”

“And there were so many prisoners.”

“Largest escort in history.”

“Perfect.”

“Forging identities is one of my talents.”

D’Ordume’s face twisted.

“You were once paid by the Imperial Family as an arrest-team torturer.”

“Why betray them?”

“You were even considered for a Warden position here once.”

Souaré sneered.

“Imagine.”

“We could have ended up with **six** Wardens.”

“Fortunately—

her traitorous bloodline disqualified her.”

Sadi smiled.

“Do I look insane enough to spend my life in this wet, ugly hole with you pigs?”

“No elegance at all.”

That ended the discussion.

Then Sadi looked at Vikir.

“Oh, right.”

“Did you meet the man I planted in Nouvelle Vague?”

“Leviathan-born.”

“Named **Sakkuth**.”

“Competent little pig.”

“…No.”

“Really?”

“Well.”

“He was never very stable.”

“I sent him because he sounded useful.”

“I didn’t expect much.”

Sadi laughed lightly.

Then stepped between Vikir—

and the three Wardens.

“I’ll handle these.”

“Do what you came to do.”

Dark energy poured from her.

Vikir recognized it instantly.

Her left eye.

The eye she had lost—

and the strange demonic scent once leaking from the socket.

Sadi removed the eyepatch.

A golden artificial eye gleamed beneath it.

Vikir understood.

*Belial.*

*The relic from the Sixth Corpse.*

When Belial died—

Vikir had wondered why no relic appeared.

Now he knew.

Sadi already had it.

Black and gold aura erupted.

Her whip expanded.

Lengthened.

Thickened.

CRACK!

A black-and-gold serpent tore through the battlefield.

D’Ordume—

Souaré—

even Flubber—

were thrown back.

Vikir swallowed.

In the Hell Tree—

an average fighter like Underdogma had risen to roughly A+ monster-level after becoming a Majin.

Sadi had already been a combat genius capable of threatening Master-class opponents.

Her present strength—

was difficult to estimate.

[Hehehehe.]

[Using this leaves me recovering for at least half a year.]

Sadi did not fully become a demon.

She maintained a dangerous midpoint.

Half human.

Half transformed.

That balance was possible only because her bodily control and combat instincts had long exceeded normal human limits.

D’Ordume and Souaré could no longer push forward.

Flubber—

vulnerable to broad attacks that burned everything in their area—

also recoiled.

Sadi’s attacks were wasteful.

Wild.

Explosive.

Exactly what Vikir needed.

Every shockwave traveled through the ground—

into Poseidon.

*Almost.*

*Just a little more.*

The blue flames intensified.

CRACK.

Lines appeared along Poseidon’s shell.

Then—

BOOM!

Something crashed into the battlefield.

Every person there felt it.

Danger.

Even Sadi.

She had once ignored an instinct like that—

and lost her left eye.

Not again.

She flipped through the air—

three times—

then landed low.

A black fur coat descended into Level Ten.

White hair.

White beard.

Warden Orca.

He looked at Sadi.

His expression immediately soured.

“…Sadi.”

Cold sweat ran down her back.

“Don’t say my name, old man.”

Her whip snapped toward his face.

Orca raised one armored leg.

CLACK.

The whip wrapped around his boot.

He twisted.

Pulled.

Sadi nearly lost the weapon.

She yanked back with both hands.

Nothing.

The whip remained trapped under Orca’s boot.

Then the ancient Warden stopped looking at her.

His eyes moved across the battlefield.

Past Aiyen.

Past the Wardens.

Toward the young man beside Poseidon.

Vikir Van Baskerville.

Young in appearance.

Old in everything else that mattered.

Two veterans—

from completely different eras—

looked at each other.

The battlefield grew still.
