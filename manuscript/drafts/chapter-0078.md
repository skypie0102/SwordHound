Quovadis**, **Mozgus Quovadis**, **Quovadis**, **Colosseo Academy**, **Rune Religion**, **Leviathan Clan**, **Bourgeois Clan**, **Baskerville**, **Morgue**, **Red Death**, and **Hound of the Night**.

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

Sixteen years old.

Saintess of Quovadis.

One of the strongest candidates to become the family's next head.

A prodigy of Colosseo Academy who had entered early, remained at the top of her class, and now served as student-council vice president in her second year.

Vikir looked at her and remembered the future.

In the Age of Destruction, Dolores would go wherever the fighting was worst.

She would heal the wounded on battlefields soaked in blood.

Soldiers would call her the **Angel of the Battlefield**.

She would save lives until the idea of a living saint no longer sounded like an exaggeration.

Even now, at sixteen, the foundation was already there.

A firm will.

A clear conscience.

A refusal to turn away from suffering.

Those who survived the Age of Destruction remembered her with respect.

Vikir did too.

He inclined his head.

The paladins tensed.

Dolores paused.

Her spiritual senses were unusually sharp.

From the masked stranger she sensed an animal-like presence.

Blood.

Oil.

Violence.

Anger.

Hatred.

And beneath all of it—

grief.

Something old, deep, and exhausted.

“You said you were a lost lamb?”

“……Yes.”

Dolores studied him.

“More like a puppy.”

Vikir said nothing.

“A scarred little puppy.”

The paladins stared at her.

Mozgus objected immediately.

“Saintess! That is not a puppy. That is a blood-soaked wolf. Please step back—”

“Tea.”

Mozgus stopped.

Dolores raised a hand.

“I need to speak with him.”

◆◆◆

A small reception room was prepared.

Table.

Two chairs.

Two cups of peppermint tea.

Mozgus stood at the entrance holding a tiny flower-painted teapot in hands far too large for it.

Dolores sat opposite Vikir.

“When I heard Mozgus himself had gone out, I came because I thought the visitor would be badly injured.”

Mozgus coughed.

“Saintess, I did not lose. In a prolonged fight, the situation would have favored me.”

Dolores silently decided that saying this at all meant he had lost.

Mozgus was one of the Empire's rare Graduator-class fighters.

The masked man across from her had defeated him far too easily.

She could not read his face.

She could barely read his intentions.

Only that strange wounded soul.

Dolores looked at the untouched tea.

“You cannot drink through that mask, can you?”

Vikir nodded.

She sighed.

“I have been under a great deal of stress lately.”

Vikir looked up.

School?

Family duties?

Dolores shook her head.

“Academy work and family affairs are mostly paused for the holiday.”

Her expression cooled.

“The nobles and merchants outside are the problem.”

They came demanding healing.

Money in hand.

Titles on their tongues.

Most did not suffer from anything immediately fatal.

Impotence.

Venereal disease.

Drug side effects.

A collection of problems that could wait while genuinely dying people could not.

“If I have the strength to heal someone, I would rather use it on the people suffering most.”

She looked into her tea.

“Justice. Mercy. Charity. Equality. Perhaps those are parts of God's will.”

A faint frustration entered her voice.

“But people keep seeing only what is directly in front of them.”

Vikir answered without thinking.

“Theology is fundamentally the process of understanding human beings. That cannot be helped.”

Dolores froze.

Mozgus did too.

“Old Testament. Book of Azmoth, chapter six, verse nine.”

Dolores leaned forward.

“That is an ancient passage. Even theologians rarely remember it.”

Vikir remained silent.

“Are you a follower of Rune?”

He knew almost nothing about theology.

He simply remembered words the future Dolores herself had repeated many times.

Changing the subject seemed wise.

“The plague in Saint Mecca's slums is more important.”

Dolores became serious immediately.

“Is the report certain?”

“You saw the water I brought.”

“You were not involved?”

“If I were, why would I come here myself?”

Not exactly the whole truth.

But enough.

Vikir continued.

“I do not want innocent people harmed. This is my homeland too.”

Dolores's eyes brightened slightly.

“You are from here?”

Vikir did not elaborate.

“I also believe absolute good exists.”

He looked at her.

“I hope the plague is contained quickly.”

Dolores's expression softened.

“Rough around the edges, but……you seem like a good person.”

Then the subject returned to the suspicious figures.

Mozgus had told her Vikir claimed to have seen people contaminating wells.

“If this plague is artificial,” Dolores said, “who would spread it? Why?”

The political consequences were enormous.

Plague devastated institutions where many people lived together.

Monasteries.

Schools.

Religious communities.

Priests died.

Fear created superstition.

A shortage of trained clergy pushed inexperienced people into religious authority.

Heresies multiplied.

Quovadis lost control.

Red Death could become a political weapon against the family.

Vikir drew a map.

The Seven Great Clans surrounded the center of the Empire.

“Start with those who profit.”

His finger stopped at two names.

“Leviathan and Bourgeois.”

Leviathan specialized in poison.

A plague created opportunities to gather victims, collect samples, refine toxins, and weaken Quovadis—an old enemy.

Bourgeois benefited differently.

Plague did not destroy money.

It killed owners.

Workers.

Farmers.

Merchants.

Factories, farms, stores, guilds, warehouses, land, coin, and equipment could then be acquired cheaply by whoever still possessed capital.

New industries would follow.

Luxury for the newly rich.

Health goods for survivors terrified by disease.

Profit built on catastrophe.

Vikir remembered Bourgeois merchants arriving in the jungle and buying up medicinal materials.

*Leviathan released the Red Death.*

He was nearly certain now.

*And Bourgeois may be cooperating with them.*

Quovadis and the jungle tribes would become victims.

Vikir did not mention Baskerville or Morgue.

If he revealed that the original outbreak had begun deep in Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, then those houses also appeared to benefit.

In the previous timeline, more than forty percent of the mountain tribes had died.

Monster populations exploded.

Border crises multiplied.

Baskerville and Morgue gained political importance.

Hugo strengthened his house.

More hounds died to maintain it.

Vikir had been one of them.

His present plan touched every part of that future.

Save Ballak.

Block the Red Death.

Strike Leviathan.

Disrupt Bourgeois.

And change the chain of events that had killed so many Baskerville hounds.

He returned his attention to Dolores.

“For those reasons, I believe Leviathan and Bourgeois are closely connected to this plague.”

A beat.

“Of course, it is only a hypothesis.”

Vikir had taken things he had personally witnessed before regression and presented them as inference.

He expected questions.

None came.

He raised his head.

Dolores and Mozgus were staring at him.

Both had their mouths slightly open.
