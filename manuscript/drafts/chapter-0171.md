“…”

Vikir closed his eyes.

He remembered an enemy that had nearly killed him more than once.

> **Madam Eight-Legs**
>
> **Danger Rating:** S  
> **Size:** ?  
> **Discovery Location:** Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, 10th Ridge  
> **Alias:** ‘Madam of the Flood’  
> **Species:** Unknown

Long ago—

enormous spiders were said to have inhabited the deepest reaches of Hell.

Madam Eight-Legs was believed to descend from those ancient monsters.

Her body had been filled with horrific venom.

Her web—

stronger than steel—

could not be cut by ordinary means.

Only hellfire had reliably burned through it.

Even Vikir had nearly died multiple times while fighting her.

The ancient demons themselves—

according to old stories—

had found giant spiders of that bloodline difficult enough to handle that the creatures claimed an isolated domain in the depths.

And now—

in Vikir's hand—

was the last offspring of that terrifying spider.

[Chirp—whine—]

A black puffball rubbed against his fingers.

Its shaggy body looked almost like a smudged doodle.

Stubby legs appeared from beneath the fur whenever it sat or moved.

*Madam's… offspring?*

**Baby Madam.**

The tiny creature rubbed the back of Vikir's hand—

leaving white saliva behind.

Its behavior was much closer to a puppy than a spider.

“…Is it because wolves carried the egg around?”

Apparently prenatal influence mattered more than he had expected.

Vikir found himself thinking of parents who played music—

or spoke foreign languages—

around unborn children.

Then—

sticky.

He looked down.

White mucus stretched from the place Baby Madam had licked.

The moment it touched air—

it hardened into a fine thread.

“…Spider silk.”

Vikir pinched it.

Pulled.

“…Strong.”

Ordinary spider silk already had exceptional tensile strength and elasticity compared with equal-weight steel.

Baby Madam's—

descended from Madam Eight-Legs—

was on another level.

Vikir pulled harder.

Aura entered his hand.

Snap!

Only then did the thin strand break.

And that was a tiny amount—

produced almost accidentally.

“Elasticity.”

“Tensile strength.”

“Both dozens of times beyond steel.”

“And adhesive too.”

It also resisted heat far better than ordinary webbing.

*Right.*

*Even I needed Cerberus's hellfire to cut her mother's web.*

Vikir remembered the old battle.

[Chirp—whine—hah!]

Baby Madam continued rubbing against his hand.

Perhaps because its mother was gone—

the hatchling seemed desperate for affection from the first creature it had seen.

Vikir felt a small amount of pity.

*Your mother was terrifying.*

Madam Eight-Legs had devoured her own eggs without hesitation.

In that sense—

the hatchling might be safer without her.

Whatever the reason—

imprinting had already happened.

The first thing Baby Madam saw after hatching—

was Vikir.

So Vikir had become the creature it followed.

Then—

gurgle.

Baby Madam sagged against the back of his hand.

“…Hungry?”

[Whine…]

The black dust-ball looked miserable.

Like a pastry somebody had burned by accident.

*What does this thing eat?*

Insects?

Meat?

Vikir opened a drawer.

Biscuits.

Salted ham.

A piece of cold spinach pie.

Pigi had bought them as a late-night snack and shared them with him.

Baby Madam sampled the biscuit.

Then the ham.

[Nom…]

Barely interested.

The spinach pie—

it immediately spat out.

“…Picky.”

Vikir let the hatchling climb onto his left hand.

Pop.

Baby Madam settled over his wrist like a watch.

Two legs wrapped securely around him.

“Fine.”

“We'll find food.”

He left the dormitory.

The first destination was obvious.

The library.

Information first.

◆◆◆

Colosseo's library was almost a park unto itself.

Vikir crossed the grounds—

entered the huge lobby—

then walked into rows of shelves that seemed to continue forever.

Nature.

Creatures.

Monsters.

He scanned titles.

“Spiders…”

“Spiders…”

Then—

“Ah!”

“Vikir!”

Two round eyes appeared through a gap in the shelf opposite him.

White hair.

A bright smile.

**Sinclaire.**

She hurried around the shelf.

If she had possessed a tail—

Vikir thought it would probably have been wagging.

“I've never seen you here before.”

“I come often.”

“This is just the first time you've seen me.”

“Studying?”

“Borrowing something?”

A badge on Sinclaire's chest identified her as a library assistant.

Convenient.

Vikir asked—

“Do you have books about spiders?”

Sinclaire brightened.

“Pest control?”

“You want to kill one?”

“Is there a spider in your room?”

Technically—

yes.

Vikir's left wrist twitched.

[Grnn…]

Baby Madam's crude little brow furrowed.

It tried to look threatening.

Vikir quietly moved his wrist behind his back.

“Not extermination.”

“Raising.”

“I need a book on raising spiders.”

“Oh!”

“Spider breeding?”

“We have some.”

“Are you keeping one?”

“Do you like spiders?”

“Not especially…”

Vikir stopped.

Looked down.

The burnt little puffball on his wrist stared up at him.

“…But I think I might start.”

Sinclaire's expression became more curious.

“You like spiders…”

“Interesting.”

She kept asking questions as they searched.

“Why?”

“The fur?”

“The legs?”

“The eyes?”

“What about scorpions?”

“Centipedes?”

“Millipedes?”

“Beetles?”

“Do you like all insects?”

“Oh.”

“Spiders aren't insects.”

Vikir gave her a sideways look.

*Was she always this talkative?*

Normally—

Sinclaire seemed quiet.

Studious.

Reserved.

Eventually—

she found several books.

Vikir borrowed them—

then moved to a quiet place outside.

He opened the first.

“…Raising spiders.”

Temperature and humidity control.

Sensitivity to mold.

Lighting often unnecessary.

Extra care during molting.

Molting intervals varied by individual.

House-training required patience and repetition.

Early environmental influence mattered.

Large spiders should be muzzled when taken outside.

Ordinary husbandry advice.

Vikir frowned.

*Small insects or pieces of meat.*

*Then why won't this one eat?*

Baby Madam lay limp on his left wrist.

Gurgle.

He turned page after page.

Then—

near the end—

found something different.

Some mythical—

“extraordinary”—

spider lineages did not grow primarily on ordinary food.

Their young consumed **poison**.

“…Poison?”

Vikir kept reading.

The development of such hatchlings depended heavily on what toxins they ingested throughout life.

Many kinds.

Increasing strength.

Increasing complexity.

A spider raised on diverse powerful poisons—

could combine useful properties from them—

eventually becoming an apex creature in its ecosystem.

A spider fed only weak or repetitive toxins—

would remain limited regardless of quantity.

“So poison is food.”

Vikir acted immediately.

He bit his little finger.

A dark-red bead of blood formed.

Blood carrying **Madam Eight-Legs's venom**.

Baby Madam licked it.

Then—

[Ugh…]

The hatchling shuddered.

Fell sideways.

Vikir stared.

“…Now what?”

He reopened the book.

A later note explained the problem.

Newly hatched spiders of these lineages—

should begin with weak toxins.

Only as their bodies matured—

should they move toward stronger and more complex venom.

“…Right.”

“Your mother's poison is too strong.”

Madam Eight-Legs's venom was a nightmare blend.

Powerful acid.

Neurotoxin.

Hemorrhagic toxin.

Dermal toxin.

Muscle toxin.

Gastrointestinal toxin.

Malodorous toxin.

Pain-inducing toxin.

Respiratory toxin.

Protoplasmic toxin.

Nearly every vicious property imaginable—

combined.

For a newborn hatchling—

it was far too much.

“Fine.”

“I'll find you something weaker.”

“Until then—”

“eat insects.”

Vikir caught a butterfly.

Baby Madam reluctantly put it in its mouth.

Chewed.

Clearly unhappy.

But willing to eat what Vikir gave it.

Then—

“Vikir!”

“Are you here!?”

Pigi's voice came from a distance.

Vikir looked up.

Pigi came running—

newspaper in hand.

“Vikir!”

“This is huge!”

“How did you know I was here?”

Pigi stared at him.

“How?”

“You only ever go to the dormitory, classroom, club room, library, cafeteria, or gym!”

“That's not the point!”

He shoved the newspaper toward Vikir.

“Look at this!”

Pigi caught his breath.

Then grinned.

“You're a complete celebrity now!”
