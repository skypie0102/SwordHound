“….”

Professor Banshee Morgue woke with a frown.

Morning sunlight touched his face.

The frown deepened.

He removed a cute sleeping cap that did not suit him.

Then an eye mask.

Then pajamas.

His expression never improved.

“…Bad dream.”

The nightmare had been vivid enough to soak both his clothes and sheets in cold sweat.

A gigantic one-eyed serpent.

A unicorn.

The two fought.

The serpent wrapped itself around the unicorn’s enormous body—

squeezing—

trying to swallow it whole.

The unicorn thrashed.

Its horn tore through the air.

For a moment—

it resisted.

Then the serpent opened another eye.

Its left eye.

Brilliant.

Golden.

The struggle ended immediately.

The serpent constricted once more—

and swallowed the unicorn.

Banshee rubbed his forehead.

“Of all days—

why dream something like that before reporting to the Headmaster?”

“Stress?”

He dressed.

Collected the reports prepared the night before.

Then left the faculty residence.

Students greeted him on the way.

He ignored them.

Fellow professors greeted him.

He ignored them too.

Straight to the Headmaster’s office.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

The door opened almost immediately.

Winston Smith sat inside—

wearing his usual mild expression.

“Oh?”

“Professor Banshee.”

“You’re early.”

“Yes.”

“One minute and thirty seconds early.”

“If you’re busy—

I can wait outside until the exact appointment time.”

“No, no.”

“Come in.”

Winston gestured.

Banshee placed the first report on the desk.

“Settlement report for the Academy greening project.”

Since returning to his position—

Winston had redesigned much of Colosseo’s landscaping.

The goal was simple.

Double the amount of trees and vegetation on campus.

Nobody thought it especially strange.

Winston had always been mild-tempered.

He liked plants.

Even the Headmaster’s office was filled with them.

Banshee placed down another file.

“The new magic stones have also been installed.”

During the midterm incident—

Vikir had discovered a gap in the arena barrier.

He used it to enter from below.

If someone malicious ever exploited the same weakness—

the consequences could be severe.

So Winston ordered the entire security network reinforced.

New magic stones.

More efficient mana transfer.

Harder barriers.

Banshee remained skeptical.

“The new stones are superior in mana efficiency.”

“And the barrier hardness is higher.”

“But their long-term durability has not been sufficiently verified.”

Winston laughed.

“Hahaha.”

“That’s why the old and new stones are arranged alternately.”

“Anyone attempting intrusion won’t know which section is old and which is new.”

“They’ll be confused.”

Then he smiled.

“Not that we’re expecting intruders.”

Banshee stared.

Winston looked at him warmly.

“You always think about student safety.”

“I admire that.”

“…I’m only an anxious little bureaucrat trying to preserve my position.”

“That isn’t how you really feel.”

“I know that much.”

Winston reached beneath the desk.

Then handed him something.

A bracelet.

At its center—

a glossy black seed.

Gray roots extended from beneath it.

They could expand or contract around the wearer’s wrist.

“This is a key for the new barrier.”

“A bracelet type.”

“Like the newer locker systems in the bathhouse and gym changing rooms.”

“Convenient.”

“Secure.”

Banshee inspected it.

“An artifact?”

“Mana-imbued?”

“Can these be mass-produced?”

“I make them by hand whenever I have spare time.”

“I already have enough for all the students.”

“I’ll produce faculty versions gradually.”

Winston smiled.

Banshee grunted.

Then placed another document in front of him.

“That concludes the security report.”

“Next matter.”

“Hm?”

Winston looked down.

“I wasn’t briefed on this one.”

“It isn’t a formal report.”

“Something I wanted to raise personally.”

“Oh?”

“What is it?”

Banshee hesitated.

Then spoke.

“The Imperial Capital has been strange lately.”

“Strange?”

“Look at the statistics.”

He pointed to several graphs.

“Crime has increased.”

Winston shrugged.

“That happens whenever the Capital hosts major events.”

“Population enters.”

“Crime rises with it.”

“No.”

Banshee tapped another line.

“This is the increase relative to incoming population.”

“The crime **rate** is also above normal.”

His eyes narrowed.

“Wanted criminals from across the Empire are gathering in the Capital.”

“Reports of sightings have risen sharply.”

“That sounds troublesome.”

“What are the Imperial Guards doing?”

“That is the second problem.”

Banshee moved to another chart.

“Arrest rates for serious crimes have dropped.”

“Some fugitives escape as though they knew the arrest plan beforehand.”

“Several wanted people have vanished completely.”

“Someone may be helping them flee.”

“Or hiding them.”

Winston rested his chin on one hand.

“I see.”

“But what does this have to do with Colosseo?”

“You know the final Ranking Battles and parent-visitation classes occur together.”

“Given the instability outside—

I wondered whether we should postpone.”

Winston laughed.

“This is Colosseo Academy.”

“The place educating the future heroes of the Empire.”

“Look at our professors.”

“Look at the families of our students.”

“They are the military backbone of the Empire.”

He leaned back.

“All those people will be gathered here.”

“Why fear a few criminals outside?”

Banshee said nothing.

Winston continued.

“But I respect your caution.”

“That’s one reason I’m strengthening the barriers.”

Banshee eventually nodded.

He could not really argue with the logic.

No ordinary fugitive group had the ability—

or reason—

to attack a gathering containing so many of the Empire’s powerful figures.

Then—

“One final report.”

Banshee placed down another document.

“Professor Sadi.”

For the first time—

Winston’s expression truly changed.

Awkwardness.

Sadness.

“…What did Professor Sadi do this time?”

“We received complaints concerning her duties as an advisor to the Imperial Guard.”

Winston sighed.

“Oh dear.”

“Search operations.”

“Intelligence work.”

“Arguments with colleagues.”

“Important evidence and information lost.”

“Unauthorized absence during arrest operations.”

Banshee’s tone sharpened.

“And eventually—

she was discovered drunk and asleep in the dormitory.”

Winston closed his eyes.

“Alcohol.”

“That cursed alcohol.”

“Sadi is extremely competent when she isn’t drinking.”

“That was true once.”

Banshee’s resentment finally surfaced.

“In the past—

she was outstanding as an advisor to the Imperial Guard tracking division.”

“Her criminal-capture record nearly matched the Marquis de Sade in his prime.”

“People even called her the second coming of the Marquis.”

“But that person is gone.”

“Now she drinks.”

“Complains about fate.”

“Avoids reality.”

“And neglects her work.”

Silence.

Winston kept his eyes closed for several seconds.

Then opened them.

“…I’ll discipline her.”

“A salary cut or short suspension will not be enough.”

“I understand.”

“This time—

I will impose serious punishment in the Headmaster’s name.”

Only then did Banshee stop.

The reports were finished.

He left.

◆◆◆

Step.

Step.

Step.

The corridor was dim.

A black shadow moved beside Banshee.

He stopped.

“Did you find anything?”

The shadow knelt.

“Yes.”

“There are definitely irregularities.”

Banshee had quietly ordered a background investigation into Winston.

Not because he believed Winston dangerous.

He wanted one specific answer.

*Why does he protect Sadi so much?*

Banshee wanted Sadi gone.

Completely.

The investigator lowered their voice.

“Headmaster Winston’s concern for Professor Sadi is not primarily because she descends from old nobility.”

“Not because of her former arrest record.”

“Not because he needs another member in his small Headmaster faction.”

Banshee’s eyes narrowed.

“Then what?”

The answer came almost as a whisper.

“It may be related to the **Forty-Seven People Riot**.”

Banshee’s eyes widened.

◆◆◆

“All right!”

“Today’s lecture is about major events after Imperial unification!”

A professor tapped the blackboard.

The first-years listened.

“After unification—

there were several major events.”

“The Western-Front Barbarian Subjugation.”

“The Merger of the Five Sword Families.”

“The Great Escape from Nouvelle Vague Prison.”

“But the greatest political incident was probably the **Forty-Seven People Riot**.”

“Who can explain it?”

Several hands rose.

The professor chose the first.

“Sinclaire.”

“Our Hot Department leader.”

“Go ahead.”

A chair scraped.

Sinclaire stood.

White hair.

Calm voice.

“The Forty-Seven People Riot occurred thirty-five years ago—

shortly after Imperial unification.”

“It was led by forty-seven people.”

“Because each was the head of a family—

it is also called the **Forty-Seven Families Riot**.”

“Good.”

“Continue.”

“The forty-seven launched a coup.”

“They killed numerous people and stormed the Imperial Palace.”

“Forty-six were executed on the spot.”

“The sole survivor remains imprisoned at **Nouvelle Vague**—

the prison on the remote edge of the continent.”

“Their true motive is still unknown.”

“The common theory is that they wanted the throne.”

Sinclaire sat.

Students whispered.

“Something changed about her.”

“What?”

“She’s still smart.”

“Not that.”

“Her mood.”

“She’s colder lately.”

“Yeah.”

“But somehow—

it suits her.”

“She looks older.”

“Like she matured overnight.”

The cheerful—

almost puppy-like—

Sinclaire of the beginning of the year had changed.

Nobody could quite explain how.

The professor clapped.

“Good answer.”

“I hope you perform just as well in this afternoon’s Hot Department final.”

Then the professor looked around the class.

“And as you all know—

parents will attend today’s final matches.”

Nobody had forgotten.

The entire Academy had been torn apart for landscaping and security improvements.

Trees.

Flowers.

Magic stones.

Barriers.

Most students were already tired of hearing about it.

Besides—

the finals belonged to only a few people.

Tudor.

Bianca.

Granola.

Sinclaire.

Everyone else was mostly an audience.

Some students were excited to see family.

Others were terrified that poor grades would be exposed.

Perhaps sensing the fading attention—

the professor smiled.

“One more announcement.”

“We’re getting a new student.”

That woke everyone up.

“A transfer?”

“Not exactly.”

“She is entering midway through the academic year rather than transferring credits from another Academy.”

“A new enrollee.”

“Female.”

“Nineteen years old.”

“One year younger than the normal first-year age.”

“Hot Department.”

“And she comes from the renowned mage family **Morgue**.”

The class erupted in questions.

The professor raised one hand.

“Her name is—”
