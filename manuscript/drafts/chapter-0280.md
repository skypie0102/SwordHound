# Chapter 280: What Money Can't Buy (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/280.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 278 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Damien Bourgeois, Pomeranian, Wraith Tree, Morgue Tzersi, Decarabia, and House Bourgeois.

“What if I can give you one chance to speak with your daughter?”

Damien stared.

“And what will you give me?”

The answer burst out of him.

“If Juliet wakes—

I’ll never be angry with her again.”

“I’ll tell her I love her unconditionally.”

“I’ll respect every choice she makes.”

“I’ll spend the rest of my life apologizing—”

“No.”

Vikir cut him off.

“What will you give **me**?”

Damien fell silent.

Vikir was not a priest.

He had no obligation to listen to a father’s remorse.

Damien—

after a moment—

understood.

He had heard this exact kind of desperation many times.

Borrowers asking for money.

Investors begging for backing.

Debtors asking for extensions.

Parents with sick children.

Children with sick parents.

People explaining why they deserved mercy.

And Damien—

for most of his life—

had thought only one thing.

*That is your problem.*

A transaction required value.

Not pity.

The other side had to gain something.

Damien’s merchant instincts returned.

“If you restore my daughter—

I will give you everything within my power.”

Vikir shook his head.

“I didn’t say I could restore her.”

“…What?”

“I said I could make conversation possible.”

A flicker of fear crossed Damien’s face.

“You’re not going to kill us both so our souls can talk—

are you?”

“That would technically work.”

“…You.”

“But it would be a bad solution.”

“If you were dissatisfied—

there would be no refund.”

Vikir stood.

Turned.

Then disappeared behind the moving curtains.

Damien struggled upright.

Night Hound was already gone.

Only one voice remained—

low—

close—

almost inside Damien’s head.

“Death isn’t atonement.”

“It’s escape.”

“If you regret something—

live long enough to take responsibility for it.”

◆◆◆

One month later.

Vikir arrived at Damien’s mansion.

The perimeter security was severe.

But—

as arranged—

one narrow route had been left open.

Vikir entered.

Deep inside the estate—

Damien waited.

Pacing.

Checking time.

Restless as a rabbit carrying a pocket watch.

The moment he saw Vikir—

“…You’re late.”

“This was fast.”

No further greeting.

Damien led him into a private annex.

Then—

into a bedroom.

A large bed dominated the center.

Devices regulated temperature.

Humidity.

Light.

Scented candles filled the room with calming fragrance.

A woman lay with her eyes closed.

Wild white hair.

Clear pale skin.

Large eyes.

Long lashes.

**Juliet Bourgeois.**

Damien’s only daughter.

Once one of the two young candidates expected to compete for future family leadership.

A girl once described as a genius.

Vikir stared.

*I’ve seen that face before.*

He was certain.

But where?

Damien’s voice cut in.

“Is staring at a sleeping woman part of the procedure?”

Vikir looked away.

“Come out.”

Something moved beneath his cloak.

Then—

a small figure emerged.

**Pomeranian.**

Damien stared.

“…Who is this child?”

“She looks too young.”

“She’s the one who will call your daughter.”

“If you want the conversation—

be quiet.”

Vikir ignored Damien’s questions.

Then lowered himself toward Pomeranian.

“Your sensitivity to souls is unusually strong.”

“Can you do it?”

“Mhm!”

“Samchun!”

“Uncle.”

“Not samchun.”

“Anyway—

do what we discussed.”

Vikir—

Pomeranian—

and Damien—

stood beside Juliet’s bed.

Pomeranian stared at the sleeping woman.

Then spoke.

“This sister…”

“doesn’t have a soul.”

Damien went white.

“What?”

“What do you mean she doesn’t have a soul?”

“Mmm…”

“Not here.”

“Somewhere else.”

“What does that mean?”

“Explain properly!”

Damien almost dropped to his knees just to look Pomeranian in the eyes.

Vikir translated.

“Your daughter has become a **living spirit**.”

“Her body is alive.”

“Her soul is somewhere else.”

“Neither properly among the living—

nor properly among the dead.”

Damien swallowed.

“A living spirit?”

“She believes she’s dead.”

“So her soul wanders.”

“Unable to understand why she cannot cross over.”

“And because her body remains alive—

she cannot completely leave either.”

“Then what do we do?”

“That is why I brought her.”

Vikir placed a hand on Pomeranian’s head.

“Can you summon the owner of this body here?”

“Visible—

if possible.”

Pomeranian thought.

“Mmm…”

“I can use the tree Uncle gave me!”

Then she closed her eyes.

Mana gathered.

Pomeranian had been born with an extraordinary aptitude for black magic.

Even as a child—

she had casually learned necromantic techniques by watching others.

Her time inside Baskerville had developed that gift.

Then—

ssssss.

Dark aura rose behind her.

A tree took shape.

Branches.

Roots.

Leaves.

The **Wraith Tree**.

An ancient black-magic artifact associated with the legendary Morgue ancestor **Tzersi**.

*So this is what it can really do.*

Vikir watched.

The Wraith Tree—

now rooted inside Pomeranian’s spiritual world—

responded to her aptitude.

*It had a physical form before.*

*Now it doesn’t.*

Decarabia opened one eye at Vikir’s chest.

[Ancient artifacts often exist both as form and formlessness.]

Vikir pushed the pendant back under his clothes.

Then focused.

Pomeranian’s eyes opened.

“Sister is at the end of the main road!”

“At the forest entrance!”

“Under the huge tree split by lightning!”

Damien stiffened.

“That’s the carriage-accident site!”

“The tree struck by lightning!”

“I know.”

“Be quiet.”

Black mana gathered at Pomeranian’s fingertips.

The Wraith Tree’s branches extended—

not physically—

but spiritually.

Toward something far away.

Then—

a translucent figure appeared.

[…Huh?]

[…Where am I?]

Juliet.

Damien surged forward.

“Daughter!”

Then stopped.

Because Pomeranian tilted her head.

“Huh?”

“I brought another one too.”

Another translucent figure formed beside Juliet.

A man.

**Romeo.**

◆◆◆

[…So that’s what happened.]

Juliet looked down at her own body on the bed.

Breathing.

Warm.

Alive—

in the most technical sense.

But empty of love.

Empty of will.

Empty of soul.

Damien knelt.

“I was wrong.”

“Daughter…”

“I ignored what you wanted.”

“I forced my own choices on you.”

“I had no right.”

“You never have to forgive me.”

Juliet reached toward him.

[…Stand up, Father.]

Damien looked up.

Romeo stood beside her.

Damien bowed deeply.

“To you too.”

“I have no excuse.”

“I was a worthless father-in-law.”

Romeo’s eyes widened.

Juliet’s did too.

[Father…]

“Why are you surprised?”

“You two are already husband and wife.”

“Whether my approval matters anymore—

I’ve decided to recognize it.”

Tears filled Juliet’s eyes.

Romeo’s too.

[Thank you, Father.]

Damien’s voice cracked.

“No.”

“I should thank you.”

“For growing into someone better than me.”

“For still calling me Father.”

Years of resentment—

finally—

began to loosen.

Father and daughter.

Father-in-law and son-in-law.

A painful—

necessary reconciliation.

Then Vikir spoke.

“There isn’t much time.”

“Finish what matters.”

Damien stared.

“What do you mean?”

“My daughter’s soul only just returned.”

“Not her.”

“Him.”

Everyone looked at Romeo.

Only now did Damien realize—

Romeo had barely spoken.

His form was already fading.

Vikir explained what Pomeranian had sensed.

“Romeo is fully dead.”

“Juliet is not.”

“He was summoned here from the place where his spirit remained bound.”

“He cannot stay.”

“He has to cross over.”

Damien’s face collapsed.

“What?”

“Why didn’t you tell me sooner?!”

“You asked to speak to your daughter.”

“I did that.”

“That’s not the point!”

“My son-in-law is about to leave forever!”

“You’re the one who had him beaten.”

“Don’t complain to me.”

Damien’s mouth opened.

Then shut.

Pomeranian nodded solemnly.

“Dead people go to the other side.”

“That’s the rule.”

Romeo’s body continued fading.

Juliet clung to him.

[…Please.]

[Don’t leave me.]

[I can’t live in a world without you.]

Damien grabbed his own hair.

“Again.”

“I separated them again.”

“I’m still doing the same thing.”

Romeo would cross the boundary.

Juliet—

as a living spirit—

could not.

Her soul remained tethered to a living body.

The two held each other.

Cried.

Damien’s regret deepened.

“I swore I wouldn’t make my daughter cry again.”

“I swore I would never repeat the same mistake.”

“And yet…”

He thought regret had already reached the bottom.

There was another floor below it.

Then—

a hand settled on his shoulder.

Vikir.

“It’s time to send your daughter away.”

There were only two places where a father normally heard words like that.

A wedding.

And a funeral.

Here—

somehow—

the two meanings had become one.

Damien’s eyes trembled.
