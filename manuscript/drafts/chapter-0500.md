WHOOOO—

Dry wind swept the salt desert.

A black cloak fluttered.

A long gray-white beard moved in the breeze.

Vikir walked across the white waste.

Once—

this land had been green.

Now—

stone.

Salt.

Wind.

Nothing else.

Vikir looked across the horizon.

“….”

Lonely.

Silent.

Time eroded many things.

Emotion.

Desire.

Attachment.

But one feeling—

still beat inside him almost as strongly as it had in youth.

**Competitive spirit.**

Who was stronger?

For someone who had lived by the sword—

that was one greed—

one delusion—

that death itself could barely erase.

And so—

Vikir walked.

For once—

free from every restraint.

Every responsibility.

Every duty.

He followed the instinct he had suppressed for most of his life.

WHOOOO—

Salt wind came again.

Vikir’s sword split the storm like a curtain.

A path opened.

And there—

the thing he had come to find.

**THE GRAVE OF SWORDS.**

A tower like a spike driven into the earth.

Black as night.

Red as blood.

Unchanged.

Vikir brushed salt from his beard.

Then murmured:

“…A true Baskerville is born in the Cradle of Swords.”

The famous saying.

But there was another line.

One almost no one knew.

“…A true Baskerville dies in the Grave of Swords.”

Perhaps—

at this point—

Vikir was the only living Baskerville who still remembered it.

He entered.

Needle-like stairs rose steeply upward.

The same suffocating loneliness.

The same sharpened pressure.

Every step—

cutting.

Grinding.

Polishing.

Countless swords filled floor—

walls—

ceiling.

Red droplets slid along old blades.

Step.

Step.

Step.

Vikir climbed.

Upward.

Until—

the iron throne.

Then—

a heavy voice—

clear as steel striking steel.

[This is the Grave of Swords.]

[The place where those who pursue the ultimate meaning of the sword arrive at the end.]

A man in black armor sat upon the throne.

Long white beard.

Black hollows around blazing red eyes.

Blade-like nose.

Closed mouth.

Blue-gray skin dried over bone.

A huge serrated greatsword beside him.

**Cane Corso Le Baskerville.**

One of the old Seven Counts.

The strongest human of his era.

A man even the Age of Destruction had failed to erase.

Cane Corso stroked his beard.

Smiled.

[We have never met.]

[Yet you feel strangely familiar.]

[Perhaps the intuition of those who reach the extreme realm—]

[transcends time and space.]

Vikir said nothing.

He remembered the first time.

Back then—

he could barely survive a single exchange.

Now?

He had not seriously tested himself since the war ended.

This—

was perfect.

SHING.

Beelzebub emerged.

Cane Corso’s grin widened.

Then—

they moved.

Cane Corso’s great serrated sword—

**Fragarach**—

tore into the air.

Vikir drove Beelzebub—

thin and needle-like—

into the storm of cuts.

**Baskerville Ninth Form.**

Against—

**Baskerville Ninth Form.**

The collision lasted less than a heartbeat.

Nine fangs.

Against nine.

Then—

“…!”

Vikir froze.

Lightning through the body.

Something blocked for decades—

opened.

The truths learned inside the Magic Abyss—

rose all at once.

Space distorted.

Time bent.

FLASH.

Nine fangs tangled.

And from among them—

a tiny point of light appeared.

A tooth.

The **tenth**.

Almost invisible.

Yet unquestionably there.

FLASH.

Cane Corso’s body was pierced.

For an instant—

he thought:

[…Is this the end?]

Then—

CRACK.

Fragarach broke in half.

Cane Corso looked at his old companion.

Warmly.

[So.]

[Now you may finally rest.]

[Congratulations.]

Black energy rose from the broken sword.

Cane Corso’s own body—

began becoming red dust.

He smiled.

[I never became a Sword God.]

[But I did become a Sword Immortal.]

[If I can serve as a milestone for those after me—]

[that is enough.]

He surrendered himself to the storm created by their colliding blades.

And disappeared.

The end of a man who had devoted his entire existence to the sword.

…

Then—

silence.

Only Vikir remained.

Cane Corso’s voice drifted away.

[Your birth was the birth of the sword.]

[Your death will be the death of the sword.]

Then—

“HEY!”

“YOU BASTARD!”

Vikir flinched.

He turned.

Five familiar faces.

“I knew you’d come here.”

“I’ve watched this place since I heard you muttering about it.”

“You people are too obsessed with tracking him…”

“We have a former prison guard.”

“A professional tracker.”

“Me?”

“I was a guard.”

“I’m better at arresting people than tracking them.”

Camus.

Aiyen.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Kirko.

All five.

Vikir—

rarely—

stuttered.

“I was going to come alone.”

“How did you—”

Camus stepped forward.

“You should have brought **me**, if nobody else.”

“You don’t even know how to open the gate into the Magic Abyss.”

“….”

Vikir shut his mouth.

Camus entered the Grave of Swords.

Looked down at the enormous magical traces carved into the bottom of the spiral staircase.

“…Ten mana-wheel traces.”

She examined the structure.

“This resembles Full Revival Magic.”

“But it’s nobler.”

“Greater.”

“Stronger.”

“What was this spell for?”

Sinclaire—

who knew no small amount of magic herself—

sweated.

“It almost feels like the structure represents truth itself.”

“If even Sister Camus doesn’t know…”

Aiyen.

Dolores.

Kirko.

All added observations.

“The traces suggest a massive explosion.”

“I heard this place was once a forest.”

“Maybe that blast turned it into salt desert.”

“Could something like a meteor have fallen?”

“That might explain the two blank periods in human history.”

Camus listened.

Then summarized.

“We’ll know if we go there again.”

A second expedition into the Magic Abyss.

Vikir agreed.

◆◆◆

Vikir and Camus returned to the Magic Abyss.

They passed the region known as the **Five Fingers of the Creator**.

Then—

two familiar statements.

**All things are born in the Magic Abyss—**

**and return to the Magic Abyss.**

**One day, when the stars move according to fate—**

**a gate to a new level will open—**

**and all things will meet their inevitable end.**

The words stood—

like gatekeepers of infinite emptiness.

Camus extended Wraith Tree roots.

Twisted locks—

between pillars.

Eight doors opened.

And then—

Vikir saw someone.

A spirit.

Human-shaped.

Female.

The instant he saw her—

he understood.

**Motherhood.**

The **First Mother**.

A distant ancestor.

A being radiant with warmth.

She looked at Vikir.

At her son.

And said:

[I missed you.]

Vikir could not speak.

The mother continued.

[I worried about all of you.]

[I do not know how many generations have passed.]

[But you are still my daughters.]

[My sons.]

She embraced him.

Tightly.

Warmly.

Then—

[Now I can finally leave in peace.]

[Go where he went.]

Vikir finally found his voice.

“Where?”

The mother answered simply.

[To grab him by the hair.]

“…?”

Vikir stared.

The mother laughed softly.

Then stroked his head.

[Live.]

“….”

[Live as you please.]

[Live fully.]

[Live freely.]

[Enjoy this world.]

She already knew—

why he had come.

Perhaps—

better than he did.

Vikir asked:

“Can I go with you?”

The mother shook her head.

[When the final moment of your life comes—]

[a very long time from now—]

[come back here.]

“….”

[Until then—]

[enjoy ordinary flirting.]

[Ordinary satisfaction.]

[Common happiness.]

[Those are the greatest forms of joy.]

[Awareness.]

[Love.]

That—

was Vikir’s last meeting with the First Mother.

◆◆◆

Vikir returned from the Magic Abyss.

Then—

lived.

For a very—

very—

long time.

His beautiful wives.

His cheerful children.

Their happy years.

Everything passed—

like a dream beneath nine layers of cloud.

How much time?

Enough for the dust of the world to disappear beneath the sand of ages.

Enough for old wounds to become stories.

Enough for children—

and their children—

and their descendants—

to become history.

Then—

Vikir entered the Magic Abyss for the **third** time.

His first journey—

had brought the truths needed for Full Revival—

and the truth behind the Tenth Form.

His second—

had brought him to the First Mother.

The third?

What waited now?

“….”

Vikir did not speak.

He climbed a staircase made from dust.

Cloud.

Starlight.

One step.

Then another.

At the end—

at the deepest edge of the Magic Abyss—

someone sat waiting.

Beyond the **Five Fingers of the Creator**—

an endless jade throne.

No.

A constellation.

An old man sat upon it.

Rolling several tiny—

individualized—

glass beads through his fingers.

“….”

Vikir stopped.

For once—

he did not know what expression to make.

**—END—**
