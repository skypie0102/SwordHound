# Chapter 66: Unfair Trade (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/066.txt` — `72108754001f9ed99d7d87d2788032319851c716`. Recovered English Chapter 65 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established terminology controls **Sir Smuggler**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Aiyen**, **Ahun**, **Bakira**, **Madam Eight-Legs**, **Underdog City**, and **Baskerville**. Source violence and racist intent are preserved descriptively without repeating slurs unnecessarily.

Clouds swallowed the moon.

Sir Smuggler's caravan struggled through the swamp.

“Damn these mosquitoes. Damn this jungle.”

The merchants swatted at insects and complained their way back to camp.

A large group of rough men waited there.

One hairy mercenary looked at Sir Smuggler and laughed.

“Judging by your face, business didn't go well.”

Sir Smuggler threw down his pack.

Others joined the mockery.

“Told you. You can't negotiate with Ballak.”

“Trading with beasts isn't business.”

“Kill them. Take slaves. Problem solved.”

The mercenaries were wanted men.

Convicts.

Fugitives.

Deserters.

Sir Smuggler warned them.

“Do not underestimate Ballak. You're new to this swamp.”

“Then why did you bring us? We're here in case trade fails, aren't we?”

“You are guards against monsters first. Starting a war with Ballak was not the plan.”

Even Sir Smuggler sounded uneasy.

But the valuables left behind in Ballak village were difficult for him to abandon.

The newcomers were even louder.

“We didn't spend that much winning a place in the merchant-guild bidding just to leave empty-handed.”

“Our protection fee is a percentage of profit. You think we're here for scraps?”

“Hit them at night. Burn the village. Take the goods.”

Veteran merchants knew how dangerous Ballak warriors were.

Greed won anyway.

Sir Smuggler drew his sword halfway.

“Fine. We go back after dark and recover the goods. And you all know who dies first.”

Everyone remembered the soot-blackened boy who had ruined the market.

Several men made racist threats and promised to kill him.

One mercenary hesitated.

“Was he really Ballak? His palms were black too. Maybe it was soot.”

“He spoke Imperial perfectly.”

“Then he's probably Imperial.”

Weapons were checked.

Fire-making supplies prepared.

Before moving out, the mercenaries performed a small ritual.

They smoked.

SCRITCH.

A match flared.

One man lit his cigarette.

A second used the same flame.

When he tried to extinguish it, a third stopped him.

“Why waste it? There's still flame left.”

The first two laughed.

“Newbie.”

“You don't know the rule? Never let three soldiers light from the same match.”

“What rule?”

“One match. Two men.”

The third mercenary snorted.

“Superstitious nonsense.”

He leaned toward the flame.

The third cigarette ignited.

The match died.

THUD.

So did the mercenary.

His head vanished into the dark.

The first two men stood frozen beneath a spray of hot blood.

THUD. THUD.

Two more arrows arrived.

Mouth.

Throat.

The smokers dropped.

Sir Smuggler threw his cigarette away.

An arrow struck it the instant it hit the ground.

“Throw away the lights—”

Another speaker took an arrow through the mouth.

Then the night filled with arrows.

Men who screamed were shot where the sound originated.

Men who exposed tiny points of light were shot where the light appeared.

The force behind Ballak arrows was catastrophic.

A force of more than a hundred began collapsing within seconds.

◆◆◆

On the other side of the darkness, Aiyen issued commands.

“The first cigarette gives position. The second gives distance. Fire on the third.”

TWANG.

Her arrow disappeared.

She smiled.

“So there really are lunatics who attack Ballak first.”

Ballak had never been a people who avoided battle.

Aiyen shot happily into the darkness.

Ballak hunters could work from tiny traces of light and sound.

A cigarette ember was enough.

A breath or half-formed shout was enough.

Then Vikir caught Aiyen's arm.

“Enough.”

She stared at him.

“What?”

“Don't kill everyone. Leave survivors.”

“Why?”

“Not mercy.”

Vikir watched the scattered movement in the distance.

“A force this size may have a rear camp. Let some escape and follow them.”

Aiyen blinked.

Then smiled.

“……Passed again.”

Vikir still had no idea what she kept grading.

◆◆◆

His guess was correct.

An arrow had pierced Sir Smuggler's shoulder.

He fled through a narrow passage between rocks.

Beyond it lay a broad camp.

Another hundred mercenaries emerged from tents to cover the survivors.

“We can win in close combat!”

“Stack shields! Arrows can't get through!”

“Aura users forward!”

“Mages! Barriers!”

Protective magic rose across the camp.

Then something strange arced overhead.

Arrows.

Ropes tied behind them.

And at the ends of several ropes—

large wooden barrels.

“Oil?”

The barrels smashed against the ground and barriers.

Oil spread everywhere.

Then came fire arrows.

WHOOM.

Flames swallowed the camp.

Even men who escaped the fire had a greater problem.

Food.

Water.

Medicine.

Weapons.

All burned inside the tents.

Surviving the jungle afterward would be almost impossible.

Sir Smuggler shook with fury.

“They hung oil barrels from arrows? Since when are those savages this clever?”

Then he saw someone across the flames.

Vikir.

Sir Smuggler bared his teeth.

“You! This was your plan?”

Vikir glanced around at the burning camp.

“You're quick to notice.”

It was not a compliment.

Sir Smuggler charged.

Vikir picked up a bow.

TWANG.

The arrow buried itself low in Sir Smuggler's abdomen.

He screamed.

Vikir clicked his tongue.

“Not where I aimed.”

His archery was improving.

It was not Aiyen's.

He nocked another arrow.

Sir Smuggler waved a bloody hand.

“Wait! You can't kill me! You'll regret it!”

“Why?”

The merchant dragged a bloodstained document from inside his coat.

“An exploration permit from Underdog City. Genuine. Not forged. It has the deputy magistrate's seal. Baskerville authority is behind us.”

Vikir stopped.

“Show me.”

Sir Smuggler limped forward and held out the permit.

His other hand quietly moved toward a hidden knife.

Vikir wiped the soot from his face.

Sir Smuggler's eyes widened.

“You—”

The knife slipped from his hand.

Vikir looked down at it.

Then back at the permit.

“Revoked.”

With a soot-stained finger, he drew a cross through the authorization mark and declared the permit void.

The paper went into the fire.

THUNK.

An arrow struck Sir Smuggler between the eyes.

Then four more landed almost on the same point.

The merchant's head became unrecognizable.

Aiyen lowered her bow.

“Idiot.”

“Captain! Time to withdraw!”

Ahun's signal came from beyond the flames.

Aiyen scooped Vikir up before he could object and jumped onto Bakira.

The wolf raced away.

Behind them, surviving mercenaries rallied.

“They're retreating!”

“There weren't that many of them!”

“The fire is almost under control!”

“We can counterattack!”

Aiyen laughed contemptuously.

“Fools. They think the fire was for *them*.”

“They'll understand soon,” Vikir said.

The swamp changed.

Leaves across a wide area began leaning in the same direction.

Something enormous was moving through the dark.

BZZZZZZZZ—

A heavier darkness settled over the burning camp.

Noise.

Flame.

Explosions.

Screaming.

Exactly the kind of disturbance that attracted the nightmare of this swamp.

**Madam Eight-Legs.**

“Put out the fire!”

“What is that over there?”

“Something huge—”

“Help—!”

The screams vanished one after another.

Aiyen and Vikir rode Bakira as fast as the wolf could run.

They did not slow down.

Not until nothing at all could be heard behind them.
