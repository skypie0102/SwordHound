BOOM!

Vikir and Orca’s battle had destroyed everything around them.

Mountains.

Hills.

Outer walls.

Watchtowers.

Bridges.

And—

the solitary cells.

CRASH!

One after another—

the strange structures Souaré had built collapsed.

From most of them—

only dried—

skeletal corpses emerged.

There was one exception.

**Marquis de Sade.**

The single human whom even Level Nine’s harshest solitary confinement could not truly contain.

There were even old rumors that Nouvelle Vague—

as an institution—

existed largely because of him.

Vikir took one step backward.

A chill passed through his entire body.

Like countless snakes licking his skin.

The old man had not even looked directly at him.

*I should have understood the moment I heard the name Angajumang.*

Old Man Angajumang.

Marquis de Sade.

The war-mad aristocrat who once convinced forty-seven families to rebel against the Empire.

A man who wanted the age of blood—

where only strength determined survival—

to return.

He rolled his thin shoulders.

“Puhihihihi.”

“It has been a while since I moved properly.”

Every joint sounded like breaking wood.

Then—

“…Grandfather.”

Sadi called to him.

Her voice trembled.

Something almost unheard from her.

The old man looked over.

“Sadi?”

“My.”

“You grew.”

Sadi grinned.

“Hehehe.”

“I worked very hard to get you out.”

“You’ll praise me, right?”

“Of course.”

“Come here.”

“Let Grandpa hold you.”

Sadi—

covered in blood—

stood and embraced him.

The Marquis stroked her hair.

“I’m glad you survived.”

“I’m glad you’re all right too, Grandfather.”

“I thought our family line had ended.”

“Perhaps the sun finally rises again.”

“What?”

“I have no interest in finding a man.”

“I only said it because I have some interest in finding a woman.”

Both laughed.

For one absurd moment—

the ruined Level Ten battlefield felt like a peaceful café terrace.

Then Orca spoke.

“So the provocateur finally crawled out.”

The Marquis turned lazily.

“Still alive, White Whale?”

“You have no right to say that.”

Orca grabbed his club.

Then struck.

The Marquis laughed.

“Violence against an unarmed old man?”

“Cruel.”

He picked up Sadi’s fallen whip.

“Granddaughter.”

“I’ll borrow this.”

The club descended.

The whip rose.

BOOM!

Orca’s weighted club-head—

and the metal weight at the tip of the whip—

collided.

The explosion reshaped the battlefield again.

Stone split.

Reformed.

Twisted.

Shattered.

Then split again.

The shocking part—

was not the destruction.

It was that Marquis de Sade matched Orca.

A man who had spent decades inside Level-Nine solitary confinement—

still possessed that much power.

Orca lowered his stance.

His arm trembled.

The Marquis smiled down at him.

“…This reminds me of that day.”

“More than forty years now?”

The **Forty-Seven Riot**.

An uprising so enormous that—

although only forty-seven people had led it—

each was the head of a family.

Thus it became known as both the Forty-Seven-Man Riot—

and the Forty-Seven-Family Riot.

They had rebelled against the newly unified Empire.

Invaded the Imperial Palace.

Nearly taken the Emperor’s head.

Officially—

the motive had been ambition for the throne.

The Marquis laughed.

“I never cared about the throne.”

There had been only one reason.

**Fun.**

After Imperial unification—

the enormous wars stopped.

The battles where tens of thousands died in a day.

The clashes where powerful people tore each other apart.

Strength could no longer be used freely.

Revenge became illegal.

Grievances were handed to courts.

Rules.

Law.

Peace.

The Marquis despised it all.

He missed war.

Killing.

Plunder.

Revenge.

The collision of the strong.

The era where only strength guaranteed survival—

and life’s value was proved through violence.

The old Warring States Era.

“Ah.”

“I miss it.”

“I lived almost my entire life in that world.”

“Then—

in old age—

someone replaced it with boredom.”

“So I started a riot.”

“It was fun.”

“Then it ended.”

“And the boring world returned.”

He looked at the younger criminals around him.

“Soft.”

“Peace made them soft.”

“Lazy too.”

“So I thought—

perhaps solitary confinement would make a good vacation.”

“Cool my head.”

“Organize my thoughts.”

Orca glared.

“Then why leave now?”

The Marquis smiled.

“Because.”

“I hear the surface has become interesting again.”

Orca’s expression hardened.

That was the one thing he had not wanted the old monster to learn.

Before returning to Nouvelle Vague—

Orca had seen the surface.

The Gates.

The wars.

The collapsing order.

And the Marquis saw the truth in Orca’s face.

War.

Murder.

Massacre.

Blood.

Violence.

A world where the strong survived.

The kind of age he had spent decades longing for.

Like the fiercest days of the ancient Warring States Era—

the age of the great black mage **Tzersi**—

and the great swordsman **Ornati**.

“Puhihihihi!”

“At my age—

to feel my blood run hot again.”

“I think I’d like to go outside.”

“Help me, child?”

Sadi smiled.

“Of course, Grandfather.”

“I’ll escort you to the surface.”

A loving conversation.

Between family.

And yet—

madness hung around both smiles.

BOOM!

Orca struck the ground.

“You are not leaving.”

“Any of you.”

His hatred of criminals was naked.

“Social evil.”

“My duty is to prevent the chaos your existence creates.”

“To remove the fear you cause ordinary people.”

“Even if I die—

you remain here.”

The Marquis laughed.

“Friend.”

“How much do you think one life is worth?”

The two old monsters faced each other.

Orca lifted his club.

The Marquis coiled the whip like a sword.

Aura pressure fractured air and ground around them.

Aiyen leaned toward Vikir.

“What do we do?”

“Run?”

Vikir shook his head.

“No.”

“This is useful.”

Their collision—

could finish charging Poseidon.

*Timing.*

*That is all.*

Orca launched first.

Like an enraged whale.

The Marquis moved with him—

light as a ghost.

Both committed everything to a single strike.

Black aura gathered at Orca’s club.

A wave like a black tsunami.

The Marquis rotated the whip.

His aura followed—

a coiling serpent of explosive cutting force.

Then—

“Now!”

Vikir entered between them.

**Baskerville Eighth Form.**

**Black Sun.**

“…!?”

“…!?”

Too late for either old man to stop.

Orca’s black wave.

The Marquis’s cutting serpent.

Vikir’s Black Sun.

Aiyen moved.

“Brother!”

“Catch!”

BOOM!

like a bulb torn from soil—

LIGHT.

BOOOOOOM!

Vikir’s eardrums ruptured.

Crack.

breaking.
