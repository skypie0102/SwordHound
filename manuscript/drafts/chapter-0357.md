At the center of the Imperial Capital stood the Great Court—

the place where the Empire judged its most serious criminals.

Night Hound stood before it.

Expressionless.

Calm.

Contrary to everyone else’s fears—

Vikir felt almost light.

*Everything I needed to do at the Academy is finished.*

A great deal of work had been removed from his shoulders.

The plan had entered its final stage.

And so—

he arrived at the trial almost expectantly.

The Seven Great Families were all represented.

Baskerville.

Morgue.

Quovadis.

Bourgeois.

Leviathan.

Donquixote.

Usher.

Their representatives sat as jurors.

Other family members filled the audience.

The Emperor himself was absent.

An Imperial proxy—

dressed in the nun-like formal clothing associated with the Imperial Family—

conducted the proceedings with mechanical calm.

“Iron-Blooded Sword House Baskerville.”

“Juror Hugo Le Baskerville.”

Hugo sat in a wheelchair.

His back faced the court.

The proxy read the Baskerville accusations.

Rebellion.

Kin-slaying.

Attempts against members of the family.

The theft of the red military horn that could mobilize Baskerville’s armed forces.

“Does House Baskerville affirm these charges against Vikir?”

Hugo did not answer.

The poison still affecting him had left movement and speech difficult.

Osiris stood beside him.

He nodded.

Nothing else.

The Imperial proxy summarized Baskerville’s position.

“The crimes are grave.”

“However—

the accused carries Baskerville blood.”

“House Baskerville therefore requests that noble status be considered in sentencing.”

No objection followed.

The proxy turned to Morgue.

Raspane Morgue stood as that family’s juror.

Her expression was stiff.

Morgue’s position was almost the same.

The crimes were serious.

But Vikir—

as a member of great-noble blood—

was still entitled to noble treatment.

Raspane spoke.

“There is precedent.”

“Thirty-five years ago—

during the **Forty-Seven Men’s Riot**.”

The courtroom became quieter.

“That incident was more dangerous than this one.”

“The Empire itself might have fallen.”

“Two heroes were central to suppressing it.”

“Winston.”

“And Orca.”

“The first is dead.”

“The second is not present here.”

Raspane continued.

“Both asked for the ringleader to be executed.”

“But the Marquis de Sade was not sentenced to death.”

“Why?”

“Because even then—

the law preserved a degree of treatment for a great noble.”

Therefore—

in her judgment—

executing Vikir outright would exceed that precedent.

Raspane sat.

Next came Quovadis.

Pope Nabokov I.

He chuckled softly.

“That child is a believer in Rune.”

“We cannot ignore the dead.”

“But neither can we abandon mercy entirely.”

“I find the death penalty excessive.”

Damien Bourgeois reached the same conclusion.

“The defendant has already paid an astronomical bond.”

“That amount alone demonstrates a willingness to answer for his crimes.”

“I also consider execution excessive.”

Four Houses.

Baskerville.

Morgue.

Quovadis.

Bourgeois.

All argued against death.

Then the opposition spoke.

“This is absurd.”

“If someone who killed so many people is not executed—

who should be?”

“At minimum—

he must be isolated permanently.”

“And execution remains the cleanest answer.”

The Donquixote and Usher representatives were openly hostile.

Their patriarchs—

Cervantes and Roderick—

were still recovering from the Hell Tree incident.

So younger relatives attended instead.

**Donquixote La Mancha Passamonte.**

**Usher P. Madeline.**

Both served as family proxies.

And both were close relatives of Tudor and Bianca.

Finally—

the last voice.

Leviathan.

**Hobbes de Leviathan.**

His verdict was simple.

“…Death.”

“No lesser punishment is enough.”

The jury was split.

Four Great Families wanted mitigation.

Three demanded the extreme sentence.

Even the Imperial proxy looked troubled.

Then he turned to Vikir.

“Before judgment—

do you have anything to say?”

Vikir raised his head.

His gaze moved across the jurors.

Hugo.

Turned away in his wheelchair.

Osiris—

brow furrowed.

Raspane—

thinking about her daughter.

Nabokov.

Damien.

Then—

the remaining three.

Passamonte.

Madeline.

Hobbes.

Outwardly—

calm.

Lawful.

Severe.

Inside—

something else entirely.

*So the remaining Ten Corpses are finally gathering.*

Vikir watched them carefully.

The Gate of Destruction had originally required all ten demons.

Now—

only four remained alive.

That meant the survivors would likely need a more extreme method to complete the mission.

And before they attempted it—

they would certainly want Vikir removed.

*Good.*

*I’ll cooperate with that much.*

Their plan—

and Vikir’s—

did not conflict yet.

So Vikir remained silent.

The audience grew furious.

“Shameless!”

“He killed all those people!”

“He doesn’t regret anything!”

“I thought he was a hero!”

“Execute him!”

“Show everyone that justice still exists!”

The guards eventually forced the courtroom back into order.

The Imperial proxy asked again.

“For the final time.”

“Do you have anything to say before sentencing?”

This time—

Vikir answered.

He looked toward the Imperial proxy.

But the message was aimed elsewhere.

“Retreat is not flight.”

“And when danger exceeds hope—

simply waiting is not wisdom.”

“A wise man preserves tomorrow.”

“He does not gamble everything on a single day.”

An old saying from a courageous Donquixote knight.

Silence followed.

Passamonte.

Madeline.

Hobbes.

The three exchanged meaningful looks.

Then—

judgment.

The Imperial proxy stood.

“The defendant—

Vikir of House Baskerville.”

“A believer in Rune.”

“A son of Baskerville.”

“A man with ties to Quovadis.”

“A prospective son-in-law of Morgue.”

“A person whom Bourgeois recognizes through family obligation.”

He continued.

“However—

the defendant has committed repeated acts of murder.”

“The dead were people considered important to the security and development of the Empire.”

“The gravity of the crimes is therefore extraordinary.”

The courtroom held its breath.

“Considering the defendant’s noble lineage—

the death penalty is denied.”

Relief moved through Baskerville, Morgue, Quovadis, and Bourgeois.

Then—

“However.”

“Ordinary imprisonment would be meaningless.”

The other three Houses leaned forward.

The final sentence came.

“Vikir of House Baskerville is sentenced to—

**3,021 life sentences without parole.**”

“And confinement will not be in an ordinary prison.”

“He will be sent to a special facility designed for permanent isolation.”

A sentence beyond absurdity.

Even death—

then another life—

then another—

would not satisfy it.

The court intended that Vikir never return to society.

But Vikir cared about only one phrase.

*Special prison.*

There was only one place that could mean.

**Nouvelle Vague.**

The prison at the farthest edge of the Empire.

A place so terrible—

stories claimed even the souls of the dead remained trapped there.

More feared than execution.

And Vikir—

was being sent there for 3,021 life terms.

The four leniency factions fell silent.

The three harsher factions—

seemed almost satisfied.

Vikir stood at the center of all those emotions.

And quietly sharpened the blade inside his mind.

*Now we enter the final stage.*

Endgame.

Few pieces remained on the board.

And from this point forward—

every move would matter.
