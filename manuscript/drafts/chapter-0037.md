# Chapter 37: Slave Auction (3)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/037.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 37 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. **Boston Terrier Le Baskerville** and **Pit Bull Knights** follow current Fandom evidence. The tribal girl remains unnamed to preserve Chinese reveal chronology. **Infernal Buffalo ‘Murcielago’** follows the Chinese/transliterated source plus the independently aligned English witness; no dedicated current Fandom monster-name entry was located.

“The way to destroy an underground economy is actually simple.”

Vikir repeated what he had told Chihuahua before.

“You strike through the core hidden behind all the separate organizations.”

Underdog City's enormous underground economy had a place where the people controlling it gathered together.

Vikir had known about that place since before his regression.

But no matter how strong he was, erasing an organization of this scale by himself would have been difficult.

That was why he had prepared in advance.

◆◆◆

‘...Could I borrow them once?’

The conversation had taken place before Vikir left the Baskerville estate.

He had made his request clearly.

‘Could you lend me one of the knight orders?’

Hugo had thought for some time before granting permission.

For half a day, Vikir would be allowed to command one Baskerville knight order freely.

In other words, Hugo had handed him a limited portion of the clan's military authority.

‘I trust you won't do anything foolish, my son.’

Even with restrictions, entrusting military power carried enormous meaning.

Hugo was acknowledging Vikir as his son and treating him accordingly.

And so one of the Baskerville Clan's core military forces had come under Vikir's temporary command.

The **Pit Bull Knights**.

One hundred Sword Graduators.

An iron-blooded order said to leave no survivors once it was mobilized.

For six hours, all one hundred of them belonged to Vikir.

And now he was using them to sweep away Underdog City's corruption in a single operation.

‘Hugo is especially sensitive about military authority. I'm fortunate he agreed to lend me the Pit Bulls so easily.’

Hugo was intensely suspicious of territorial officials who gathered private soldiers.

Ordinarily, he would never surrender control of a knight order without a compelling reason.

Yet his trust in Vikir had apparently grown deeper than expected.

Vikir looked over the Pit Bull Knights standing behind him.

A fighting dog should never bare its fangs carelessly.

A third-rate dog trusted only its own strength and looked down on the enemy.

A second-rate dog reacted too quickly to every movement the enemy made.

A first-rate dog had patience and composure, though killing intent still filled its eyes.

But the fighting dog standing at the very peak did not react at all.

No matter how wildly the enemy shouted or threatened, it remained as still as something without life.

The virtue of the wooden fighting cock.

As though carved from timber.

By that measure, the Pit Bull Knights were indeed a perfectly trained combat unit.

They stood behind Vikir like wooden posts, waiting for his command.

Vikir gave it.

“Bite them all to death.”

Only then did the hundred hounds bare their fangs.

As the Pit Bull Knights surged forward, Vikir shouted after them.

“Don't kill anyone who lies still. But if they move even a little, execute them without mercy.”

It was also a warning to the enemy.

Vikir's voice carried across the entire auction hall.

People whose will to resist had already broken threw down their weapons and dropped flat to the floor, raising their hands and feet over their heads.

Most were not quick enough.

“Aaaaaagh!”

“Help me!”

A noblewoman screamed.

Her head was cut off before the scream ended.

It rolled beneath her blood-soaked fur coat.

A middle-aged man tried to flee and was cut open on the spot.

Men and women who ran, screamed, or continued resisting were killed one after another.

But lying quietly on the ground did not mean leaving unharmed.

“J-Just stay down. Once things calm down, we'll find a chance to run.”

Several people flattened themselves against the floor and held their hands and feet up.

They assumed it was a signal that they were ready to be shackled.

Then a Pit Bull Knight approached with a faint smile.

“We don't carry handcuffs.”

The people on the floor looked up, confused.

Before they could ask what would be used to restrain them—

Slash!

Slash!

The knight's sword passed over their limbs.

Wrists and ankles dropped to the floor.

“Aaaaaagh!”

“What are you doing?!”

Those who tried to crawl away collapsed like insects.

The Pit Bull Knight did not spare them another glance.

He simply moved on to the next target that was still moving.

Meanwhile, a dark-haired middle-aged man stood beside Vikir with a thick cigar between his lips.

His muscular body formed an inverted triangle.

Square jaw.

Heavy cheeks.

Dark glasses hiding his eyes.

Scars covering his face.

He was **Count Boston Terrier Le Baskerville**, Knight Commander of the Pit Bull Knights.

“Nephew. Satisfied?”

“Yes, Uncle.”

“Heh. Kid, if you need anything else, say the word.”

Boston Terrier was pleased with Vikir.

There was no complicated reason.

He simply liked how cleanly and decisively his nephew handled things.

Besides, Vikir had Hugo's favor. Building a good relationship with him carried no downside.

Vikir, for his part, had no reason to dislike Boston Terrier.

The Count had little interest in status, reputation, or political maneuvering.

He cared about blood and battle.

That was simple enough.

Chihuahua, standing nearby, spoke in a trembling voice.

“Deputy Magistrate... Is this really all right? They're nobles. Cutting off their wrists and ankles like that...”

“What does it matter? They'll be executed soon anyway. Handcuffs would be a waste. This is enough to keep them from escaping.”

Boston Terrier laughed even more loudly at the answer.

“Heh heh heh! Nephew, you have to join our Pit Bull Knights someday. Your uncle will raise you properly.”

Vikir answered only with a faint smile.

A minute after the Pit Bull Knights entered, the situation was over.

A clean sweep.

Underdog City's most important criminals were either dead or captured.

A few had been lucky enough not to attend tonight's auction, but that no longer mattered.

The ledgers and contact records taken from the people here would expose the rest.

“This... This is unprecedented! Absolutely perfect!”

Chihuahua shook with excitement as he watched criminals being dragged away.

The war on crime had been declared suddenly.

And then it had ended with astonishing speed.

There were still remnants to clean up, but compared with what had already been accomplished, that would be trivial.

Even the local families whose heirs had been executed would find it difficult to protest after tonight.

Underdog City's future suddenly looked cleaner and brighter.

“Congratulations! Now no one in the city will dare oppose Lord Vikir—...?”

Chihuahua turned eagerly.

The person he meant to congratulate was already gone.

◆◆◆

‘...It should be around here.’

Vikir had returned to the rear of the auction grounds.

The slaughter on the far side of the stage was still continuing.

While everyone else focused on the Pit Bull Knights, Vikir slipped into the warehouse where the auction merchandise had been stored.

Gold and silver treasures were piled everywhere.

The nobles who attended the auction had paid in cash.

“...Tax revenue is secure.”

All of it would be transferred into the city treasury.

Baskerville's financial position would only grow stronger.

The thought of Hugo being pleased left Vikir with mixed feelings, but that was irrelevant for now.

He searched farther inside.

Ledgers.

Receipts.

Promissory notes.

Records piled high.

They would become valuable evidence when the remaining criminal organizations were hunted down.

Vikir set them aside.

But they were not what he had actually come to find.

He grabbed a white cloth covering something in one corner and pulled it away.

“...Hm?”

It was not his target.

An iron cage stood there.

The feral tribeswoman from the failed auction was still inside.

The girl who had fought so violently before was now curled up in a corner.

“...Ngh.”

A weak groan escaped her.

Looking more closely, Vikir saw more than malnutrition.

Her body was covered in fresh signs of abuse.

Whip marks crossed her skin.

‘They punished her after she bit off that trader's finger.’

Vikir clicked his tongue.

Then he took a bottle of medicine reserved for the knights from his belt.

He opened the cage and poured the potion over her injuries.

Hisssss—

New flesh knitted together as the wounds rapidly closed.

The girl opened her eyes.

The instant she realized Vikir was inside the cage, she recoiled into the corner again.

Vikir studied her silently.

Black hair streaked with silver.

Pointed ears.

Charcoal ash smeared over her face.

And familiar bite and claw scars across her arms and thighs.

‘Cerberus?’

A memory returned.

When Vikir was eight, he had crossed the safety boundary during his practical exam and encountered the A+ Danger Rating monster Cerberus in Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

At the time, Cerberus's flank had been covered in old arrow-shaped wounds.

‘Could she belong to the tribe that hunted that Cerberus?’

It was a reasonable possibility.

By now, the girl's injuries had healed enough for some of her strength to return.

She crouched in the corner and glared at him.

Vikir spoke one short word in her language.

“Go.”

Her eyes widened.

Vikir shrugged.

He knew a little of the languages used by the mountain tribes.

Before his regression, he had fought them countless times throughout the mountains.

The girl only stared at him when he told her to escape.

Vikir placed the half-empty potion bottle on the ground and stepped out of the cage.

Only then did she cautiously follow.

“...”

She watched him for several seconds.

Then she tore through the tent and fled.

She took the remaining potion with her.

At the last moment, the girl shouted something back.

She spoke too quickly for Vikir to understand.

“Fine. Now to find what I actually came for.”

He turned back toward the warehouse.

Gold and silver.

Ledgers and receipts.

Bills of exchange.

Antiques.

Artwork.

None of them were his true target.

“There it is.”

At the deepest corner of the auction storage area, Vikir pulled away a red cloth.

A huge horned monster lay beneath it.

Beelzebub at Vikir's wrist trembled with hunger.

In the distance, the clash of Pit Bull blades and the screams of their enemies were drawing closer.

Vikir looked down at the carcass.

He would have to finish his meal and leave quickly.
