# Chapter 77: The Hound of the Night (3)

The residence door opened.

No person stood behind it.

Only a stone statue.

A woman's voice came from its mouth.

“What business brings you here?”

Dolores.

She was communicating through the statue rather than appearing herself.

Vikir understood why.

His current appearance was not reassuring.

Broad black hat.

Bird-beaked plague mask.

Heavy black cloak.

He approached the statue.

“I came to report a plague spreading through the slums.”

He placed a bucket of well water before it.

The water carried the unmistakable taint of Red Death.

A priest with sufficient divine sensitivity should notice immediately.

Silence.

The statue stopped speaking.

Then—

SLAM.

The door behind Vikir closed.

So did the others.

SLAM. SLAM. SLAM.

Five exits sealed.

The entrance hall transformed into a containment chamber.

White-armored paladins appeared along the second-floor gallery.

Vikir looked around.

A reception hall in normal times.

A battlefield when necessary.

No useful cover.

No open corridor.

Paladins above.

Judgment below.

He had expected something like this.

“Who are you?”

A deep voice came from the one passage that remained open.

A huge man entered.

More than two meters tall.

Shaved head.

Scars.

White armor and cloth.

No eyebrows.

Large nose.

Large mouth.

His eyes bent slightly as though smiling, but nothing about his presence felt gentle.

He carried a massive holy book in arms thick as logs.

Vikir recognized him immediately.

*Mozgus Quovadis.*

Inquisitor.

A man Vikir had fought beside during the Age of Destruction.

A comrade whose divine power had once illuminated battlefields and who had died fighting demons.

Vikir also remembered something less sentimental.

*Mozgus taught me a few interrogation methods.*

Those memories belonged only to Vikir.

This Mozgus had never met him.

The inquisitor raised his book.

“You claim to report plague, yet you hide your face and dress like a criminal.”

His voice hardened.

“Remove the mask. Show proper respect. Then perhaps you may meet the Saintess.”

“I can't remove it.”

Vikir gestured toward the bucket.

“I delivered the warning. My business is finished.”

He raised both hands and began stepping back.

“Blasphemer!”

Mozgus attacked.

The enormous holy book came down like a hammer.

Vikir moved inside the swing.

BOOM.

Stone cracked where the book struck.

White aura ran along the edges of its pages.

Vikir assessed him.

*Mid to High Sword Graduator equivalent.*

Weaker than the Mozgus Vikir remembered from the future.

But this was Quovadis territory.

Mozgus could replenish and heal himself with divine power.

Under these conditions, the fight would not be simple.

Mozgus growled.

“That water reeks of evil. If it truly contains plague, perhaps *you* spread it.”

“If I were the culprit, why report it?”

“To deceive us. Or to lure the Saintess out.”

Vikir shrugged.

Reasonable.

“I saw suspicious people spreading something in the slums.”

“And you look more suspicious than any of them.”

Mozgus opened the book.

Pages snapped in the air.

White aura sharpened their edges into blades.

“Ever been cut by paper?”

Stone statues split as the pages swept through the hall.

Vikir slipped between the attacks.

Ballak footwork.

Silent changes of direction.

Predatory timing.

Then Beelzebub extended from his wrist.

Dense red aura gathered around it.

Several paladins flinched.

“A Graduator?”

Vikir met Mozgus's white aura head-on.

Mozgus attempted to overwhelm him physically.

Vikir activated Beelzebub's stored Oxbear ability.

**Kilogram Hammer.**

His weight surged toward six hundred kilograms.

Mozgus pushed.

Vikir did not move.

The inquisitor's eyes widened.

*Like pushing a boulder.*

Vikir struck.

Not a public Baskerville pattern.

A hidden predatory application.

Six Fangs.

The red arcs tore through Mozgus's aura and traced past his head, neck, shoulders, and waist.

Mozgus tried to raise the holy book as a shield.

Too slow.

“If you wanted a shield, you should've used it earlier.”

Beelzebub cut across the back of his hand.

Then Vikir released **Incinerate — Cerberus (A+)**.

Dark infernal fire clung to the wound.

Mozgus roared and rolled across the floor.

Water and sand would not easily extinguish Cerberus flame.

The holy book's torn pages began burning too.

Vikir lowered his weapon.

He had not come here to kill anyone.

Mozgus had other ideas.

“Blasphemer!”

Divine power surged.

Water gathered around the statues.

Mozgus converted it into holy water and poured it over the burning hand.

The flame finally died under the volume of divine power.

But the exchange had already established the balance.

Mozgus looked down.

His book was gone.

Vikir stood on it.

“You're standing on scripture!”

“You throw it and hit people with it.”

Mozgus had no immediate answer.

Vikir extended Beelzebub.

“Again?”

Mozgus clenched his teeth.

He was already a formidable Graduator.

Few people in the Empire could pressure him this badly.

He gathered aura around both arms and charged.

Vikir did not meet him directly.

He kicked the fallen book upward.

Mozgus turned instinctively as the spinning volume crossed his vision.

For less than a second, the broad face of the book blocked his view.

Vikir disappeared.

*Where—*

A voice came from behind him.

“Looking for me?”

Mozgus spun.

Too late.

Vikir's aura struck like a hammer.

CRACK.

Heavy plate broke.

Chain beneath it shattered.

Mozgus flew through three stone statues before crashing down.

The paladins on the gallery stared.

Their inquisitor—

their military pillar—

had been beaten.

Then training overcame shock.

Swords came free.

“Seize the blasphemer!”

White aura rose across the hall.

Vikir adjusted his mask.

“Rough treatment for a lost lamb.”

The paladins surrounded him.

Among the broken statues, Mozgus stood again.

His armor hung in pieces.

Blood ran down his body.

His expression remained furious.

Vikir drew Beelzebub farther.

High Sword Graduator aura thickened around the blade.

The room became still.

Mozgus understood.

One against one, he had lost.

Many against one—

the outcome was uncertain.

And the Saintess was still inside the residence.

A reckless full battle could put her at risk.

Before either side moved—

“Enough.”

The voice cut through the killing intent.

Everyone looked up.

A figure had appeared on the third-floor balcony.

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

The Saintess had finally come in person.
