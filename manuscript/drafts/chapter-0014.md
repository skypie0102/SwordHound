# Chapter 14: Solitary (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/014.txt`. Canonical English forms for Hugo Le Baskerville, Vikir Van Baskerville, John Barrymore, Cerberus, the 10,000 Book Library, Sword Master, and Baskerville Fangs are normalized from current project/Fandom evidence. The practical examination was over.

Now came the hounds’ rewards.

Ordinary hunting dogs were rewarded with portions of the prey they brought down—meat and organs divided according to rank.

The children of Baskerville were much the same.

During the practical examination, they had taken pieces from the monsters they hunted and turned them into trophies: shields, blades, necklaces, and other keepsakes.

The young hounds emerged from the ordeal more mature than before, hardened in both body and mind.

The tense war of nerves that had lasted through the competition disappeared once the rankings were decided.

If anything, many of the children seemed closer afterward.

Perhaps spending so long together under extreme conditions had created a strange sense of brotherhood throughout the class.

Of course, some siblings still hated one another.

But most grudges had either been settled during the examination… or one side had died quietly somewhere along the way.

As a result, there was surprisingly little infighting once everyone returned.

For a few days every year, the Baskerville Clan enjoyed this unusual calm after its practical examinations.

◆◆◆

In the patriarch’s office, Hugo Le Baskerville listened to a report from Head Butler John Barrymore.

“… Who hunted what?”

A Sword Master had such perfect command of his body that doubting his own senses was almost unthinkable.

At that moment, however, Hugo was unmistakably doubting his ears.

Barrymore, still unable to hide his excitement, read the report again.

“Vikir Van Baskerville, eight-year-old class. During the practical examination, he ‘accidentally’ crossed the boundary and entered the forbidden zone. He successfully hunted Cerberus, an A+ Danger Rating monster. End of report.”

It was the second time Barrymore had said it aloud.

It still sounded absurd.

An eight-year-old had entered the forbidden zone and returned alive.

That alone would have been an extraordinary achievement.

Even testimony about the terrain and ecosystem beyond the boundary—or a rough map drawn from firsthand observation—would have been tremendously valuable.

Yet this unprecedented eight-year-old had not merely gone into that hell and survived.

He had killed Cerberus.

“… Cerberus is a high-ranking monster even our guardian knights find troublesome.”

Hugo stroked his beard with a disbelieving expression.

The detailed report that followed was even harder to accept.

He lifted the document again and put on his reading glasses.

“He lured Cerberus into a trap and killed it with a spear? Does that sound reasonable to you?”

“It was already wounded by barbarians, my lord. The spear had also been coated with poison.”

“Wounded or not, what poison can kill Cerberus? Where did he get something like that?”

“… The hunting log contains no further details. I intended to ask the young master, but he said he was exhausted and went straight back to his quarters.”

Hugo froze for an instant.

Then he burst out laughing.

“The boy already knows how to hide his cards. Information is power, and power has value. A man must raise his own value and protect it himself.”

He looked back at the report.

Vikir had not immediately revealed what poison he had used.

Nor had he dragged Cerberus’s enormous corpse straight to the Guide Dogs and announced his achievement.

Instead, he had concealed the body under the earth until the examination ended.

Barrymore was impressed by that almost as much as the kill itself.

“His patience is remarkable. If I had killed Cerberus at eight years old, I would have run straight to the guardian knights just to be praised and noticed.”

“Heh. Then you would have ended up like old Santiago.”

The old man Hugo referred to was a fisherman from an ancient tale.

He once caught an enormous fish far out at sea, only for sharks to strip away all its flesh while he towed it home. In the end, he returned with nothing but a massive skeleton.

“If Vikir had gone around boasting that he killed Cerberus while dragging that enormous carcass behind him, every ambitious brother nearby would have targeted him. He might have lost more than the prize. He might have been killed.”

Barrymore was reminded once again of just how merciless Baskerville family custom could be.

“To be frank, I was surprised. I never expected such talent to emerge among children bearing the middle name ‘Van’.”

“You still don’t understand me, Butler.”

Hugo’s tone remained flat.

“I do not worship bloodline the way the previous patriarch did. Good blood produces failures. Low blood produces excellence. I do not care whether a son was born from an imperial princess or a street prostitute. Talent and nerve are what matter.”

His gaze drifted through the window toward a distant tower.

His second son.

Barrymore realized Hugo was thinking of the boy currently secluded in training at the top of that tower.

“Even excellent blood can produce something useless. Understand?”

“… My apologies.”

Hugo waved a hand.

“Forget it. Worrying about a foolish son only costs me. I could use a change of mood.”

His eyes returned to the report.

Barrymore understood immediately and bowed his head.

“I will summon Young Master Vikir.”

◆◆◆

Several hours later, Vikir stood before Hugo.

Hugo did not bother with the head or tail of the matter.

He went straight for the meat.

“How did you kill Cerberus?”

“I coated the spear with chocolate and caught it in a trap.”

Vikir was just as direct.

Question.

Answer.

There was no wasted fat in a conversation between these two.

A flicker of interest passed through Hugo’s eyes.

“Chocolate?”

“It is poison to canine monsters.”

“So that is why you asked for chocolate before?”

“Yes.”

Vikir’s answer was short.

Hugo considered it for a moment.

“If that information is true, it is extremely valuable. It will be useful in subjugations against canine monsters. It could improve expedition results, and an information guild would pay well for it.”

“I agree.”

The calm reply lifted one corner of Hugo’s mouth.

Then his voice cooled slightly.

“I heard that when the butler questioned you, you refused to tell him the truth about killing Cerberus.”

“Yes. Because he is not my master.”

“Your master?”

“Yes.”

“Then who is your master?”

Vikir answered without hesitation.

“I belong to this clan. Wouldn’t that make the master of the clan my master?”

Hugo finally nodded, openly satisfied.

“You learned well. As a reward, the entire corpse of the monster you hunted will belong to you.”

An A+ monster like Cerberus was worth a fortune even in death.

Its teeth and claws could become weapons.

Its organs and meat could be made into strengthening medicines or food.

Its bones and hide could become armor.

Not a single part of a high-ranking monster needed to be wasted.

Receiving the entire corpse was already an enormous prize.

Hugo offered another.

“You placed first in both the written and practical examinations. The clan has high expectations of you. Tell me something you want.”

“Did you not already give me Cerberus’s corpse?”

“That was what I wanted to give you. I am asking what you want.”

Vikir’s eyes widened slightly.

Hugo almost never asked his children what they thought.

You gave orders to a hound.

Who asked a dog for its opinion?

But unexpected opportunities were always welcome.

Vikir immediately realized that his goal might be easier to reach than expected.

“I would like permission to enter the 10,000 Book Library.”

“…!”

Hugo’s eyes narrowed.

The 10,000 Book Library lay deep within the Baskerville Clan’s main castle.

It was one of the largest libraries in the empire, vast enough to be compared with the great library of the Morgue Clan.

Hugo rubbed his chin and fell briefly into thought—an unusually long hesitation for him.

“… The 10,000 Book Library. Only the Clan Head, the Vice Clan Head, and members of the direct bloodline are permitted inside. Did you know that when you asked?”

This time Vikir’s surprise was genuine.

He had not known.

‘In my previous life, I never had any reason—or opportunity—to enter it.’

The clan had given him enough lessons in etiquette, swordsmanship, and practical knowledge to perform his duties.

He had been content with that.

He had never wanted to learn more than his master required.

That had been considered a virtue in a hound.

That was how thoroughly he had been trained.

“… I didn’t know. If it is impossible, I will give up.”

Vikir retreated immediately.

It did not matter much.

If necessary, he could always sneak inside under cover of darkness.

But the problem resolved itself more easily than he expected.

“You may enter.”

Hugo decided quickly.

Before Vikir could react, he continued.

“I cannot give you unlimited time. Will ten days be enough?”

“One day is enough.”

There was no reason to linger in the library long enough to make Hugo suspicious.

Hugo looked down at him with the faintest smile.

“What can you possibly read in one day?”

“I learned about the clan’s history in my general studies. I wanted to know more about it—as a Baskerville.”

It was an answer Vikir had invented on the spot.

Hugo appeared to like it.

“Clan history. Good. Learning the glorious history of Baskerville and taking pride in it is worthwhile.”

Pride had never even entered Vikir’s answer.

Hugo went further and recommended a book himself.

“In the deeper section of the 10,000 Book Library, look at the middle shelf in the Sixth Restricted Area. There is a manual for the **Baskerville Sixth Fang**. Read it.”

Vikir wondered whether he had heard correctly.

Hugo was personally recommending an advanced swordsmanship manual to him?

The Sixth Fang.

A level far beyond the four Fangs normally accessible to someone of Vikir’s standing.

Swordsmanship capable of drawing six Fangs was reserved for high-ranking members of the clan and elite direct descendants.

Yet Hugo was allowing Vikir to read it, even if only briefly.

The present Hugo could draw seven Fangs.

The Hugo Vikir remembered from before his regression had eventually reached nine.

Compared with the four-Fang swordsmanship Vikir had been allowed to learn in his first life, the Sixth Fang belonged to an entirely different level.

But Vikir had come here for something else.

‘… Only the Sixth Fang.’

Anyone who heard that thought would have been stunned.

Vikir was not foolish enough to show it on his face.

“Thank you. I will study it seriously and do my best to meet your expectations.”

He bowed with impeccable humility and respect.

If Hugo had known what Vikir truly intended to find inside, he might have burned every secret manual in the 10,000 Book Library rather than let him enter.

“…”

Believing the matter settled, Hugo casually turned away.

Vikir offered one final respectful farewell and left the patriarch’s office.

Then he headed directly toward what might have been the single most valuable treasure in the entire Baskerville Clan.
