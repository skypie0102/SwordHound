The five-star assignment ended just like that.

Far too easily.

*I expected it to be simple.*

*I did not expect it to be this simple.*

Vikir was turning away when Hugo called after him.

“Right.”

“Son.”

“Since you finally came home, you may as well receive a promotion.”

Vikir’s eyebrow twitched.

A promotion.

A formal rise in rank within House Baskerville.

Hugo continued soothing Pomeranian as though what came next were nothing important.

“From this moment on, I appoint you as a Senator.”

“…”

Vikir fell silent.

A Senator.

A core position beneath the family’s highest governing authority and above the lower representatives.

A rank with command authority over countless Baskerville hounds.

Lower-ranking representatives primarily dealt with internal family affairs.

Senators, however, handled external matters as well.

Their responsibilities were heavier.

And so was their authority.

In practical terms, very few people stood above them.

The Head of House.

The Deputy Head.

And, depending on how one counted the Seven Counts and the retired elders of the Senate, almost no one else in active service.

Within Baskerville’s functioning power structure—

Senators were among the people who actually moved the House.

“You are probably the youngest Senator this family has had in almost a century.”

“Thank you.”

Vikir nodded.

“I will do my best.”

Pomeranian had made Hugo look ridiculous lately.

But Hugo had not become stupid.

Promoting Vikir was not sentimentality alone.

Hugo had already thought through what Vikir would do with the rank.

“Even as a Senator, you may continue moving freely for the time being.”

“Your current position remains Imperial Capital Special Envoy.”

At present, House Baskerville did not officially treat Vikir as an ordinary Academy first-year.

He was an operative.

Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro had genuinely gone to the capital to study.

Vikir’s mission was different.

He had been sent there to identify hostile forces around the Imperial Capital—

and quietly remove them.

Being a Colosseo student was only one layer of cover.

Hugo continued.

“You may extend the mission as long as you judge necessary.”

“When you believe the work is finished, return to the family and take up administrative duties.”

“The magistrate’s seat remains open.”

“Yes.”

And so—

at eighteen—

Vikir formally became one of House Baskerville’s real centers of power.

A Senator came with enormous privileges.

First—

he could appoint up to eight aides at his own discretion.

Becoming such an aide meant formally entering House Baskerville.

It also granted the right to use the Baskerville surname.

More than eight aides required notification and approval from the House.

A Senator could teach those aides up to the Fourth Fang of Baskerville swordsmanship.

The House would provide medical treatment.

Food.

Lodging.

And other institutional support.

Aides received generous salaries.

Vehicles.

Travel expenses.

Tax exemptions.

Access to knightly manpower.

And a range of additional privileges.

The Senator himself could access second-class and higher Imperial military secrets.

In wartime—

he had authority to conscript civilians.

He could independently command troops assigned or conscripted under his authority.

In other words—

he could build his own armed force.

Now that Vikir was a Senator—

he could form a personal task force.

He already had several people in mind.

*Cindywendy.*

*Chihuahua.*

*MiniPin.*

Cindywendy—

a specialist in economics and finance.

Chihuahua—

an administrative master.

MiniPin—

an expert in terrain and monster ecology, with enough combat ability to be useful in the field.

Together—

they would be extremely valuable.

*And there are many capable people among the jungle tribes too.*

Aiyen’s face came to mind first.

An archer capable of killing a mosquito from hundreds of feet away.

And beyond her—

the jungle held many warriors and shamans whose abilities would shock the Empire.

Vikir closed his eyes.

For a moment—

he pictured a force that did not yet exist.

*First, I’ll need Cindywendy’s help.*

Until now, Vikir had refused to lean on her even when he needed money.

But that would not last much longer.

Soon—

he would need her financial power.

It was the instinct of a veteran hunter.

Hugo interrupted his thoughts.

“So.”

“What results have you produced in the Imperial Capital?”

Vikir gave the answer Hugo expected.

“As reported.”

“Last quarter, I eliminated the principal members of three hostile groups.”

“The eunuch faction that falsely reported to the Emperor that Baskerville intended rebellion.”

“The maternal-relative faction that tried to sever Baskerville’s finances through embezzlement and tax manipulation.”

“And the core members of an intelligence guild attempting to steal Baskerville military secrets.”

He continued evenly.

“Over three months, one hundred seventy-four people were removed.”

“Every death was made to appear natural, accidental, the work of another hostile faction, or an unexplained disappearance.”

“No usable trail was left behind.”

All 174 had been connected to demons.

Vikir had not touched people who merely opposed Baskerville politically without demonic involvement.

Even with that limitation—

the result was enormous.

Hugo smiled with clear satisfaction.

Then—

“Not that.”

“?”

“You’re attending the Academy.”

Hugo looked at him strangely.

“Have you met any girls?”

“??”

“I hear young people from other Houses go to school and start relationships.”

“Apparently there is even a term for couples from the same campus.”

“???”

Vikir frowned.

Hugo laughed.

“Osiris seems to have a woman he is seeing too.”

“But he never lets himself get caught.”

“And when I ask, he refuses to answer.”

Hugo sighed.

“At this rate, when am I supposed to see daughters-in-law?”

“…”

“You should make an effort too.”

“Marrying early is best.”

A chill ran through Vikir.

This Hugo—

was deeply unfamiliar.

The difference from the man in Vikir’s previous life was almost unsettling.

He changed the subject immediately.

“…What happened with the request I made?”

“Ah.”

Hugo stroked his carefully maintained beard.

“Erasing your past.”

Before enrolling at Colosseo—

Vikir had asked Hugo to erase traces of his former public identity.

The statues.

The honors.

The evidence that Vikir Van Baskerville had existed openly as one of the family’s hounds.

“Rumors of your survival have been suppressed.”

Hugo spoke calmly.

“Public opinion now treats the idea that ‘Vikir Van Baskerville survived and returned’ as a fabrication created by Baskerville to exaggerate its prestige.”

Vikir nodded.

Countless Baskerville hounds had appeared briefly—

then disappeared.

Too many were born.

Too many died.

That violent cycle had slowed after Vikir changed Hugo.

But in the public mind—

Vikir was still only a half-remembered rumor.

Imperial commendations were issued in huge numbers every year.

Most honors were remembered longest by the people who received them.

Everyone else moved on.

The people of Underdog City might still mourn and remember him—

but that alone was not enough to expose him.

“…So my past is effectively gone.”

“Yes.”

“Our special agents are handling the remaining traces.”

“You need not worry about rumors exposing you.”

Hugo’s expression remained casual.

“A few particularly noisy people have already been dealt with.”

He left the decision to Vikir.

Whether to continue hiding his Baskerville identity.

And when to reveal it.

The promotion ceremony might have ended there.

Quietly.

Almost anticlimactically.

Then—

“Samchun!”

Pomeranian burst into the conversation.

“This one!”

Vikir looked down.

She was holding a book.

**<100 Myths Children Love>**

Pomeranian opened it and excitedly showed him one chapter.

**<The Return of the Magic Hound>**

An ancient myth.

So old that almost no one believed it anymore.

The tale concerned one of House Morgue’s distant ancestors—

Morgue Tzersi.

Pomeranian pointed at an illustration.

A dead tree.

Blackened.

Withered.

The **Wraith Tree**.

A legendary artifact coveted by black mages.

The story claimed that the great mage Tzersi had planted the strange tree.

“Want it!”

Pomeranian looked up at Vikir with shining eyes.

Vikir slowly turned his head.

Hugo’s eyes were shining too.

“I do not know what it is.”

Hugo spoke with absolute seriousness.

“But if she wants it, bring it back.”

“…”

“I would go myself.”

“But House Morgue is unstable right now because of changes at the top of the Dark Faction.”

“And Baskerville cannot afford to ignore an opportunity like that.”

The two Houses were engaged in a tense power struggle.

Morgue’s internal disorder gave Baskerville room to expand influence.

Hugo could not leave.

Instead—

he threw something toward Vikir.

Snap.

Vikir caught it.

A **Black Whistle**.

An object that represented authority over the entirety of Baskerville’s military strength.

For once—

Vikir was caught off guard.

Hugo was completely serious.

“If necessary, take every knight in the House.”

“Go get what Pomeranian wants.”

He paused.

“Actually, you may as well count that as another assignment.”

Vikir sighed softly.

Did Hugo understand what he had just authorized?

*The Wraith Tree is real.*

And Vikir knew where it was.

His memories from before regression had preserved the location.

*It is not far from Baskerville territory.*

More importantly—

Vikir had already planned to visit that place.

Because—

the swordsmanship he now possessed stopped at the Seventh Fang.

The next clue was there.

*The realm beyond Sword Master.*

*And the clue to Baskerville’s Eighth Fang.*

He had intended to investigate during his return home anyway.

Now he had also been given military authority over House Baskerville.

There was no downside.

The Academy scroll assignment was already complete.

All that remained afterward was reaching the joint vault and confronting the Seventh Corpse—

Decarabia.

If Vikir could obtain a clue to the Eighth Fang first—

even better.

Pomeranian, it seemed—

had brought him extraordinary luck.

“Then I will leave immediately.”

Vikir gently patted her head.

Then turned away.

Fast.

Decisive.

He intended to finish everything within three days—

and return to the Academy.
