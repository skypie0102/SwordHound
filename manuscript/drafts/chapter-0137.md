# Chapter 137: Test Your Skills (5)

Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Tudor Donquixote, Sancho Barataria, Highbro/Middlebro/Lowbro Le Baskerville, Baskerville Trident, Naphtali, Styx blessing, Cold Department A/B, and the Age of Destruction.

A **buzzer beater** was a special Naphtali rule.

If the ball had already left a player's body when the end signal sounded—

a score made immediately afterward still counted.

Vikir's throw had left his hand almost exactly as time expired.

The result—

Class A: 1.

Class B: 1.

Tie.

Overtime.

On the ground—

Tudor finally woke again.

*What is happening?*

He still could not explain the previous collisions.

Tripping?

No.

Tudor did not make mistakes that simple.

Questions piled up.

Then—

a large hand settled on his shoulder.

“I'll handle him.”

**Sancho Barataria.**

Scholarship student chosen from the northern mercenary guilds.

Since enrollment—

Tudor had quickly come to regard him as a reliable companion.

Sancho normally avoided taking a major role in games against students his own age.

His physical strength made the contest too one-sided.

Now—

he stepped forward.

Tudor blinked.

“You're leaving goal?”

Sancho looked toward Vikir.

“A friend has been hit twice.”

“How can I sit back?”

Tudor looked moved.

He slapped Sancho lightly on the arm.

Overtime began.

Golden-goal rules.

The first team to score—

won.

Because the match counted toward practical assessment—

everyone stopped holding back.

“This is it!”

“All of Class B forward!”

“Defenders too!”

“Stop them!”

“Break through!”

The entire field became a collision of bodies.

Then—

“Someone take it!”

Highbro found a gap and passed.

He chose a uniform—

not a face.

The ball flew.

And landed in Vikir's hands.

“…?”

Vikir looked at Highbro.

The message in his eyes was simple.

*Should I lose it?*

Highbro's face changed.

“…Sorry.”

He lowered his gaze.

Middlebro and Lowbro did the same.

The triplets' sudden retreat confused Class A—

and boosted its confidence.

Tudor and Sancho charged.

“Why did they suddenly back off?”

Tudor grinned.

“Maybe our pressure finally got to them.”

Sancho moved first.

Thud.

Thud.

Thud.

The northern warrior came forward like a charging bull.

His arms locked against Vikir's chest.

Then—

Sancho's expression changed.

For one instant—

the field vanished.

A childhood memory replaced it.

A boulder.

Far larger than his own small body.

A stone he had once tried to move during training.

Again.

Again.

Again.

No matter how hard he pushed—

nothing happened.

That same feeling returned now.

*What is this?*

Vikir's body looked lean.

Ordinary.

But under Sancho's hands—

it felt like an enormous rock.

He pushed harder.

Vikir moved slightly.

Sancho's eyes lit up.

*There.*

He drove forward.

Then Vikir planted one foot.

And everything changed.

The “rock” became a mountain.

Sancho's arms trembled.

He could not move Vikir even a fraction.

*Impossible.*

Sancho had never lost a contest of pure strength.

Not against people his age.

Often not against older warriors.

The frozen north had produced him.

Strength was the one thing he had always trusted.

He pushed again.

Nothing.

Vikir held the ball easily.

Used no mana.

He simply thought.

*Should I let him knock me down?*

His body carried the Styx blessing.

Bones.

Muscles.

Ligaments.

Everything had been strengthened since childhood.

His prior-life experience had hardened the way he used that body.

Baskerville blood—

plus a soul trained through countless battles.

If Vikir resisted seriously—

Sancho would not move him.

But there was no reason to win openly here.

So—

Vikir stepped back.

“Oh.”

His balance gave way.

He fell.

The ball went flying.

Immediately—

everyone chased it.

“Sancho took the ball away!”

“Wait.”

“Is the other guy okay?”

“Who cares?”

“Get the ball!”

The match moved on.

Only Sancho remained still for a moment.

His palms hurt.

*What was that?*

The opponent had fallen.

Yes.

But before that—

for one impossible moment—

Sancho had felt as though he were trying to push a mountain.

Tudor noticed his face.

“That guy's body is absurdly hard.”

Sancho nodded slowly.

“Not just a bookworm.”

“His base physique might be better than ours.”

Tudor looked back.

“Maybe.”

“If he learned to use mana properly—”

Sancho frowned.

“Everything would change.”

“What a waste.”

They ran after the play.

Not long afterward—

Class B scored the golden goal.

Highbro finished.

Middlebro and Lowbro created the opening.

Baskerville's triplets received most of the cheers.

Fizz—

Class B students sprayed carbonated drinks over one another.

Laughing.

Celebrating.

Vikir stayed slightly outside the cluster.

Then—

splash.

Something cold poured over his head.

“…?”

He looked up.

Tudor and Sancho stood there—

both already soaked.

Tudor grinned.

“How can the hidden hero of Class B stand off by himself?”

“If you hadn't made that buzzer beater—”

“they never would have reached overtime.”

He flipped the bottle upside down and laughed.

Then his expression changed.

“Honestly.”

“I was jealous after your answer in Professor Banshee's class.”

“I tried to embarrass you during practical.”

Tudor scratched his cheek.

“Instead—”

“I embarrassed myself.”

He bowed his head slightly.

“Sorry.”

Vikir nodded.

No lecture.

No resentment.

He remembered another Tudor.

The Tudor of the Age of Destruction.

A hero who fought through endless campaigns.

Killed countless demons.

Saved civilians.

Helped close the Gate.

Then died.

Vikir remembered that man—

and accepted the apology from the student standing in front of him.

Sancho stepped closer.

“What kind of training did you do?”

He poked Vikir's shoulder.

“Your body is ridiculous.”

“I thought you were using mana.”

Vikir smiled faintly.

“If I'd used mana—”

“I wouldn't have beaten you.”

“My mana capacity is pathetic.”

Tudor winced.

“What a waste.”

“With bones like that—”

“if you'd trained properly from childhood, you could already be at expert level.”

He pointed toward the Academy.

“It's not too late.”

“Learn a proper mana breathing method.”

Vikir only smiled.

Sancho laughed.

“How much can you bench?”

“No mana.”

“Come train with me sometime.”

Tudor shoved a bottle toward him.

“Stop talking about exercise for one minute.”

“Drink.”

“It's soda.”

Sancho recoiled.

“Keep that away from me.”

“It ruins muscle!”

The two started spraying each other again.

Vikir watched.

Then turned away with a small smile.

Other classes brought water and towels for the Naphtali players.

“Tudor!”

“Here!”

“Use this towel!”

“Drink this!”

Tudor accepted them easily.

Sancho and the Baskerville triplets washed the drink from their hair and faces.

Vikir did the same.

Sweat.

Dust.

Sticky soda.

He needed to clean it off.

So—

he removed his glasses.

Pushed his wet bangs back.

Washed his face with clean water.

The features normally hidden beneath hair and thick lenses were exposed.

Vikir lifted his head—

shook away the water—

and walked toward the fountain.

Then—

the training field went quiet.
