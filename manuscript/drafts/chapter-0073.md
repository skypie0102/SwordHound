The rainy season arrived.

Clouds covered Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

The rain seemed endless.

Even the oldest Ballak elders could not remember a season this severe.

Rivers escaped their banks.

Flat ground vanished.

The jungle became a moving swamp.

Everything drowned.

Everything—

except Ballak village.

“Pull tighter!”

“Raise that fence!”

“Anchor it before the wind takes it!”

The village had not moved to higher ground.

Vikir had created higher ground.

Platforms.

Tree houses.

Bridges.

Shelters lashed between enormous trunks at least fifteen meters above the earth.

Thick rope connected tree to tree.

Logs and planks formed walkways.

Heavy supports kept shelters from being torn away by the wind.

Below the floodwater, drainage trenches had been dug between trunks.

Elsewhere, earth and stone redirected the current.

The result was not perfect.

But Ballak remained habitable while the surrounding jungle disappeared beneath brown water.

Two men working on a vine bridge waved as Vikir passed.

“Vikir!”

“Our house is still here because of you!”

“Good thing we listened!”

Vikir did not answer.

His eyes were on the water.

“Danger.”

The men froze.

A huge shadow moved beneath the muddy surface only a few meters below.

Then—

SPLASH.

A massive flat head exploded from the water.

Its mouth was more than five meters across.

For an instant it looked like a giant catfish.

The creature lunged toward the bridge workers.

“Get away from them!”

Aiyen's arrows fell from above.

THUNK. THUNK.

Several struck the head.

The creature twisted and crashed back into the torrent.

Vikir crossed the rope bridge quickly.

“Catfish?”

Aiyen grinned.

“That wasn't a catfish.”

“I saw the head.”

“You didn't see the body.”

The river erupted again.

A body impossibly long rose behind the flat head.

Not a fish.

A snake.

> **Mushuhushu**  
> **Danger Rating:** A+  
> **Size:** 32 m  
> **Found at:** Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, 8th Ridge

A gigantic serpent with a grotesquely long, intestine-like body.

Its mouth was large enough to swallow an elephant.

Old stories claimed ancient specimens could consume an entire country.

Despite their size, Mushuhushu moved almost silently on land or through water.

This one had survived for decades.

Its scales rose as it hissed at the village platforms.

Aiyen clicked her tongue.

“Old one.”

She fired again.

Hard scales.

Heavy rain.

Poor visibility.

The arrows struggled to reach anything vital.

Then Vikir vanished.

“Snakes are easier up close.”

A shadow appeared behind the creature's neck.

Beelzebub extended from Vikir's wrist.

Dense High Sword Graduator aura wrapped the blade.

Six Fangs appeared.

Predatory arcs of blood-red aura spun forward.

CRACK.

Mushuhushu's scales shattered.

Soft flesh burst beneath them.

[SKREEEEE!]

The serpent twisted.

Vikir seized its tongue and pulled himself closer.

Then drove Beelzebub into one eye.

Blood sprayed hot against the cold rain.

The wounded snake recoiled and plunged into the flood.

The current seized it.

Vikir considered pursuing.

Then abandoned the idea.

Not in this storm.

“Waste.”

A rope tightened around his waist.

Aiyen had reached him.

The pair used the safety lines to return to the platform.

Villagers were already cheering.

“Vikir!”

“You drove off Ka'ah!”

“That monster ruled this area for decades!”

Aiyen snapped.

“Did none of you see *my* arrows?”

Children booed her.

She scowled.

“This is why I hate children.”

Then she looked at Vikir.

Both were soaked.

“Think we'll catch Red Death from this?”

“Maybe. Dry yourself by the fire.”

Vikir walked away as though the battle had been routine.

Aiyen watched him.

Then repeated the word she had used for two years.

“Passed.”

◆◆◆

Vikir entered his shelter.

Outside, the storm screamed.

Inside, firelight turned the walls warm and red.

Heavy stones and rope kept the hide walls from tearing loose.

Ballak now praised Vikir as the man who had found a way to reduce the Red Death and saved the village from floodwater.

The new problem waiting inside was much harder.

“Uncle!”

Pomeranian ran to him.

Until moments earlier she had been sitting in a corner staring at the old family portrait.

She avoided almost everyone else.

Vikir was different.

She followed him to meals.

To sleep.

Everywhere he allowed.

Vikir still had no idea what one was supposed to do with a five-year-old child.

“Food.”

It was the safest solution he knew.

He lit a small cooking fire.

The rains had brought enormous numbers of snails onto the trees.

Vikir cooked the meat in butter and herbs.

Some went into a sweet egg-and-sugar mixture.

The rest he seasoned more heavily and served with vegetables.

Pomeranian ate.

“Good.”

Aiyen had somehow acquired a bowl too.

“Of course it's good. Your uncle cooks well.”

Pomeranian looked from Vikir to Aiyen.

Then smiled.

“Mom. Dad. Same.”

Aiyen rubbed beneath her nose, pleased.

“Children are wonderful.”

Vikir stared at her.

“You just said you hate children.”

“When?”

He sighed.

The shelter had already become crowded with Pomeranian.

Aiyen made it worse.

“Don't be too hard on her. At least she can use the toilet by herself now.”

Vikir said nothing.

Aiyen spread her legs again and swished her leather skirt. Vikir could only sigh.

Then the entrance curtain flew open.

Wind and rain burst inside.

A heavy stone normally held that curtain down.

Someone had moved it.

Ahun stood in the doorway.

Vikir and Aiyen both frowned.

Ahun usually arrived looking for an argument.

Not this time.

His face was bloodless.

His mouth trembled.

He looked close to tears.

“Vikir.”

Ahun's voice cracked.

“Please.”

He stepped forward.

“My sister—help my sister.”
