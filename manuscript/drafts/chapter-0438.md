# Chapter 438: Dreaming the Impossible Dream (2)

Red hair rippled like fire.

Four years had changed Camus.

She was taller.

Sharper.

The childish softness had mostly disappeared.

The moment she saw Vikir—

her eyes lit up.

“There you are!”

Then—

“Boyfriend!”

“How long has it been?!”

Camus waved.

Dark clouds gathered.

No—

not clouds.

Birds.

Crows.

Jackdaws.

Black wings packed together until they formed a bridge through the air.

Camus walked across it.

Hundreds of meters.

Slowly.

Gracefully.

Like a model crossing a stage.

“….”

For once—

something disturbed Vikir’s unreadable expression.

Camus reached him.

Then threw both arms around him.

“You got taller!”

“You grew up properly!”

She inspected him for several long seconds.

Then finally looked past him.

“…Oh.”

“Hello.”

The warmth vanished almost completely.

Dolores.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Sinclaire.

All greeted her back.

Camus had once been part of the Night Walkers.

A Colosseo student—

though only briefly.

Her legend at the Academy had lasted much longer than her enrollment.

*Why would I stay in school if Vikir isn’t there?*

Dropping out almost immediately—

had become part of the story people told about her.

Dolores spoke evenly.

“It has been a long time, Queen of the Night.”

Camus grimaced.

“Yeah.”

“You too.”

“But that nickname is embarrassing.”

“It came from Night Hound.”

Dolores’s expression did not change.

Camus brightened immediately.

“Then it’s fine.”

“Our Night Hound is incredible.”

She grabbed Vikir’s arm again.

A vein appeared faintly on Dolores’s forehead.

And Sinclaire’s.

Sinclaire stepped between them.

“More importantly—”

“what happened with the Morgue Dark Hall?”

“You said you would bring support.”

“Of course I did.”

“I’m the Dark Hall’s senior representative.”

Camus’s eyes sharpened.

Earlier—

she had promised unconditional Dark Hall support to the Night Walkers.

But the promise had one specific objective.

Find Vikir.

Confirm he was alive.

Then bring him safely to one place.

Where Camus was.

“I heard you escaped.”

“I knew you would.”

“How you escaped was completely outside my expectations…”

“But after that I mobilized every force I could.”

“Coasts.”

“Islands.”

“Mainland shoreline.”

“Anywhere you could’ve washed up.”

“That’s why I’m late.”

Then she looked at Tudor.

And held up one of the iron rods.

The small tool that had broken the Invincible Cavalry’s charge.

“Your family doesn’t have these, does it?”

Tudor stared.

“…Of course not.”

“Why would we?”

“Then thank me.”

“I could’ve left them straight instead of bending them.”

“If I wanted credit—

I could’ve skewered the cavalry.”

Tudor’s expression tightened.

If Camus had used the rods that way—

the horses and riders would have been butchered.

Instead—

they had merely been thrown from their charge.

Under ordinary conditions—

the Invincible Cavalry would have noticed and avoided the trap.

Under Chimeries’s recklessness—

they had charged blindly.

Everyone turned toward the demon.

Chimeries’s teeth ground inside the hollow center of his face.

[So there was another ambush.]

[A black mage carrying Seere’s power.]

Chimeries had sensed Seere.

Camus carried unmistakable traces of that black magic.

Yet the demon remained composed.

[I knew you humans would struggle to recover Donquixote.]

[Did you truly think I prepared for nothing?]

Passamonte’s face rose from the horse-like chest.

The gaze settled on Bianca.

[Bianca.]

[I will tell you why I wanted you alive.]

The outer walls changed.

SHING.

SHING.

SHING.

Gray-cloaked archers appeared.

Rows of them.

Bows drawn.

Arrows aimed.

Bianca’s face went white.

“…The God-Killing Sniper Squad.”

House Usher’s elite assassins.

Long-range killers.

The force that made up the overwhelming majority of Usher’s practical combat reputation.

Their presence meant only one thing.

The demon controlling House Donquixote—

and the demon controlling House Usher—

were cooperating.

Bianca finally understood the pattern.

*That’s why Pedro wanted Tudor and me alive.*

If simple elimination had been the goal—

their heads would have been enough.

Bianca turned toward Vikir.

“This is bad.”

“My father said that if the God-Killing Sniper Squad gathered in full—

they could kill even a god.”

Vikir did not disagree.

House Usher had fewer people than the other great families.

Yet no one dismissed them.

Because their snipers existed.

Chimeries laughed.

[Once Usher’s snipers establish an ambush—]

[even the combined elites of the other great families cannot escape annihilation.]

Killing intent surrounded them.

The fortifications hid many of the shooters.

That did not matter.

Usher snipers could punch through thick armor.

Walls were not safety.

Chimeries raised his voice.

[FIRE.]

[KILL THEM ALL.]

BOOM!

The first arrow arrived.

It pierced several walls—

then punched through the floor.

Then—

a rain of heavy arrows followed.

THUD.

THUD.

THUD.

The sound was almost like cavalry hooves.

Vikir poured mana into Decarabia.

A red inverted pentagram expanded.

The Wailing Wall formed around the group.

BOOM!

CRACK!

Every arrow shook the barrier.

But something felt wrong.

The shots were fast.

Powerful.

Accurate.

Yet—

too few.

The intervals were long.

The directions did not repeat.

There simply were not enough archers.

Everyone thought the same thing.

*Is this really the full sniper force?*

Chimeries thought it too.

[…What?]

He craned his neck toward the outer walls.

The truth became obvious.

The shooters were sparse.

Far too sparse.

[This is not an elite formation.]

[This is barely a formation at all!]

[Where did the rest go?!]

Dolores.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Sinclaire.

All looked just as confused.

Camus—

meanwhile—

wrapped both arms around Vikir’s waist.

“Boyfriend.”

“I really missed you.”

“So don’t run away again.”

“I’m tired of chasing.”

“I can usually guess where you’ll end up—

but still…”

Everyone looked at Vikir.

Tudor asked first.

“Wait.”

“Was Camus your plan?”

“You said you had a countermeasure.”

“But you two sound like you just met.”

“No.”

Vikir answered immediately.

“She came on her own.”

Camus laughed.

“I really did.”

“I was busy searching beaches and deserted islands.”

“I didn’t set up anything here.”

Then—

what had Vikir meant?

BOOM!

An explosion answered them.

The outer wall collapsed.

Aura surged.

Usher snipers fell from the ramparts.

And through the dust—

two old men walked forward.

“Puhishishi.”

“Too much dust.”

“Old man, fight somewhere farther away.”

“Keep talking and I’ll arrest you again.”

“You filthy old escapee.”

One—

thin.

Carrying a whip.

Smiling.

The other—

massive.

Holding a heavy club.

They walked side by side like men taking an afternoon stroll.

The destruction around them—

said otherwise.

CRACK!

One whip strike sent snipers flying.

BOOM!

One club strike collapsed an entire section of wall.

“The Usher family lost plenty of people back then.”

“Would’ve been easier if our revolution succeeded.”

“That was not a revolution.”

“It was a riot.”

“Stop talking and do your job.”

“Anyone listening would think you ordered me around.”

“At least I work for my meals.”

They continued forward—

breaking through House Usher’s elite.

The mastermind of the Forty-Seven People Riot.

**Marquis de Sade.**

And the former warden of Nouvelle Vague.

**Orca.**

They stopped side by side.

Then looked up.

Toward Vikir.
