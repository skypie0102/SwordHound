# Chapter 24: Camus Morgue (5)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/024.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 24 is an aligned secondary witness only. The clothing accident involving the two eight-year-old characters is rendered neutrally and non-sexually while preserving the source events. Canonical family/location/rank forms follow current project/Fandom evidence.

“Uwaaaaah!”

Fire, ice, lightning, and stone tore across the training ground as Camus attacked wildly.

Vikir slipped between the spells, gradually retreating toward the boundary of the neighboring arena.

“You! Stop running and fight me properly!”

Camus shouted after him.

She seemed to be regaining confidence now that she believed she had him cornered.

The three small bumps lined up on her forehead somewhat undermined the effect.

While dodging another spell, Vikir raised one hand and tapped his own forehead three times.

A reminder.

One.

Two.

Three.

He even smiled faintly.

Camus completely lost her temper.

“I really won't forgive you! You little runt! Never!”

She made one last furious rush.

Vikir stopped retreating.

His calculation had reached its intended point.

BOOOOM!

The fifteen-year-old Baskerville and Morgue combatants in the adjacent arena collided head-on.

A blade shattered.

Magic detonated.

Fragments and flame scattered across both arenas.

The Morgue teenager had unleashed a particularly large fire spell, and part of the blast spilled over the boundary.

BOOM!

The spectators cried out.

“Camus!?”

“Lady Camus!”

“No—!”

The nearby mages panicked.

In actual destructive terms, the accident was minor.

The stray fire was not powerful enough to seriously threaten either child.

But Camus suddenly screamed.

“Aaaaaah!”

The outer layer of her clothes had caught fire and burned badly.

She hurriedly stripped away the smoldering fabric before it could burn her, leaving her in her underclothes in the middle of the smoke-filled arena.

For the moment, flame, dust, and smoke hid her from the crowd.

A gust of wind would change that within seconds.

Because the referees saw no serious injury, they had not yet rushed in.

Only worried voices carried through the smoke.

‘No. No, no, no—!’

Camus's face was already wet with tears and runny-nosed from crying.

The thought of being left standing in her underclothes before both great clans was, to her, infinitely worse.

A humiliating memory surfaced as well: once, after talking back to her mother, she had been thrown outside without her clothes as punishment.

‘Someone help me. Anyone!’

She crouched and tried to cover herself while the smoke thinned.

Then—

Flutter.

Heavy, soft fabric dropped over her shoulders.

Camus looked up.

A black Baskerville training cloak had been wrapped around her.

“At least put this on.”

Vikir stood in front of her in his underclothes, having given up the outer garment himself.

Camus blinked.

“…You?”

Vikir shrugged.

To him, being naked did not strip a child of dignity.

Shame at exposing what lay beneath one's clothes was largely an adult convention; children sometimes ran around naked without treating it as some grave emotional matter.

That was his practical, thoroughly unromantic conclusion.

Camus clearly did not share it.

Still, she clutched the cloak tightly around herself.

Then her eyes widened.

“Y-your forehead. You're bleeding!”

Vikir touched his brow.

His fingers came away red.

A fragment from the neighboring shattered sword had apparently flown with the magical blast and nicked him.

The wound was trivial.

But this was still an official match between eight-year-olds.

Once blood had been drawn, the result was effectively decided.

Whoosh—

A wind swept across the arena.

Dust and smoke rolled away.

The crowd saw Camus sitting on the ground wrapped tightly in Vikir's black training cloak, while Vikir stood bare-bodied nearby with a thin line of blood running down his forehead.

No one knew exactly what had happened inside the smoke.

Everyone could tell the fight was over.

One look at Camus made that obvious.

She had completely lost the will to continue.

The Baskervilles stared at Vikir with a different kind of surprise.

“Did he lose to the Morgue girl? Or win? I can't even tell.”

“That kid is built sturdily for eight. The River Styx really did change him.”

“His training certainly hasn't been wasted.”

Thus ended the unusually memorable match between the two eight-year-old prodigies.

Not with a decisive spell or sword strike.

With Camus no longer willing to fight.

◆◆◆

After the friendly competition had fully ended, Hugo called Vikir aside near one corner of the training ground.

They walked together.

“How was your match with the Morgue girl?”

“Interesting. It reminded me why this event exists.”

“Fighting a mage is very different from fighting another swordsman.”

“I'll keep learning.”

If the rest of the Baskerville Clan had seen the exchange, many would have been shocked.

Hugo simply was not the sort of father who casually discussed a child's experience this way.

Vikir found it strange too.

‘Was he always capable of acting like this?’

His memories of Hugo from childhood were vague.

Cold.

Severe.

Merciless.

That was the man Vikir remembered.

There were stories that Hugo had become even harder after losing his first wife and eldest daughter.

‘…Not that it matters to me now.’

Hugo moved to the real subject.

“What do you think about the ruby mine?”

The question was a continuation of the argument from before the competition.

Camus had essentially argued that Baskerville could not profitably mine the ruby vein anyway, so Morgue should receive the mining rights and compensate Baskerville for access.

That was also the position Adolf had come to negotiate.

Hugo watched Vikir with a faint trace of expectation.

Vikir answered immediately.

“I think we should give up the mining rights.”

Hugo's eyes opened slightly wider.

“Give Morgue the right to mine the ruby vein?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Red Fang Mountain, where the ruby vein lies, sits downstream from the deeper stretches of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain. A little farther in, the territory becomes crowded with powerful monsters and barbarian tribes. I saw enough of that when I crossed the boundary during the practical examination.”

Vikir remembered the arrows embedded in Cerberus's flank.

Hugo's expression brightened by a fraction.

“Go on.”

“Baskerville has always expanded by clearing the wilderness and taking new territory. If we use Morgue properly, we can reduce the losses Baskerville takes during that expansion.”

A sharp light appeared in Hugo's eyes.

“Heh. Good.”

He understood immediately.

Grant Morgue the mining rights and large numbers of Morgue personnel would move into the lower region around the ruby mine.

Baskerville could expand around them.

Monsters and barbarian groups displaced by Baskerville's advance could be driven toward the Morgue-held mine instead.

Taking new territory required more than defeating whoever currently occupied it.

Baskerville doctrine demanded that the roots of resistance be destroyed.

Kill the monsters and hostile tribes holding the land.

Hunt down those who fled.

Do not allow survivors to regroup, return, and retaliate later.

Completely exterminating fleeing enemies was slow, exhausting work.

It would be far easier to drive them in one consistent direction—toward the territory Morgue would now have to defend.

Hugo laughed.

“Hahaha. Then the rubies may grow even redder with Morgue blood. Raspane's people will regret stepping into our territory.”

He was thoroughly pleased.

Morgue would get its rubies.

Baskerville would get its land.

On paper, everyone won.

“Well done. Your strategy matches my own plan almost exactly.”

Hugo rested a hand briefly on Vikir's head.

Vikir lowered his eyes.

‘Of course it does. It was your plan in the first place.’

In his previous life, Vikir had been one of the hounds tasked with driving monsters and barbarians toward Morgue territory.

He knew exactly how the strategy would unfold.

Still, he answered respectfully.

“Even so, Morgue personnel entering our territory should be watched carefully.”

“No need to worry. I've already been increasing the number of hounds assigned there.”

That answer surprised Vikir.

Not because the measure itself was unexpected.

Because Hugo had chosen to tell him.

The way Hugo looked at him had softened almost imperceptibly.

Just as they reached the meeting room, a voice called from behind.

“Clan Head.”

Adolf Morgue approached.

Camus stood beside him, her eyes still red from crying.

She clutched her uncle's cloak with one hand and kept her head down.

The black Baskerville cloak Vikir had given her was still wrapped around her.

Hugo narrowed his eyes.

“Acting representative Adolf. Is there still unfinished business?”

“One important matter.”

“Then let's go inside.”

They entered the room.

Adolf sat across from Hugo.

Camus remained unusually quiet.

Before passing Vikir, she glanced at him once, then quickly lowered her eyes again.

Adolf began.

“Morgue has considered several ways to resolve the ruby-mine issue amicably. Territorial rent is only one possibility.”

“Then what do you propose as the price of opening the territory?”

“A betrothal.”

Hugo's eyebrows drew together slightly.

Not anger.

Interest.

“A betrothal?”

“Yes. A marriage between Morgue and Baskerville. Accepting money in exchange for opening land can create an ugly appearance. A marriage tie would present the arrangement as cooperation between families.”

A marriage between two famous rival houses was an awkward proposal, but politically understandable.

Hugo gave a dry laugh.

“And who marries whom?”

“How about our eldest daughter and your eldest son?”

Adolf looked toward Camus.

Her head snapped up.

This was clearly the first she had heard of it.

“My niece is an extraordinary child. Beauty, intelligence, strength—she lacks nothing. Pairing her with Baskerville's Young Patriarch would be an excellent match.”

Hugo ignored the praise.

“My eldest is twenty this year. Camus is eight. Twelve years is a considerable gap.”

“Eight and twenty. Twelve years can be overcome.”

Hugo looked at Adolf.

“You're forty this year, aren't you? Then how would you feel about someone fifty-seven?”

“…”

Adolf fell silent at the deliberately irritating comparison.

Hugo waved one hand.

“Besides, my eldest son is already engaged.”

“What? Since when?”

“The arrangement was made before birth. You wouldn't know about it.”

Adolf grimaced.

Then Camus suddenly shouted.

“I won't marry anyone weaker than Mother!”

Both adults looked at her.

Only then did they seem to remember that nobody had asked what she wanted.

Adolf smiled and patted her head.

“My dear. If you refuse every man weaker than your mother, you may never marry at all. Or you'll need to choose someone far older than you.”

“That depends on potential! And I don't like huge age differences. I like someone my age or younger. There were lots of boys my age at the competition today.”

Adolf had little answer to that.

Hugo, increasingly tired of the subject, looked at her.

“Girl. This isn't a buffet.”

“I know. I'm not interested in picking randomly either.”

Camus looked up.

Her gaze settled somewhere across the room.

On Vikir.

Hugo noticed.

So did Adolf.

“No! Absolutely not!”

Adolf shot to his feet and blocked Camus's view.

“He's Van, not Le! A dragon may rise from a ditch, but it still came from a ditch!”

He whispered the words into Camus's ear.

Hugo, a Sword Master, heard every syllable.

“…True or not, hearing you say it is irritating.”

Then Hugo looked at Vikir.

“In the end, marriage concerns the person involved. Son. What do you think?”

Vikir answered without hesitation.

“If Father orders it, I will obey.”

The answer was absolute loyalty.

Hugo smiled in satisfaction.

Adolf looked appalled.

Marriage was not supposed to be treated like receiving an assignment.

And this eight-year-old was speaking as though marrying Morgue's treasured direct-line daughter were no more consequential than being sent on a mission.

Hugo added, pointedly,

“In Baskerville, direct and collateral blood are not everything. Inferior children can be born under Le. Superior ones can be born under Van.”

“…Is that your position regarding this child?”

Adolf smiled down at Vikir.

The smile was cold.

“I didn't get a proper look at you during the competition. May I take a closer look now?”

For the first time, Vikir studied Adolf directly.

A **Sixth-Circle** mage.

One of the strongest people in the Morgue Clan.

A senior political figure who routinely handled diplomacy and external disputes.

And, by reputation, a man absurdly protective of his niece.

Adolf leaned down near Vikir and spoke quietly.

“Child. This isn't really meant for you to hear, but becoming our Camus's husband is not easy. The betrothal is merely a family proposal. Personally, I wouldn't consider even Baskerville's eldest son good enough for her.”

A subtle pressure accompanied the words.

Vikir stood perfectly still.

Adolf frowned.

“Why aren't you answering? Are you listening to me?”

Vikir finally replied.

“You said it wasn't meant for me to hear, so I stopped listening after that.”
