# Chapter 496: Side Story 1

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from the **target-496 segment** of shared raw `source/chinese/chapters/495.txt`, beginning at the Dark Hall’s 666th floor after the target-495 “five pursuers” credit-cookie ending. Recovered English Chapter 493 contains the aligned embedded section **Side Story Chapter 1**, used as a segmented secondary witness. Established project terminology controls Camus Morgue, Vikir, Seere, Snake Morgue, Baal, Dark Hall, Wraith Tree, and Full Revival Magic.

Deep beneath House Morgue—

below more than six hundred underground levels—

lay the deepest secret of the Dark Hall.

The **666th floor**.

A place beyond Imperial surveillance.

Beyond demonic observation.

Beyond even the ordinary reach of House Morgue’s head.

Only the Dark Hall’s highest senators knew it existed.

At the bottom of an endless spiral staircase—

a woman sat alone upon a throne.

Eyes closed.

Sssssssss—

Mana circulated through every vessel in her body.

Red hair.

Pale skin.

An archmage who had reached a terrifying realm.

**Camus Morgue.**

She trusted no one.

Relied on no one.

All her life—

she stood alone against the world.

Sometimes winning.

Sometimes losing.

Never acting as though wounds mattered.

“Haa…”

Camus opened her eyes.

A mage examining the flow of internal mana was unusually vulnerable.

There was an old saying:

*A mage inspecting her mana is as defenseless as a crab or shrimp that has just shed its shell.*

That was why Camus meditated here.

No guards.

No attendants.

Absolute isolation.

If her body needed protecting—

she would protect it herself.

…Except—

“Finished meditating?”

“You took longer than usual.”

Camus’s eyes snapped open.

A woman wearing a golden bird-beak mask—

stood behind a stone pillar.

Impossible.

First—

an outsider had entered the 666th floor.

Second—

*longer than usual* meant the intruder knew how long Camus normally meditated.

She had been watched before.

RUMBLE.

Mana boiled.

Flames.

Black iron spikes.

Camus attacked instantly.

“Whoever you are—

die first.”

“Questions later.”

Necromancers—

after all—

often found corpses easier to interrogate than living prisoners.

BOOM!

BOOM!

BOOM!

Camus’s eyes widened.

Flame met flame.

Iron spikes met iron spikes.

Her attack—

perfectly canceled.

Then—

thin branches extended toward her.

Fine as hair.

Black.

Wrong.

**Wraith Tree roots.**

A tree rooted inside a mage’s soul.

Feeding on soul-karma.

Projecting metaphysical harvest into physical reality.

And—

the symbol of the Eighth Corpse.

Seere.

“No…”

Camus recoiled.

“Impossible!”

“Seere died!”

“Snake and I destroyed him!”

The masked woman stopped attacking.

“Calm down.”

“I’m not here to fight.”

“What are you?”

“A demon puppet?”

“How do you have the Eighth Corpse’s power?”

“Like this.”

Something crawled onto the woman’s shoulder.

Camus’s face went pale.

“S-Seere…?”

Then she stopped.

The creature was tiny.

Pathetic.

Almost pitiable.

“…Why is it so small?”

“Because I absorbed almost everything.”

“You absorbed a Demon King’s power?”

“Is that even possible?”

“Yes.”

Camus stared.

Then her expression became disgusted.

“You’re insane.”

“You just eat whatever you find?”

“Even demonic power?”

“Someone like you is a danger to the world.”

The masked woman sighed.

“Don’t criticize yourself while lying down.”

“…What?”

Camus tilted her head.

The intruder removed the mask.

Red hair.

Red eyes.

The same face.

“…!”

Camus froze.

Standing before her—

was Camus Morgue.

Another Camus.

“What is this?”

“What does it look like?”

“You.”

The other Camus smiled.

“I thought you’d be much older.”

“You still look good.”

“Well—

of course.”

“You’re me.”

“…?”

The younger Camus stared.

The other one gestured casually.

“For convenience—

you’re **Camus of this world**.”

“I’m **Camus from the other world**.”

“Because I came from a parallel timeline.”

“What kind of bullshit—”

“Listen to yourself.”

“….”

The Camus of this world looked up.

The other Camus was older—

but not dramatically different.

Then—

“Take this first.”

Something flew through the air.

THUD.

A severed head.

Camus’s eyes widened.

“This is…”

“Baal.”

“The First Demon Lord.”

“You killed Baal!?”

“Not exactly.”

“Only a shell.”

“The real body seems to be somewhere else.”

The other Camus shrugged.

“I never found it.”

“I killed plenty of demons and none knew where the real Baal was.”

“Maybe Baal itself never cared much about conquering the human world.”

“The real problem was the lower demons carrying on its will.”

“How do you know all that?”

“I told you.”

“I’m you.”

“That’s why I can enter this place.”

The other Camus looked around.

Seals that reacted only to Camus’s body.

Familiar pillars.

Familiar magic circles.

Then—

her eyes became wet.

“This is where Master died.”

“Thinking about Uncle Snake…”

“Snake?”

The Camus of this world stiffened.

“Master?”

“That filthy bastard?”

The other Camus went silent.

“…What did you call my master?”

“Bastard.”

“Snake is a shameless traitor.”

“He sold his soul to a demon.”

“He got Uncle Adolf and Mother killed.”

“Not in my world.”

“You just said you’re me.”

“Apparently not completely.”

The brief truce—

created by Baal’s severed shell—

froze over instantly.

Then—

a low voice emerged from behind another pillar.

“I told you to cooperate.”

A scarred man stepped between them.

An old hound—

who had crossed more than one life.

**Vikir.**

The Camus of this world froze.

Even she—

a woman once called empress-like in power—

felt her strength drain simply by looking into his eyes.

Her heart—

strangely—

stumbled.

“Hey!”

The other Camus rushed over.

Grabbed Vikir’s arm.

Then glared.

“Don’t look at someone else’s husband.”

The Camus of this world blinked.

“I thought you said you’re me?”

“Apparently that part is different.”

“…Right.”

“Then why are you here?”

The other Camus answered.

“Join hands with us.”

“Join hands?”

“For what?”

“To destroy demons?”

“More fundamental than that.”

The Camus of this world frowned.

“What could matter more than killing demons?”

“Taking back what humanity lost.”

“…!”

Vikir continued.

“After the Age of Destruction—

99.99 percent of humanity died in this timeline.”

“Tudor.”

“Bianca.”

“Sancho.”

“Pigi.”

“Countless others.”

“Many people who became demon hosts.”

“Their fates diverged between our worlds.”

The Camus of this world lowered her eyes.

“But the dead are dead.”

“They can’t come back.”

Her voice became wet.

Perhaps she remembered Raspane.

Adolf.

Everyone lost.

Then Vikir spoke.

“There is a way.”

“A way to revive them all.”

Camus looked up.

Across from her—

the other Camus stood smiling.

THUMP.

She stamped one foot.

A vast—

unfinished magic circle—

appeared across the floor.

Recognition struck.

Both Camus stared.

Then—

their voices overlapped.

“The **Full Revival Magic**.”
