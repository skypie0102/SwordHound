# Chapter 21: Camus Morgue (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/021.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 21 is an aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English forms for Camus Morgue, Adolf Morgue, Morgue Clan, Hugo Le Baskerville, and Baskerville terminology follow current project/Fandom evidence.

Camus's astonishing question froze the entire room.

“Uncle, are you the thief who stole our ruby mine?”

The servants of both houses stiffened first.

“…”

“…”

Even Adolf Morgue was caught off guard.

His mouth hung slightly open.

Hugo said nothing at all.

His expression plainly asked what, exactly, he had just heard.

For a moment, the Clan Head's chamber was so quiet it seemed devoid of life.

Camus broke that silence herself.

“Give our ruby mine back.”

She strode forward and held out one small, pale hand.

Hugo continued staring at her.

No one had ever said such a thing to his face.

Certainly not an eight-year-old child.

Adolf recovered first.

“Y-you little—! What kind of rude nonsense are you saying all of a sudden?”

Camus looked at him as if the answer were obvious.

“But Uncle said this man stole our ruby mine.”

“W-when did I ever say that!?”

“Before we got out of the carriage. You said the amount of ruby allocated to my research was being reduced starting this month because of that useless old mustached thief.”

“I-I-I-I said no such thing!”

“If you're asking exactly when… about one hour and forty-two minutes ago? I heard you clearly.”

Camus looked faintly offended by his denial.

Cold sweat formed on Adolf's back.

He did not need to look at Hugo to feel the temperature of the room dropping.

“…A useless old mustache. And a thief.”

The voice came from directly ahead.

Hugo's eyes had narrowed.

Adolf hurriedly raised a handkerchief to his mouth and coughed.

“Ahem. There appears to be some misunderstanding. We can clarify it afterward.”

“A child merely repeats what she is taught. There is no need to explain Morgue's household education to me.”

The barb was unmistakable.

Adolf turned toward Camus.

“Camus. You cannot speak so rudely to an elder of a neighboring house.”

“But Uncle said—”

“Even if the servants around you say unpleasant things, you must not repeat every rumor word for word. Common gossip is often exaggerated. Sometimes understated too, of course.”

Adolf and Camus began arguing with one another.

Hugo rapidly grew tired of the spectacle.

Barrymore's earlier report came back to him.

The first matter had concerned bloodshed between Baskerville and Morgue around the ruby mine near the border.

The dispute was not new.

Between the territories of the two clans lay a region treated as a joint security zone.

A rich vein of high-quality ruby had been discovered there.

The geography was the problem.

The mine entrance and the only practical route down from the surface lay on Morgue territory.

But the underground ruby vein continued beneath Baskerville land.

Morgue could enter the mine from its own side.

Following the vein far enough, however, inevitably meant digging beneath Baskerville territory.

The Morgue Clan had tried several ways around the problem.

The most common was simply to dig farther in secret.

But Baskerville hounds had sharp noses.

They repeatedly discovered signs of covert tunneling and raided Morgue mining parties.

Several clashes had already ended in bloodshed.

Neither family had filed an official complaint.

On the surface, relations remained perfectly cordial.

Underneath, resentment had been boiling for some time.

That was the real reason Adolf had come today.

The annual friendly competition provided a convenient excuse to discuss the mine and the violence surrounding it.

Hugo had prepared accordingly.

What he had not prepared for was an eight-year-old girl loudly demanding the mine back.

“Give me our ruby mine! I can't continue my research properly if you keep taking our rubies!”

Hugo pressed a hand to his forehead.

“Acting representative Adolf. What exactly is the meaning of this official visit?”

“Ahem. My apologies, Clan Head. Still… the child's wording is crude, but what she says is not entirely wrong.”

“…What?”

“The words came from a child, but the issue itself is real.”

Hugo and Adolf stared at one another.

“…”

“…”

Hugo let a trace of the pressure belonging to a Sword Master leak into the room.

The intent was simple.

Make the child in front of him step back.

Before that pressure could settle, Adolf wrapped his cloak around Camus and cut in.

“What are you doing? Is one of the empire's great swordsmen trying to bully an eight-year-old girl?”

Hugo's expression twisted.

Arguing seriously with a child would damage his dignity.

Ignoring her would allow Adolf to continue using that child's blunt innocence as a political weapon.

It was infuriating.

Was he truly going to reopen a border dispute that both sides had kept unofficial for years?

For Baskerville, the mine was largely a matter of territorial pride.

For Morgue, it was materially valuable.

Rubies were important ingredients in magical research.

Baskerville swordsmen had little practical need for them.

Hugo would much rather have ignored the mine entirely.

Training his hounds and pushing the frontier deeper into Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain already consumed enough of his attention.

He had no desire to waste time arguing with mages over red stones.

“Please return the ruby mine to us. Without those rubies, my studies will suffer.”

“Camus. The adults are speaking. Don't interrupt.”

Adolf coughed again, but did little to stop her.

Hugo's irritation deepened.

‘This is obviously political theater.’

And yet the performer was a child.

Even Hugo found himself unable to respond as freely as he wanted.

Then a new voice entered the chamber.

“Does the Morgue Clan not teach its children that they shouldn't covet what belongs to someone else?”

Clear.

Young.

Eight years old.

Hugo's ears practically perked up.

Vikir Van Baskerville had arrived.
