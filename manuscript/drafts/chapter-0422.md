# Chapter 422: How to Become a Wandering Knight (1)

Above ground—

in a remote village on the southern continent called **Wakewack**—

the world had become almost unrecognizable.

The village had once been warm and humid.

Fruit grew easily.

Water had never been scarce.

Now—

for the first time in decades—

perhaps centuries—

Wakewack was suffering from catastrophic drought and famine.

The ground had split open.

Trees and grass had withered—

then burned.

Wildfires swept through mountain ranges.

Lakes dried up.

Animals died by the thousands.

And as new dungeons appeared across the continent—

monster attacks increased sharply.

Hot wind carried ash from mountains already reduced to charcoal.

It stripped away what little moisture remained—

and exhausted people even further.

Across that barren land—

a traveler walked.

A torn black cloak.

Dull black armor.

A long-beaked plague-doctor mask hanging below his waist.

A long object wrapped in bandages across his back.

CLANK.

CLACK.

Every exhausted step made the joints of his armor grind softly.

A wandering knight.

In an age when even conversation had become rare—

figures like him were no longer unusual.

He entered Wakewack.

Walked directly to the nearest tavern.

Pushed open the dried wooden door.

Then sat in the darkest corner.

The drought had reached the menu too.

Two handfuls of boiled beans.

A glass of flat beer.

That was all money could buy.

The wandering knight checked that the mask remained at his waist—

then drank in silence.

Nearby—

two village militiamen argued over politics.

“So what are you?”

“Conservative or progressive?”

“Progressive, obviously.”

“And you?”

“What do you think?”

“Conservative!”

The argument quickly became the state of the Empire.

“The throne belongs to the eldest!”

“The First Prince should inherit!”

“Donquixote, Usher, and Leviathan are right!”

“I think the Second Prince is fine.”

“I understand Baskerville, Morgue, Bourgeois, and Quovadis too.”

“So you approve of a coup?”

“Coup?”

“It’s a power struggle.”

“A civil war.”

The Empire had become deeply divided.

The Seven Great Houses—

Baskerville.

Morgue.

Quovadis.

Donquixote.

Usher.

Leviathan.

Bourgeois.

After the Emperor’s death—

the houses split over succession.

The First Prince—

**Jack Meridieu**—

was supported by Donquixote, Usher, Leviathan, and Quovadis’s Old Testament Faction.

The Second Prince—

**Ralph**—

was supported by Baskerville, Morgue, Bourgeois, and Quovadis’s New Testament Faction.

Both princes descended from the founding emperor.

Both possessed prophetic power.

And both stood beneath an oracle:

**One will destroy the world.**

**The other will save it.**

For ordinary people—

the succession struggle no longer felt like distant court politics.

Choosing the wrong emperor—

they believed—

could mean the end of the Empire itself.

The two militiamen continued bickering.

“Of course the First Prince should take the throne!”

“He’s the eldest.”

“He’s appeared before the Imperial people for years and always led from the front.”

“That’s how inheritance should work. In my family, the eldest son is expected to inherit everything too.”

The other man laughed.

“Your family is the same as the Imperial Family now?”

“And isn’t your second son smarter than your eldest?”

“What does my family have to do with this?!”

“You brought it up first.”

The Second Prince had not appeared before the people in years.

But the First Prince’s side had its own problems.

The Quovadis Old Testament Faction had collapsed.

And rumors surrounding Donquixote and Usher were growing worse.

Their heads—

Spear King **Cervantes** and Bow Demon **Roderick**—

had not appeared publicly for years.

Rumor said both were seriously ill.

Donquixote was effectively ruled by the head’s younger brother.

Usher—

by the head’s younger sister.

Both houses had relatively few direct-line members, which made collateral relatives unusually important in their internal politics.

The argument was crude.

But accurate enough.

The civil war had exploded into the open after the **Night Hound incident**.

The Second Prince’s faction demanded a retrial.

Three of the demons exposed around Vikir’s case—

Pedro Donquixote.

Isolde Usher.

Thomas Leviathan.

All belonged to the First Prince’s camp.

Opposing them stood testimony from Isabella Baskerville, Banshee Morgue, Lovebad Bourgeois, and Mozgus Quovadis.

Their evidence was extensive.

Yet the retrial was rejected.

The First Prince himself publicly backed the accused houses.

**To doubt them is to doubt me.**

That hard line transformed a long political rivalry into open civil war.

Trade between hostile territories collapsed.

Borders tightened.

And while the Seven Great Houses fought—

ordinary people endured drought.

Fire.

Famine.

Monsters.

Public faith in the great powers had fallen almost to nothing.

Yet one rumor still gave people hope.

“Enough politics.”

“Have you heard about those vigilantes?”

“What vigilantes?”

“The ones traveling across the whole Empire.”

“The wandering knights.”

“You mean the **Night Walkers**?”

The wandering knight in the corner remained still.

The two men continued.

Stories said the Night Walkers could cross thousands of kilometers in a day.

That they destroyed monster hordes.

One militiaman leaned forward.

“I went to the city across from here.”

“They said the Night Walkers appeared, wiped out a monster pack, and led refugees away.”

“People who followed them were promised food—”

“water—”

“even divine blessing.”

“About half the city went with them.”

The other man frowned.

“If heroes like that really exist, why haven’t they come here?”

“We can barely survive the monsters anymore.”

“…Maybe they just don’t have enough people.”

“The disaster zone is too wide.”

“Maybe.”

He exhaled.

“Still, having something like that to hope for helps people keep going.”

But everyone knew the names.

**Saint of the Night.**

**Knight of the Night.**

**Sniper of the Night.**

**Warrior of the Night.**

**Wealthy of the Night.**

**Queen of the Night.**

Childish titles—

perhaps.

But the people who carried them had become symbols.

Their slogan spread with them.

**Even the deepest darkness retreats before the weakest light.**

Like the Pied Piper of old stories—

the Night Walkers led desperate people toward a distant place of salvation.

People waited for them with admiration and hope.

Others watched with curiosity.

Some felt anxious.

Some stayed vigilant.

Some were afraid.

And skeptics called the whole story ridiculous and mocked it every day.

Everyone talked about them.

Then—

BANG!

The tavern door flew open.

“A raid!”

“Monster attack!”

The village warning horn sounded.

TUUUUU—

The two militiamen abandoned their argument immediately.

Weapons came out.

The tavern keeper opened the cellar.

“Everyone underground!”

People moved with the efficiency of those who had survived this before.

Within seconds—

the tavern was empty.

Almost.

CREEEAK.

The wandering knight—

who had sat motionless in the corner—

finally stood.
