# Chapter 4: The Baskerville Dog (1)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/004.txt`. Canonical English names and sword-rank terminology checked against the user-designated Fandom wiki. Recovered English Chapter 4 is an aligned secondary reference only.

Eight years later.

Vikir Van Baskerville was now eight years old, receiving the same education as the other children of the Baskerville Clan.

Their tutor asked,

“All right. Last question. How do we classify the stages of swordsmanship?”

Vikir answered calmly.

“Regardless of pure skill with a blade, someone who cannot channel mana into a sword is called a Sword Beginner.

“Someone who can channel mana into the tip of a sword, but whose aura is still fragile and diffuse like a gas, is a Sword Expert.

“Someone whose aura becomes dense and viscous like a liquid is a Sword Graduator.

“And someone who can harden aura like a solid and freely change its shape according to their will is a Sword Master.”

Of course, those divisions existed largely for convenience.

A real battle was influenced by countless variables—physical condition, terrain, climate, humidity, gravity, experience, the density of ambient mana, and far more besides.

One could calculate all of them, but by the time the calculation was finished, the answer might already have changed.

For a group of eight-year-olds, however, things did not need to be that complicated yet.

As soon as Vikir finished his flawless answer, the children around him began raising their hands and shouting over one another.

“If a Sword Beginner fights a Sword Expert, the Expert wins!”

“If a Sword Expert fights a Sword Graduator, the Graduator wins!”

“A Sword Graduator is weaker than a Sword Master!”

“And the Patriarch is a Sword Master!”

Vikir listened without a word.

One child’s excited declaration lodged sharply in his mind.

“The Patriarch is a Sword Master!”

Hugo Le Baskerville, patriarch of the Baskerville Clan.

As a mage, the amount of mana he possessed was comparable to a Seventh Circle.

As a swordsman, however, he converted that mana into aura and poured it into his blade, reaching the realm the world revered as Sword Master.

The tutor nodded.

“Correct. Lord Hugo, supreme head of the Baskerville Clan, is a Sword Master. Publicly, only seven people in the Rok Empire are known to have reached that realm—the heads of the Seven Great Clans.”

Vikir already knew that much.

He even knew how many Sword Master-level monsters existed inside and outside the Empire beyond those seven.

For now, that was information no one else possessed.

While Vikir sat lost in thought, the tutor kept glancing at him.

‘What an extraordinary child.’

His talent was almost overwhelming—so great it bordered on violence.

He had already become a minor legend in the family by remaining submerged in the River Styx for more than seven minutes before he was even a hundred days old.

But his body was not the only exceptional thing about him.

His intellect, merely overshadowed by his absurd physical aptitude, was just as unmatched among children his age.

Teach him one thing, and he seemed to understand a hundred.

‘The Patriarch will be pleased again today.’

The tutor fully intended to report everything to Hugo.

Ordinarily, Hugo showed little interest in children other than his eldest and second sons.

He had once visited the nursery castle only occasionally, like a livestock trader checking the growth and value of his stock.

Over the past few years, however, his interest in the place had grown noticeably.

Naturally, Vikir was the reason.

The nursery castle housed only children under ten. Hugo’s visits were so rare that the tutors responsible for the place had recently felt an enormous increase in pressure whenever he appeared.

‘Thank you, Young Master. I will serve you with everything I have.’

Because of Vikir, their budget had risen dramatically and their standing had become more secure than that of many of the family’s knights.

The tutors had every reason to look upon him favorably.

Vikir himself did not care in the slightest.

◆◆◆

After class, as the children returned to their rooms throughout the castle, Vikir reviewed his own situation.

‘Hugo really is a Sword Master.’

Regrettably, that fact had not changed.

Only a Sword Master could manifest solid aura.

Compared with the dense, viscous, liquid-like aura of a Sword Graduator, a Master’s solid aura was on an entirely different level in both density and strength.

Grind—

Vikir clenched his teeth.

In this life, he would never again live—or die—in such a wretched state.

‘At the very least, I need to recover all the strength I had before my death before the coming-of-age ceremony.’

He was eight years old now.

Without anyone realizing it, Vikir had already reached the High stage of Sword Expert.

In his previous life, he had not managed to reach that level until he was sixteen.

Even by the standards of the outside world, that had been enough to call him an extraordinary genius.

Inside the Baskerville Clan, however, it had been ordinary—perhaps even slightly lacking.

Baskerville children commonly reached High Sword Expert around the age of twenty, then began knocking on the door of Sword Graduator around thirty.

Only after they could wrap a blade in that dense, liquid-like aura did they generally rise into the clan’s upper ranks. Those who failed remained near the bottom.

Even among Baskerville elites called geniuses among geniuses, reaching High Sword Expert around twenty was considered exceptional.

Yet Vikir had already stepped into that realm at eight years old.

At an age when, in his previous life, he had not even awakened to mana.

It was a level that even the clan’s greatest young elites could scarcely imagine touching so early.

As Vikir reflected on that fact—

“Hey.”

A voice came from behind him.

He turned.

Three nine-year-old boys stood there.

Highbro Le Baskerville.

Middlebro Le Baskerville.

Lowbro Le Baskerville.

The legitimate boys of the direct line bore the particle ‘Le’, while girls of that line bore ‘La’.

That alone marked the three as unmistakable members of the main bloodline.

As predictably as one might expect, Highbro, the eldest and the one standing in front, began the provocation.

“Hey, Van-trash. Where do you think you’re going?”

“Where are you going?”

“Where are you going?”

Highbro led; the other two echoed him.

It was the sort of childish harassment one would expect from nine-year-olds.

The Baskerville Clan worshipped talent and strength so thoroughly that this sort of petty bullying generally disappeared as the children grew older.

But Vikir watched the triplets for another reason entirely.

‘… Hugo Le Baskerville’s Trident.’

Ten years from now, those three would grow into extremely troublesome enemies.

In his previous life, it had been these three who relentlessly pursued and surrounded him when he tried to escape, making his breakout hellishly difficult.

‘Come to think of it, they bullied me constantly when we were children too.’

They had stolen Vikir’s food so often that his childhood growth had been badly stunted.

During evaluations, they had tripped him in secret, swung blunted weapons at him, and sabotaged him whenever they could.

The consequences had followed him for years. There had been missions where the damage they caused in childhood nearly got him killed.

Flash—

A frightening red light passed through Vikir’s eyes.

Oblivious to it, the newly nine-year-old triplets surrounded him with grins.

“Hey, trash. I heard you stayed under the Styx for seven minutes. And that you strangled two venomous snakes in the cradle. Quit lying. What eight-year-old tells stories like that?”

“Liar!”

“Liar!”

Highbro, the nastiest of the three, stepped forward.

“Can you do this?”

A faintly glowing sphere formed over his palm.

Baskerville children possessed extraordinary sensitivity to mana from an early age, together with an unusual ability to manipulate it.

Even at nine, they could condense mana into a rounded mass.

They were not yet capable of attaching it to a sword, but anywhere outside the Baskerville Clan that alone would have been enough to call them geniuses.

Even within the clan, it was worthy of considerable praise.

“This is talent. Come on, you try too. Let’s see how much mana you can gather.”

“Show us!”

“Show us!”

The triplets kept pressing him.

“…”

Vikir glanced at the fist-sized mana sphere Lowbro had formed.

For a child their age, it was impressive enough.

But Vikir had already done as much when he was only eight months old.

Back when he had twisted the necks of two venomous snakes in his cradle.

Thirty years of experience from his previous life, added to eight more years spent refining mana after regression.

Just how far had that carried him now?

Vikir looked around.

Only children lived in this nursery castle, so there were few eyes to worry about.

Even the small number of guardian knights and tutors assigned here had gone out toward the outer castle.

Flash—

Vikir released his power.

He raised one hand high and began gathering mana.

The triplets stared at him in alarm.

“…!”

“…!”

“…!”

Nothing appeared above Vikir’s palm.

For a moment, the triplets simply blinked.

Then they burst out laughing.

“Ahahahaha! What a useless bastard! You’re this old and you still can’t even sense mana?”

“You can’t?”

“You can’t?”

They mocked him so viciously that tears of laughter gathered in their eyes.

… And yet.

At that same moment, atop a watchtower not far from the nursery castle, a knight on guard duty stared out the window in confusion.

“W-what is that? Why are there two suns above the nursery castle?”
