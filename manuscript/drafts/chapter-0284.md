Countless candles ignited at once.

Wax ran down mountains of gold coins—

then hardened in shining streams.

“…My God.”

Dolores stared.

There was only one word for the world around her.

**Wealth.**

Not ordinary wealth.

Not even the kind of wealth most people could imagine.

Gold coins.

Gold bars.

Jewels.

Precious metals.

They formed hills.

Then mountains.

Then ranges disappearing into the distance.

Even the stalactite-like shapes hanging from the high ceiling—

were made of gold and gemstones.

Golden cups.

Swords.

Crowns.

Necklaces.

Earrings.

Rings.

Brooches.

Antiques.

Rare gems bright enough to hurt the eyes.

Stacks of documents proving ownership of land and buildings.

Priceless art.

Bottles of liquor too rare to value.

Treasure everywhere.

So much that estimating the total became meaningless.

The vault looked like some legendary golden kingdom.

A pirate king’s hidden fortune—

multiplied beyond myth.

“…It really is another world.”

Dolores felt intimidated despite herself.

She had never cared much about money.

But this was no longer merely money.

At this scale—

wealth itself seemed to take on the majesty of a natural phenomenon.

*People who visit my brother are often ignorant little pups.*

*They arrive bursting with petty vanity and eager to display their wealth.*

*Perhaps this is how he breaks their confidence before negotiations even begin.*

Dolores finally understood Damien’s explanation.

How many self-made millionaires—

after seeing this—

could continue believing they stood at the summit of wealth?

Bartolomeo’s vault existed to make them feel tiny.

Then—

“…!”

Beyond the golden hills—

Dolores finally saw the deepest part of the vault.

A great mound of coins rose like a small mountain.

Steps climbed its face.

At the summit—

an enormous table carved from a single piece of ivory.

Across it sat a man.

Long white hair.

Thick beard.

Gentle eyes.

A firm mouth.

Everything about his face fit together with almost unnatural harmony.

He raised one hand.

“Welcome.”

“Saintess.”

“No—

representative of Oracle.”

**Bartolomeo Bourgeois.**

Head of House Bourgeois.

◆◆◆

“Remarkable.”

Those were Bartolomeo’s first words after dinner began.

Dolores looked up.

He smiled.

“I reviewed Oracle’s investment record.”

“You accepted an extraordinary amount of risk.”

“Putting that much capital into a difficult trade takes courage.”

“I learned something from it myself.”

Dolores answered exactly as Night Hound had instructed.

“I believed in Cindywendy’s business.”

Bartolomeo nodded.

“Cindywendy.”

“A young merchant making a name in the west.”

“I’ve been impressed more than once that she managed to open sustained trade with the jungle tribes.”

“She isn’t yet widely recognized among the Capital’s financiers.”

“So your eye is excellent.”

“Experienced.”

Dolores gave a restrained smile.

“Experienced?”

“I’m still only a teenager.”

“Experience has nothing to do with age.”

Bartolomeo rested his knife beside the plate.

“I’ve prepared.”

“Challenged.”

“Failed.”

“Repeated those things many times.”

“That cycle is what creates experience.”

His tone was warm.

Respectful.

Almost paternal.

“If someone has already achieved something extraordinary—

why should it matter whether they are ten or one hundred?”

“Achievement proves ability.”

“Anyone who has walked far enough from their starting point has earned the right to feel proud.”

“Someone who refuses to move—

then complains about where they stand—

has not.”

If an ordinary Colosseo student were sitting here—

receiving praise from the richest man in the Empire—

their heart might have soared.

Recognition after enormous effort.

Approval from someone powerful.

A pleasant loosening of the mind.

Exactly the kind of opening—

a skilled demon could enter through.

*Night Hound warned me.*

*The Sixth Corpse is especially dangerous with words.*

Dolores watched Bartolomeo.

Gentlemanly appearance.

Soft voice.

Flattering words delivered as if every one of them came from sincere admiration.

None of it moved her.

Partly because her mental discipline was strong.

Mostly because—

Oracle’s success had not truly been hers.

Praise for someone else’s plan did not intoxicate her.

“You praise me too highly.”

Bartolomeo misread her cool response.

“Hm.”

“The Quilt matter really was unfortunate.”

“…?”

Dolores looked up.

“You are part of the New Testament Faction, correct?”

“…Yes.”

“I’ve always been close with Old Testament priests.”

Apparently—

Bartolomeo assumed Dolores disliked him because of those ties.

So he began explaining.

“I would regret it if that alone made you distance yourself.”

“You are correct that I have many close relationships in the Old Testament Faction.”

“But I would gladly build the same relationship with the New Testament.”

“The obstacle is simply that they often treat accumulated wealth as something inhuman.”

“Dirty.”

“Ugly.”

“Tempting.”

Bartolomeo cut into the bleeding meat on his plate.

“Money itself is colorless.”

“Odorless.”

“Formless.”

“It has no morality.”

“It is perfectly neutral.”

“Use it well—

and it produces good.”

“Use it badly—

and it produces evil.”

He lifted one shoulder.

“Like fire.”

“Fire can destroy a mountain.”

“Or save a freezing child in winter.”

“People who call wealth evil are usually one of two things.”

“Failures who never possessed enough of it to understand.”

“Or victims taught to hate wealth by people who already control society and wish to keep competitors poor.”

He smiled.

“If large fortunes can be used for good—

then perhaps you need not hate the Old Testament merely because it is wealthy.”

Dolores had heard versions of this argument many times.

Humbert loved making them.

“….”

Her gaze moved to the hourglass at the edge of the table.

Time was passing.

Dolores counted silently.

Then replied:

“If large wealth is truly used for good—

I hope you are right.”

Bartolomeo laughed.

“Hahaha!”

“Exactly.”

He cut another piece of meat.

Chewed with obvious pleasure.

“Saintess.”

“Why aren’t you eating?”

“Does the meal not suit you?”

“The meat?”

“No.”

“It looks excellent.”

“You certainly seem to enjoy it.”

“Of course.”

“This is the finest quality.”

“I’m very particular about meat.”

“Eat slowly.”

“We still have plenty of time.”

Dolores smiled.

“Forgive me.”

“I don’t particularly enjoy eating human flesh.”

Bartolomeo’s knife stopped.

“…!”

Slowly—

he lifted his eyes.

“Saintess.”

“That joke goes too far.”

“Are you making some metaphor about wealth being built from the flesh and blood of exploited workers?”

“If so—

I may need to reconsider what I said earlier.”

“No.”

Dolores’s smile disappeared.

“I mean human flesh literally.”

“I already know.”

Bartolomeo became still.

“…Know what?”

Dolores answered firmly.

“That Bartolomeo Bourgeois—

head of House Bourgeois—

has not merely maintained close relations with Quovadis’s Old Testament Faction.”

“You intend to swallow it.”

“Hehehe.”

“To do that—

you funded and cultivated heresies and cults across the Empire.”

“You used them to destabilize Quovadis from below.”

“Hehehe.”

“And the one behind the entire structure—”

Dolores stared directly at him.

“is a demon.”

The laughter stopped.

For one breath—

silence.

Then—

“Hehehe…”

“HAHAHAHAHA!”

Bartolomeo threw back his head.

The laugh boomed so loudly that the mountains of coins around them rattled in sympathy.

Dolores did not move.

“Aren’t you afraid?”

“A visitor knows what you are.”

“Afraid?”

Bartolomeo looked genuinely amused.

“Why would I be?”

He leaned back.

“Who in this world could possibly frighten me?”

The pressure around Dolores thickened.

Demonic.

Crushing.

Then—

from somewhere across the golden dark—

a dry voice answered.

“Here I am.”

The pressure on Dolores vanished.

On a distant hill of coins—

a red light flickered.

Night Hound had arrived.
