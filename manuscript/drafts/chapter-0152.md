# Chapter 152: The Drinking Game (1)

Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Tudor Donquixote, Sancho Barataria, Pigi, Bianca Fo Usher, Sinclaire, Dolores L. Quovadis, and Colosseo Academy. Later relationship developments remain spoiler-guarded.

“Please.”

“Spare me, Vikir.”

“Just this once!”

Tudor—

the confident young man who dreamed of becoming a great hero—

was reduced to begging.

Sancho looked just as defeated.

“Ugh.”

“So this is how I die.”

He sighed.

“…Fine.”

“Kill me.”

“Begging you for mercy wouldn't work anyway.”

Even the future mercenary king—

who dreamed of ruling the North—

had lost the will to resist.

Pigi looked the most betrayed of all.

“Vikir!”

“You betrayed me!?”

“How could you do this to me!?”

Pigi trusted Vikir more than anyone in the room.

That made the last-minute betrayal hurt the most.

Vikir showed no mercy.

“If you can't handle it, die.”

His voice was perfectly dry.

Clack—

a piece moved.

Colorful tokens crossed the flat game board.

Vikir's black piece captured Tudor's blue piece—

Sancho's red piece—

and Pigi's yellow piece.

All three were sent back to the starting point.

Along with—

another round of penalty drinks.

“Aaaagh!”

“I lost again!”

Tudor grabbed his head.

“Why is this bastard so good at Yut!?”

Sancho stared at the glasses in front of him.

“I've already had several penalty drinks.”

Pigi looked pale.

“…I feel sick.”

The three boys stared bitterly at the progress they had built up—

now erased in an instant.

Across the room—

the girls laughed.

“Vikir, you're really good at Yut~”

“Is there some trick to throwing the sticks?”

“You look like someone who does nothing but play games all day! Hahaha~”

The Academy girls were dressed casually now.

Tracksuits.

Loose clothes.

Light makeup.

The faint scent of soap instead of perfume.

Without their usual neat uniforms—

they seemed much more ordinary.

Much closer.

The boys—

who had slipped into the girls' dormitory late at night—

were enjoying the thrill of the gathering.

Earlier—

Tudor had led the group out of the men's dormitory.

They followed the plan.

The girls had loosened the latch on a first-floor restroom window.

The boys slipped inside—

climbed a drainpipe to the second floor—

used the emergency route to reach the third—

then finally made it to the fourth-floor girls' wing.

A place boys were normally forbidden to enter.

They had nearly been caught several times.

But tonight—

the supervisors were strangely lenient.

Perhaps the adults had guessed what was happening—

and decided not to interfere too harshly with a harmless secret meeting among young students.

Whatever the reason—

the infiltration succeeded.

Now boys and girls sat together in one room—

playing **Yut**.

Most penalties involved drinking.

Others were written directly onto spaces on the board.

> Hold the hand of the person you like most for five seconds.
>
> Kiss someone wearing red for ten seconds.
>
> Hug the person opposite you for thirty seconds.

Almost every special square had a different penalty.

And the closer a player came to the finish—

the bolder those penalties became.

Bianca sat on the bed in comfortable clothes and stared at the board.

“…This isn't a drinking game.”

“It's a flirting game.”

Tudor snorted.

“Don't complain just because nobody wants to flirt with you.”

Bianca's eyes sharpened.

“Shouldn't that be my line?”

“Idiot.”

Another Tudor-Bianca argument immediately began.

Sinclaire laughed and stepped between them.

“Oh, come on~”

“Why are you fighting during something this fun?”

She smiled like a friendly puppy.

Sinclaire—

normally a flawless model student in a neatly arranged uniform—

was wearing loose sleepwear.

The boys began whispering.

*…Maybe we should've put more thought into what we're wearing.*

*Are you stupid?*

*You think the girls didn't think about their clothes?*

*Those are battle uniforms.*

While Tudor and Sancho whispered—

“Yut.”

Vikir moved again.

Tudor's piece died.

So did Sancho's.

Then—

“Yut.”

“Mo.”

“Yut.”

“Mo.”

“Yut.”

“Mo.”

“Do.”

Vikir's black pieces moved forward—

retreated—

captured—

and destroyed the others one after another.

“Aaaagh!”

“Dead again!”

Tudor slammed a hand on the floor.

“Let's change the penalty!”

Sancho lifted another glass.

“…At this rate, all we're doing is drinking.”

Both had begun to regret inviting Vikir.

Then—

“Everyone…”

“You're still students.”

“Don't drink too much.”

The voice belonged to Dolores.

She too was dressed comfortably—

an oversized shirt and pajama pants—

sitting cross-legged on the bed.

Internally—

she was conflicted.

*They're volunteering through Golden Week.*

*Surely one night of harmless fun is fine.*

*Am I being too strict?*

*But if someone gets hurt…*

*Maybe I should tell them to drink less.*

*Would that make me look old-fashioned?*

Tudor noticed her expression.

He immediately raised a bottle.

“Don't worry, President!”

“This isn't alcohol!”

“It's soda!”

Dolores relaxed.

“Oh.”

Of course—

she did not realize the “soda” contained alcohol.

Meanwhile—

Vikir continued dominating the board.

Why was someone who normally avoided attention—

suddenly trying this hard?

*…It reminds me of the past.*

That was the reason.

Yut.

Throw four sticks.

Depending on which sides land upward—

the result becomes **Do, Gae, Geol, Yut,** or **Mo**—

traditionally linked with pig, dog, sheep, cow, and horse.

Pieces move across the board.

Land on an opponent—

and you capture them.

Send them back to the beginning.

During the Age of Destruction—

the game had been common among soldiers stationed on the front lines.

Not because it was especially exciting.

But because—

during long periods of waiting—

there was often nothing else to play.

*…I played this until I was sick of it in the trenches at Hill Seven on the Western Front.*

Long wars contained long stretches of boredom.

Men trapped together for months—

sometimes years—

found ways to pass the time.

Yut was one of them.

Back then—

the game had been an old-fashioned classic known mostly among front-line soldiers.

That meant plenty of veterans had developed strange tricks and techniques.

Now—

in this era—

it had returned as a fashionable new board game.

Most students barely knew how to play.

Even Pigi—

the second-best player in the room—

had terrible throwing technique.

*…Throw like that in the army and the other soldiers will take your entire pay.*

Front-line veterans in the Age of Destruction had been frighteningly good at these games.

Vikir never cared much for gambling.

But superiors had dragged him into enough matches—

that his skill had become exceptional.

Clack!

The sticks fell.

Vikir's black piece advanced—

captured one of Sinclaire's white pieces five spaces ahead—

then on the next turn retreated one space—

and captured another.

Both of Sinclaire's pieces returned to the start.

She groaned.

“Bro!”

“Were you a professional player or something?”

“Why are you so good at this!?”

“Losing to you in studying was bad enough…”

“Now games too?”

Vikir had already told her not to call him Big Brother.

Sinclaire had simply shortened it to “Bro.”

Tudor—

whose pieces Vikir had captured again and again—

stared at him.

“Dice.”

“Yut.”

“Cards.”

“Is there *any* game you're bad at?”

“No.”

Vikir answered immediately.

The confidence was so absolute—

everyone in the room stared.

A few girls edged closer.

“Vikir, Vikir!”

“Can you let me ride your piece?”

“I want to see your face.”

“Can I move your bangs just once?”

“Take off your glasses.”

“Aren't they uncomfortable?”

“Want me to cut your hair?”

“Especially those bangs…”

Hands lightly tapped his arm.

His knee.

His sleeve.

Then another girl showed interest.

“…You're good at board games?”

Dolores.

The student council president looked at him carefully.

“Then…”

“Can you play Go?”

Dolores knew almost none of the other games being played tonight.

But Go—

was different.

Vikir nodded.

“A little.”

Dolores's eyes brightened.

“Oh?”

“What's your rank?”

“…Around second dan.”

Dolores nearly smiled.

*Second dan.*

*That's good enough to brag about.*

For comparison—

Dolores was **amateur fifth dan**.

As a child—

she had become so strong inside her family that they needed to bring in outside teachers.

During her first year at Colosseo—

she had been the ace of the Go club.

By second year—

she led it.

She had thrown herself into the activity with real enthusiasm.

Then—

no new members joined.

The club eventually dissolved.

After that—

almost no one around Dolores knew how to play.

And even when someone did—

her student-council and Saintess duties left little time for matches.

Her old hobby quietly disappeared.

But now—

in front of her—

was a first-year claiming to be good at every game.

Second dan.

Dolores looked at him.

*Then maybe I can finally play properly.*

The others caught on immediately.

“Whoa!”

“President, are you challenging Vikir!?”

“That's so cool!”

“The all-capable student council president against the board-game specialist!”

“Who wins!?”

The room enthusiastically welcomed the match.

Dolores rose as though pushed along by the cheers.

Until now—

she had stayed at the edge of the party because she did not understand the other games.

Go—

she understood very well.

It had been a long time since she had sat at a board.

*Then…*

*shall we play properly?*

Dolores picked up the simple Go set Tudor had brought.

She smiled.

Her hidden strength—

amateur fifth dan.

And as far as she knew—

Vikir was only around second dan.

“How about one penalty drink per captured group?”

The students Vikir had been crushing all night cheered.

Finally—

someone might stop him.

Vikir looked at Dolores.

Then gave the same indifferent nod as always.

“…Fine.”
