Sakkuth froze.

The killing intent Garam had shown earlier—

was not worth comparing.

This one was so dense—

the target could almost see it.

Raised eyes.

A mouth ripped wide.

Blade-like teeth.

A black dog.

Burning—

like a flame ready to tear apart heaven and earth.

Then—

the one behind that shape stepped forward.

Not an ordinary dog.

A hound.

A hellhound bred to tear demons apart.

Vikir’s red eyes reflected Sakkuth’s fear.

“Stop.”

Quiet voice.

Almost gentle.

And beneath it—

violence compressed so tightly it felt worse than shouting.

Sakkuth knew.

He stepped back.

Two years ago—

he had fought Vikir once.

And been destroyed.

*At least know whether you are predator or prey.*

Even now—

the memory of removing nails from his own mouth made him grind his teeth.

Still—

he could not simply submit forever.

If he ever reached Level Nine—

they would meet again.

Sakkuth growled.

“What if I refuse?”

“I have nothing left.”

“It can’t get worse.”

“There is always another basement below the basement.”

Vikir spoke casually.

“If you want—

I can show you one now.”

“….”

Sakkuth knew exactly what hid behind that calm face.

Vikir did not bluff.

If he said he would show you the bottom—

he would.

If he said there was something beneath it—

you would see that too.

After two years—

Sakkuth understood him.

*Insane bastard.*

*What kind of eyes are those?*

Vikir had the face of a noble young master.

His eyes—

did not belong to someone young.

They carried hatred and killing intent accumulated over a lifetime of battle.

How many fights to the death—

how many killing fields—

did a person have to cross for eyes like that?

Even Sakkuth—

who had committed illegal human experiments and massacres—

could not imagine it.

Grit.

He stared at Vikir.

Then spoke through clenched teeth.

“I’ll remember today.”

“I will repay you.”

“Try.”

“If you can.”

“But hurry.”

Vikir shifted the conversation.

“I’m leaving today.”

“…What?”

Sakkuth stared.

Then laughed.

“You really are insane.”

“Escape Nouvelle Vague?”

“….”

“Even **He** couldn’t do that.”

“What makes you think someone like you—”

Vikir interrupted.

“Why do you assume escape is impossible?”

“…What?”

“Is your imagination that limited?”

Sakkuth frowned.

He did not understand.

Vikir smiled faintly.

“You think you were abandoned.”

“You weren’t.”

“…?”

“Because I like what I see.”

Vikir’s gaze traveled over him.

“I watched you for two years.”

“You’re not especially strong.”

“Your will isn’t impressive either.”

“But your recklessness.”

“Your ability to act.”

“Your loyalty.”

“Those are useful.”

“What the hell are you saying?”

“You’ll understand soon.”

“After I escape.”

Sakkuth clicked his tongue.

Turned away.

“Not worth listening to.”

“Stop talking nonsense about escape.”

“One day—

I’ll kill you myself.”

But his eyes had changed.

A tiny shake.

Vikir saw it.

The instinct of an old hound did not miss things like that.

Tap!

Sakkuth turned.

Ran through the rocky canyon.

Plague-filled blood mist trailed behind him.

“…Easy personality to use.”

That was Vikir’s judgment.

Then—

he looked down.

Two guards.

**Kirko Grimm.**

Unconscious.

A severe wound on her head.

Large eyes.

Straight nose.

Stubborn mouth.

Skin pale from living without sunlight.

Hair falling to her waist.

If not for the uniform and sword—

she might have looked like a sheltered noble daughter.

“Dead?”

Vikir supported the back of her head.

Checked her pulse.

“…Mm…”

Kirko groaned unconsciously.

Her brow tightened.

Cold sweat remained.

Her body temperature had dropped.

But the bleeding had stopped.

A weak pulse remained.

Alive.

*Lucky.*

With proper treatment—

and enough rest—

she should recover.

The problem—

was Garam.

*Bad.*

One glance was enough.

Sakkuth’s repeated attacks had destroyed him beyond repair.

Bones crushed into fragments.

Fragments driven through organs.

Pressure ruptures throughout the body.

Vital organs failing.

Body temperature rising under plague contamination.

If he could be moved instantly to a surface temple—

treated by senior priests—

maybe there was a chance.

Not here.

Inside Nouvelle Vague—

death was inevitable.

Minutes at most.

And the pain—

every nerve feeling burned alive—

would continue until the end.

Then—

“I…”

Garam spoke.

Barely.

“Kir…”

“ko…”

Both eyes were destroyed.

He could not see.

His teeth were shattered.

His tongue torn.

Still—

he forced out words.

Vikir stood beside him.

Listened.

Garam’s hand moved weakly through empty air.

Like someone searching for the hand of the person he loved.

“I wanted…”

“to show her…”

“the outside…”

“world…”

The sentence did not become beautiful.

Not like a novel.

Not like a heroic tale.

It simply broke.

Fragments.

Weak.

Empty.

Then Garam’s body relaxed completely.

“….”

Vikir stood beside him for a moment.

Then knelt.

Closed the ruined eyes.

“Unfortunate.”

“I did not intend for this.”

Vikir had only needed Garam’s uniform materials.

Badge.

Nameplate.

Rank insignia.

Military decorations.

The situation had moved faster than expected.

Garam’s death was not something Vikir had directly caused.

Still—

it left bitterness.

“At least your resistance kept her alive.”

“Rest.”

“Go somewhere better.”

Vikir used **Starvation Drought**.

The Blood Daylily ability drew blood and moisture out of Garam’s corpse.

Blood dried.

Turned powdery.

Flesh shriveled.

Bone.

Organs.

Everything collapsed slowly toward dust.

Sssss—

Soon—

Garam’s remains became ash.

Wind carried them away.

The dream and soul of a young man who had come from the distant surface—

now wandered in this deep-sea prison.

Even after death—

guards and prisoners alike could not truly leave Nouvelle Vague.

Clink.

Badges.

Epaulettes.

Rank insignia.

They fell to the ground.

Vikir gathered them.

Uniform.

Decorations.

Rank markings.

Everything he needed.

Then—

“…!”

His head lifted.

Footsteps.

Many of them.

Coming from beyond the steam-filled canyon.

Thud.

Thud.

Thud.

Thud.

Reinforcements.

Vikir glanced back.

The warmth that should have vanished with Garam—

seemed to linger around Kirko.

A small wind circled her.

Brushed through her hair.

Wiped sweat from her brow.

Then disappeared beyond the firelight.

“….”

Vikir bowed his head briefly toward the wind.

Then left through the opposite side of the canyon—

carrying Garam’s clothes.

The materials were complete.

No reason to delay.

**Escape.**

Tonight—

the hound would leave Nouvelle Vague.

The plan would begin immediately.
