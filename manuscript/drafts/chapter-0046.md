# Chapter 46: The Husband Hunt (1)

Recovered English Chapter 46 is a secondary alignment witness only and contains severe Camus/gender corruption. “So this is what you meant by something we needed to do together?”

Vikir frowned at the table before him.

Across from him, Camus rested her chin in both hands and smiled brightly.

Steaming bread, potatoes, boiled meat, and vegetables covered the table.

“You need to eat if you want strength. Come on, eat more.”

Camus looked ready to feed him herself.

Vikir put a hand to his forehead.

He had already spent more than ten days at this fortress.

During that time, Camus seemed to have overcome much of the grief brought on by Rosie's death.

The way she had chosen to do so, however, was becoming a serious headache for Vikir.

She insisted on eating with him every time, even coming to make a scene outside his quarters.

Five or six times a day.

“...Why are we eating six meals a day?”

“Small meals more often are good for you.”

“I normally eat once a day. I'd rather skip the next one.”

“Oh, no. Not eating lowers combat effectiveness. That's against military regulations.”

“You're saying that when you're barely eating yourself.”

Caught out, Camus looked away awkwardly.

Then she casually stuffed a little salad into her mouth and chewed.

“I'm already full just watching you eat.”

“...”

Vikir chewed his meat with a frown.

He genuinely could not understand why a fifteen-year-old girl from another family, whom he had only just seen again after seven years, was being so attentive to him.

*Is this about a political marriage?*

Before his regression, Camus had discussed strategic marriages with numerous candidates, but she had never actually married any of them.

If Morgue had decided that Vikir was an excellent candidate for a political marriage, that would be troublesome.

*I'm going to leave Baskerville soon.*

Not merely leave it.

Someday, he intended to destroy it.

He had spent his first life doing every filthy task the clan demanded of him. What had he received in return?

A false charge, a blade hanging over his neck, and the revolting sensation of waiting beneath the guillotine.

He intended to repay every bit of it.

So it would be better not to become entangled with a daughter of Morgue.

A needless love triangle or similar complication would only become a problem later.

The same applied not merely to Camus, but to any woman in the world.

Completely unaware of what was going through his head, Camus tilted hers innocently.

“Is there shit in the meat?”

“...?”

“You've got the face of someone chewing shit.”

Vikir blinked a few times without reacting, then picked up his fork and continued eating.

He still did not look at her.

Meanwhile, Vikir's stubbornly indifferent attitude felt strangely fresh to Camus.

Whenever she invited someone to eat with her, people normally accepted with delight, whether they were inside or outside Morgue, young or old, male or female.

Men from outside the clan in particular usually arrived at the table already wearing expectant smiles.

It was only natural.

Camus was young, brilliant, beautiful, and likely to hold enormous power in the future.

Yet Vikir overturned fifteen years of assumptions and experience as if none of them mattered.

“We should eat separately from now on.”

“What? Why?”

“It's uncomfortable.”

Vikir set down his fork.

Camus nodded as though she had finally understood.

The dining hall around Vikir was indeed almost entirely filled with women.

Women received the meals. Women distributed them. Hardly any men could be seen.

Camus gave a small laugh.

“Oh, is that all? Morgue is matriarchal. Most of our combat personnel are women. The men generally handle the cooking and other work behind the lines.”

It was the opposite of the overwhelmingly male Baskerville Clan.

Just as the male body offered a slight advantage in learning swordsmanship, the female body offered a slight advantage in learning magic. Morgue's fighting forces had naturally developed around that difference.

The men, in turn, handled much of the rear-line logistics and supply work, which was every bit as important as fighting.

“The man in charge of supplies is down with chest pain right now, so we're short on provisions. Eat while there's still plenty here.”

Camus completely ignored Vikir's suggestion that they eat separately.

Instead, she placed several boiled potatoes on his plate and smoothly changed the subject.

“You don't have these back home, do you?”

She was talking about the potatoes.

They were a new variety improved through Morgue's plant magic, with higher nutritional value and faster absorption than ordinary potatoes.

The problem was that the breeding had focused so heavily on nutrition that the taste had suffered.

Camus chewed one of the dry potatoes.

“Nutritionally, the improvement was a success. The only problem is that they taste awful. Still better than the haggis you eat back home, isn't it?”

Vikir studied the potato for a moment before picking up his fork.

Apparently, he would not be getting rid of Camus anytime soon.

This was Morgue territory, and she was the person in charge here.

For now, Vikir decided to follow the conversation where she wanted it to go.

“The taste isn't bad.”

“...What?”

Camus stared at him.

Vikir picked up one of Morgue's potatoes with a perfectly serious expression.

“I said it isn't bad. Your cooking method is the problem.”

“What? Excuse me, but the cook at this fortress came from the Imperial Capital. I brought him here specifically because I'm picky about food. If even *he* can't make these taste good, then they really don't taste good.”

Vikir shook his head.

*If supplies are disrupted, we'll probably be eating these for a while.*

He took the potato and walked into the kitchen.

There, he cut it into several pieces and lightly coated them with salt and vinegar.

Then he put them into a pot with dried Mentrake leaves and juice from the fruit of a Sabik tree and began to simmer them together.

One of the cooks approached in surprise.

“Hey, what are you doing—”

Camus, who had followed Vikir inside, stopped him.

“...”

She watched Vikir's hands closely.

A little later, Vikir held out one of the lightly cooked potatoes to the cooks.

“Preparing them this way removes the earthy smell and brings out the sweetness. The texture also becomes soft like an ordinary potato.”

The kitchen erupted in amazement.

“Oh! You can prepare this variety like that?”

“That's incredible. This potato hasn't existed for long. How did you know?”

“It's true! The earthy taste is gone! And the texture really is like a normal potato!”

The cooks immediately began using Vikir's simple method.

Morgue's warriors were delighted when the potatoes in their rations suddenly tasted much better.

So were Staffordshire and Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro, who were eating among the Morgue soldiers.

“Young Master! How did you know how to prepare potatoes like this? Even the Morgue people didn't know!”

Staffordshire stared at him with wide eyes.

Vikir did not answer. He merely looked away.

This variety of Morgue potato had only just been developed.

A little more than a decade from now, however, it would become a battlefield staple.

During the Age of Destruction, when supplies were desperately scarce, warriors fighting demons had spent countless campaigns devising ways to make limited food as palatable as possible.

Veteran mercenaries had wrung every possible flavor out of the simplest ingredients. Techniques for removing bitterness and earthy odors from potatoes were among the results.

Vikir had survived countless battlefields himself, so he had naturally learned many kinds of survival cooking.

Much of that knowledge had been acquired through miserable trial and error, but some of the results had been surprisingly good.

Meanwhile—

“...!”

Camus's eyes widened when she tasted a potato prepared with Vikir's method.

“Hey. Want to come cook at my house?”

“...”

“Come on. I'll take you in and let you live with me.”

Vikir sighed quietly.

Camus's eyes continued to sparkle.

“Come to think of it, when are you entering the academy? Let's coordinate. I might enter a year or two early. Wouldn't it be fun if we became first-years together—”

At that moment—

“...!”

The playfulness vanished from Camus's eyes.

She stopped in the middle of her sentence and closed her mouth.

A moment later—

“...”

Vikir fell silent as well.

The atmosphere beyond the window had changed in an instant.

Both of them sensed the ominous presence before anyone else did.

Then—

CLANG! CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!

A harsh alarm bell rang outside.

It was the signal for a barbarian attack.
