# Chapter 36: Slave Auction (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/036.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 36 is an independently aligned secondary witness only. Established project/Fandom terminology controls **Sword Graduator**, **5th Fang**, **Beelzebub**, and **Pit Bull Knights**. Baron Gambino's secretary remains unnamed because the Chinese source has not revealed her identity yet.

The face of the slave boy who stepped onto the stage silenced the entire auction hall.

His cheeks still carried a trace of youthful softness, but beneath it was a clean, slender jawline.

A straight nose.

Firm crimson lips.

Thick brows and long eyelashes.

And, above all, the rare combination of black hair and red eyes.

Most of his exposed skin had been browned by the sun, but paler marks beneath what little clothing remained suggested that his natural complexion had once been nearly flawless.

Even with nothing more than the basic grooming given to a slave before auction, the boy was strikingly handsome.

Vikir.

Vikir Van Baskerville.

He stood on the auction stage in chains.

The auctioneer spread his arms.

“Ladies and gentlemen! What do you think? This is one of our final lots tonight! With merchandise of this quality, I'm expecting a spectacular bidding war!”

The room remained silent for a moment longer.

Even the feral tribeswoman in the iron cage, who had continued struggling after her failed auction, had gone strangely quiet when Vikir appeared.

Then—

A bidding paddle rose.

“Six hundred million!”

The noblewoman in the butterfly mask shouted almost breathlessly.

“Six hundred million? No—six hundred fifty million!”

A middle-aged man across from her shot to his feet.

“Eight hundred million!”

“What?! You're a man!”

“What does that have to do with eight hundred million?”

“That's not what I meant! What are you planning to do with the boy?”

“Hmph! Train him as a gladiator, obviously!”

“Don't make me laugh! Look at him! Could he even lift a sword?”

“Enough, both of you. Fight at home if you want to fight. Nine hundred million.”

“One billion!”

“One billion one hundred fifty million!”

“One billion three hundred million!”

“One billion five hundred million!”

The bidding exploded.

The numbers leapt into the billions while the auctioneer gleefully calculated his commission.

Then—

“Six billion.”

The figure landed like a bomb.

Every head turned toward a corpulent man clicking his tongue impatiently.

“...That is...”

“Baron Gambino.”

“You don't see him in places like this often.”

“I heard he's swallowed up a huge amount of underground money lately. No wonder he can throw that kind of cash around.”

Several bidders grimaced and lowered their paddles.

A few noblewomen looked tempted to pool their money, but competing with Baron Gambino—whose influence in the underworld had recently surged—was another matter entirely.

Then the woman standing beside him spoke.

“...Baron. Isn't six billion excessive?”

She was his secretary.

Short greenish-blond hair.

A monocle.

A cold, haughty air.

Baron Gambino frowned at the interruption, then cleared his throat.

“A slave this beautiful could fetch far more in the Imperial Capital. Buy him now, resell him later, and there will still be profit.”

“He is extraordinarily handsome, yes. But six billion is still too much. And reselling him for profit assumes, of course, that Your Excellency leaves him in a condition to be resold.”

“Hmm. Hmph!”

Baron Gambino noticed the looks around him and suddenly raised his voice.

“Shut up, woman! I give you a little favor and you think you can climb over my head?”

“...”

“I like that boy! I'm buying him with my own money!”

The secretary sighed and shook her head.

A short time later, Baron Gambino had completed the enormous purchase and brought Vikir to his side.

Then he reached out and began groping Vikir's buttocks.

“...”

For once, Vikir looked genuinely surprised.

His gaze moved from Gambino's face to the thick hand kneading him, then back again.

Gambino grinned at the reaction.

“What? Not used to it? You'd better get used to it. This will happen often from now on.”

“...”

“Hahaha! Don't worry. I'll let you touch mine too. Tonight—”

His gaze crawled over Vikir's body.

At that moment, Vikir raised both hands.

Gambino assumed he wanted the cuffs removed and reached for the key with a smile.

Snap!

Crack!

The steel restraints tore apart in an instant.

Handcuffs and chains ripped like paper beneath Vikir's bare hands.

Then Vikir extended those hands toward the speechless baron.

“Turn around.”

Gambino never had time to resist.

Vikir flipped the heavy man over with ease and seized him from behind.

Rrrrip!

A horrible tearing sound filled the room.

“Aaaaaagh!”

Gambino screamed like an animal being slaughtered as blood sprayed across the floor.

The surrounding guests began screaming as well.

Mercenary guards rushed in.

But—

Slash! Slash! Slash!

Their heads separated from their bodies before they could reach him.

A black blade had emerged from Vikir's wrist.

Bzzzzzz—

The magic sword Beelzebub drank the blood and trembled with pleasure.

“Get him!”

“Kill him!”

The nobles called in their private guards.

Mercenaries and knights drew weapons and charged, aura flaring from their blades.

Vikir released his own aura.

Dark crimson.

Dense.

Almost liquid.

Then came the Baskerville Fang Sword Style.

The **5th Fang**.

Five hidden strikes burst outward at once and bit into five throats.

Thud.

Thud.

Thud.

Headless bodies collapsed to their knees beneath a rain of blood.

The fighters who had survived only because they stood farther back froze in horror.

That viscous, blood-red aura could mean only one thing.

A **Sword Graduator**.

A living weapon powerful enough to be counted among a nation's military assets.

A killing machine forged for battle.

Every time Vikir stepped forward, another head fell.

One step.

One kill.

He crossed only a few dozen paces, yet blood already rained around him.

The same thought filled every mind.

‘We can't win against a Sword Graduator!’

The moment the swordsmen recognized his liquid aura, many abandoned the fight and tried to flee.

But the nobles and wealthy patrons screamed after them.

“You idiots! He's only one man! Numbers can overwhelm even a Graduator!”

“Run away and you'll end up slaves yourselves!”

“If you leave now, you won't get paid!”

“Think of your families!”

Some mercenaries stopped.

More armed men flooded into the hall.

Soon hundreds of mercenaries and retainers had Vikir surrounded.

“What the hell is that boy?”

“How could someone that young be a Graduator?”

“That swordsmanship... Isn't that Baskerville?”

“Is he even human?”

Swords.

Spears.

Arrows.

Magic.

Layer after layer of killing force surrounded him.

Even a Sword Graduator could be buried under concentrated attacks from that many enemies.

Then came another problem.

“You bastard! Drop your weapon and surrender, or your companion dies!”

The clown auctioneer had taken a hostage.

A knife pressed against the throat of Chihuahua Baskerville.

Chihuahua's goatee trembled as he looked toward Vikir.

“...”

Vikir stopped.

The mercenaries tightened the circle around him.

Then Chihuahua shouted.

“Deputy Magistrate! I'm fine! Worry about yourself!”

With a determined expression, he grabbed the clown's arm and yanked it toward himself.

He was trying to force the knife into his own throat.

The clown panicked.

“Are you insane?! What are you doing?!”

“Let go! There is no word for compromise in my dictionary! I'd rather die than become a burden to my superior!”

“With a face like yours, how can you say something that dramatic?! Let go!”

The two struggled over the knife.

Watching them, Vikir gave a faint smile.

Then—

Tsk!

A tiny drop of aura shot through the clown's forehead.

Vikir caught Chihuahua as he fell free and stepped down from the stage.

The killing intent around them intensified.

Mercenaries and knights belonging to the local families aimed blades, bows, and spells at both men.

“M-My lord. Are you really all right? Even if you're an extraordinary fighter, there are far too many of them... and I'm only making things harder.”

Chihuahua's concern was reasonable.

Vikir remained calm.

“Don't worry.”

He reached down to his thigh.

Then he tore open a narrow cut in his own flesh.

From inside it, he extracted something that had been hidden beneath the skin.

A small horn shaped like a red fang.

“Before I left the Baskerville estate... I received one trump card from the Patriarch.”

Vikir raised the blood-covered horn to his lips.

Screeeech—!

A piercing note split the air.

The people surrounding him tensed, unsure what he had just done.

But the real change began overhead.

Rrrip!

The great tent roof, spread across the clearing like a false night sky, tore open.

Black shadows poured through.

One after another, knights in dark cloaks dropped from above.

Thud!

Thud!

Thud!

Wherever they landed, blood erupted and heads flew.

“What are these things?!”

“There are a hundred of them!”

“They're all Graduators!”

One hundred Sword Graduators.

One hundred killing machines with crimson aura coating their blades.

They slaughtered everything immediately in front of them, then formed ranks behind Vikir with perfect discipline.

The survivors finally noticed the red fang emblems on their chests.

Their faces went white.

“D-Don't tell me...”

“No. It can't be.”

“That emblem... They're the only ones who use it!”

The **Pit Bull Knights** of the Baskerville Clan.

The empire's most vicious specialists in extermination had arrived.

The black-cloaked hounds stood silently behind Vikir.

He gave them a single order.

“Bite them all to death.”
