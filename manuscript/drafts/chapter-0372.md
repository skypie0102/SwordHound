# Chapter 372: Solitary Confinement (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/372.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 370 is the verified shifted secondary witness. **Angajuman** remains a source-given alias; Vikir reaches a private identity hypothesis in this chapter, but the true name is not stated aloud and is therefore not promoted to production canon. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Baskerville, Cane Corso, Black Sun, D'Ordume D'Orcdile, Winston Smith, Warden Orca, the Seven Counts, and the numbered Baskerville Forms.

The old man who called himself Angajuman—

apparently had nothing but time.

He kept talking.

“I’ve been in solitary for years.”

“Let’s see…”

“Every time I count sixty seconds in my head—

I draw one line.”

“I’ve reached about three million lines now.”

Vikir clicked his tongue internally.

Even for him—

one day inside this coffin-like cell felt long.

Oppressive.

Endless.

*Years?*

*That’s absurd.*

He could not see the old man.

Probably never would.

But someone who had actually survived this place for years—

could not be dismissed lightly.

“How haven’t you starved?”

“Hisss.”

“Every two days—

they drop a few crumbs of black bread through the tube.”

“That can’t provide enough water.”

“Dew.”

“Collect it from the air.”

Vikir paused.

*Dew?*

Inside this scorching prison?

Angajuman continued as though explaining an ordinary household trick.

“Truthfully—

you can survive even without the bread.”

“Slow the body.”

“Reduce metabolism until it barely moves.”

“Break down stored nutrients—

little by little.”

“….”

“Young people today live too desperately.”

“Too much passion becomes poison.”

“You spend all your time charging forward.”

“Sometimes—

stop.”

“Rest.”

“Look back.”

“Live like stone.”

“Like water.”

It sounded like the lazy preaching of an old man in retirement.

Here—

it was different.

This was Nouvelle Vague’s solitary confinement.

A place worse than Level Nine.

And the man speaking—

claimed he had survived inside it for years.

*If he hasn’t gone insane—

he must be an extraordinary criminal.*

Of course—

perhaps he **had** gone insane.

Angajuman continued talking.

“You sound young.”

“What did you do to end up here?”

“Crime has no age.”

“Hisss.”

“Fair.”

“So tell this old man.”

“What was your crime?”

Vikir answered shortly.

“Silent assassination.”

The old man laughed.

“Why imprison you for doing useful work?”

“Your family discarded you?”

“Something like that.”

“Oh dear.”

“A hound, then.”

“When the mountain rabbits are gone—

the hunting dog goes into the cooking pot.”

“Some things never change.”

Then—

casually—

“You’re Baskerville, aren’t you?”

Vikir’s thoughts stopped.

Angajuman laughed again.

As though he had seen the reaction through solid stone.

“Hissss.”

“So I was right.”

“A hound who worked too hard for the family—

then got thrown into a place like this.”

“Not many families are cold enough for that.”

“The Iron-Blooded Sword House.”

“Or perhaps the Poisonous Dark House.”

“You know Baskerville well.”

“A little.”

“My family—

was once called an Iron-Blooded Sword House too.”

Vikir searched his memory.

Today—

Baskerville stood at the summit of Imperial swordsmanship.

But that had not always been true.

Before Imperial unification—

four other sword families had possessed even greater prestige.

**Bahamut.**

**Gis.**

**Sade.**

**Nibelungen.**

Compared with them—

Baskerville had once been relatively modest.

A wandering mercenary family—

without even a fixed territory.

In that age—

those five lineages had collectively been called the great Iron-Blooded Sword Houses.

Angajuman sighed nostalgically.

“Back then—

our family was famous for the sword too.”

“But beginning with my generation—

we laid the sword down.”

“The inheritance disappeared.”

“…No.”

“Maybe the family itself disappeared.”

“No descendants?”

“Hm.”

“One granddaughter.”

“Whether she’s alive—

who knows?”

“If she is—

she should be older than you.”

“Old enough to be your mother…”

“No.”

“Maybe your aunt.”

“Or older sister.”

“You’ve lived a long time.”

“Yes.”

“Very long.”

“My entire life was war.”

Angajuman laughed softly.

“Then the world suddenly became peaceful.”

“They expected an old thing like me—

to adapt.”

“How could I?”

“War and fighting were my life.”

“You’re still living violently enough.”

“Nouvelle Vague solitary confinement.”

“Consistent to the end.”

“Hissss.”

“Violently?”

“Here?”

“This is a resort.”

“I’m only resting after all the fighting.”

“But soon—

I plan to leave.”

Vikir became more curious.

What kind of life—

made this place feel like vacation?

Apparently—

Angajuman felt the same curiosity.

“You seem able to follow my conversation.”

“Can you discuss the sword?”

**Sword discourse.**

Like masters of Go—

able to describe a game without a board—

great swordsmen could conduct a duel through words alone.

But only if both sides understood sword principles deeply enough.

Vikir accepted.

“You know Baskerville swordsmanship?”

“Roughly.”

“Then begin.”

Angajuman opened with a line.

Vikir answered.

The duel took shape—

inside the mind.

**First Form.**

A fang that pierced deeply into the opponent.

**Second Form.**

A fang that drove deeper—

fixing the target in place.

**Third Form.**

A fang that bit and imposed weight.

**Fourth Form.**

A fang that tore the body open.

Vikir used the first four forms sequentially.

Warm-up.

Angajuman immediately recognized the level of mastery behind them.

“Amazing.”

“A young man not even twenty—

using those first four forms this perfectly?”

“It’s like watching a middle-aged veteran—

who spent his whole life on battlefields.”

Then—

he laughed.

“…Still.”

“Compared with this old man—

you’re a chick.”

Angajuman countered.

Vikir knew nothing about his sword style.

That did not matter.

Angajuman built his counters directly against Vikir’s theory.

And because Vikir knew his own swordsmanship completely—

he could understand exactly how he was being dismantled.

A mental image formed.

Vikir’s First Form—

swallowed by a soft sword movement.

The Second—

exposed his own position.

Before the Third and Fourth could even emerge—

Angajuman’s answer consumed them.

In the imagined duel—

Vikir’s body was already on the verge of being torn apart.

*What is this?*

*This man is not ordinary.*

Vikir abandoned the idea of merely testing him.

His full sword entered the discourse.

**Fifth Form.**

A fang that ripped the body apart.

**Sixth Form.**

A fang that crushed it.

**Seventh Form.**

A fang that ground it down.

**Eighth Form.**

The living limit—

all fangs gathered and fixed into one.

The summit Vikir had reached.

His mental world darkened.

A **Black Sun** rose.

The same ultimate strike—

that had torn through Amdusias.

The imagined battlefield shook.

Yet—

“Hisssss.”

“You really are incredible.”

“At your age—

how can you possibly have reached this realm?”

Angajuman sounded surprised.

Not afraid.

Not pressured.

Then—

the old man answered.

His sword met Vikir’s head-on.

There was nothing frail about it.

Nothing elderly.

Only—

madness.

A life devoted entirely to conflict.

Killing.

Victory.

A berserk competitive instinct—

so violent that even Vikir had to concede ground.

*There are people like this in the world?*

Vikir felt something close to shock.

This was a man produced by only one purpose.

Fight.

Kill.

Conflict.

Every breath—

killing intent.

Every step—

destruction.

A desire to harm others refined until it became almost noble.

A distorted extreme.

A twisted kind of greatness.

Now—

Vikir understood why humanity had buried this man in the deepest solitary cell of the cruelest prison.

Decarabia murmured.

[Mad.]

[Absolutely mad.]

[Demon or human—

this is exactly the type you never involve yourself with.]

Angajuman laughed.

“Talking sword with you—

woke something up in me.”

“I feel younger.”

“Glad to help.”

“You helped a great deal.”

“How long has it been since I felt this?”

“Since Winston and Orca?”

“No…”

“Earlier.”

“Back when I fought Baskerville’s Seven Counts.”

“The Seven Counts?”

“Boston Terrier?”

“Great Dane?”

“Isabella?”

“Who?”

“I don’t know those children.”

“I mean the generation **before** them.”

Of course.

At Angajuman’s age—

his memories reached back at least two generations.

Then the old man asked:

“Who leads Baskerville now?”

“Hugo Le Baskerville.”

“Hugo…”

“Hugo…”

“Who’s that?”

“Never heard of him.”

“Why didn’t that stubborn Cane Corso inherit the House?”

Vikir went still.

“…You knew Cane Corso?”

“Of course.”

“He was harsh.”

“Particular.”

“When I was young—

I picked a fight with him without knowing better.”

“He taught me quite a lesson.”

Angajuman spoke nostalgically—

as if remembering childhood games.

“His cuts still make my skin crawl.”

“Hisss.”

“He must be dead by now.”

“Too much time has passed.”

“Maybe some hallucination lured that fool away—

and he wandered into the Grave of Swords.”

Vikir said nothing.

No reason to mention—

that Cane Corso had indeed reached the Grave of Swords.

Died.

Become a Death Knight.

Reached the Ninth Form.

But Vikir no longer needed more clues.

He had already formed a strong hypothesis—

about the man beside him.

Before regression—

Nouvelle Vague’s guards initially refused to join the war between humanity and demons.

That changed later.

Not only because the situation became desperate.

But because—

Nouvelle Vague itself collapsed.

The prison ceased to exist.

And the guards and prisoners—

were forced onto the surface.

*Warden Orca destroyed Nouvelle Vague himself.*

Why?

There had been many theories.

One—

had always sounded more convincing than the rest.

A single prisoner tried to escape.

Orca—

trying to stop that one man—

chose an extreme solution.

Destroy the entire prison.

If one fugitive justified sacrificing Nouvelle Vague itself—

then that fugitive had to be something monstrous.

There were endless rumors about the identity.

*Probably him.*

If so—

Vikir had a very good idea who Angajuman truly was.

The sword discourse.

His age.

The lost great sword families.

His memories of old Baskerville Counts.

Cane Corso.

Everything pointed in one direction.

Vikir became confident in his deduction.

And reached an immediate conclusion.

*Avoid him.*

As much as possible.

Inside the cell beside him—

was a man more frightening than most demons.
