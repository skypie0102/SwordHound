# Chapter 187: Aftermath (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/187.txt` — `11443659b730d1936b42577d78ec83aa8d772559`. Recovered English Chapter 185 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Sinclaire, Cindywendy, Dolores L. Quovadis, Highbro / Middlebro / Lowbro, Hot Department, Cold Department, and the accepted public **Sword Expert Advanced** cover. The hidden-royal rumor remains speculation; Sinclaire's later identity and post-Academy fate remain unrevealed.

“…Vikir?”

No makeup.

Short silver hair—

still damp.

A faint shampoo scent.

**Sinclaire.**

A commoner with no surname.

And—

despite that—

the top student of the Hot Department.

Vikir raised one eyebrow.

Why here?

Now?

Sinclaire answered before he asked.

“You know about this place too?”

“The Milky Way looks amazing from here.”

She smiled.

Held out a small can.

Beer.

Pssht.

Sinclaire opened hers.

Foam rushed up.

She drank too quickly—

leaving a white mustache above her lip.

She laughed.

Then handed Vikir the second can.

“I brought two.”

“You can have the other one.”

“…Thanks.”

Vikir hesitated.

Accepted it.

“I didn't expect anyone else here.”

Sinclaire leaned against the railing.

“I thought this was my secret spot.”

“When I'm frustrated—”

“I come up here and look at the Milky Way.”

Her mood—

already—

seemed brighter.

Sinclaire apparently felt she had discovered a shared habit.

Vikir thought—

*Dangerous.*

*I need a new meeting point with Cindywendy.*

One person thought—

*Maybe I'll see him here again.*

The other—

*Never use this rooftop again.*

Then Sinclaire noticed the owl.

“Oh.”

“An owl.”

“Cute.”

“Did it bring a letter?”

[Hoo.]

Sinclaire laughed.

“What a strange cry.”

She rubbed the owl's round head.

Then looked at Vikir again.

“You got a letter?”

“Yes.”

“From who?”

Vikir paused.

How exactly did one describe Cindywendy?

Sinclaire's eyes narrowed.

“A girl?”

The owl wore a green-gold ribbon—

the color of Cindywendy's hair.

And Cindywendy—

was indeed a woman.

Vikir nodded.

Sinclaire's expression stiffened slightly.

“Oho.”

“Campus romance?”

“You're confident your grades won't drop while dating?”

“Dating someone from school is one of those freshman dreams, isn't it?”

“It isn't like that.”

Immediate.

Flat.

Certain.

Sinclaire froze.

Then—

exhaled.

Relief.

Subtle—

but real.

“If you're secretly dating someone and hiding it from me—”

“I'll be disappointed.”

“We're close enough to talk about that sort of thing.”

“So if you are—”

“you have to tell me.”

*Close enough?*

Vikir did not understand exactly when that happened.

He did not ask.

Sinclaire kept talking.

More than she usually did at school.

Then—

as if remembering something—

she looked sideways.

“What about the president?”

“Your relationship with her.”

President—

Dolores.

Vikir answered immediately.

“Nothing.”

Sinclaire's eyes narrowed.

“Really?”

“If it's nothing—”

“why has President Dolores been looking for you for days?”

“…?”

That—

Vikir genuinely had not known.

Too many people were already asking to see him.

Principal.

Faculty leaders.

Researchers.

Commanders.

The Crown Prince.

Appointments—

endlessly.

*I barely have time to study.*

*Much less assassinate anyone.*

Cindywendy's one-month intelligence delay—

almost felt convenient.

Sinclaire shrugged.

“I don't know why.”

“But Dolores has definitely been trying to find you.”

Vikir remembered the archery range.

“So that's why she was heading there.”

Sinclaire blinked.

“You saw her?”

“Why didn't you say hello?”

“…We passed each other.”

He could hardly explain—

*I turned into a dog to avoid her and then fled when she threatened to neuter me.*

Sinclaire accepted the vague answer.

Finished her beer.

The dark malt smell drifted through the night.

Then she leaned against the railing.

“I envy you.”

“?”

Vikir tilted his head.

Sinclaire smiled.

“You're always like that.”

“Like what?”

“You don't ask first.”

Vikir gave a faint laugh.

A hound—

did not interrogate its master.

It waited.

Listened.

Moved when ordered.

Perhaps—

to someone constantly approached by others—

that silence felt unusual.

“What I mean is…”

Sinclaire looked across the Academy.

“I'm jealous of you.”

“We're in the same year.”

“But the gap is huge.”

First in the written exam.

Already responsible for multiple research results capable of shaking academic fields.

A first-year operating at a level—

far beyond normal undergraduate study.

And now—

practical strength too.

Vikir—

once suspected of being barely a Beginner—

had publicly shown **Sword Expert Advanced** ability.

For a commoner—

astonishing.

Comparable to—

or better than—

many talented younger members of the Seven Great Houses.

“Written.”

“Practical.”

“You're ridiculous.”

Sinclaire smiled without hostility.

“And people one or two generations above us are already asking to meet you.”

“Everyone's jealous.”

Vikir—

was irritated by exactly that.

Others saw opportunity.

Money.

Authority.

Recognition.

Vikir saw temporary debris.

When the Age of Destruction arrived—

money—

power—

status—

would collapse with everything else.

“All of it is pointless.”

Sinclaire turned.

“You really don't sound like you're from this world sometimes.”

The sentence—

landed harder than she knew.

Then she laughed.

“You talk like an old man.”

“Who'd believe you're eighteen?”

“If everything here is pointless—”

“are you planning to disappear somewhere?”

Crack.

She crushed the empty beer can.

Held it in her palm.

Mana gathered.

Heat.

Then—

metal softened.

Reshaped.

A tiny rabbit—

holding a pocket watch—

formed in her hand.

Sinclaire toyed with the figurine.

“Oh.”

“Have you heard the rumor?”

“What rumor?”

“One of the twentieth class is secretly Imperial blood.”

Vikir frowned.

“Gossip.”

“Maybe.”

Sinclaire's tone—

oddly uncertain.

“But think about it.”

“If someone descended from the Imperial line entered under a hidden identity—”

“they'd probably be talented.”

“They couldn't stand out too early.”

“Otherwise hiding the identity would be pointless.”

“Maybe they're here to recruit people.”

She looked at Vikir.

“And if close friends were suddenly in danger during an exam—”

“someone like that might reveal strength they had been hiding.”

“Enough.”

Vikir cut her off.

“I'm not Imperial blood.”

Sinclaire blinked.

“…I never said you were.”

“?”

Vikir stared.

Sinclaire laughed.

The exchange stirred an old memory.

*Sinclaire was mysterious before too.*

In Vikir's previous life—

after graduation—

Sinclaire disappeared.

No contact with classmates.

No contact with seniors or juniors.

Gone from public history.

Years later—

even Highbro—

Middlebro—

Lowbro—

would occasionally wonder.

*What happened to Sinclaire?*

*She was top of the Hot Department for four years.*

*Where did she come from?*

*Where did she go?*

No one had known.

Sinclaire's voice pulled him back.

“What are you doing after midterms?”

“We get a week off.”

“Any plans?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

“Back training.”

“Lower-body training.”

“Archery.”

Sinclaire pouted.

“I meant plans that aren't exercise.”

Then—

“Come to my house.”

“When you're free.”

“Let's eat together.”

Vikir raised an eyebrow.

She had made a similar invitation before—

at Indulgentia.

Back then—

during fundraising—

Sinclaire had asked why Vikir did not donate.

His answer—

because he had no money.

True.

He had refused Baskerville support.

And—

to preserve the commoner cover—

had not been using Cindywendy's funds either.

Sinclaire had challenged his harsh view of orphanhood.

Vikir—

raised as a Baskerville hound—

and tempered by the Age of Destruction—

saw parents in almost purely functional terms.

Essential during childhood.

Guides through the earliest stage of survival.

After that—

the world had to be faced by one's own strength.

Children under institutional protection—

fed.

educated.

sheltered—

did not look especially pitiful to someone raised as a hunting dog.

War had shaped his values too deeply.

People who had never lived through it—

could not easily understand.

Vikir expected no one to.

Yet Sinclaire—

then—

had looked at him as though she understood at least part of what produced the belief.

She wore that same expression now.

Less cheerful.

More serious.

Almost sad.

“You have to come.”

“All right?”

Then she stood.

“I have to go.”

“My shift is starting.”

*A shift?*

*Now?*

Sinclaire walked toward the rooftop door.

Smiled.

Waved.

Bang.

The door closed.

Vikir looked down.

She had left the metal figurine behind.

A small rabbit—

running desperately—

pocket watch in hand.
