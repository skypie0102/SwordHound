Sinclaire suddenly remembered something.

“Oh!”

“Should we ask my brother to join too?”

Dolores’s attention snapped into focus.

There was only one person in the entire Academy Sinclaire called **brother**.

Vikir.

Dolores pictured him immediately.

Bangs.

Glasses.

Expressionless face.

A tone and manner that often made him seem completely uninterested in the world.

And yet—

she and Vikir had already been through more together than she expected.

The orphanage.

The festival.

The University League.

Then—

the drinking-party accident.

Dolores’s ears turned red.

She buried her face in the papers for a moment.

Then forced herself to calm down.

“…Is he interested in this kind of thing?”

Sinclaire brightened.

“Very.”

“Vikir reads every newspaper he can get his hands on.”

“He always knows what’s happening in society.”

“And lately he’s been interested in economics too.”

“Trade with the western tribes.”

“Old economic newspapers.”

“He’s surprisingly serious about it.”

Pigi nodded.

“Vikir can do almost anything.”

“He’d be a huge help.”

“Honestly—

just having him around makes people feel safer.”

Dolores thought briefly.

Then nodded.

Vikir’s character and ability were already proven.

There was no need to test him.

“Then if you see him—

ask whether he’ll join our temporary club.”

“Okay!”

Sinclaire and Pigi answered together.

“He comes to the library often.”

“I’ll tell him if I see him while I’m on duty.”

“And I’m his roommate.”

“I can tell him tonight.”

“Though lately—

I barely see him at school.”

Sinclaire tilted her head.

“Now that you mention it—

where does he keep going?”

“Pigi, you’re his roommate.”

“Do you know?”

“No.”

“I signed up for late-night independent study lately.”

“By the time I get back, I usually collapse into bed.”

“So I haven’t paid much attention.”

The two continued talking about Vikir.

Dolores stood.

“I’m going to pick up some materials.”

“I left something in the student council office.”

“Okay!”

“See you later!”

Dolores left the classroom.

She walked the corridor while mentally organizing the next stages of the plan.

Then—

near the first-floor post office and drink machines—

she saw someone familiar.

“…Vikir?”

He was carrying a ridiculous stack of letters and parcels.

So many that turning his head was difficult.

Dolores approached.

“Do you need help?”

“That looks heavy.”

“No.”

“It’s lighter than it looks.”

“…All right.”

By coincidence—

they were headed in the same direction.

For several minutes—

they walked side by side in silence.

“….”

“….”

The corridor was strangely empty.

Eventually—

Dolores spoke first.

“That’s a lot of mail.”

“Did you order something?”

“No.”

“Then what is all that?”

“I don’t know either.”

“They came from other schools.”

“The post office told me to collect them.”

Vikir truly seemed confused.

“Can I look?”

“Go ahead.”

Dolores checked the labels.

“Perfume.”

“Skin lotion.”

“Dress shoes.”

“Belts.”

“Hats.”

“Sunglasses.”

“Shirts.”

“Bags.”

“Fountain pens.”

“Ties.”

“Wallets.”

“Sneakers.”

“Hairpins…”

She blinked.

“There’s even an artifact from the University League prize pool.”

“And this—”

“a carriage key?”

Most packages had nearly identical senders.

**<To Vikir>**

**<With love — all members of the Temisquira fan club>**

Another read:

**<These horses and this carriage are my personal offering!>**

**<P.S. — Merelini Lovegood, president of the Vikir fan club>**

Ninety-nine percent of the mail was from Temisquira Women’s College.

The remaining one percent—

from Varangian.

Dolores glanced sideways.

“…That’s an aggressive amount of affection.”

“You’re popular.”

“I have nowhere to put any of it.”

“That’s the problem.”

Dolores almost felt exasperated.

But Vikir genuinely looked inconvenienced rather than pleased.

“It must be difficult being handsome.”

She said it casually.

Only what she actually thought.

Vikir answered immediately.

“I’m not handsome.”

“The people around me are unusual.”

For someone who had spent his pre-regression life with a scarred face—

the answer felt natural.

Dolores did not know that.

She stared.

“…How can you say that with your face?”

“There’s a limit to humility.”

Vikir’s tone stayed stiff.

“Hearing that from you, Senior—

I’m embarrassed.”

Dolores froze.

*Wait.*

*Was that…?*

That sounded suspiciously like a compliment.

Then she remembered the festival.

Someone had asked Vikir about a pretty female student watching him.

And Vikir—

without hesitation—

had identified Dolores.

Heat rushed into her ears again.

“S-speaking of which, Vikir!”

“Are you interested in mock investing?”

Pigi and Sinclaire had planned to ask him later.

But—

since he was here—

Dolores asked directly.

Vikir paused.

“…This competition?”

“Yes.”

“I’m entering.”

“I even made a temporary club.”

“Pigi and Sinclaire are joining too.”

“If you’re interested…”

Vikir shook his head.

“I don’t have time for more club activities.”

“Other than the newspaper club—

I don’t plan to join anything.”

Dolores felt a little disappointed.

She really had hoped he would join.

Still—

club participation was personal.

And she was already grateful he continued fulfilling his newspaper duties despite being busy.

*Right.*

*I still have Mr. Night Hound.*

They reached a junction.

“I’m going this way.”

“All right.”

Dolores turned away.

Then—

“Senior.”

She stopped.

Looked back.

Vikir was watching her.

Then—

“…Good luck.”

Unexpected encouragement.

And—

just barely—

a faint smile.

“Huh?”

“Oh.”

“Thank you.”

“You too.”

Dolores raised both fists awkwardly.

Her ears were even redder than before.

◆◆◆

About three weeks later.

Dolores.

Pigi.

Sinclaire.

The three sat around a table in an empty classroom.

“…This is unbelievable.”

They stared at the pile before them.

Gold.

Coins.

Money stacked high.

In less than three weeks—

Oracle had made a fortune.

First place in the mock-investment competition was no longer even in question.

“President!”

“I’ve never seen this much money in my life!”

“How did you even do this?”

“This is insane!”

Pigi and Sinclaire stared at Dolores in awe.

Dolores looked just as stunned.

*I only followed Mr. Night Hound’s instructions…*

She remembered the first step.

“Find around ten thousand people in finance who are easily persuaded.”

“…Then?”

“Send half of them a letter predicting that Stock A will rise tomorrow.”

“Send the other half a letter predicting it will fall.”

Pigi built the list.

Sinclaire handled the mailing.

Ten thousand letters.

The next day—

Stock A rose.

Vikir immediately narrowed the pool.

Of the five thousand people who had received the correct prediction—

two thousand five hundred were told **Stock B** would rise.

The other two thousand five hundred were told it would fall.

Stock B fell.

So only the two thousand five hundred who had received the “fall” prediction continued.

They were split again.

One thousand two hundred fifty were told **Stock C** would rise.

The other one thousand two hundred fifty were told it would fall.

Stock C rose.

The surviving one thousand two hundred fifty were split again.

Six hundred twenty-five received an “up” prediction for **Stock D**.

The other six hundred twenty-five received a “down” prediction.

Then the same process continued.

Stocks.

Gold.

Beans.

Wheat.

Bronze.

Orichalcum.

Real estate.

Bonds.

The asset did not matter.

Every prediction was divided between two opposite outcomes.

So every round—

roughly half the recipients received a correct prediction.

After about a week—

only around thirty remained.

From their perspective—

Oracle had predicted the market correctly seven days in a row.

They came running.

“Is this Oracle?!”

“You predicted everything!”

“Seven straight days!”

“This must be what prestigious Academy geniuses are like!”

“Please!”

“Take my money!”

“I sold my house!”

“Please invest it!”

The people who had received wrong predictions early—

forgot Oracle almost immediately.

The people who kept receiving correct ones—

became believers.

They never realized they had survived a probability filter.

Then Night Hound gave the next order.

“Tell them we can double their assets.”

“Collect the investment money.”

Dolores did.

Then—

Vikir put that money into Cindywendy’s trading company.

The same company conducting dangerous but highly profitable trade with the western jungle tribes.

Most investors avoided it because of the risk.

But Cindywendy’s third large trade succeeded.

Spectacularly.

Huge dividends followed.

Vikir had told Dolores only one thing.

“I said I had a rich friend.”

Dolores had expected him to borrow money from that friend.

Or receive money.

Instead—

he had turned the friend into the engine of the investment itself.

Pigi and Sinclaire stared at the piles of profit.

“President, you’re incredible!”

“If people learn about this, the Empire’s investment world might change!”

“At this point—

House Bourgeois has to notice us.”

That was the key.

Not merely how much money was made.

But **how**.

Academy students—

using a strange near-scam—

had gathered enormous investment capital.

Then converted it into even larger, legitimate profits.

The result was shocking.

The money trail—

clean.

The creativity—

undeniable.

What Bourgeois elite would not want to meet the young geniuses behind it?

Dolores smiled faintly at Pigi and Sinclaire’s excitement.

*Recruit believers through selective information.*

*Then use privileged information for one decisive move.*

*Mr. Night Hound has definitely done something like this before.*

It was too early to celebrate.

The operation had only reached its middle stage.

Oracle was becoming what Vikir needed.

A financial blade—

pointed toward House Bourgeois.

*Who are you really…?*

Dolores’s curiosity about Night Hound only deepened.
