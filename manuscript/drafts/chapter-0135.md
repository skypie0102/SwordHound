Wide eyes.

Half-open mouths.

The entire classroom stared at Vikir.

Even Professor Banshee Morgue looked different.

People who did not know him might have called it mild surprise.

Anyone familiar with him knew better.

For a man nicknamed the **wax figure** because his expression almost never changed—

this was shock.

Banshee recovered first.

“…Correct.”

Then—

a pause.

“However.”

He adjusted his spectacles.

“Your answer includes several disputed points that academia has not verified.”

“And several facts that have never been formally reported.”

That was true.

Some of Vikir's details were new even to Banshee.

The Fourteenth Height.

The Twenty-Seventh.

Those were not battles a normal undergraduate would ever be expected to know.

Even graduate students might never study them.

The Three-Hundred-Sixth Height—

meanwhile—

was a recent historical discovery.

Research had barely begun.

*This is information senior Imperial officers might know.*

Banshee's gaze sharpened.

*Or perhaps senior Baskerville councilors.*

The question had been far beyond freshman level from the beginning.

Tudor and Bianca had already performed well simply by knowing several of the major engagement sites.

Sinclaire had exceeded ordinary undergraduate knowledge.

But Vikir—

what category was he supposed to fit into?

Banshee spoke.

“How do you know unpublished material?”

“And why did you state it as fact?”

Vikir remained calm.

*I can't exactly say I lived through it.*

When it came to the Ballak and the western Black Mountains—

few people in the Empire knew more than he did.

So he gave Banshee a plausible answer.

“Trade with the western frontier tribes has recently begun.”

“Merchant groups are competing aggressively.”

“Market circulars and investment bulletins carry a great deal of information.”

He lowered his voice slightly.

“If I sounded too certain, I apologize.”

A reasonable retreat.

Banshee had already received more than he expected.

He did not press further.

But his eyes changed.

Contempt—

to curiosity.

“You follow economics?”

“Nothing serious.”

Vikir answered.

“I'm merely sensitive to profit.”

Banshee narrowed his eyes.

“Modest too.”

He flipped through the roster.

Then—

stopped.

“…I see.”

The class waited.

Banshee exhaled.

“A perfect score on the written examination.”

Whispers exploded.

“That's him?”

“The written-exam first place?”

“A perfect score?”

“Is that even possible?”

“Colosseo's theory exam is brutal.”

“I ranked fourth and thought I was going to die.”

Students began looking at Vikir differently.

One person looked more stunned than most.

Sinclaire.

“…Perfect?”

She had scored **931 out of 990**.

An extraordinary result.

Third place sat far below that.

Fourth lower still.

But first place—

990.

The maximum.

A perfect score did not reveal how much more someone might have known.

Only that the exam had run out of points before the student ran out of answers.

Sinclaire stared at Vikir.

Meanwhile—

Banshee closed the roster.

“Vikir.”

“For the quality of your answer—plus ten attitude points.”

Vikir said nothing.

“But.”

Banshee's eyes hardened.

“You had your eyes closed in my class.”

“Minus ten.”

“And one point to the Cold Department group score.”

Then—

as if nothing had happened—

he resumed teaching.

Vikir's personal total remained unchanged.

The Cold Department recovered a point.

Relief spread through the room.

But goodwill did not.

“That guy's arrogant.”

“He aces theory, so he thinks he can close his eyes in class?”

“He almost got all of us penalized.”

“Lucky his answer recovered the group point.”

“Bookworms like that irritate me.”

Colosseo's culture valued practical ability over theory.

That bias was especially strong inside the Cold Department.

Hot Department students—

mages—

respected written study more.

Cold Department students—

warriors—

tended to care far less.

Several students began whispering among themselves.

“He looks like a pure study type.”

“Let's see whether he's still smug this afternoon.”

“Don't we have practical physical education?”

“Naphtali.”

A grin.

“Perfect.”

“We can teach him what this Academy actually values.”

Another student cracked his knuckles.

“Leave him to me.”

The new semester had barely begun—

and competition among freshmen was already sharpening.

◆◆◆

**Tudor Donquixote.**

Class representative of Cold Department A.

He stood on the central training field with a delighted grin.

“This is the class I've been waiting for.”

The afternoon practical was **Naphtali**.

A ball sport.

Forty players divided into two teams.

The objective—

carry or send the ball through the opposing goal.

The field stretched fifty meters by one hundred.

A goal stood at each end.

Players could kick.

Throw.

Run with the ball.

Body-check opponents.

Strike or grapple within the sport's rules.

The choices were broad.

Evade.

Contest.

Pass.

Break through.

Only two things were forbidden.

Weapons.

Magic.

Tudor laughed.

“Finally.”

“Something honest.”

Class A faced Class B.

Across from Tudor—

three familiar faces.

Highbro.

Middlebro.

Lowbro Le Baskerville.

The Baskerville Trident.

Tudor grinned.

The triplets did not.

“What are you muttering about?”

Highbro glared.

“I'll crush you.”

“Crush you,” Middlebro echoed.

“Crush you,” Lowbro added.

No magic.

No weapons.

Pure physical contest.

Donquixote against Baskerville.

The match began.

“Ball!”

Tudor waved.

A leather oval flew toward him.

He caught it cleanly.

Then accelerated.

*This is a real class.*

Theory mattered.

But to Tudor—

a true warrior should run through resistance.

Break lines.

Reach the objective.

He became a blur.

Three defenders moved to stop him.

Highbro.

Middlebro.

Lowbro.

Tudor smiled.

“Sorry.”

“But I have no intention of meeting Baskerville monsters head-on.”

He pivoted.

Slipped between Middlebro and Lowbro.

Exploded forward.

A ghostlike dodge.

A lightning burst of speed.

Students watching from other classes shouted.

“That's Tudor?”

“Ridiculous!”

“No magic?”

“No weapon?”

“That's pure physical ability?”

“Donquixote really produced a monster this year.”

The cheering lasted only a moment.

A shadow closed from behind.

“Where are you going?”

Highbro.

He caught Tudor's line.

“Give me the ball.”

Hand met hand.

Then—

Tudor slipped aside.

His palm struck Highbro's chest just enough to disrupt him.

Highbro staggered half a step.

Tudor drove through.

“Hahaha!”

“Catch me if you can!”

The Baskerville Trident could not stop him.

Neither could the remaining defenders.

Tall.

Heavy.

Strong.

It did not matter.

Tudor had speed.

Power.

Balance.

No obvious weakness.

He ran with the waist of a predator—

and the back of a bear.

At last—

the Class B goal appeared.

A huge Y-shaped iron structure.

One clean throw through—

one point.

Tudor pulled the ball back.

Then noticed someone near the goal.

“…Ah.”

Vikir.

Apparently assigned some unimportant rear-defense position.

Tudor's mouth curved.

*The theory genius.*

That morning—

Vikir's effortless answer had made Tudor's own response look childish.

A little jealousy remained.

*Maybe I'll tease him.*

Instead of throwing immediately—

Tudor kept running.

Shoulder lowered.

Straight toward Vikir.

*Friend—*

*consider this a lesson.*

*Studying isn't everything.*

Then—

impact.

And Tudor's thoughts stopped.
