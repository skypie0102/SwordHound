# Chapter 251: Recap (2)

“Vikir.”

Dolores slipped through the curtain—

and onto the terrace.

“Mm.”

His usual answer.

Short.

Dolores watched him for several seconds.

Then moved beside him at the railing.

“Thank you.”

Vikir glanced over.

“For what?”

“…Everything.”

Strictly speaking—

Dolores’s first-place finish owed a great deal to Vikir.

Early in the contest—

he had drawn Hohenheim and Bakilaga into the same place.

Let them collide.

Let them exhaust one another.

Then—

Hohenheim fell into Vikir’s trap.

Later—

the Baskerville triplets turned on Vikir.

Even after that—

he finished seventh.

Dolores was certain—

“If the triplets hadn’t betrayed you, you would have ranked higher.”

“Top five, at least.”

“What’s done is done.”

Dolores studied him.

“You’re always so calm.”

“No regret.”

“No lingering resentment.”

Her eyes stayed on his.

There it was again.

That strange familiarity.

They had known each other only since his admission.

Yet sometimes—

Vikir felt like someone she had known for years.

“When I thanked you just now—”

Dolores rested her chin on one hand.

“—I wasn’t talking only about the League.”

“Is that so?”

“Yes.”

She leaned against the railing—

looking up at him from the side.

“You really are strange.”

“I keep wondering where a junior like you came from.”

“I’m an ordinary freshman.”

Dolores almost laughed.

“An ordinary freshman with Advanced Sword Expert-level ability?”

“Grades that can compete with professors?”

“A first-year who defeats third-years and becomes a League ranker?”

“Luck.”

Dolores fell silent.

Colosseo was enormous.

Students.

Professors.

Staff.

Nearly twenty thousand people.

A place where people who had always been called geniuses—

gathered in one community.

That meant—

even extraordinary people could disappear inside the crowd.

Dolores herself had accomplished comparable things as a first-year.

Hohenheim.

Bakilaga.

Lovegood.

Their freshman records had also been exceptional.

Older students she once admired—

had since climbed even higher.

Vikir used that fact.

“Bakilaga, Hohenheim, and Lovegood are all impressive.”

“So are the seniors people call legends.”

“I learned a great deal from them.”

Some of that—

was sincere.

Reaching Sword Graduator—

or the fourth circle—

in one’s early twenties was genuinely remarkable.

Of course—

the reaction would be very different—

if the world knew Vikir had reached Sword Master at eighteen.

Dolores narrowed her eyes.

“Why does that sound like an excuse?”

“…?”

“Like you’re wrapping something extraordinary in ordinary paper.”

She straightened.

“Most people exaggerate what they have.”

“You do the opposite.”

“You hide it.”

Vikir said nothing.

“You don’t want anyone to recognize you?”

“I don’t know what you mean.”

“You even speak like a soldier.”

Her curiosity was no longer subtle.

“Your archery was absurd too.”

“I watched you during the contest.”

“Most of those shots never appeared in your score.”

Vikir’s expression did not change.

“Ricochets off rocks.”

“Off logs.”

“In a dark jungle.”

“Then the secret covering shots for Tudor, Sancho, and Pigi.”

Dolores folded her arms.

“There aren’t many archers who can call all of that luck.”

“My teammates performed well.”

“You mean the Baskerville triplets?”

“They were the stars of the tournament.”

Dolores looked directly at him.

“Then why do I feel—”

“—like you were hiding behind them?”

For an instant—

Vikir’s face hardened.

“If you keep watching one person—”

“—anything will begin to look strange.”

“No.”

Dolores answered immediately.

“You’re different.”

“If you weren’t—”

“I wouldn’t notice you wherever I go.”

She stepped closer.

“Recently, you’re the person who keeps entering my sight.”

“There is no way you’re ordinary.”

A pause.

“So tell me.”

“What are you hiding?”

Baby Madam shifted on Vikir’s left wrist.

Her tiny expression seemed to ask—

*Should I remove her, Master?*

Vikir pressed one hand over her.

No.

He exhaled.

He was about to answer—

when Dolores leaned close enough to whisper.

“You.”

Her voice dropped.

“Are you the Crown Prince?”

Vikir—

actually froze.

*What?*

Dolores mistook his silence for confirmation.

“The current Emperor has no surviving children.”

“They died in accidents.”

“But there’s a rumor.”

“One illegitimate child survived.”

“And that child enrolled at Colosseo.”

She stared.

“That’s you, isn’t it?”

Vikir had heard the same story.

It had circulated even before his regression.

Nothing had ever proven it true.

So—

he had ignored it.

Dolores continued.

“His Majesty is said to possess the power to foresee the future.”

“If you inherited something similar—”

“then predicting exam questions or future events would explain a great deal.”

“Enough.”

Vikir cut her off.

His voice lowered.

“I have nothing to do with the Imperial Family.”

“You’re lying.”

“I swear on my father’s honor.”

Vikir’s gaze did not waver.

“And before almighty Rune.”

Dolores faltered.

A declaration that strong—

was difficult for her to dismiss.

Vikir—

meanwhile—

felt no particular concern about staking Hugo’s honor on anything.

Dolores looked frustrated.

“Then open your heart.”

“Let me know who you really are.”

She meant it literally.

The Saintess could read a soul—

if the other person opened themselves to her.

Most people—

whether they knew it or not—

left some gap.

Vikir—

did not.

His heart was locked.

And he had no intention of opening it.

Osiris had already driven Seth’s remaining people from Baskerville.

Revealing his Baskerville blood—

by itself—

was becoming less dangerous.

But—

*Then Night Hound would be exposed too.*

Vikir did not intend to hide forever.

Only—

not yet.

*It won’t be long.*

Baskerville’s hound.

Night Hound.

When the two identities finally became one in public—

the consequences would be enormous.

Vikir closed his eyes.

Dolores’s impatience sharpened.

“Say something—”

Then—

shouting rose outside.

“Open up!”

“Give us the right!”

“Freedom!”

Dolores turned.

A crowd had gathered outside the inn.

Colosseo professors struggled to hold them back.

Glow sticks.

Placards.

Red headbands.

And at the front—

“…Lovegood?”

Merelini Lovegood.

Student council president of Temisquira Women’s College.

Behind her—

Temisquira students.

A great many of them.

“We demand freedom!”

“Unite!”

“Human beings unite for noble causes!”

“This is what separates us from animals!”

They looked nothing like the image of refined restraint associated with their academy.

Hair loose.

Voices raised.

Charging like angry beasts.

Vikir looked toward the commotion.

“What happened?”

Dolores listened.

At first—

all she could distinguish were words.

Freedom.

Love.

Opportunity.

Equality.

Then—

she used divine power to sharpen her hearing.

Individual voices separated.

“Let us into Colosseo’s celebration!”

“We don’t need anything else! Just let us see Vikir!”

“We want to congratulate him too!”

“Open the celebration to students from other academies!”

“Vikir! Celebrate with us!”

“Joy should be shared!”

“I fell for you on the Magic Train!”

“Brother! I don’t want to part like this!”

“He’s five years younger than you!”

“Handsome men are all ‘brother’!”

Dolores slowly let go of the railing.

Vikir looked at her.

“Can you hear them?”

“Ah.”

Dolores turned stiffly.

“Well…”

Then—

brightly—

“They’re saying the hot water isn’t working!”

“I should go check!”

“It’s late.”

“You should rest too!”

She fled—

before Vikir could ask another question.
