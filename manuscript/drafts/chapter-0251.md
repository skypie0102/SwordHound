“Vikir.”

Dolores pulled the curtain aside and stepped onto the terrace.

She called his name softly.

Vikir’s answer was as brief as ever.

“Mm.”

Dolores studied his face in silence for a moment.

Then she moved beside him at the railing.

“Thank you.”

“For what?”

“…I just wanted to say it.”

She paused.

“For everything.”

Dolores’s first-place finish had, in truth, owed a great deal to Vikir.

Early in the competition, he had maneuvered Hohenheim and Bakilaga into fighting each other, forcing both to waste strength.

That sequence had ultimately contributed to Hohenheim’s elimination.

And although Vikir himself had been knocked out late in the competition after the Baskerville triplets turned on him, he had been in position to contend for a top-five finish.

Dolores continued.

“If the Baskerville triplets hadn’t betrayed you, your rank would have been higher.”

“That is already over.”

“…You really are calm about everything.”

She gave him a faint, searching look.

“No attachment. No regret.”

Dolores looked directly into Vikir’s eyes.

It was strange.

She had known him only a short time since his enrollment.

Yet something about him felt deeply familiar.

She spoke again.

“What I thanked you for just now wasn’t only about the competition.”

“Is that so?”

“Yes.”

Dolores rested her chin on one hand and leaned forward against the railing.

She looked up at Vikir from the side.

“…You really are strange.”

Her eyes narrowed.

“I keep wondering where a junior like you came from.”

“I am an ordinary first-year.”

“An ordinary first-year has Sword Expert-level skill, academic scores comparable to professors, and defeats third-years in the University League?”

“I was lucky.”

Dolores fell silent at his flat answer.

Colosseo Academy was enormous.

Students, professors, employees—

roughly twenty thousand people lived within the community.

Many entered believing themselves geniuses.

As a result, the place was full of prodigies, eccentrics, and people with exceptional talent.

Vikir’s achievements were remarkable.

But even they did not necessarily stand out among the monsters who had passed through the Academy before him.

Dolores herself had built a similarly exceptional record in her own first year.

So had rivals like Hohenheim, Bakilaga, and Lovegood.

And the second-, third-, and fourth-years who had once stood above them had gone on to achieve even greater things.

Vikir pressed the point.

“Bakilaga, Hohenheim, and Lovegood were all extraordinary.”

He spoke without irony.

“The title of ‘legendary upperclassman’ was not undeserved. I learned a great deal from them.”

At least part of that admiration was genuine.

To reach graduate-level standing or the fourth circle while still so young was no ordinary accomplishment.

Of course—

if the world learned that Vikir had reached Sword Master at eighteen, its reaction would be rather different.

Dolores continued watching him.

“Why does that sound like an excuse?”

“…?”

“Like you are wrapping something extraordinary in something ordinary.”

Her eyes sharpened.

“Most people exaggerate what they can do.”

“You are the opposite.”

“You have something, and you keep trying to hide it.”

“…”

“Are you trying to make sure no one recognizes you?”

“…I do not know what you mean.”

“You talk like a soldier.”

Curiosity about Vikir’s origin and identity had filled Dolores’s voice.

“Your archery is absurd too.”

She crossed her arms.

“I watched you during the competition. Most of the people you caught were taken with thrown shots or ranged attacks, even if the rankings don’t show it.”

“I was lucky.”

“…Right.”

Dolores did not sound convinced.

“The forest was dark. There are not many archers who can bounce a shot off rocks or logs and still hit the target.”

She tilted her head.

“But luck is part of skill too.”

Then—

“You were the one secretly covering Tudor, Sancho, and Pigi with ranged fire, weren’t you?”

“My teammates did well.”

“You mean the Baskerville triplets?”

Dolores nodded slowly.

“Yes. They were some of the biggest stars of the tournament.”

Then she turned back toward him.

“But why?”

“…?”

“Why do I keep getting the feeling that you were hiding behind the Baskerville triplets?”

Vikir’s expression stiffened for the briefest moment.

“If you keep watching one person that closely, anything will look unusual.”

“No.”

Dolores’s answer came immediately.

“You are unusual.”

“If you were not, there is no way I would keep noticing you anywhere and everywhere.”

Her voice grew firmer.

“These days, my eyes keep finding you.”

“There is no way someone like that is ordinary.”

“…”

“Tell me.”

Dolores leaned closer.

“What are you hiding?”

Vikir remained silent.

On his left wrist, Baby Madam looked up at him with a worried expression.

As if asking—

*Should I remove her, Master?*

Vikir pressed one hand over Baby Madam and sighed.

He was about to answer.

Dolores stepped closer and whispered.

“You.”

A beat.

“Are you the Crown Prince?”

“…?”

For once—

Vikir was genuinely stunned.

What was she talking about?

Dolores mistook his silence for hesitation and continued.

“The current Emperor has no surviving children.”

“They all died in accidents.”

“…”

“But there is a rumor that one illegitimate child survived.”

“And that the child secretly entered Colosseo Academy.”

“…”

“That is you, isn’t it?”

Vikir had heard the rumor before.

It had circulated even before his regression.

But it had never been proven.

So he had never paid it much attention.

Dolores, unaware of his thoughts, continued.

“The Emperor possesses the power to foresee the future.”

“If you inherited his blood, perhaps you inherited something similar.”

“If you could foresee examinations or events before they happened, then your achievements would make sense…”

“Enough.”

Vikir cut through the theory.

Then, in a low voice—

“I have nothing to do with the Imperial Family.”

“You’re lying.”

“I swear on my father’s honor.”

He added—

“And before the almighty Rune.”

Dolores’s gaze wavered.

A declaration that strong was difficult to dismiss.

Of course—

Vikir did not care in the slightest about Hugo’s honor.

So it cost him nothing to wager it.

Dolores, however, looked dissatisfied.

Her frustration showed openly.

“Then open your heart!”

“Let me know who you are!”

She wanted to read his soul.

Most people, consciously or not, left some part of themselves open.

Vikir’s inner door, however—

was shut.

Locked.

Sealed.

For Vikir, granting her request was impossible.

At this point, revealing that he belonged to House Baskerville would not be disastrous by itself.

Osiris had already purged Seth’s followers from the family.

But—

*Then the Night Hound’s identity would be exposed too.*

Vikir did not intend to hide forever.

Still—

according to the plan in his head, this was too soon.

*…Not much longer.*

The Hound of Baskerville.

Night Hound.

The instant those identities became one in the public eye—

a storm would follow.

Vikir silently closed his eyes.

Dolores watched him.

For reasons even she could not explain, impatience rose inside her.

“Answer me…”

Then—

voices erupted from outside the inn.

“…Open it! Open it!”

“…Give us the right!”

“…Freedom!”

The shouts came from beyond the grounds where Colosseo’s students were staying.

A large crowd appeared to be demonstrating.

“…?”

Dolores turned away.

Then stared.

Colosseo’s professors were struggling to hold back an enormous crowd.

Glow sticks flashed.

Placards waved.

Red bands were tied around heads.

And at the very front—

a face Dolores knew well.

“…Merelini Lovegood?”

The student council president of Temisquira Women’s College.

Behind her—

a mass of Temisquira students.

They were physically pushing against Colosseo’s professors, fighting to force their way toward the inn.

“We demand our freedom!”

“Everyone, together!”

“Human beings unite for noble causes!”

“That is what separates us from animals!”

They sounded like revolutionaries.

Dolores grabbed the railing and listened more closely.

Temisquira Women’s College—

a school known for dignity, refinement, and composure.

Yet its students were charging forward with their hair loose like enraged beasts.

“What happened?”

Vikir smoothly shifted the subject toward the disturbance.

Dolores frowned.

“I… can’t hear clearly.”

Too many voices overlapped.

Most of it dissolved into noise.

The only words she could distinguish were things like—

“freedom,”

“love,”

“opportunity,”

and—

“equality.”

“….”

Dolores reluctantly opened her hearing with holy power.

The tangled noise separated.

Individual voices became clear.

And with them—

the fierce emotion behind each cry.

It was almost like listening to the confessions of a flock of very excited lambs.

“Let us join Colosseo Academy’s celebration banquet!”

“We don’t need anything else! Just let us see Vikir!”

“We want to congratulate Vikir too!”

“Open the celebration banquet to students from other schools!”

“Open the banquet!”

“Let us celebrate together, Vikir!”

“Joy should be shared!”

“I fell for you when I saw how sexy you looked on the Magic Train!”

“Big Brother! I don’t want to separate from you!”

“What do you mean ‘Big Brother’!? He’s five years younger than us!”

“Who cares!? If he’s handsome, he’s Big Brother!”

The goal of Temisquira’s students was suddenly very obvious.

They wanted into Colosseo’s celebration banquet.

Specifically—

they wanted Vikir.

“….”

Vikir glanced at Dolores.

“Can you tell what they’re saying?”

“Huh?”

Dolores stiffened.

“Uh…”

She released the railing and turned around with visible difficulty.

“They…”

A pause.

Then she stood abruptly.

“Oh!”

“They said the hot water isn’t working!”

“I should go check!”

She was already retreating.

“You should rest too. It’s late!”

And with that—

Dolores fled.
