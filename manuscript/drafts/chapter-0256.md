# Chapter 256: The Grave of Swords (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/256.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 254 is the verified shifted secondary witness.

The salt-dried parchment crackled as Vikir unfolded it.

The handwriting inside was familiar.

**To my dear elder brother C—**

**From your younger brother Hugo, with concern.**

Vikir checked the sender first.

As expected—

Hugo Le Baskerville.

Only the Head of House could use the military seal stamped on the envelope.

But one thing bothered him.

“…Elder brother?”

Hugo was now the eldest son of Baskerville.

The Head of House.

Who could he possibly address that way?

Then Vikir remembered something Hugo had once said while disciplining Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro.

*Age comes for free.*

*Heh. True. This father of yours also worked very hard to become the eldest son before taking the seat of Head.*

At the time—

Vikir had wondered how someone could “work hard” to become the eldest son.

Now the meaning was obvious.

Hugo had not originally been the oldest.

He had become the eldest after the brutal succession struggle removed the brothers above him.

In other words—

he had become the eldest son afterward.

And yet—

there was apparently still someone Hugo called elder brother.

Vikir continued reading.

The letter said that Hugo’s fifteen-year-old son had returned after surviving alone for two years in the depths of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

A small banquet would be held to celebrate.

If circumstances allowed—

Hugo hoped the recipient would attend.

It had been a long time.

And the return of the strongest among the Seven Counts would greatly strengthen the family.

Vikir lowered the letter.

It had not been written long ago.

And Hugo’s unusually respectful tone narrowed the possibilities.

*Brother C.*

*One of the Seven Counts.*

House Baskerville had seven Counts whose lives revolved around battle.

The **Seven Counts**.

The institution was ancient.

Older than the current Empire.

Back in the age when the continent had been divided among countless competing states—

one great House could function almost like a kingdom.

Baskerville controlled the western frontiers and the barbarian lands.

Donquixote ruled over wandering peoples of the sea.

One House—

one nation.

The Seven Counts had reached their peak in that era.

Each could move family troops without waiting for the Head of House.

They fought wherever the western front demanded.

Monsters.

Barbarians.

Rival Houses.

All feared the seven great fangs that had driven Baskerville’s golden age.

But after the continent was unified—

the age of enormous wars ended.

Local fighting never disappeared.

Yet the old days—

when bloodlines vanished overnight and dozens of castles fell in a single campaign—

were gone.

The Seven Counts remained.

But their power had naturally diminished.

Older members of the Baskerville Senate sometimes spoke nostalgically of the Counts of the past.

Warriors who did not need to watch anyone’s expression.

Seven tyrannical fangs free to race across a chaotic battlefield.

To those old hounds—

that was what a true Seven Count had been.

Modern Counts entangled in politics were something lesser.

Vikir could understand the sentiment.

In the past—

their military authority had been close to that of the Head of House himself.

When Vikir returned from Ballak, Hugo had held a great banquet.

The current Seven Counts had all been invited.

Each commanded one of Baskerville’s great knight orders.

Isabella Le Baskerville of the Doberman Knights.

German Le Baskerville of the Shepherd Knights.

Metzgerhund Le Baskerville of the Rottweiler Knights.

Cu-Chulainn Le Baskerville of the Wolfhound Knights.

Boston Terrier Le Baskerville of the Pit Bull Knights.

Great Dane Le Baskerville of the Mastiff Knights.

All six had wanted to attend.

In practice—

only Boston Terrier and Great Dane had arrived in time.

Cindywendy later told Vikir something important.

There had only been six responses.

One of the Seven Counts had cut himself off from the world completely.

The oldest.

The strongest.

Countless messengers had been sent in search of him.

None returned.

Not even the couriers.

So in practical terms—

people treated the Seven Counts as six.

“…The unreachable seventh Count.”

Vikir looked again at Hugo’s letter.

“Then this must be him.”

The man Hugo called **Brother C**.

The only member of the current Seven Counts whose name also belonged among the old Counts from the age of chaos.

A true relic of the ancient era.

The one older kinsman even Hugo—

who had forced his way to the position of Head—

could not touch.

**Cane Corso Le Baskerville.**

◆◆◆

Vikir now understood why the nameless Pit Bull knight had died delivering the letter.

A monstrous salt storm swept across the desert.

White grains twisted into walls.

Anything caught inside lost its moisture.

Then the sharpened salt stripped away what remained.

Vikir advanced beneath his black cloak.

*This is brutal.*

*Even a Graduator could die here.*

Simply surviving the storm was difficult.

Add an S-rank Basilisk—

and even a peak Sword Graduator would be walking into death.

A huge crow crossed overhead.

The storm caught it.

Its neck snapped.

Its wings broke.

Before the body even reached the ground—

the salt wind had already dried it into a mummy.

It struck the sand like dead wood.

“…If the Basilisk were still alive too, even I’d have trouble getting in.”

Vikir continued forward.

His River Styx-hardened body endured the dehydration.

So did the regenerative powers stored through Beelzebub.

Eventually—

the storm broke.

Beyond it stretched another white expanse.

And in the center—

something impossible stood.

A tower.

Blood-red.

Black.

It rose from the desert like a blade driven upward from beneath the earth.

Its material was impossible to identify.

Its true height was equally difficult to judge.

The only thing Vikir could say with certainty was that the spatial distortion inside it surpassed even the advanced magic used in ordinary mage towers.

“…So it really existed.”

Vikir had seen this place before.

Not with his own eyes.

In Baskerville instructional books from childhood.

Right after leaving the Cradle of Swords—

young hounds were taught the myths and sayings of the family.

This tower had appeared there.

A fantasy.

A symbolic place that no one believed was real.

Yet now—

it stood before him.

Vikir reached the entrance.

Rough letters had been carved above it.

**THE GRAVE OF SWORDS**

From cradle—

to grave.

A final destination.

For someone.
