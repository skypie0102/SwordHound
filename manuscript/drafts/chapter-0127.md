# Chapter 127: Admission to the Academy (2)

“So this is where I'll be studying from today!”

A boy marched toward the Academy's main gate.

His head was high.

His face shone with ambition.

All around him—

a huge stream of students followed the same road.

The incoming **20th class**.

They climbed toward Colosseo's gates like carp swimming upstream.

Then—

a middle-aged woman's voice rang out behind them.

“Oh, honestly! Look at this child!”

She hurried up the road.

“You forgot your bag!”

Apparently the mother of one of the freshmen.

She shouted again.

“Vikir! Don't forget your bag!”

Several heads turned at once.

“Huh?”

“Me?”

“Did she call me?”

More than ten boys scattered through the crowd looked back.

**Vikir** was not an unusual name.

One embarrassed boy came running downhill.

“Mom! Don't yell it that loudly!”

His face was bright red.

He snatched the forgotten bag and hurried back toward the gates.

The other Vikirs turned away again.

All except one.

He had never looked back in the first place.

Heavy bangs covered most of his face.

Thick horn-rimmed glasses hid his eyes.

His shirt and trousers were plain and worn.

The luggage on his back was equally modest.

Vikir.

**Vikir Van Baskerville.**

Or—

for the moment—

simply **Vikir**.

He had set aside the Baskerville surname and taken the Academy examination as a commoner.

*That was the right decision.*

The name **Vikir Van Baskerville** had become inconveniently famous.

He had been recognized by the Imperial Family.

His achievements had spread.

Using that name openly at Colosseo would ruin the entire reason he had come.

*…It would defeat the purpose.*

Vikir had a clear objective at the Academy.

Find the demons hiding in the Imperial Capital.

Find the human traitors and collaborators serving them.

Kill them.

And by doing so—

prevent the coming Age of Destruction.

He remembered his previous life.

Ten high-ranking demons descended into the human world.

Together—

they erected ten enormous pillars.

Those pillars formed a Gate to the Demon Realm.

Andromalius—

the demon that had worn Seth Le Baskerville's body—

had been one of those ten Demon Kings.

Fortunately—

Vikir had killed him before he could accumulate enough power to fulfill his role.

A little later—

and the consequences might have been catastrophic.

Without Vikir's prior knowledge—

no one might ever have discovered Andromalius in time.

Which meant one thing was now certain.

The remaining nine Demon King-class enemies were also somewhere in the human world—

hiding inside human skins.

*Cindywendy's investigation found several high-ranking people who had contact with Seth gathering here in the Imperial Capital.*

The larger the population—

the easier it was for demons to hide among humans.

That was why Vikir had come to Colosseo.

And why he needed an identity that attracted as little suspicion as possible.

A commoner freshman.

Someone ordinary.

Someone who could leave the Academy for classes, errands, or private business without every movement becoming gossip.

Becoming famous would only make his assassinations harder.

So he had entered as a commoner—

and deliberately focused almost entirely on the written examination.

*Theory is one area I'm confident in.*

First place had been a little more conspicuous than he intended.

Fortunately—

Colosseo cared far more about practical ability.

Vikir had also felt a strange satisfaction while taking the written exam.

Before regression—

he had never properly attended the Academy's theory courses.

Now—

he could answer questions drawn from experience no textbook writer possessed.

> **<Gaps and Contradictions in Modern Swordsmanship Theory…>**
>
> **<An Anthropological Perspective on the Ecology of the Newly Classified Mushuhushu…>**
>
> **<Habits and Effective Strategies Against Yetis of the Northern Continent's Snow Mountains…>**
>
> **<On the Possible Existence of Undead-Type Monsters Unaffected by Divine Power, and Proposed Countermeasures…>**

A veteran who had survived the Age of Destruction possessed a great deal of practical knowledge.

Turning that experience into theory and written explanation was surprisingly easy.

Vikir completed the examination without difficulty.

That alone earned him first place.

Later—

he headed toward the dormitory to unpack.

The route was crowded with upperclassmen.

“Interested in the Way?”

One stepped forward with a pamphlet.

“Want to explore it with our Domino Research Society?”

Another waved from the side.

“Hey! You look lucky!”

He thrust forward another form.

“How about joining our pufferfish cuisine study group?”

A third tried to pull Vikir aside.

“Come here for a second! Our meditation society—”

Another student shoved a questionnaire into view.

“It'll only take a minute! Choose the club you like, then put your fingerprint and seal here~”

Clubs.

Research societies.

Interest groups.

Student organizations.

Upperclassmen were doing everything they could to recruit freshmen.

The most popular groups were predictable.

Organizations that strengthened one's résumé.

Or clubs that simply sounded fun.

Student council.

Sports.

Band.

Dance.

Theater.

Economics research.

Cooking.

Those drew large crowds.

Less popular were groups such as the newspaper club.

Horticulture.

Art.

Reading.

Long-lived beetle research.

Meanwhile—

the recruiters were watching for specific freshmen.

Tudor Donquixote.

Bianca Fo Usher.

Both were obvious targets.

So was Sinclaire—

first in the Hot Department practical examination and second in the written test.

Vikir ignored all of it.

He only wanted to reach his room.

Unpack.

Rest.

Because tonight—

his real work would begin.

*How many pages are left in the kill ledger alone?*

The blacklist.

There were plenty of humans who deserved death even without being demons.

Traitors.

Collaborators.

People who would sell humanity to the enemy.

*…There are too many.*

Vikir moved through the crowd.

Then—

“Newspaper club! Newspaper club!”

A familiar voice.

“Stay informed about everything happening inside the Academy!”

Vikir stopped.

He knew that voice.

A young woman stood nearby handing out flyers and application forms.

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

The saintess.

The youngest student ever admitted early.

Now—

the top student of the third year.

Student council president.

Head of the newspaper club.

For three years she had remained one of the Academy's most celebrated students.

She was distributing forms personally.

“Senior! Please look over here!”

“Senior! I'm your fan!”

“I enrolled just to see you!”

“She really is beautiful…”

“So devout.”

“So holy!”

Freshmen crowded around her—

desperate to receive a flyer directly from her hand.

Vikir lowered his head.

He pressed down his bangs.

Adjusted the thick glasses hiding his eyes.

*I've already met her.*

Not as Vikir.

As the **Night Hound**.

The holy water Dolores had prepared had played an important role in ending the plague.

But Vikir had worn a mask then.

She did not know the face beneath it.

There was no benefit in taking a chance.

Vikir detoured around her and continued toward the dormitory.

Being noticed would accomplish nothing.

◆◆◆

The dormitory was comparatively peaceful.

The building itself was enormous.

But individual students rarely needed to travel far outside their own floor, corridor, and stairwell.

The structure was designed efficiently.

The boys' and girls' dormitories formed connected wings but remained strictly separated.

Different years occupied different floors.

Fourth-years lived on the first floor.

Third-years on the second.

Second-years on the third.

First-years on the fourth.

Perhaps—

Vikir thought—

the designers had simply decided that older students deserved fewer stairs.

The rooms were standard doubles.

Not large.

Not cramped.

Each contained a large bunk bed—

one wide desk—

two chairs—

and an aging bathroom with a shower.

More youth hostel than noble academy.

Click.

Vikir entered his assigned room.

He placed his sparse luggage on the desk.

A few changes of clothes.

Little else.

Then—

“Um…”

A hesitant voice came from near the bathroom.

“Hi.”

Vikir turned.

A short boy stood there.

Blond hair.

Soft cheeks that still held a trace of baby fat.

Slightly prominent front teeth.

A cute, almost androgynous face.

A handwritten name tag hung from his chest.

**Pigi.**

Vikir stared.

His roommate for the next year.

And someone he already knew.

*Pigi.*

*We meet again.*

Before regression—

Vikir remembered him as a genuinely kind person.

When Vikir served as an attendant to the Baskerville triplets, Pigi had treated him well.

Even when Vikir was doing menial work—

Pigi had offered help.

That kindness had apparently survived unchanged into this life.

Pigi gave a nervous smile.

“Um… my name is Pigi.”

He bowed awkwardly.

“It's nice to meet you.”

“If I'm a little slow or clumsy, please bear with me.”

Then he pointed toward the bed.

“Oh! And you can choose whichever bunk you want first.”

Vikir let out a quiet breath.

Pigi's sincerity was almost painful.

Academy students were young—

but rarely naïve.

Most had been called elites long before arriving here.

They understood superiority.

Betrayal.

Politics.

Exclusion.

Bullying.

Someone as gentle and straightforward as Pigi could be swallowed easily by the freshman hierarchy.

*…That's exactly what happened before.*

Pigi had been a long-term victim of bullying.

His small frame.

Timid personality.

Cute appearance.

Preference for quiet reading and information analysis.

None of those fit well in an institution that glorified visible strength.

Vikir remembered something else.

*Before regression, Highbro was his roommate.*

He already knew what kind of Academy life Pigi had endured.

Pigi, of course, knew none of this.

He simply looked relieved that his new roommate—

though quiet—

did not seem frightening.

Then Pigi brightened.

“Vikir.”

“Hm?”

“Are you going to O.T. tonight?”

Freshman orientation.

Officially—

it existed to introduce newly admitted students to Academy life.

In practice—

it was also a welcome gathering.

Food.

Drinks.

Upperclassmen meeting freshmen.

Freshmen meeting one another.

Attendance was not technically mandatory.

Almost everyone went anyway.

These were the people students would spend the next four years beside.

And the seniors they met tonight could become future contacts.

The network built at Colosseo could shape an entire career.

Many students had fought fiercely to enter the Academy precisely because of those connections.

But—

“No.”

Pigi blinked.

Vikir's answer did not change.

“I won't be going.”

Networking held no interest for him.

Tonight—

he had an assassination route to begin.

The time for real bloodshed had arrived.
