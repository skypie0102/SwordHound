# Chapter 454: Infiltration of the Water Source (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/454.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 452 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established project terminology controls Vikir, Cindywendy, Camus Morgue, Dolores L. Quovadis, Sinclaire, Pigi, Chihuahua, MiniPin, Tochka, Night Walkers, Poison Humans, Red Death, and House Leviathan. Chinese controls the remaining-water estimate at **three days even under optimistic rationing**.

“We don’t have enough water.”

Cindywendy’s words made every face in the conference room turn grave.

**Tochka.**

A natural fortress.

A perfect stronghold where one soldier could hold off a hundred.

But it had one fatal weakness.

There was no accessible source of drinking water nearby.

Cindywendy continued.

“Refugees are still pouring in from all over the Empire.”

“Even if food is sufficient—

the drinking water isn’t.”

Camus frowned.

“Can’t we buy more?”

“We still have money.”

Cindywendy shook her head.

“I’ve used up almost all of my own assets.”

“The Bourgeois support funds are still there.”

“But money isn’t the problem.”

“There are no sellers.”

Dolores spoke next.

“Leviathan keeps cutting the supply routes.”

“The water can’t get through.”

“At this rate—

we’ll collapse from thirst before the real war even begins.”

A human body was mostly water.

Roughly fifty liters inside an adult.

Even without drinking—

the body could create a small amount by breaking down fat.

About a quarter liter.

But that only delayed the inevitable.

Without water—

a human being could survive only around a week.

And Tochka’s stored drinking water—

would not last even that long.

Soon—

every eye turned toward the upper end of the conference table.

Cindywendy.

Camus.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

The senior members of the Night Walkers.

All of them looked toward the man above them.

Vikir.

Expressionless as always.

Impossible to read.

At last—

he spoke.

“Hold out.”

The reactions varied.

Camus simply nodded.

Dolores immediately began wondering how she could persuade everyone else.

Sinclaire frowned—

already trying to calculate how long they could endure.

Cindywendy sighed.

The expression on her face said only one thing.

*Here we go again.*

Pigi cautiously raised his voice.

“Vikir.”

“We trust you.”

“But a lot of the refugees gathered here don’t.”

“I don’t know what we’re supposed to tell them.”

“We can’t just tell everyone to hold on blindly.”

Vikir did not change expression.

“That’s still all I can tell them.”

His voice remained calm.

Confident.

“You all performed better than I expected.”

“That’s why we managed to gather this many people.”

“Because of that—

my original prediction was slightly off.”

“But the delay is temporary.”

“Things will resolve naturally.”

“I can guarantee that.”

“…Waiting may not be possible.”

Chihuahua cut in from the side.

As one of Vikir’s most loyal aides—

he was also one of the few willing to criticize him directly.

“You always keep the whole plan inside your own head.”

“Of course everyone around you gets nervous.”

“You should share information.”

“We have **three days** of water left if we’re optimistic.”

“And you still want everyone to wait without knowing why?”

Several people quietly agreed.

Vikir’s answer did not change.

“If I reveal the plan too early—

the enemy may learn it.”

“And even if the enemy doesn’t—

our own side may lose confidence before it comes together.”

“So for now—

they have to judge me by what I’ve done before.”

Chihuahua clicked his tongue.

“You’re still stubborn at your age.”

“You look young—

but inside you’re already some middle-aged old man.”

“How are you ever going to get married?”

Several women nearby stiffened at once.

Vikir finally gave in—

slightly.

“…Is the water situation really that bad?”

“Can’t we hold even a week?”

“You’ve seen the refugees.”

Chihuahua’s voice became sharper.

“They’re still coming.”

“Three days is already generous.”

“A week from now—

Tochka will be filled with people dead from dehydration.”

Collecting dew had limits.

So did squeezing moisture from plants.

If Vikir’s original solution did not arrive in time—

disaster would come first.

“…Then we still have no choice but to wait.”

Worry spread across the room.

Then—

someone in the corner raised a hand.

MiniPin.

“I have something to report, sir…”

He hesitated.

“No.”

“Senator.”

Vikir looked toward him.

MiniPin placed a small cage on the table.

Inside—

something with blue wings fluttered weakly.

Sinclaire’s eyes sharpened.

“Is that a nymph?”

“Yes.”

“I managed to capture it two days ago.”

MiniPin nodded.

Before joining Vikir—

he had been a professional searcher.

One of the Imperial Capital’s foremost experts on monster ecology.

“Nymphs only live near water.”

Every face changed.

Vikir gestured for him to continue.

“I found this one on a mountain peak not far from Tochka.”

“It’s a very steep rock formation.”

“Dense vegetation.”

“You can barely see the summit from below.”

“I nearly fell to my death several times while collecting it.”

Camus leaned forward.

“Why would a nymph be living there?”

“There’s only one answer.”

MiniPin sounded almost certain.

“Water.”

“I believe there’s a volcanic lake hidden inside the crater.”

A water source.

A lake—

on the summit of a mountain facing Tochka.

“I’d put the probability at ninety-nine-point-nine percent.”

“And if it’s there—

it should be a clear freshwater lake.”

Vikir narrowed his eyes.

“…Then why has nobody found it before?”

“It took me days and nights to follow the nymph’s traces.”

“I nearly fell dozens of times.”

“If even a specialist collector like me struggled this much—

ordinary people wouldn’t attempt the climb.”

Vikir rubbed his chin.

A water source near Tochka—

had never been discovered in the future he remembered.

Perhaps this—

was another butterfly effect created by recruiting MiniPin.

*If it’s real—*

*this is extremely valuable.*

Camus bent over the map.

“It’s not too far.”

“Based on the terrain—

if we blast the right rock face—

we may be able to create a channel leading toward Tochka.”

Sinclaire nodded.

“The crater is large.”

“If there’s a lake inside—

the volume should be substantial.”

Dolores added:

“And nymphs don’t survive around tiny puddles.”

“They prefer large bodies of clean water.”

“The possibility is real.”

Vikir looked again at MiniPin.

He had recruited the man in the Imperial Capital precisely because of talent like this.

After a long silence—

Vikir nodded.

“Fine.”

“We change the plan slightly.”

“First—

we secure that water source.”

For someone as conservative as Vikir when it came to altering plans—

it was an unusual decision.

A temporary measure.

Nothing more.

They only needed enough water to survive roughly another week.

Cindywendy’s shoulders relaxed.

At last—

she smiled.

“Finally.”

“A way out.”

“I’ll support the operation with everything I have.”

The others hardened their expressions.

Determination replaced uncertainty.

Then—

“Emergency!”

“Emergency!”

“Emergency!”

A sentry shouted from the watchtower outside.

“Poison Humans from House Leviathan are advancing!”

“Number unknown!”

The fragile hope created by the newly discovered water source—

vanished in an instant.
