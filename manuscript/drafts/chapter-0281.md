# Chapter 281: What Money Can't Buy (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/281.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 279 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Damien Bourgeois, Pomeranian, and Night Hound. Juliet Bourgeois / Romeo remain source-local forms pending direct Fandom access.

Pomeranian had been right.

Time was almost gone.

Sssssss.

A massive gate appeared before Juliet and Romeo.

The **Gate of Death**.

Huge.

Heavy.

The kind of door that felt difficult even to imagine opening.

And once it closed—

there would be no taking back what crossed through.

Romeo looked at Juliet.

Juliet looked at him.

Both were crying.

[Remember what I told you.]

[Wherever you go—

I’ll follow.]

A promise from that stormy night.

Romeo—

ready to go where the dead belonged.

Juliet—

determined to follow him.

Damien stepped between them.

“No!”

“Absolutely not!”

He shouted like a man losing his mind.

“Juliet!”

“You’re alive!”

“How can you follow someone who’s dead?”

Juliet looked at him sadly.

[You said the same thing that night.]

Damien stepped back.

He remembered.

*Juliet.*

*You’re an heir of House Bourgeois.*

*How can you throw yourself away on a man with no standing?*

“This is different!”

Damien stammered.

“If you wanted to marry him—

I’d approve now!”

“I was wrong!”

“I admit everything!”

“But this—”

“Romeo is dead!”

“You are alive!”

Juliet answered softly.

[Only technically.]

“…What?”

[Without him—

I’m no different from dead.]

“Daughter!”

[You could say it a thousand times.]

[Even if I woke again—

I would choose the same.]

Romeo touched Juliet’s face.

Juliet covered his hand with hers.

Damien whispered desperately.

“But you’re a living spirit.”

“You can’t go.”

“Your body is still alive.”

“You’re connected to it.”

“Romeo is already dead—

but you cannot cross the Gate of Death…”

His words stopped.

Damien stared at Juliet.

“…Juliet.”

“You…”

Juliet lowered herself before him.

Tears in her eyes.

[Father.]

[Please let me go this time.]

Everything—

was the same as that night.

*I always listened to you.*

[I was your proud daughter.]

*I never disappointed you.*

[So this once—]

*Please.*

[Can I make my own choice?]

Damien could no longer tell whether he was hearing the past—

or the present.

Tears he thought had dried forever—

kept coming.

“…I see.”

“I really did cross the point where things could be restored.”

For one moment—

when Juliet’s voice returned—

Damien had imagined everything could be fixed.

That illusion existed only because he still had not fully understood the weight of his actions.

Now—

he did.

Vikir asked:

“What will you do?”

Damien’s voice broke.

“…How much time?”

“About thirty minutes.”

Damien stood still.

Several seconds.

Then—

he made a decision.

He ran to the servants outside.

And shouted:

“Prepare a wedding!”

◆◆◆

The wedding was small.

Fast.

Complete.

The bride—

Juliet.

The groom—

Romeo.

The bride’s father—

Damien.

Family custom prevented a relative from officiating.

So—

Vikir did.

Pomeranian handled the edge of Juliet’s dress and bouquet.

Vikir adjusted the plague-doctor mask.

Then stood before them.

His words were short.

“Remember this.”

“You exist for each other by choice—

not as possessions.”

“Live well.”

There were no hours of noble ceremony.

No endless formalities.

Only the essentials.

Juliet and Romeo looked at each other.

Smiled through tears.

Then kissed.

Damien watched.

Cried.

Afterward—

he stood.

“Live well.”

His eyes moved from Juliet’s soul—

to Juliet’s body lying on the bed.

“Live well, my daughter.”

“Truly.”

His hand shook.

He lifted a pillow.

Then—

with deliberate finality—

he pressed it over Juliet’s face.

Her long-immobile body was weak.

The shallow breaths beneath the pillow—

grew quieter.

Then stopped.

Juliet’s body relaxed completely.

At the same moment—

her spirit changed.

Sharper.

Brighter.

More complete.

She turned toward Romeo.

[Oh!]

[Romeo!]

[I can hear you now!]

Juliet threw herself into his arms.

Smiling.

Then the two stood before Damien.

Romeo bowed deeply.

Juliet performed a noble daughter’s farewell.

[Father.]

[We’ll live well.]

Damien laughed once—

brokenly.

“…You’re both dead.”

“How exactly are you planning to live well?”

His eyes were swollen red.

The clock struck midnight.

Twelve chimes.

Juliet and Romeo turned.

Hand in hand—

they walked down the carpet toward the Gate of Death.

Bride.

Groom.

Then—

they crossed.

The boundary between this world—

and the next.

The massive door closed.

BOOM.

Life—

and death—

separated.

Silence settled.

Damien lowered his head.

“…Be happy.”

“Be happy, daughter.”

“Both of you.”

“Wherever you go—

be happy.”

Tears fell onto the red carpet.

Then—

BANG!

The Gate of Death flew open again.

[Dad!]

Juliet stuck her head through.

[Goodbye!]

[I love you!]

[I really love you!]

[We’ll meet again someday!]

She was crying—

and smiling more brightly than Damien had ever seen.

Then—

BOOM.

The gate closed for good.

It faded.

Disappeared.

Damien collapsed to his knees.

His composure vanished completely.

He sobbed until his face was wet with tears—

mucus—

saliva—

everything.

He leaned against the bed—

and cried for a long time.

Vikir waited silently.

Pomeranian came to his side.

“Uncle.”

“Why is that man crying?”

Vikir looked at her.

“I don’t know.”

“You don’t know?”

“Of course there are things I don’t know.”

“If you don’t know—

you should learn!”

Vikir looked away.

A woman’s face surfaced in his mind.

*If death separated us—*

*I wanted to stay beside you even after death.*

He spoke quietly.

“I never wanted to learn much about this kind of thing.”

“But…”

Pomeranian tilted her head.

“…Maybe understanding some of it wouldn’t be bad.”

Much later—

Damien finally raised his head.

Vikir waited for him to regain enough control to speak.

Damien looked at Vikir near Juliet’s now-empty bed.

“…Are you actually alive?”

“If you want this to be a dream—

dream it yourself.”

“It’s time to settle the bill.”

Reality returned immediately.

Damien nodded.

“Yes.”

“Our transaction.”

“But before that…”

“What?”

“Thank you.”

Damien bowed deeply.

Vikir waited.

Damien straightened.

“Because of you—

I was able to send my daughter away properly.”

The phrase could mean marriage.

It could mean death.

Here—

it meant both.

Damien managed a small smile.

“Haha.”

“I finally sent off that stubborn daughter.”

“I feel lighter.”

“A wedding.”

“A funeral.”

“Not so different, really.”

“Marriage is a grave, after all.”

“Wipe your face.”

“Oh.”

“Right.”

Damien pulled out a handkerchief.

Cleaned himself.

Then—

when his face had recovered something like its old seriousness—

he asked:

“What do you want?”

Vikir answered immediately.

“The head of House Bourgeois.”

Damien did not hesitate.

“Take it.”

A cold bargain—

completed in seconds.
