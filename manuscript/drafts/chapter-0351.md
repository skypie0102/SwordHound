# Chapter 351: Outside the Tower (1)

[How could a human possibly reach this place!?]

Amdusias stared in disbelief.

Vikir answered by moving his sword.

Crimson aura shattered across the darkness.

Amdusias’s expression changed immediately.

[That blood…]

[Why is it here?]

Something flowed from Vikir’s blade that did not belong to the ordinary laws of the Hell Tree.

A substance that twisted causality itself.

Vikir gave him no explanation.

“Demons must die.”

That conviction had hardened across two lives—

and now a third passage through the river beneath the Hell Tree.

CLANG!

Amdusias’s horn met Vikir’s blade.

The collision split the air like glass.

Black cracks flashed.

Sparks poured through them.

Amdusias felt the difference immediately.

Vikir was faster.

Stronger.

More precise.

The attributes accumulated inside the tower explained part of it.

But not the rest.

[That unsettling feeling…]

[Is it because of the blood on your sword?]

He retreated.

[What did you coat your blade with!?]

[Where did you get it!?]

“Die.”

Eight crimson slashes crossed the darkness.

Amdusias swept them aside with his massive unicorn horn.

Most broke apart.

Not all.

Fragments of aura cut shallow wounds into his body.

Then slipped inside.

[…What?]

His vision blurred.

Images flashed through his mind.

A swamp of blood and flesh.

Mountains built from bones.

Air stripped of mana.

A gigantic mushroom cloud rising in the distance.

An endless desert.

A huge tower standing in its center.

An old man in black—

walking toward it alone.

The visions came as broken fragments.

[What is this!?]

Even Amdusias lost composure.

Unknown matter—

something that bent the laws of cause and effect—

was entering through his wounds.

Vikir watched.

And reached one conclusion.

*There really is something in Pigi’s blood.*

He still did not know what Amdusias was seeing.

He did not know why the blood interfered with demon causality.

But one fact was undeniable.

Pigi’s blood was poison to demons.

Enough—

even—

to shake a Demon King’s concentration.

That was sufficient.

Vikir raised his sword again.

Amdusias changed tactics.

[How much more can you endure!?]

Black vapor rose from the abyss.

Nightmares.

Fear.

Pain.

All the negative emotions produced by the people trapped inside the Hell Tree—

were drawn into Amdusias.

He inhaled them.

[You know this already.]

[Everything released by the challengers became nourishment for me.]

Grief.

Pain.

Anger.

Screams.

Betrayal.

Hatred.

Inferiority.

Every negative emotion the tower forced from its victims—

fed him.

[And now I will consume yours.]

Vikir tilted his head.

“My emotions?”

“Do I still have those?”

[You entered my domain.]

[You cannot hide them.]

[No matter how deep they are buried in your subconscious—

I can see them.]

Amdusias’s eyes burned.

His horns leveled toward Vikir’s chest.

[Your fear.]

[Your rage.]

[Your sorrow.]

[Every fragment of negative emotion inside you will become my strength.]

Then—

he charged.

The ghostly unicorn’s hooves cut a straight black road across the ground.

Not toward Vikir’s body.

Toward his mind.

◆◆◆

BOOM.

Amdusias landed inside Vikir’s mental world.

Entering another person’s mind—

was no more difficult for him than dragging victims into his own.

He looked around.

Dry black earth.

Dead trees.

Scorched land.

Smoke everywhere.

[Hmph.]

[For a human—

your inner world is desolate.]

He walked through the haze.

Eventually—

a massive stone statue appeared.

Weathered.

Damaged.

But recognizable.

[Hugo and Osiris.]

[The head of Baskerville—

and its young patriarch.]

Amdusias studied the statue.

[Large.]

[So they occupy a large place in your heart.]

In a mental world—

scale reflected emotional weight.

Wounds.

Attachments.

Obligations.

The greater the feeling—

the larger its representation.

Amdusias continued.

He crushed dead trees and boulders beneath his hooves.

[This place is large for a human mind.]

[There are several unusually massive presences here.]

Smoke and fog prevented him from seeing the true scale.

Still—

this was the mind of a young man not yet twenty.

How large could it possibly be?

How terrible could it possibly become?

Amdusias climbed.

A steep rise emerged through the haze.

A mountain.

He started upward.

Step after step.

The soil was dead.

The trees burned.

No insects.

No animals.

Only bones.

And the mountain—

was much higher than expected.

So high that even Amdusias became tired.

[A mountain inside the mind usually represents responsibility.]

[Burden.]

[Debt.]

[Duty.]

[How much emotion has this human piled up?]

He kept climbing.

Eventually—

he reached the peak.

A narrow summit.

Empty.

Amdusias looked back down through the smoke.

Nothing was visible.

He considered the height he had climbed.

[Even a veteran who has survived hundreds of battles would struggle to accumulate this much negative emotion.]

For the first time—

Amdusias acknowledged Vikir.

This was not an ordinary human mind.

Still—

[You are human.]

[And no human can oppose me here.]

He looked down.

Searching for Vikir’s small ego—

some tiny self hiding somewhere in the mindscape.

Then—

RUMBLE.

The mountain trembled.

A storm swept the smoke away.

And Amdusias finally saw the land below.

“…!”

It did not end.

The horizon stretched beyond anything he could measure.

And the scale was not the most horrifying part.

Blood.

Flesh.

Bones.

Rivers and lakes of blood.

Human corpses tangled with demon corpses.

Hills.

Valleys.

Ravines.

Lava.

Sulfur.

Monsters devouring the dead.

Screams without end.

Amdusias shuddered.

[How can a human possess a mental landscape like this!?]

He had never seen such a battlefield.

Not even as a Demon King.

It was more brutal than hell.

[A human could never witness something like this in one lifetime!]

[Even I have never seen a battlefield like this!]

[Why does it exist inside a child!?]

Then—

the mountain shook again.

Amdusias looked up.

Another mountain stood nearby.

Larger.

Then another.

And another.

And another.

Four more.

Five mountains in total.

The one he had climbed—

was the smallest.

Amdusias froze.

These mountains represented burdens.

Responsibilities.

Missions.

Weights carried by the mind.

A young human—

should not have five such mountains.

Not unless he had endured endless karma.

Endless destruction.

The collapse of an entire world.

[What are you?]

Amdusias’s voice trembled.

[Where were you born?]

[Where did you grow up?]

[How can such things exist inside you?]

Vikir did not answer.

The land moved.

The mountains rose.

Five peaks shifted toward Amdusias.

Spiraling patterns appeared at their ends.

He stared.

Then understood.

[…Fingers?]

The five mountains were fingers.

And beneath them—

something unimaginably vast began to rise.

The true center of Vikir’s mental world.

A returner from the Age of Destruction.

A man carrying the ruins of an entire future inside himself.

Vikir’s own self—

stood up.
