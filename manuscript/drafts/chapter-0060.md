Vikir surged to his feet.

His half-healed joints screamed.

There was no choice.

If he stayed down, he would die.

CRASH!

The shelter tore apart like paper.

An Oxbear's claws ripped through the night.

Aiyen gave a short, incredulous laugh.

“So *we* were the ones being baited. Clever bastard.”

The male Oxbear was only about half the size of the old female.

That still meant more than four meters of carnivorous muscle and well over a ton of weight.

It had known it was being followed.

So instead of sleeping until dawn, it had waited for the hunters to settle down.

Then it attacked at their weakest moment.

Aiyen seized her bow and moved outside.

Bakira crouched beside her, snarling.

Aiyen studied the beast.

“Now I understand why that old female chose you.”

The giant female had rejected many males.

Why choose this smaller one?

Not simply because it was less dangerous after mating.

There had been plenty of small males.

This one possessed cunning.

Its intelligence complemented the female's overwhelming body.

ROAR!

The Oxbear charged with horns and forepaws.

Aiyen vaulted onto Bakira and nocked arrows.

TWANG! TWANG!

Two arrows flew at the monster's face.

It dipped its head, using the horns as a shield.

The arrows changed course in flight and struck its shoulders instead.

Ballak archery deserved its reputation.

And Aiyen stood among its finest young practitioners.

She loaded several arrows across the unusual Ballak bow.

Its multiple string points allowed staggered rapid fire or simultaneous release.

THUD-THUD-THUD-THUD-THUD!

Arrows followed one another faster than most eyes could track.

Drawing five at once demanded enormous strength.

Aiyen reinforced herself with the liquid aura of a **Low Sword Graduator**.

The barrage slowed the Oxbear.

Even its thick hide and muscle could not ignore her arrows.

Her shots did not travel only in straight lines.

Some climbed and dropped in arcs.

Some curved from the side.

Others ricocheted from trees or stone.

Aura turned every angle into a possible attack.

Aiyen's talent was extraordinary even by Baskerville standards.

Comparable to the prodigies of Morgue.

But talent did not erase the difference between a human teenager and an A-rank monster.

The Oxbear was smaller than the female and badly depleted after days of breeding.

It was still an Oxbear.

Aiyen's arrows began to run low.

Bakira was tiring.

The valley was cold, open, and moonless.

The beast pressed every advantage.

Oxbears commonly opened with horns, followed with a crushing forepaw.

The truly lethal attack came after that—

the bite.

ROOOAR!

The monster lunged, horns and forelegs spreading as it drove toward Aiyen and Bakira.

Its daggerlike teeth opened toward Aiyen's head.

*Retreat? Or commit everything?*

Aiyen had only an instant to decide.

“Move.”

Vikir appeared above them.

Still badly injured, he stood on a thick tree limb.

Then he jumped.

“Are you insane?!”

To Aiyen, it looked suicidal.

Vikir held several arrows.

The Oxbear did not react in time.

He had dropped from its left.

The female's earlier blow had swollen the male's left eye nearly shut.

Vikir had chosen the blind side deliberately.

STAB!

Several arrowheads plunged into the damaged eye.

SIZZLE—

Burning fat.

Scorched flesh.

The shafts had retained heat from Aiyen's aura.

They tore through the swollen lid and soft eye and damaged the nerves behind it.

The Oxbear's roar changed.

Fear entered it.

Vikir snapped the shafts and hit the ground.

“……!”

Landing alone nearly broke him again.

The Oxbear immediately turned on him.

Aiyen fired.

Her arrows buried themselves in the beast's back.

Bakira bit into one hind leg.

Neither stopped it.

ROAR!

The monster ignored horns and claws.

It opened its mouth and committed to a killing bite.

Vikir's expression remained calm.

He was not resigned to death.

He was calculating whether his recovering body could survive one more technique.

“Come.”

A loyal hound bared its fangs.

A black spike burst from the artery at Vikir's wrist.

**Beelzebub.**

The demonic blade manifested.

BZZZZZZ—

A sound like beating fly wings filled the air.

One of the powers stored within its gemstones answered.

> **Beelzebub — Blade**  
> **Slot 1:** Incinerate — Cerberus (A+)

Cerberus.

The three-headed hound that embodied Hell.

Fire surged along Beelzebub's point.

It was not ordinary flame.

As long as its master's will remained, the hellfire refused to die.

The Oxbear opened its jaws.

Vikir drove the burning fang straight into its mouth.

The beast stopped.

Only now did instinct finally tell it what stood in front of it.

What it had mistaken for an injured human concealed the presence of a far greater predator.

CRUNCH!

Beelzebub pierced the tongue.

Then the gums.

The palate.

Finally the skull.

Flame erupted from the spike's tip.

Heat cooked through the Oxbear's brain.

Its death scream shook the valley.

The attack was brief.

It was enough.

The Oxbear's monstrous vitality kept the body moving for a few final seconds.

It threw its mass toward Vikir as it died, trying to crush him under its weight.

Vikir could barely move.

Aiyen did.

She rushed in and wrapped him up.

Bakira caught them across her back and sprang away.

“Are you all right?!”

Aiyen searched Vikir for injuries.

He managed a difficult nod.

Behind them—

BOOM!

The Oxbear's corpse struck the ground.

The male that had spent the previous days sheltered in a warm den had become prey instead.

That was the jungle.

Predation.

Death.

Reproduction.

All of them intertwined.

Aiyen held Vikir tightly for a moment.

Then, panicking over how badly injured he already was, she blurted out the first Ballak-style contingency that came to mind.

“If you think you're dying, tell me! We need to collect your seed before it's too late—!”

Vikir could only sigh after their successful hunt.
