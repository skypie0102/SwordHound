# Chapter 451: Declaration of War (2)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/451.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 449 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Fresh Fandom indexing confirms **Raspane Morgue** as Morgue’s matriarch. Existing project terminology controls Hugo Le Baskerville, Osiris Le Baskerville, Seven Counts, Martin Luther, Damien Bourgeois, Adolf Morgue, Professor Banshee Morgue, Basilios, Hippolyte, Whitebeard Whale, Red Death, Poison Humans, and Tochka.

A pile of reports covered the table.

Different fronts.

Different commanders.

Different losses.

But every report ended with the same word.

**Defeat.**

**Defeat.**

**Defeat.**

**Defeat.**

Raspane Morgue looked down at them.

Then spoke.

“…The western front has collapsed.”

The atmosphere sank.

The leaders of four great houses had gathered.

Baskerville.

Morgue.

Quovadis.

Bourgeois.

And the central issue—

was the civil war.

House Leviathan.

The poisonous clan.

A family feared for its toxins since the Warring States Era before Imperial unification.

Recently—

its power had expanded at frightening speed.

Donquixote remnants.

Usher remnants.

Poison Humans.

Territory.

Forces.

Everything was being absorbed into Leviathan’s war machine.

Raspane frowned.

“The Poison Humans are worse than expected.”

“Mindless.”

“Violent.”

“And contagious.”

Martin Luther nodded grimly.

“Red Death.”

“Those infected lose strength and mana.”

“Eventually they die.”

“And afterward…”

He hesitated.

“They move like puppets.”

“Living corpses.”

“Whatever name we choose—

the result is the same.”

Morgue and Quovadis had suffered less than the others.

Magic.

Holy power.

Distance.

Those gave them ways to fight without direct contact.

Baskerville—

did not have that luxury.

Their knights fought with swords.

Close.

Inside the poison.

The losses were severe.

Only Osiris—

the Seven Counts—

and Baskerville’s veteran forces—

had kept large portions of the territory from collapsing.

Then—

all eyes moved to Damien Bourgeois.

“Wasn’t House Bourgeois allied with Leviathan before?”

Damien gave a faint shrug.

“That was the previous head’s arrangement.”

“I don’t know every detail.”

“You are the head now.”

“Acting head.”

Damien corrected him.

“The true successor is elsewhere.”

Martin Luther spoke more gently.

“You said some of the old confidential records survived.”

“No one is accusing you.”

“But if those records contain anything useful—

please share it.”

Damien nodded.

“Most were lost.”

“But there is one thing.”

“The earliest Red Death tests were conducted on the indigenous people of the western jungle.”

The room quieted.

“The tribes resisted it better than Leviathan expected.”

“So Leviathan improved it.”

“More contagious.”

“More effective.”

“The result—

is the Poison Human army defeating us now.”

They were physically strong.

Violent.

Mindless.

Controlled by Leviathan handlers.

And the red mist around them—

drained mana.

Drained stamina.

Long enough exposure—

turned ordinary humans into the same thing.

Damien continued.

“Leviathan also used soldiers absorbed from the allied Donquixote and Usher forces.”

“So their numbers continue growing.”

Adolf Morgue slammed a hand against the table.

“And we lost the western ruby veins!”

“Do you have any idea how much labor went into those mines?”

Banshee’s eyes sharpened.

“People are dying.”

“And you’re talking about gemstones?”

Adolf snapped back.

“When did I say people matter less?”

“I’m responsible for the mines!”

“I mentioned the mines!”

The two Morgue men had never been especially compatible.

Martin Luther sighed.

“Heresies and cults are spreading again too.”

Damien looked toward the window.

“Wildfires.”

“Drought.”

“Famine.”

“Monster outbreaks.”

“Plague.”

“And now religious chaos.”

“It really does resemble the end of the world.”

Then—

a cold voice cut across the room.

“…I said we should have prepared earlier.”

Everyone turned.

Professor Banshee Morgue.

No.

**Principal Banshee Morgue.**

He folded his arms.

“Family heirs are dying.”

“Commoners are dying.”

“And people are still talking as though this crisis appeared yesterday.”

Adolf glared.

Banshee ignored him.

“If every case had been properly reopened when my student was unjustly sentenced—

perhaps Leviathan would never have grown this far.”

Vikir.

When he had been imprisoned—

Banshee had been among the people who fought hardest to save him.

Now he looked directly at those who had opposed—

ignored—

or half-heartedly tolerated the effort.

“You saw important evidence.”

“You protected your own interests.”

“You kept quiet.”

“Now demons are moving openly.”

“What exactly were all of you doing?”

Several people looked down.

Others looked away.

The representatives of the other academies hurried to calm the room.

Basilios.

Hippolyte.

Whitebeard Whale.

“This isn’t the time for blame.”

“Principal Banshee.”

“We have a larger problem.”

“Stopping Leviathan’s Poison Human army.”

Banshee remained visibly displeased.

Someone tried a more optimistic angle.

“The academies have joined the war now.”

“Student participation increased our numbers.”

“At this rate—

perhaps the front can at least stabilize.”

Banshee’s expression became colder.

“So adults make mistakes.”

“Then use student deaths to cover them.”

“….”

“The fact that the lines appear more stable after Academy intervention does not prove the students changed the strategic balance.”

“They are still children who need professors protecting them.”

“Do not confuse coincidence with victory.”

No one had an easy answer.

Then Banshee looked back at the reports.

Something bothered him.

“Leviathan is withdrawing.”

Raspane frowned.

“From the western front?”

“After winning?”

“Yes.”

Banshee tapped several reports.

“Retreats.”

“Abandoned positions.”

“Deliberate losses.”

“Repeatedly.”

“Why?”

Silence.

Banshee’s face tightened.

“They are concentrating forces.”

“Making the movement look natural.”

The room changed instantly.

Everyone understood.

Leviathan was not weakening.

It was gathering.

Preparing one decisive battle.

A force representing Leviathan—

plus absorbed Donquixote and Usher strength—

could be enormous once concentrated.

Raspane looked at the map.

“The problem is that we don’t know where.”

“Or when.”

“Or how.”

The most important question—

where.

A defensive army had to arrive before the attack.

Candidates began filling the room.

“The Red Spire.”

“The ruby vein matters strategically.”

“Dorsmeer.”

“If the magical-train infrastructure falls—

our logistics collapse.”

“St. Mecca.”

“The first Red Death outbreak began near the slum wells there.”

“Maybe Leviathan left something behind.”

Political value.

Military value.

Cultural value.

Resources.

Supply.

Every expert proposed another target.

Every answer sounded reasonable.

Which meant—

none were useful.

Then one voice spoke.

“Tochka.”

No argument.

No explanation.

Only certainty.

Everyone turned.

Hugo Le Baskerville stood over the map.

The head of the Iron-Blooded Sword House.

Cold.

Sharp.

Blood and steel in human form.

“They will go to Tochka.”

The others frowned.

“Tochka?”

“That remote plateau?”

“It is defensible.”

“But it has no proper water source.”

“Strategically—

it is almost worthless.”

“Anyone with basic military knowledge would avoid it.”

“Why would Leviathan attack there?”

“Do you have evidence?”

Hugo answered simply.

“My son is there.”
