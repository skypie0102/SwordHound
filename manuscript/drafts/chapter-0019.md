# Chapter 19: Bared Teeth (2)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/019.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 19 is an aligned secondary witness only. Canonical English terminology for Troll, Superspeed Regeneration, Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, Sword Expert substages, and Baskerville Fangs follows current project/Fandom evidence.

A prodigy had appeared in House Baskerville.

A young warrior who had reached Low Sword Expert at only eight years old.

“Is there anything bigger than an Orc?”

Vikir’s question sent a current of excitement through the training ground.

Baskervilles were generally indifferent to other people.

Battle was the exception.

When it came to combat, they watched closely.

Even the cold-blooded Hugo was no different.

“…Hmm.”

Hugo began to think.

Watching him, Vikir did the same.

‘Showing a moderate amount of strength is better.’

He had no intention of turning this into a spectacle for its own sake.

If anything, displaying results while he was still very young would make Hugo less suspicious of him.

Showing promise early could also give Vikir a plausible way to slip outside Hugo’s immediate surveillance.

A posting to the border, for example.

Or study in the imperial capital.

‘…Or entering the Academy.’

He did not particularly like the third option, so he set it aside for now and cleared his thoughts.

The present mattered first.

Hugo seemed to have reached his own conclusion.

“Vikir has a point. This is the age when practical experience is necessary.”

It was an absurd thing to say about an eight-year-old anywhere else.

Within House Baskerville, it sounded almost reasonable.

Hugo continued.

“An Orc collected from the outskirts apparently does not meet my son’s standard. Do we have anything stronger in the family’s holding pens?”

He sounded almost as though he were searching an insect box for a better specimen.

The creatures kept by the Baskervilles, however, were uniformly vicious.

None was an appropriate opponent for an ordinary eight-year-old.

Barrymore and the others knew that, so no one answered quickly.

Hugo broke the silence himself.

“Come to think of it, didn’t we still have one specimen captured during the barbarian suppression?”

“…Yes. One remains.”

Barrymore could only answer truthfully.

Hugo gave a quiet laugh.

“It has not been fed properly in a long time. It should be weakened.”

“But that may only have made it more vicious. For a young master who has only just turned eight…”

“If anything goes wrong, the Guardian Knights will stop it. That is not something the Butler needs to worry about.”

Hugo waved away the concern.

He had spent little time considering the matter.

His decision came even faster.

Before long, the Guardian Knights brought Vikir’s next opponent up from deep below the estate.

Thud. Thud. Thud. Thud.

The ground trembled faintly.

A gigantic creature emerged beneath layers of heavy shackles and restraints.

A short neck.

Two massive arms and legs.

Twisted teeth.

More than four hundred kilograms of body mass packed into a frame four meters tall.

The green-skinned giant stepped into view.

Thick saliva hung between its cracked lips and the crooked fangs protruding from its shriveled gums.

ROOOAAARGH!

The Troll shook the iron chains and heavy lead weights fastened around its neck, wrists, and ankles.

Its gaunt condition suggested that it had been starved for a long time.

“…”

Vikir narrowed his eyes at it.

The moment the Troll reached the center of the training ground, the hunt began.

Tap.

Vikir tightened his grip on the shortsword and charged.

His complete lack of fear drew sharp looks from Hugo and several of the older Baskervilles.

Then—

WHOOM!

The Troll swung one enormous arm.

Vikir lowered his body, slipped beneath the fist, and entered the blind spot along its flank.

A perfect opening for a decisive blow.

But Vikir did not stab.

He passed the creature instead and reset his stance.

Hugo smiled faintly.

“Good judgment.”

Everyone knew Trolls possessed monstrous regeneration.

A wound to the flank or abdomen would close with little trouble.

Unless the strike reached a truly vital point, attacking was simply wasted effort.

Thud. Thud. Thud. Thud.

The Troll kept attacking.

Fists smashed into the earth.

Heavy legs kicked through the space Vikir had occupied a moment earlier.

An ordinary eight-year-old Baskerville might have panicked beneath attacks large enough to fill his entire field of vision.

Vikir did not.

‘Too slow. There’s no tension at all.’

He had spent more than forty years being battered around battlefields.

Pressure like this was nowhere near enough to disturb his composure.

Holding back a yawn was harder.

Vikir avoided every attack with ease.

The Troll, meanwhile, exhausted itself with each outburst.

The experienced Baskervilles watching from the sidelines were astonished.

“Is he deliberately draining the Troll’s stamina? Impressive.”

“Is that child really eight? I can barely believe it.”

“Heh. To think our family would produce a child like this. Live long enough and you really do see everything.”

Vikir made the correct decision at every moment, continuously following the most efficient path through the fight.

The restraints only made the Troll angrier.

Its rage burned through its remaining strength faster.

Its attacks became less accurate.

Then slower.

Then simpler.

Vikir watched the creature destroy its own stamina and waited for the instant that would decide everything.

Before long—

ROOOAAARGH!

The Troll threw its entire body into a charge.

Vikir knew the moment had come and sprang forward.

An eight-year-old body could evade a Troll.

It could not normally kill one.

Genius or not, that was common sense.

Vikir knew it better than anyone.

If he released the strength he was truly hiding, killing this Troll would be easy enough.

But the consequences would become troublesome.

So he had waited for a way to kill it that could still be explained by the strength he intended to reveal.

THUDTHUDTHUDTHUD!

He used the Troll’s own charge.

If he thrust from the opposite direction while that massive body rushed forward at full speed, the combined force could punch through hide and muscle and reach the nerve bundles in the neck.

A faint gaseous aura gathered at the point of Vikir’s shortsword.

The same power that had stunned the crowd during the Orc fight.

Hiss—

The aura sharpened into a visible Fang and flew straight toward the Troll’s throat.

Thrust!

One Fang punched deep into its neck.

At the instant of impact, Vikir twisted away, tearing open a large hole as the creature thundered past him.

“Oooooh!”

The watching Baskervilles cried out in admiration.

Vikir’s lifetime of battlefield experience looked, to them, like impossible genius.

But—

ROOOAAARGH!

The Troll did not die.

It shoved its own fist into the hole in its throat, flesh grinding and knitting around it as **Superspeed Regeneration** went to work.

A faint sigh passed through the spectators.

“Even the 1st Fang isn’t enough against a Troll.”

“With gaseous aura, that much regeneration is difficult to overcome.”

“A shame. Still, for an eight-year-old to get this far is already historic.”

The **1st Fang** alone could not kill a Troll.

Vikir had known that from the beginning.

Given enough time, Beelzebub’s Hemorrhage would eventually make the creature bleed to death anyway.

But winning that way had no value for what Vikir was trying to demonstrate.

“That’s enough. Continuing would be dangerous. We’ll stop here—”

Hugo raised one hand to end the fight.

“Those restraints.”

Vikir spoke before he could finish.

“Please remove them.”

Every Baskerville present went rigid.

Release the Troll’s restraints?

Now?

“…”

“…”

“…”

Their eyes shifted between Vikir and Hugo.

Even Hugo could not conceal his surprise.

After a moment, he finally spoke.

“My son. You have already demonstrated your worth. Knowing when to be satisfied and step back is also part of a swordsman’s discipline.”

The almost admonishing concern in his voice was something the surrounding Baskervilles had never heard from him before.

Their shock lasted only until Vikir answered.

“I’m almost there. If I cannot kill it with the next attack, I’ll stop.”

One strike.

That was all he was asking for.

The declaration stunned everyone, Hugo included.

How was an eight-year-old supposed to finish a creature this tenacious in a single blow?

“…”

Hugo closed his mouth.

That silence was permission.

ROOOAAARGH!

The Troll raged against its restraints.

Several Guardian Knights moved in carefully, released its shackles and chains, then withdrew beyond the training ground.

At last the Troll stood completely free.

Its eyes were so bloodshot that the pupils had nearly disappeared beneath the red.

RAAAAAAAH!

With a roar violent enough to sound as though it might vomit up its own organs, the Troll charged Vikir at full force.

Vikir merely watched it with cold eyes.

‘How much should I show?’

He had already answered that question earlier in the evening.

Flash!

The aura at the point of his shortsword brightened.

Several veterans noticed the change immediately.

Its density had increased.

They had no time to react.

Vikir moved.

Crack—crack!

The blade swung toward the charging Troll.

Visible Fangs formed along its path.

Without the restraints, the Troll was faster than before.

That only increased the counterforce Vikir could use against it.

Thrust!

A stronger, faster body drove itself onto a stronger, faster blade.

This time, two Fangs entered the Troll’s neck.

The first pierced straight through.

A second—smaller and fainter, but unmistakably present—bit savagely into the side of the wound.

The **2nd Fang**.

**Mid Sword Expert.**

…!

…!

…!

Hugo and every other Baskerville stood frozen with their mouths open.

THUD!

The Troll’s headless body dropped to its knees.
