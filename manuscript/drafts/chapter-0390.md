# Chapter 390: The Worst Torture (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/390.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 388 is the verified shifted secondary witness and is used locally to repair pronoun/name drift in the Kirko-observes-Garam passage. Established repo terminology controls Vikir, Garam Nord, Kirko Grimm, Poseidon, D'Ordume D'Orcdile, Souaré, Orca, BDISSEM, Flubber J Tarbond, Black Tongue, Bastille, Starvation Drought, and Nouvelle Vague.

Several more months passed.

The abnormality in the monthly rankings did not disappear.

It became normal.

**1st — Garam. Task completion: 114%.**

Then—

**121%.**

**136%.**

**142%.**

**155%.**

**161%.**

**178%.**

And finally—

**185%.**

The lower guards could only stare.

Nobody looked down on Garam anymore.

Treating him as a proper colleague had become natural.

More and more guards approached him first.

“Hey, Garam.”

“First again this month.”

“Your prisoners look completely trained.”

“What’s your secret?”

“Poker after dinner?”

Ironically—

the first people to act friendly were the same guards Vikir had disciplined earlier.

Then others followed.

There was one person who still kept her distance.

Kirko Grimm.

She had once occupied first place so consistently that nobody questioned it.

Now—

she was permanently second.

And for months—

she had been watching Garam.

While eating silently—

Kirko remembered the first time she secretly observed his work group.

*I still can’t believe he did that.*

At first—

Garam’s method looked ordinary.

He punished prisoners who tried to slack off.

But Vikir understood exactly how to use pain without ruining a worker’s ability to move.

The baton came down again and again.

Prisoners who had ignored the old Garam changed almost immediately.

Level One.

Level Two.

Violent prisoners.

Clever prisoners who preferred manipulation over force.

Natural idlers.

After a month under him—

they worked.

They obeyed.

They feared him.

*That much I could have thought of too.*

Kirko did not see anything impossible in discipline.

Harsh—

efficient—

but recognizably Nouvelle Vague.

Then Garam did something absurd.

THUD.

CLANG.

He picked up a shovel.

Then a pickaxe.

And joined the labor himself.

Kirko had almost failed to understand what she was looking at.

*A guard doing prisoner labor?*

*Why?*

Yet Garam worked with terrifying efficiency.

Like someone who had spent decades doing exactly this.

He corrected prisoners while working beside them.

If somebody slowed down—

he noticed.

If somebody became inefficient—

he noticed.

There was no blind spot in which to rest.

But he did not simply starve the group.

He gave away his own food.

Every meal.

Kirko had heard the old rumors.

That Garam was so pathetic prisoners stole from him.

Reality looked different.

Even without eating lunch—

Garam alone completed more work than all twelve prisoners combined.

The prisoners looked at him like a monster.

Overwhelming strength.

Overwhelming presence.

And—

strangely—

a kind of charisma.

Strong criminals.

Manipulators.

Lazy men.

All of them became quiet around him.

Knowing the method was one thing.

Imitating it—

another.

Kirko shook her head.

*Even if doing the work yourself is faster…*

*what guard would actually do it?*

◆◆◆

Vikir’s answer was simple.

*Why waste time forcing them to do something I can finish faster myself?*

The goal was not dignity.

The goal was results.

And promotion.

He needed authority over higher-level prisoners as soon as possible.

He needed freer access to the deeper work zones.

He needed **Poseidon**.

Every pickaxe strike.

Every collapse of stone.

Every impact from the prisoners working around the Level Ten excavation—

fed the blue sphere.

But indirect impact was inefficient.

*Direct impact on the surface is better.*

So Vikir wanted rank.

Higher rank meant stronger prisoners.

Larger work groups.

More access.

More force applied to Poseidon.

If prisoners refused to work—

he had enough tools to make them reconsider.

Starvation Drought.

Experience from the Age of Destruction.

A lifetime of understanding what broke resistance.

Vikir did not need elaborate cruelty.

Only compliance.

*Eventually D'Ordume will issue the order himself.*

*Remove that troublesome explosive object.*

That would be the opening.

The true escape.

He needed to be ready when it came.

Then—

“E-everyone!”

A guard rushed into the dining hall.

A hibiscus-style rank insignia marked him as a Major—

the level Vikir himself wanted to reach.

“Riot!”

“All guards currently on duty—

assemble immediately!”

“Standby guards too!”

“Anyone off shift—

report!”

The room changed.

Then the major said the words that made every junior guard pale.

“Level Eight.”

A riot had broken out on **Level Eight**.

Nothing like the training riots of Level One and Two.

The junior guards exchanged looks.

Even mid-ranking officers stiffened.

The major wiped sweat from his face.

“We’re mobilizing the lower guards for numbers.”

“Because the senior force isn’t here.”

D'Ordume and Souaré—

both on the surface.

Orca needed additional personnel for a major prisoner transport.

“Colonels D'Ordume and Souaré are topside with the convoy.”

“BDISSEM and Flubber are not practical options here.”

“And you know Black Tongue.”

“If that lunatic gets involved—

we may create a worse problem than the riot.”

“So we handle it ourselves.”

A Level Eight riot frightened Majors.

The Second Lieutenants and Lieutenants said nothing.

Then—

SCRAPE.

A chair moved.

Everyone turned.

One guard had already stood.

Calm.

Immediate.

No hesitation.

“…!”

A hand caught his wrist.

Kirko.

She had been sitting diagonally across from him.

“Are you insane?”

Her voice was low.

“Those are Level Eight prisoners.”

“If we go—

we die.”

“What do you suggest?”

Vikir asked.

“We were ordered to go.”

“….”

Kirko’s mouth closed.

He was right.

An order in Nouvelle Vague was not optional.

The survival rate might be terrible.

That changed nothing.

“We look at the situation first.”

Vikir walked forward.

Relaxed—

almost as though he were leaving to perform some harmless errand.

The other junior guards eventually stood too.

Heavy steps.

Anxiety.

Fear.

At the front of the group—

Garam Nord.

Vikir’s eyes remained calm.

*This may be useful.*

Nothing improved a performance record—

like successfully handling a riot by high-level prisoners.
