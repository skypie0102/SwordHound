# Chapter 434: The Returned Hound (1)

[…Demons must die.]

Vikir muttered in a low voice.

Dolores and the other Colosseo graduates stared at him in shock.

Then—

they understood.

That was not Vikir.

It was a fragment of the dream-world left behind when Amdusias was destroyed four years ago.

Residual thought—

imitating the shape of Vikir.

“…Even so.”

“It looks exactly like him.”

Tudor and Bianca broke into cold sweat.

Everyone else had changed over the last four years—

inside and out.

The Vikir in front of them had not.

“Looking at him now…”

“Vikir really was young.”

“He hunted monsters like that with a body this young?”

Sancho and Pigi could only groan.

Dolores and Sinclaire said nothing.

Their eyes were full of grief—

nostalgia—

longing.

Then the mood shattered.

[…Demons must die!]

The remnant raised its sword.

BOOM!

A terrifying slash swept across the land.

Everyone scattered.

Now was not the time to pity Vikir.

Whatever had happened to his heart—

the Vikir of that era had already reached Sword Master.

“Why is he attacking us?!”

“Maybe four years alone in here turned him completely feral!”

Tudor and Bianca—

standing nearest the front—

were targeted first.

RUMBLE!

Fangs tore through the air from every direction.

To face them felt like becoming prey before a colossal beast.

Run.

Every instinct screamed the same command.

The remnant’s aura burned like black fire.

Its eyes were hollow.

Not the eyes of someone thinking about victory.

Only someone incapable of doing anything except kill.

[…Demons must die.]

Hatred.

So much of it—

that it no longer burned only demons.

It burned everything.

“…Of course.”

Dolores gritted her teeth.

“To him—

the whole world must have become demons.”

Behind the remnant rose mountains.

Peaks.

Weight layered upon weight.

It was easy to imagine how much burden Vikir had carried.

*I felt it the first time I met Night Hound.*

That lonely emotion in his back—

when he treated plague victims in the slums.

Dolores finally understood it clearly.

Meanwhile—

Sinclaire kept analyzing.

“That form…”

Bianca looked at her.

“What?”

“A humanoid monster that exists only to fight.”

“A noble knight’s lingering will after death.”

Sinclaire wiped sweat from her brow.

“A Death Knight.”

Everyone looked again at Vikir.

And counted.

“One…”

“Two…”

“Three…”

“Four…”

“Five…”

“Six…”

“Seven…”

“Eight…”

During the Hell Tree incident—

they had seen Vikir’s sword draw eight fangs against Amdusias.

They remembered it clearly.

But now—

“…Nine.”

Nine.

The remnant drew nine teeth.

RUMBLE—!

Nine slashes ripped through earth and sky.

No discipline.

No order.

But overwhelming destructive power.

“Damn it!”

“We have to survive this in a world that’s already half-dead?!”

“Now I understand why that Amdusias fragment shriveled up.”

Tudor and Bianca retreated.

**Baskerville Ninth Form.**

The remnant had somehow reached it.

CRACK.

CRACK.

It twisted its neck.

It did not look human.

A piece of Vikir’s ego—

left wandering through a dead world—

fighting—

suffering—

until something inside it awakened.

“We have no choice.”

Dolores bit her lip.

“We fight.”

FLASH!

A sacred barrier rose.

[…?]

The remnant hesitated for the slightest moment at the divine aura.

Then—

BOOM!

No exception.

The Death Knight destroyed anything in the way.

**Baskerville Fourth Form.**

Four fangs hammered into Dolores’s barrier.

“Bro!”

Sinclaire joined her.

She pulled a vein of gold upward from the earth—

and layered a golden wall over Dolores’s sacred shield.

RUMBLE—

The impact tore through both.

And then—

“…!”

“…!”

Something crashed into their minds.

Not aura.

Emotion.

Dolores.

Sinclaire.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Every time steel met steel—

every time blood splashed—

a wave of Vikir’s pain entered them.

Not wounds of the body.

Pain.

Solitude.

Hatred.

Loneliness.

Grief.

Loss.

The feelings Vikir had carried.

The memories he relived.

They flooded everyone at once.

**Empathy.**

In its truest meaning.

They understood.

The loneliness of being raised as a hound under discrimination.

The loss of the only comrades who had cared.

The grief of betrayal.

The despair of being discarded by the master he had served.

The helplessness of watching people he wanted to protect die like trash.

Losing everything.

Being tortured.

Fighting.

Biting.

Killing.

Dying.

Struggling.

Always struggling.

An illegitimate son.

A child of a discarded concubine.

Even the middle name **Van**—

a mark of his position.

No **La**.

No **Le**.

Nothing granted to children born higher in the family.

So he worked hundreds of times harder.

And still—

his life remained lonely.

Painful.

This was the life of Night Hound.

The life Vikir had lived before regression.

A life fused completely with the Age of Destruction.

Tears fell.

No one knew whose came first.

Their bodies hurt beneath the barrage—

but the pain in their hearts was worse.

“Vikir lived with all of this…?”

“Even feeling it directly—

I can barely believe it.”

“And he survived?”

“How does a human do that?”

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca were shaken.

Sinclaire bit her lip until it bled.

*I thought I was the saddest person in the world.*

She remembered their old conversation.

*Now isn’t the time for romance.*

*…Now?*

*Not now, right? Just now?*

*Then after you achieve your goal—*

*could you accept me?*

How childish that plea had been.

How much pressure it must have placed on him.

And Vikir’s answer—

*If that day comes.*

She had thought it was only a way to escape the situation.

Now—

she understood how heavy it really was.

Dolores could barely stop her hands from trembling.

*Vikir.*

*Stay yourself.*

*You’re stronger than this.*

The storm of fangs remained erratic.

If it became even slightly more precise—

her barrier would shatter like paper.

She stepped forward anyway.

Slashes cut through the barrier and left long wounds across her body.

Dolores did not stop.

She reached into her clothes.

And drew an artifact.

**Mirror of Truth.**

A relic that revealed the true form of whatever it reflected.

Dolores raised it toward the remnant.

She wanted Vikir—

even this broken leftover piece of him—

to see what he truly was.

How valuable.

How brilliant.

How much more than hatred.

Then—

the storm stopped.

The Death Knight stood still.

Dolores raised the mirror.

FLASH.

Vikir appeared in the reflection.

But not the youthful face from four years ago.

A man.

Older.

Sharper.

Broader.

A face showing honesty.

Conviction.

Resolve.

Dolores smiled weakly.

“Yes.”

“That clean, strong face…”

“That’s who you really are.”

She meant every word.

If she could lighten even a fraction of his burden—

even if she turned to dust here—

it would be enough.

Then—

“I know.”

The Vikir in the mirror spoke.

“…?”

Dolores froze.

*The Mirror of Truth can do that?*

No.

That was not it.

A hand quietly covered the mirror.

Dolores had lost strength in her arm.

Someone else was standing in front of her.

The remnant remained in the darkness.

[…Demons must die.]

And another Vikir stood facing it.

“I agree.”

Dolores slowly looked up.

The man turned toward the others.

“It’s been a while.”

Short.

Plain.

Unmistakable.

Not a memory.

Not an illusion.

The real Vikir.
