“Who in this world could possibly frighten me?”

Bartolomeo’s answer had barely finished—

when the red slash arrived.

SHING!

A mountain of gold coins split diagonally behind him.

Coins—

cut—

warped—

and torn—

collapsed in a roaring metallic avalanche.

Bartolomeo touched his cheek.

Black blood stained his thumb.

“…Oh.”

His pleasant expression hardened.

“You must be Night Hound.”

“The one who killed Quilt.”

Vikir stepped into the candlelight.

“Not Quilt.”

“The Ninth Corpse.”

“Dantalian—

the Thousand Faces.”

For a fraction of a second—

Bartolomeo’s face changed.

Then smoothed again.

“…Ninth what?”

“Thousand Faces?”

“Dantalian?”

“I have no idea what you’re talking about.”

The performance was already pointless.

Vikir continued.

“Andromalius.”

“The Tenth Corpse.”

“Dantalian.”

“The Ninth.”

“Seere.”

“The Eighth.”

“Decarabia.”

“The Seventh.”

“I dealt with all of them.”

“Do you understand now?”

Bartolomeo stopped pretending.

“….”

He reached casually into a pile of treasure.

Then pulled out a sword.

Black blade.

Golden hilt.

A dark-blue gemstone embedded in the pommel.

A golden cord hanging beneath it.

Vikir recognized it immediately.

**Balmung.**

Also known as **Balmung of the Deep Sea**.

Or—

**Balmung of Bahamut**.

An old demonic sword once tied to the martial lineages later absorbed by Baskerville.

Its edge could cut steel almost casually.

Its legend—

equally old—

said prolonged possession could turn its wielder into a tyrant.

The weapon had passed through the ancient Nibelungen line—

then Bahamut—

then into Baskerville custody after the old houses merged.

*Why is it here?*

Bartolomeo answered before Vikir asked.

“When I was young—

I was considered a remarkable swordsman.”

“The previous Baskerville head admired my talent.”

“He wanted me as a disciple.”

“This sword was his gift.”

Bartolomeo raised Balmung.

“You drew a sword against me?”

“Hehehe.”

“Interesting.”

Then—

he vanished upward.

BOOM!

Balmung and Beelzebub collided.

Aura detonated between them.

Gold.

Jewels.

Treasure.

Everything nearby shattered into fragments and dust.

The two figures crossed.

Switched positions.

Bartolomeo was the first to react.

Black blood slipped from his nose.

Then his mouth.

The body he wore had once possessed talent comparable to Hugo’s.

But decades had passed.

Training had been neglected.

The flesh had aged.

The brilliance had rusted.

Bartolomeo smiled anyway.

“…Grace fades.”

“Skill remains.”

At the same moment—

blood burst from Vikir’s side.

Balmung had cut through his defenses.

Even the high-grade regenerative power stored in Beelzebub could not erase the wound instantly.

Vikir stepped back.

Blood dripped onto the cold gold beneath him.

Bartolomeo laughed.

“Financial relationships are exchanges.”

“I dislike owing anyone.”

“So I repay immediately.”

Vikir said nothing.

“There’s no such thing as a free lunch.”

“Everything has a price.”

“Everything balances.”

“Even things that appear free—”

“even losses blamed on bad luck—”

“eventually demand payment.”

Bartolomeo stopped.

Vikir answered:

“I agree.”

Then—

PSSH!

PSSH!

PSSH!

Blood erupted from Bartolomeo’s body.

“…What?”

Cuts appeared everywhere.

So clean—

so fast—

that his nerves had registered them late.

The hound’s fangs had already passed through.

Bartolomeo staggered.

“Hehehe…”

“What a tired trick.”

Vikir shook Beelzebub once.

“If you don’t realize you were cut—

that’s your failure.”

He remembered warriors from before regression.

Swordsmen whose edges were so fast—

so precise—

that blood sometimes did not flow until several steps after the strike.

Vikir had trained relentlessly in Colosseo’s gravity rooms to refine that kind of cutting.

Now—

the effort showed.

Beelzebub remained unmarked despite clashing with Balmung.

Of course.

It had been forged from the remains of an ancient Demon King.

Balmung—

on the other hand—

screamed faintly under the impact.

“Now.”

“Die.”

Vikir poured aura into Beelzebub.

Solid aura.

Sword Master.

Eight trajectories opened.

Eight fangs.

**Baskerville Eighth Form.**

The path Vikir had finally reached after meeting Cane Corso in the Grave of Swords.

Dolores stared.

*He’s stronger.*

*Far stronger than before.*

At the orphanage—

Night Hound had managed only six shifting trajectories.

With her awakened blessing—

he had forced out a seventh to kill Dantalian.

Now—

without that miracle—

he already had eight.

Bartolomeo twisted away.

Too late.

Madam’s venom—

carried into him through the earlier blood exchange—

was already spreading.

His movements slowed.

He slipped against the mountain of gold.

Vikir drove Beelzebub into his abdomen.

Bartolomeo coughed black blood.

Still—

he grabbed Vikir’s arm.

“You think this ends well for you?”

“When the vault opens—

my guards will flood in.”

“Then the Imperial Guard—”

White light descended.

Vikir’s wounds began closing.

Bartolomeo screamed.

His wounds—

instead—

burned wider.

Dolores stood atop a nearby hill of gold.

In one hand—

the **Mirror of Truth**.

“After people see what you really are—

do you think they’ll believe your story?”

The mirror showed Bartolomeo’s true nature.

Bottomless.

Black.

Malignant.

Vikir immediately turned his face away from the reflection.

Dolores did not redirect the mirror toward him.

Even now—

she refused to expose Night Hound’s identity without permission.

Bartolomeo began laughing.

“Hehehe…”

“Yes.”

“You prepared thoroughly.”

The pain vanished from his face.

Vikir pulled his sword free.

Retreated.

Black mist wrapped around Bartolomeo.

Lifted him.

Decarabia’s eye opened at Vikir’s chest.

[The play is over.]

[The Sixth Corpse is about to show his real face.]

“I noticed.”

Vikir pressed the pendant back against his chest.

The vault shook.

Gold rolled down the hills.

Then—

above Bartolomeo’s human body—

the Sixth Demon King emerged.

**<‘Sixth Corpse’ Belial>**

**Danger Rating:** S+  
**Size:** ?  
**Discovery Location:** Deep within the Gate of Destruction, ‘Serpent’s Womb’  
**Alias:** ‘Sixth Corpse’

One of the Ten Corpses.

A natural enemy of mankind.

An incomprehensible—

supposedly unkillable calamity.

**‘Your body and your storehouse shall boil.’**

**—『The Decalogue』 10:Upper—**

**Belial.**

The **Worthless One**.

Lord of greed and lies.

The sixth great calamity of the Age of Destruction—

had finally revealed himself.
