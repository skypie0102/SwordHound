# Chapter 133: Test Your Skills (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/133.txt` — `e948d42cd33e78a1da4ad882ff6442f932406edd`. Recovered English Chapter 132 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Colosseo Academy, Vikir, Pigi, Dolores L. Quovadis, Tudor Donquixote, Bianca Fo Usher, Sinclaire, Cold Department, Hot Department, Ballak Tribe, and Night Hound. Tudor's chivalric recitation is paraphrased rather than reproduced line-for-line.

At six in the morning—

the wake-up horn sounded through every dormitory at Colosseo Academy.

Students gathered outside by dormitory and gender.

Morning exercise came first.

Roughly thirty minutes of drills.

After that—

breakfast.

Showers.

Or another short nap for anyone who could afford it.

By eight—

students without special circumstances were expected in the academic buildings.

Anyone without scheduled classes or mandatory attendance could use other Academy facilities.

Training rooms.

Library.

Theater.

Other cultural spaces.

That morning—

Vikir and Pigi finished their drills, ate breakfast, and headed toward class.

On the way back from exercise—

Pigi suddenly stopped.

Several students were coming out of the infirmary.

The same six second-years from the night before.

“…Huh?”

Pigi froze.

So did they.

Then—

“Ah!”

All six scattered.

They avoided Pigi as though he were dangerous.

“…?”

Pigi stared after them.

“Why are they running away?”

Vikir said nothing.

The previous night returned to him.

His warning.

The mark he had left.

The certainty that if they continued harassing people—

the Night Hound would return.

*That should be enough.*

Anyone bold enough to keep bullying weaker students after that—

would have to be genuinely fearless.

Those six were not.

People with real courage rarely took pleasure in tormenting someone weaker.

The second-years disappeared quickly—

suddenly very aware of everyone watching them.

Vikir gave a faint, humorless smile.

Then looked toward Pigi.

Before regression—

Pigi had eventually left the Academy because he could no longer endure the bullying.

His parents had borrowed heavily to send him here.

After dropping out—

he lived a difficult life as a low-ranking civil servant.

Then the Age of Destruction came.

Pigi volunteered.

At the front—

his skills finally found their place.

Supply planning.

Logistics.

Information analysis.

Intelligence gathering.

He contributed enormously to the Human Alliance.

A few months into the war—

he was assassinated.

Vikir still remembered the faces of Pigi's parents when they saw their son's body.

*He was an honorable comrade.*

Vikir glanced sideways.

Pigi was yawning.

Completely unaware of what Vikir was remembering.

*This time—*

Vikir thought—

*I hope he doesn't live that life.*

They reached the central lecture hall.

Just before entering the shared general-education classroom—

someone stopped them.

“You two.”

A stern voice.

“Wait.”

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

Third-year.

Student council president.

Saintess.

She stood in the corridor with a serious expression.

“The center staircase is for professors.”

“Students aren't permitted to use it.”

“Use the left or right hallway stairs from now on.”

Vikir nodded.

“Understood.”

Dolores continued.

“And since you entered a restricted area—”

“one attitude point deducted from each of you.”

“State your names, departments, classes, and years.”

The records updated automatically through the Academy's enchanted score system.

Dolores checked the list.

Then frowned.

“Vikir.”

She looked again.

“Cold Department, Class B.”

Her brow tightened.

“What have you been doing?”

“You've already accumulated this many demerits?”

Vikir had been caught in restricted areas several times since enrollment.

Dormitory buildings.

Academic buildings.

Other facilities.

*Necessary.*

He needed to understand Colosseo's layout.

Every exit.

Every route outside.

Every path usable when he moved as the Night Hound.

Dolores knew none of that.

To her—

Vikir simply looked careless.

Lazy, perhaps.

Troublesome.

“Attitude points still affect your overall record.”

Dolores's tone hardened.

“Too many deductions can restrict class attendance.”

“You may be assigned volunteer work inside or outside the Academy.”

“And your behavior also affects the image of your class.”

“Be more careful.”

“Understood?”

“Yes.”

Vikir answered as though responding to a military order.

Pigi flinched.

So did Dolores.

“V-Vikir…”

Pigi elbowed him lightly.

Vikir looked over.

Pigi sighed—

then bowed deeply toward Dolores.

“I'm sorry, senior!”

“We'll be careful!”

Dolores paused.

“…All right.”

She nodded.

“I'll be watching.”

Her eyes returned briefly to Vikir.

Still uneasy.

Then she left.

Pigi grabbed his messy hair.

“Ahh…”

“She's going to remember us now.”

Vikir remained calm.

Then—

a classroom window opened.

A smooth male voice floated out.

A blond, blue-eyed student leaned against the frame.

He recited a grand chivalric verse—

about dreaming beyond the possible.

Facing unbeatable enemies.

Enduring suffering.

Correcting wrongs.

Loving with purity.

Dying for an ideal.

Reaching toward a distant star through faith.

A theatrical poem fit for old knightly romance.

**Tudor Donquixote.**

Cold Department, Class A's top student.

Strong.

Intelligent.

Handsome.

Confident.

Tudor blew Vikir an exaggerated kiss.

“First day and you've already caught the student council president's eye.”

He grinned.

“Impressive.”

“Try to be more punctual, friend.”

“I hear this year's president is terrifying about discipline.”

A cold voice answered from nearby.

“Your own demerits are your problem.”

“But don't drag down the image of the whole Cold Department.”

A girl with long hair tied back approached.

**Bianca Fo Usher.**

Cold Department, Class B's top student.

“I heard there's also a group attitude score.”

Tudor laughed.

Bianca ignored him.

Then the classroom door opened.

A short-haired girl leaned out.

“Oh, come on.”

Her tone was bright.

“Why are you two arguing already?”

She looked toward Vikir and Pigi.

“You're Vikir and… Pigi, right?”

“Come in.”

“Your seats are on the right by the windows.”

**Sinclaire.**

Hot Department top student.

Class representative for the shared foundational course.

She had already memorized Vikir's name.

Pigi looked deeply impressed that she had remembered his too.

Even if she had almost gotten the spelling wrong.

◆◆◆

Class began.

The course was mandatory for every student in both the Hot and Cold Departments.

A theory course—

focused on the ecology and cultures of monsters and so-called barbarian peoples.

The material was difficult.

Some students scribbled notes desperately.

Others gave up and slept.

A few had already skipped after upperclassmen warned them how demanding the class was.

At the front—

Tudor and Bianca were glaring at one another.

“You interrupted me earlier.”

Tudor spoke quietly.

“When I was talking to the late students.”

“Don't do that again.”

Bianca barely looked at him.

“I'll say what I want.”

“If someone is embarrassing, I'll tell them.”

“I dislike lateness too.”

Tudor's smile thinned.

“But I dislike being interrupted even more.”

“Remember that.”

Bianca turned fully toward him.

“Why should I remember what you like?”

“You're irritating.”

The two students tied for first in the Cold Department were already developing a rivalry.

They listened intensely—

each determined to outperform the other.

But they were not the only competitors in the room.

Sinclaire sat nearby.

Hot Department top student.

Excellent in both practical and written evaluation.

Tudor and Bianca watched her too.

Cold against Hot.

And Cold against Cold.

Then the professor stopped lecturing.

“…That concludes the general overview of Ballak culture.”

He adjusted the small spectacles on his hooked nose.

“Now let's see what you've retained.”

The room stiffened.

The professor spoke in a dry voice.

“Using the recorded locations where Ballak hunters have been sighted—”

“estimate the location of their main settlement.”

He folded his arms.

“In military analysis, inferring an enemy headquarters from engagement locations is fundamental.”

The question required more than memorization.

Ballak culture.

Combat style.

Known sightings.

Terrain.

Geography.

The classroom went silent.

Even many third-years would have struggled.

The professor opened the roster.

“Who ranked first in the Cold Department?”

His eyes moved.

“Tudor.”

“That's you?”

Tudor straightened.

“Yes, Professor.”

“Then answer.”

Tudor hesitated.

“The Ballak clashed with the Empire at the First, Eighth, and Seventy-Fifth Heights on the Western Front…”

The professor stared.

“Only those?”

Tudor's confidence faltered.

“…No, Professor.”

The professor clicked his tongue.

Then looked down again.

“Cold Department first place was tied.”

“Bianca.”

“Your answer?”

Bianca thought rapidly.

“The First, Seventh, Eighth, Seventy-Fifth, and Two-Hundred-Seventh Heights…”

She tried to build on Tudor's answer.

“Many of those regions are low basin terrain.”

“So their main settlement may be in a lower plain—”

The professor cut her off.

“Are those all the engagement sites?”

“And are all of them actually basin terrain?”

Bianca stopped.

“…No.”

Another click of the tongue.

The professor moved on.

“Sinclaire.”

“You ranked first in the Hot Department?”

“Yes, Professor.”

“Answer.”

Sinclaire took one breath.

Then spoke clearly.

“According to military information made available to academia—”

“the engagements occurred at the First, Fourth, Fifth, Seventh, Eighth, Thirtieth, Seventy-Fifth, and Two-Hundred-Seventh Heights.”

“Except for the Eighth Height, which sits atop a high peak, the known locations are largely basin terrain.”

She gestured toward the map.

“So current scholarship places the Ballak settlement somewhere in the low-lying interior along this route.”

A concise answer.

Accurate.

And consistent with the prevailing academic theory.

The professor nodded.

“Good.”

“Sinclaire—plus ten attitude points.”

“And one point to the Hot Department's group score.”

The Hot Department students brightened.

The Cold Department students did not.

Tudor and Bianca both looked irritated.

Then—

the professor's already severe expression tightened further.

“…Actually.”

His voice became sharper.

“Perhaps the Hot Department isn't exceptional.”

He looked toward the back of the room.

“Perhaps the Cold Department is simply disappointing.”

Every student followed his gaze.

Right side.

Window row.

Back seat.

Curtains moving lightly in the breeze.

A boy with thick hair covering much of his face—

heavy glasses—

arms folded—

eyes closed.

Vikir.
