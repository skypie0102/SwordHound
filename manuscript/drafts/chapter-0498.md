# Chapter 498: Side Story 3

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/498.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 493 contains the aligned embedded section **Side Story Chapter 3**, used as a segmented secondary witness. Established project terminology controls Vikir, Camus Morgue, Snake Morgue, Baal, Magic Abyss, Full Revival Magic, and the Wraith Tree.

Two Camus drifted through endless nothingness.

Vikir followed behind—

expression unreadable.

The **Magic Abyss**.

Clouds of gas.

Dust.

Star clusters.

All floating inside a void without direction.

The Camus from the other timeline spoke first.

“Mana wheels work like the steering wheel of a ship here.”

“I see.”

The Camus of this world answered immediately.

“Mana itself becomes fuel.”

“You inject it through the wheel.”

The two geniuses coordinated as though sharing one brain.

Which—

in a sense—

they did.

Vikir watched.

“…Do you just understand these things naturally by traveling here?”

“I understand nothing.”

Neither answered him.

They were too busy.

“So that was it!”

“All the mysteries I’ve wondered about for years are here!”

“Equations!”

“Answers!”

“Even the derivations!”

“This is incredible!”

“Yes!”

“Time flows differently depending on how an object moves!”

“Spatial mana and temporal mana coexist inside everything!”

“So both have to be calculated at once!”

“That reduces the formula error!”

“And magic circles are affected by both space and time!”

“The balance shifts!”

“That’s why identical circles still develop tiny discrepancies!”

“That’s why Full Revival kept failing!”

“We ignored the relativity of space and time!”

“This place is heaven!”

“An ocean of information!”

“Every particle is a truth mages spend entire lives chasing!”

The women practically vibrated with joy.

Vikir blinked.

“…I really don’t understand.”

When faced with something completely outside one’s field—

the smartest choice was simple.

Follow the people who understood.

So Vikir did.

The two Camus—

now moving in spirit form—

continued.

“The Magic Abyss resembles the Wheel of Reincarnation.”

“Both are abstract names for some ultimate form.”

“If we organize what we learn here—

Full Revival will become possible.”

“Then we can face Master without shame.”

The Camus of this world frowned.

“You keep calling Snake ‘Master.’”

“Why?”

“He contracted with Seere and became a corpse king.”

“Not in my world.”

“He died for me.”

“His research is one reason we’re here.”

“…Hard to believe.”

“Then who contracted with Seere in your world?”

“A Morgue.”

The Camus of this world scoffed.

“As if anyone in House Morgue would make a contract that stupid.”

They argued.

Then understood each other a little better.

Whenever the argument sharpened too much—

Vikir stepped between them.

Eventually—

they entered deeper layers.

An endless paradox.

They crossed the midpoint between beginning and end.

Then the midpoint between that midpoint and the end.

Then another.

And another.

Again.

Again.

Again.

An infinite prison of intervals.

Existences trapped between points.

But the travelers kept moving.

“We’ve thought many times about what happens when finite values are added infinitely.”

“You said observable and unobservable regions should be separated.”

“Right.”

“There are different sizes of infinity.”

“I still don’t know whether we can classify them properly.”

Vikir nodded as though he understood.

Then—

something drifted toward them.

A ruined demon body.

A shattered head opened its eyes.

[…Someone else finally came.]

[…Are you his descendants?]

The two Camus stopped.

The other Camus asked:

“Are you **Baal**?”

The Camus of this world—

and Vikir—

prepared to fight instantly.

The being’s voice was hollow.

[You know my name?]

“Of course.”

“Your followers made my life miserable.”

Baal’s gaze drifted.

[What remained in the human world was only a shell.]

[An image.]

[My true self has been trapped here—]

[thinking—]

[for a very long time.]

Time had passed beyond counting.

Then a voice returned to Baal’s memory.

*When the last moment of your life comes—*

*come here.*

Understanding flickered.

[…I see.]

[…A milestone.]

[…So that was my role.]

Baal laughed faintly.

Then spoke to Vikir and Camus.

[Travel this place.]

[You will find what you seek.]

“….”

[But even after obtaining it—]

[you must return here again someday.]

“….”

[Because that is what **he** wants.]

Those were Baal’s final words.

Vikir raised his sword.

“Demons die.”

Nine fangs opened.

The Camus of this world spun nine mana wheels.

Fire bloomed.

BOOOOM!

Baal disappeared.

A Demon Immortal—

who had never reached Demon Godhood—

became red dust in the void.

Camus watched the particles fade.

“What did ‘milestone’ mean?”

“…Doesn’t matter anymore.”

Vikir looked ahead.

“We killed it.”

“Keep moving.”

They did.

Then—

for the first time—

the journey itself became dangerous.

Sssssss—

The mana wheels began shrinking.

Their output decreased.

The other Camus clenched her fists.

“At my current realm—

adding more wheels is difficult.”

“My training isn’t enough.”

Then—

a hand touched her shoulder.

The Camus of this world.

Her expression—

complicated.

“For a long time after humanity collapsed—

I trusted no one.”

The sincerity came through clearly.

“But now…”

“I have to trust someone.”

“It’s absurd.”

“It’s strange.”

The other Camus placed a hand on her shoulder too.

“You’re not trusting someone else.”

“….”

“I am you.”

“You are part of me.”

“….”

“We’re us.”

Camus smiled.

“We can do this.”

The other Camus laughed.

“Who says otherwise?”

“No disagreement.”

Then—

the Camus of this world frowned.

“Actually.”

“One point.”

“Our judgment differs.”

“Probably because our lives were different.”

“…?”

Before the other Camus could ask—

THRUST.

The Camus of this world pushed both Vikir and the other Camus forward.

At the same time—

she released **everything**.

All her mana.

All her remaining propulsion.

She poured it into the other two.

Like a spent booster—

she began drifting backward.

The other Camus understood immediately.

“What are you doing!?”

“If you really are me—

you know exactly what I’m doing.”

“….”

“So don’t waste strength resisting.”

The distance widened.

The Camus of this world called after them.

“The method for Full Revival—”

“if you don’t find it—”

“DON’T COME BACK!”

The other Camus’s eyes reddened.

But she did not cry.

Because the departing Camus added one final thing.

“And Vikir?”

“….”

“If things get dangerous—

you can always come back to **me**.”

She winked.

The other Camus exploded.

“ARE YOU FLIRTING WITH SOMEONE ELSE’S HUSBAND!?”

The Camus of this world laughed.

“I’m you.”

“You’re part of me.”

“So doesn’t that make him our husband?”

“Hehehe.”

A laugh—

one that perhaps had not appeared on that face in years—

or decades—

drifted away.

Then vanished into the dark.

Only Vikir—

and the Camus from his timeline—

remained.

How long did they travel?

Impossible to say.

“…Shall we go?”

“…Yes.”

They crossed beyond time.

Beyond ordinary consciousness.

Eventually—

something appeared inside the void.

A reservoir.

A cloud containing more water than any world should hold.

A vast fish—

large enough to swallow stars—

burst from its surface.

Smaller fish clustered along its fins.

Vikir and Camus passed through.

Then—

a black hole thousands of times larger than a sun.

A greedy gravitational abyss.

“…Is that the true Magic Abyss?”

Camus stared.

“Maybe.”

Vikir shook his head.

“Too small.”

Camus nodded.

“Agreed.”

“Big—

but smaller than I expected.”

“Could be a trap.”

They went around it.

The detour itself consumed an immeasurable span.

They passed wandering planets.

Some had eyes.

Noses.

Mouths.

They moaned in the void.

“Perhaps they once traveled here too.”

Camus looked away.

“If we hesitate—

we’ll become the same.”

“Move.”

They continued.

Then—

the darkness became bright.

Hot.

A giant trail of fire approached.

They dodged easily.

Camus watched it pass.

“That may once have been a very long serpent.”

“It grew shorter with age.”

Farther ahead—

a disk-like celestial body appeared.

Condensed darkness.

Cold blocks of lead floated around it.

THUD.

THUD.

THUD.

Gravity pulled them inward.

Vikir drew Beelzebub.

Deflected the incoming masses.

Camus burned others away.

Together—

they forced a path.

Then—

five enormous pillars appeared.

Like the fingers of a creator.

Words stood between them.

**All things are born in the Magic Abyss—**

**and return to the Magic Abyss.**

**One day, when the stars move according to fate—**

**a gate to a new level will open—**

**and all things will meet their inevitable end.**

Both froze.

Then—

understanding struck.

Pages.

Formulae.

Black letters gathering across white space—

forming a library inside the mind.

Camus screamed.

“…YES!”

“I UNDERSTAND!”

“THE FULL REVIVAL MAGIC!”

“I FOUND IT!”

“The missing part!”

“The part Master and I never understood!”

“We can revive everyone!”

She began drawing magic circles in empty space.

Vikir continued destroying the lead blocks.

Then—

WHOOSH.

Light wrapped around both.

The knowledge they came for—

settled inside them.

Their objective—

complete.

◆◆◆

Vikir opened his eyes.

Instantly—

a voice greeted him.

“What?”

“You came straight back?”

The Camus of this world.

Vikir stared.

“How long passed?”

“I don’t know.”

“I just woke up too.”

“We opened our eyes at the same time.”

Vikir fell silent.

The journey had felt endless.

Yet—

if all that time had passed here in less than a perceptible instant—

Then the other Camus checked the pocket watch set before the ritual.

“We spent about eleven minutes inside.”

Then looked closer.

“Exactly **666 seconds**.”

The Camus of this world exhaled.

“Relativity that extreme…”

“Of course it would be the Magic Abyss.”

Vikir looked toward the portal trace.

“But we can’t go a second time.”

Camus tilted her head.

“I think I can.”

They looked at each other.

Then—

laughed.

A voice came from behind the stone pillars.

“…Where am I?”

“Why am I here?”

Vikir and Camus both turned.

Camus’s eyes immediately filled with tears.

A man was standing there.

**Snake Morgue.**

Her teacher.

“I refused the demon’s temptation.”

“I should have died in a mana rampage…”

“Why am I alive—”

“Ugh!?”

Camus slammed into him from behind.

“H-House Young Lady!?”

“How are you here!?”

“NO TIME!”

Camus postponed the reunion.

She whipped around toward Vikir.

“Husband!”

“Go outside!”

“NOW!”

Snake’s revival meant only one thing.

The spell—

had worked.

Vikir nodded.

For once—

open excitement on his face.

Then—

BOOM!

The Dark Hall doors burst open.

Vikir and Camus stepped into sunlight.

And what waited outside—

was an entirely new world.
