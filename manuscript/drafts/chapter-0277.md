# Chapter 277: Money War (5)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/277.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 275 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Vikir pressed the cat mask firmly into place.

Then moved.

His target—

Damien Bourgeois.

Long dark-brown hair.

Director of the Imperial Money Manufacturing Bureau.

Outside director of the Bourgeois Landowners Guild.

Half Imperial official.

Half private-sector director.

An unusual position—

unless one understood what had happened inside House Bourgeois.

Damien had lost the succession battle.

But he was too capable to discard.

Too politically dangerous to leave completely free.

So he occupied a narrow space between House and Empire.

He received an Imperial salary.

Managed the Money Manufacturing Bureau.

And retained a nominal role in one of House Bourgeois’s largest guilds.

But because his Imperial position came first—

he could barely interfere in internal family affairs.

His direct influence inside Bourgeois was tiny.

Almost absurdly small compared with his title of Count.

Vikir thought about the old articles.

*Maybe the promotion was Damien’s own survival strategy.*

Someone who challenged for the family throne—

then lost—

normally faced death.

Or something close to it.

Damien—

clever enough to recognize defeat early—

had likely made himself useful to the state.

A shield.

A hostage.

A person protected by Imperial value.

*Better to become the Empire’s dog than face his own family unprotected.*

Vikir approved.

*Smart.*

He understood better than most—

family could become the most dangerous enemy of all.

No one stood closer.

No one knew more.

No one could cut deeper.

For the Imperial Family—

Damien’s inside knowledge of Bourgeois also made him valuable.

That kept both sides from disposing of him casually.

A merchant.

A politician.

A survivor.

Vikir moved through the party like an ordinary servant—

passing out cocktails.

Even Dolores did not know he was hidden among the staff.

Now—

only a few meters separated him from Damien.

The surrounding guests were talking.

“Hahaha!”

“Money truly is incredible.”

“They say there’s nothing it can’t accomplish.”

“I never expected students to spend this lavishly.”

“When you have money—

you really do see a different world.”

“Now I understand why people say gold is everything.”

Another person laughed.

“Money is powerful—

but it isn’t omnipotent.”

“There are plenty of things it can’t do.”

Someone replied:

“Don’t you know the saying young people use now?”

“What saying?”

“If money can’t solve the problem—

you probably don’t have enough money!”

Laughter.

Then more.

“Having money doesn’t guarantee happiness.”

“But having none guarantees misery.”

“Good food.”

“Good clothes.”

“Good carriages.”

“Good homes.”

“Travel.”

“Gifts.”

“At least when you’re warm and fed—

you’re more likely to be happy.”

“When I was young, I thought money was the most important thing.”

“Now that I’m old—

I realize it’s even more important than I thought.”

“I envied rich people as a child.”

“I still envy them now.”

“Someone once told me money can’t buy happiness.”

“I said—

who asked to buy happiness?”

“I just want a lot of money.”

The room praised money.

Partly because everyone here lived in finance.

But partly—

because they were flattering Damien.

The man who ran the Imperial Money Manufacturing Bureau.

The man closest to the literal creation of money.

They assumed he would enjoy the worship.

He did not.

Damien gave a bitter smile.

Then quietly left the group.

He walked toward the terrace alone.

The guests looked confused.

“Did we say something wrong?”

“I don’t think so.”

“Maybe he drank too much.”

“He looked tired even when he arrived.”

Then someone spoke more quietly.

“…There’s also the issue with his daughter.”

The others nodded.

“Lady Juliet…”

“I heard about it.”

“She was so young.”

“No wonder his mood changed.”

Their sympathy lasted only until the next dancers appeared.

Compared with the expensive alcohol they swallowed—

their sympathy was cheap.

◆◆◆

Damien leaned against the terrace railing.

Night dew had gathered along the stone.

Beyond the enormous silk curtains—

the Imperial Capital glittered.

Blue night.

Quiet distance.

Almost peaceful enough to sleep beneath.

Damien looked only briefly.

Then away.

Music from the hall continued behind him.

Laughter.

Praise for the party.

Praise for money.

“…Money.”

Damien had spent his entire life around it.

Born into impossible wealth.

Built more wealth through trade.

Rose until he stood at the top of the institution that printed Imperial currency.

He had touched more money than most people could imagine.

Then—

a voice spoke behind him.

“Do you believe there’s anything in this world money cannot do?”

Damien turned.

A boy in a cat mask stood there.

He had not heard him approach.

*No presence?*

This was not merely a well-trained servant.

Damien did not shout.

Did not show fear.

He answered.

“Of course.”

“Many things.”

He looked toward the sky.

“Money is only one of many tools.”

“And the number of goals people cannot achieve—

is as large as the number of stars above us.”

“That is what it means to be human.”

Damien started back toward the hall.

The servant spoke again.

“Then which star is the farthest?”

“The brightest?”

Damien stopped.

“…What?”

A star—

something desired beyond reach.

Even by someone with Damien’s wealth and status.

The cat-masked servant answered for him.

“…Seeing your dead daughter again.”

Damien stopped completely.

The night became colder.

At last—

he turned.

“Meeting the dead.”

His eyes sharpened.

“I cannot do it.”

“But it seems you may be able to very soon.”

Killing intent exploded.

Flash.

A golden stiletto left Damien’s waist.

Fast enough to blur.

It flew toward the servant’s shoulder.

Damien’s intent was not immediately fatal.

Pain.

Fear.

A permanent injury—

punishment for an insolent servant who had spoken about the daughter of a high noble.

But—

CLANG!

A crimson blade snapped out from the servant boy’s hand.

The golden stiletto flew aside.

Damien’s eyes widened.

“…Night Hound?”
