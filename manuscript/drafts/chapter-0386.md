**Name:** Garam Nord  
**Rank:** Second Lieutenant  
**Assignment:** Junior guard, suppression unit  
**Age:** 21

Timid posture.

Gloomy expression.

Long, untrimmed hair.

A massive burn scar across the face.

This was the identity Vikir had studied over the past two years.

Vikir had never truly escaped.

More accurately—

he had staged an escape.

After leaving BDISSEM unconscious—

he returned to Nouvelle Vague.

Taking a Gulper Eel into the open sea would never have been enough.

The prison’s pursuit units would eventually catch him.

And—

more importantly—

his work here was unfinished.

*I still need to activate Poseidon.*

There was almost nothing left for Vikir to take from Nouvelle Vague.

Once he met the conditions for Poseidon’s activation—

the central purpose of his imprisonment would be complete.

*Honestly…*

*If I can finish that much—

dying here would not be the worst outcome.*

Stopping the **Long Rain of Terror** alone—

could save more than half of humanity.

After that—

the survivors could decide the future.

Still—

Vikir preferred a cleaner conclusion.

Complete the mission.

Survive if possible.

Then—

TOOOOO—

the wake-up horn sounded.

3:30 in the morning.

Guards rose thirty minutes before the prisoners.

Garam—

or rather Vikir—

sat up inside a room barely around one square meter.

The shower was a stone recess so narrow that two people would fill it.

Vikir twisted the old faucet.

Cold seawater poured down.

Soap barely foamed.

Even after washing—

salt left the skin sticky.

He looked into the mirror.

Wet hair.

Ugly burn scars.

With his regenerative body—

Vikir could repeatedly remove and recreate the damage needed for the disguise.

His voice could be changed too.

Burn the vocal cords with hot charcoal.

Let them heal.

Repeat until the sound changed.

Decarabia spoke from his chest.

[Human.]

[Doesn’t that hurt?]

[That is excessive.]

Vikir answered without emotion.

“I survived the Iron Maiden.”

“Ten thousand meters of ocean.”

“The Blade Bridge.”

“Sea monsters.”

“Sulfur showers.”

“And mining beneath a burning volcano.”

“This is nothing.”

In any case—

the first stage of the escape operation had succeeded.

Night Hound was officially dead.

And Vikir had obtained a guard identity.

That was useful.

Watching Poseidon would be far easier as a guard than as a prisoner.

Vikir looked around while preparing for roll call.

Palm-sized notebooks covered the desk and bed.

Hundreds of them.

Garam Nord’s diaries.

Every day of his life—

written down.

The room had already been tiny.

The notebooks made it even smaller.

Vikir did not throw them away.

He treated them carefully.

Read the damp, yellowed pages.

Memorized what mattered.

*Knowing the daily life of the lower guards is useful.*

Garam had recorded the structure of Nouvelle Vague in extraordinary detail.

Work schedules.

Guard routines.

Relationships.

Unwritten rules.

Useful observations.

Vikir had stayed awake through the night reading.

There were still many books left.

So he tried to focus only on information relevant to maintaining the disguise.

The prison system.

People around Garam.

Things that could expose an impostor.

Most of the remaining pages—

were personal.

Thoughts.

Beliefs.

Loneliness.

Vikir had not intended to care.

But after reading so much—

some understanding was unavoidable.

*Good.*

*He had very few close friends.*

Garam’s social circle had been extremely small.

That made impersonating him safer.

But it had also made him a careful observer.

The prison seen through a prisoner’s eyes—

and the prison seen through Garam’s—

were surprisingly similar.

Most diary entries ended with the same sentence.

**I want to leave this place.**

It read like a prisoner’s confession.

Vikir looked around the stone box.

No window.

Barely more than a coffin.

The only obvious difference from a prisoner’s cell—

was the absence of shackles and bars.

Then he reconsidered.

The guards had shackles too.

“Morning roll call!”

“Everyone out!”

The schedule.

The rotations.

The relentless work.

Invisible bars.

The guards who controlled prisoners—

were themselves controlled by Nouvelle Vague.

Vikir stood.

Closed the diary.

Or tried to.

“…Hm?”

The next page was different.

Until then—

Garam’s writing had been dry.

Detached.

Almost as if he were documenting someone else’s life.

From this point onward—

that changed.

The pages became about **Kirko Grimm**.

How strong she was.

How resolute.

How admirable.

How beautiful.

Soon—

nearly every entry mentioned her.

There were sketches too.

Surprisingly good ones.

Kirko placing first in the monthly evaluation.

Kirko stopping a riot.

Kirko becoming the first among their peers to receive a promotion.

Kirko winning a swordsmanship competition.

And eventually—

Garam’s usual final sentence changed.

**I want to leave this place.**

became—

**I want to leave this place with her.**

Then:

**I want to show her the outside world.**

**I wish she could see the blue sky where I was born.**

**A lake without salt.**

**Animals running across open fields.**

Vikir closed the diary.

He remembered things he had heard before.

The old scandal inside Nouvelle Vague.

A prisoner sexually assaulting a guard.

The prison becoming obsessively sensitive to insubordination afterward.

A child being born from that violence.

And the words Sakkuth had once used to degrade Kirko—

telling a woman born from rape that she had no right to walk beneath the surface sky.

Garam’s diary confirmed the connection.

Kirko had been born inside Nouvelle Vague.

Raised here.

She had never seen the surface.

“…Unrequited love?”

“Admiration?”

Vikir murmured.

“I still don’t understand people this young very well.”

Even after spending years at Colosseo—

that part of human life remained difficult for him.

Garam was twenty-one.

Kirko was similarly young.

Young enough that—

under different circumstances—

both of them might simply have been Academy students.

Kirko especially.

Vikir had seen enough to recognize her talent.

Her potential.

Her obsession with improvement.

Given the right environment—

she could have become one of Colosseo’s best students.

Perhaps even a student-council president.

Her potential was—

in Vikir’s judgment—

at least comparable to, and perhaps greater than, Tudor, Bianca, Sinclaire, or Dolores at the same stage.

*Reality is cruel.*

Not everyone was allowed a beautiful youth.

There was always someone above.

Someone below.

The Age of Destruction was not necessary to teach that lesson.

Vikir finally closed the book.

Left the room.

The badly fitted door shut behind him.

Creak.

Bang.

The corridor was narrow and foul.

Mold.

Wet moss.

Rotting seaweed.

Salt moisture clinging to skin.

Briny droplets falling from the ceiling.

Cheap cigarette smoke.

A cramped refrigerator in the corner leaking the smell of decay.

Through a half-open door—

a fellow guard lay on his bed reading an ancient erotic magazine.

It had been printed more than sixty years ago.

The models were probably dead by now.

Soon—

roll call began.

The lower guards lined up in two facing rows.

Their eyes looked almost as empty as the prisoners’.

Male.

Female.

Guard.

Nouvelle Vague treated those as three separate categories.

Inside the guard quarters—

everyone was functionally the same.

Then—

“….”

One pair of eyes stood out.

Bright.

Focused.

**Kirko Grimm.**

Her room was diagonally across from Garam’s.

*Strange.*

*I knew her from the records first.*

*Seeing her through this identity feels different.*

Vikir looked a little too long.

SMACK.

Someone slapped the back of his head.

“…?”

Nearby guards snickered.

“Hey, Stupid Garam.”

“Staring at Kirko again?”

“Creepy bastard.”

“Know your place.”

“Fix your uniform.”

“Try looking like someone prisoners might respect.”

“And why are you still wearing that old nameplate?”

“Did someone steal the proper one again?”

“Rotten Dog.”

“Same as always.”

**Stupid Garam.**

**Rotten Dog.**

Names his colleagues had used for years.

Vikir knew all of them from the diaries.

*Humans are the same everywhere.*

Before regression—

inside military units.

After regression—

at the Academy.

Groups often strengthened themselves by choosing someone weak enough to sacrifice socially.

Garam had been that person.

Seniors ignored the bullying.

Superiors tolerated it.

If humiliating one guard increased cohesion among the rest—

the organization considered it a bargain.

So the lower guards continued.

“Yesterday was crazy.”

“Today should be easy.”

“Then let’s bother Garam.”

“Hey, Rotten Dog.”

“Where were you during roll call last night?”

“You didn’t even work.”

“Trying to get yourself punished?”

“Keep that up and Black Tongue will notice you.”

One of the guards happened to meet Kirko’s gaze.

She was watching.

Pity.

Disappointment.

He grinned.

“Hey, Kirko!”

“Aren’t you going to save your boyfriend?”

“You two grew up together, didn’t you?”

Every eye moved between Kirko and Garam.

Kirko’s brow tightened.

“…Bastards.”

She turned away.

Laughter followed.

Then—

TOOOO—

another alarm.

Not roll call.

Everyone straightened.

A night-shift guard came running from the far corridor.

“RIOT!”

“Level One riot!”

“Suppression unit—

move immediately!”
