Morning came.

Vikir had worked late the previous night, then slept at the official residence across from City Hall.

Knowing that, Chihuahua came to fetch him early in the morning.

“Sleepy, Your Excellency. The sun is already up. It's time to go to wor—AAAAAH!?”

Chihuahua had knocked and entered, only to let out a shriek before he could finish his morning greeting.

There was blood everywhere.

The white sheets were soaked through. Blood had run down onto the floor and seeped into the seams between the marble tiles.

Blood.

Blood.

Blood.

The walls, ceiling, and bed were all splattered red.

“Ah. Good morning, Secretary.”

Vikir rose from the bed with perfect calm.

The drowsiness on his face made it obvious that he really had been asleep only moments before.

Beneath the bed lay a corpse with its head separated from its body.

The black-masked man still had a dagger in one hand. Anyone could tell at a glance that he had been an assassin.

Vikir looked down at the body.

“Hm? I don't remember this. I must have killed him while sleepwalking.”

“R-Really?”

“Of course not. It was a joke.”

*Isn't this the sort of joke a fifteen-year-old would make?*

That had been Vikir's reasoning, but Chihuahua apparently had no idea how to react.

“Your Excellency... just how did you spend your childhood at the main house?”

“Wasn't it funny?”

“This isn't really a question of whether it was funny...”

Chihuahua looked genuinely lost for words.

Vikir shook his head.

“I've been getting more and more of these flies lately.”

Two poisoned arrows.

Four poisoned drinks.

Six anonymous street attacks.

A stabbing.

Sulfuric acid.

A sniper.

Arson.

A carriage attack.

And more.

All of it had happened within the last three days.

This was the first assassin to make it all the way into his bedroom, but even he had never stood a chance.

Warriors who had survived the Age of Destruction learned to sense murderous intent even in their sleep.

Vikir was no exception.

“Well, I suppose I should look on the bright side. It means I'm getting plenty of attention.”

“You really have nerves of steel.”

“Secretary. That's rather disrespectful toward your superior.”

Vikir answered without concern as he put on his clothes.

Chihuahua followed him out of the bedroom, shaking his head the entire time.

“Looking at you, I really can't believe you're only fifteen.”

“It's in the blood, isn't it?”

“Even for Baskerville blood... no, the other Baskervilles aren't like this. The previous magistrate...”

Chihuahua continued talking, but Vikir had already stopped listening.

His thoughts had returned to the spoils from the illegal slave auction.

The magic sword Beelzebub.

It was a strange weapon capable of stealing and absorbing powers from what it consumed.

The power of the Infernal Buffalo ‘Murcielago’, whose corpse Vikir had found at the auction, had now been sealed into it.

The Troll's C+-rank **Superspeed Regeneration** had disappeared and been replaced by the Infernal Buffalo's **Tough Life**.

Superspeed Regeneration rapidly restored an injured body.

Tough Life was different.

It made the body itself extraordinarily tough, to the point that injuring it became difficult in the first place.

For general use, Vikir preferred the latter.

The Infernal Buffalo was an A-rank monster far above an ordinary Troll, so it was only natural that the ability taken from it would be exceptional.

*Finding an A-rank monster corpse at that auction really was fortunate.*

It was thanks to that meal that Vikir had dealt so easily with last night's assassin.

The man who had infiltrated the residence had already reached the level of a **Sword Graduator**.

He was only newly advanced, but the dense aura clinging to his weapon had unmistakably possessed the sticky, liquid quality of that rank.

The attack had caught Vikir at close range, and the dagger had touched his chest.

Yet the protection granted by the River Styx and the Infernal Buffalo's Tough Life reinforced one another.

All the blade had left behind was a faint scratch.

The assassin himself had clearly never imagined such a thing possible.

“W-What the hell is this body...? How can it be this hard...!?”

Those had become his final words.

“We need to find whoever is behind these assassins and root them out completely.”

“I agree.”

What Vikir did not tell Chihuahua was that, once this matter was finished, he intended to resign from his post as Deputy Magistrate.

It was in line with the advice Cindywendy had given him not long ago.

And then something happened that fit neatly into that plan.

A message arrived from House Baskerville.

A huge black carriage stood before City Hall.

The luxurious vehicle bore the tooth-shaped insignia of the Baskerville Clan.

The man who had come for Vikir so early in the morning was someone he knew very well.

Head Butler John Barrymore had come in person.

“Young Master. It has been a long time.”

“Has it? Head Butler. You look healthier.”

Barrymore smiled warmly at Vikir's reply.

“See? Didn't I tell you before you left that you would do well?”

Word of Vikir's achievements had reached even the main house.

Every rumor concerned the now-famous Deputy Magistrate of Underdog City.

“I was lucky. The timing happened to work in my favor.”

Despite being the subject of all those stories, Vikir remained as matter-of-fact as ever.

Barrymore soon explained why he had come.

“The Patriarch is asking for you. I believe he wants a direct report about this incident.”

“I already submitted a written report.”

“Haha. Is a document really the same thing as hearing the report from one's own son?”

Barrymore stroked his mustache, narrowing his eyes in amusement.

“I am looking forward to this. I have never seen the Patriarch so pleased.”

◆◆◆

Hugo Le Baskerville.

He sat behind the Patriarch's desk with the same expressionless face as always.

Yet Vikir sensed something unusual about him.

It was...

“...Well done, son.”

Warmth.

Uncharacteristic warmth.

Hugo placed the morning newspaper on the desk.

The results of a public popularity survey covered the front page.

“You have accomplished a great deal.”

“I merely did what needed to be done.”

“There are far too many useless fools in this world who cannot even do what needs to be done.”

A faint smile touched Hugo's face as he looked at Vikir.

“Illegal slave trading is a grave crime for both buyer and seller, serious enough to warrant death. Thanks to your actions, even the Imperial Court has sent a commendation.”

“It is an honor.”

“You have also given us a chance to erase the reputation that Baskervilles know only how to swing swords and have no brains. You did very well.”

Vikir's special laws, the deliberate spectacle with which he had announced them, and the uncompromising enforcement that followed had become an example not only for every city under Baskerville authority, but had even attracted the attention of the Imperial Court.

Hugo's unconventional appointment had produced unconventional results.

One of House Baskerville's principles was simple.

Reward and punishment must both be clear.

Those who performed well were rewarded.

Those who failed were punished.

Vikir had produced exceptional results.

Therefore, he deserved a corresponding reward.

That was why Hugo had summoned him to the main house today.

“Enroll in the Academy.”

The Imperial Academy, **Colosseo**.

It was the institution every elite in the world dreamed of entering.

Vikir asked,

“Isn't twenty the usual age for entering the Academy?”

“If you have the ability, age is not much of a restriction. Early enrollment and late enrollment are both permitted. You need only satisfy two conditions: be no older than twenty-five upon admission and no older than thirty upon graduation.”

“...I will live up to your expectations.”

Vikir answered with a short bow.

Normally, the conversation would have ended there.

But Hugo showed an unusual desire to continue.

“I intend to send several others to the Academy with you, but I have not settled the list.”

“...?”

“Are there any brothers you would like to accompany you?”

The question was completely unexpected.

It sounded almost like something a father would ask his son.

*Ah. Right. He is my father.*

Vikir suddenly remembered a fact he himself had almost forgotten.

Illegitimate or not, he was still Hugo's son.

Perhaps he had gone so long without being treated like one that even he had stopped thinking of himself that way.

Vikir considered the question for a moment.

“I am closest to the triplets Highbro, Middlebro, and Lowbro.”

Of course, “closest” merely meant that, if he had to keep someone nearby and beat some discipline into them, those three would do.

“...Is that so?”

Hugo's eyes widened slightly in surprise.

Then he nodded.

“I will remember that.”

That ended the discussion about the Academy.

Vikir had just bowed and turned to leave when Hugo stopped him again.

“Ah, one more thing. Son, wait.”

“...?”

Vikir turned back.

Hugo rose from his seat and spoke in a lower voice.

“I reviewed the laws you revised for Underdog City.”

“...They are still immature.”

“The changes to the agricultural laws were quite good. Efficient, too.”

“You flatter me.”

Vikir had reorganized the legal framework governing the large farms on the outskirts of Underdog City.

Beyond the ruby mines, the region contained extensive fields of sugar cane, tobacco, cotton, and other crops.

Most of the labor there was performed by captured tribespeople.

Perhaps for that reason, tribes crossing the frontier to raid Baskerville territory often chose those outer farms as their main targets.

Hugo continued.

“Before you enter the Academy, why not make a brief inspection of the territory? You should see for yourself whether the laws you wrote are actually being carried out properly. It will be useful experience.”

“As you command.”

Vikir accepted without objection.

Hugo seemed satisfied.

Then he abruptly asked,

“But do you intend to go alone?”

“...?”

Vikir tilted his head.

Was this merely a territorial inspection, or was he expected to take an armed party with him?

Seeing the question on Vikir's face, Hugo sat back down and spoke casually.

“This inspection will be a joint operation.”

Vikir remained silent and waited for him to explain.

Hugo understood.

“Do you remember the plan you proposed when you were eight? The one involving the ruby mine.”

“...Of course. The main idea was to lease the ruby-mining region to the Morgue Clan, then steer the tribes toward that area so the two forces would keep one another in check without Baskerville having to intervene directly.”

Using another's blade to do the killing.

Baskerville's expansion along the frontier would inevitably bring it into conflict with the tribes sooner or later.

Vikir's plan had been to use Morgue's strength to reduce that problem first.

A satisfied smile appeared at the corner of Hugo's mouth.

“That plan worked.”

“Worked... in what sense?”

“The Morgue Clan approached us first. Quite earnestly, in fact.”

It had been a long time since Hugo had looked so pleased.

He looked directly at Vikir.

“How would you feel about a joint punitive expedition?”
