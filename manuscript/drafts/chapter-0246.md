Four hundred students—

were dropped into the jungle.

Far apart.

Scattered.

And almost immediately—

people began disappearing.

“My stomach—! Was that fruit poisonous?”

“I drank water from a tree hollow and now I can’t stop throwing up!”

“Something bit me! My eye is swelling!”

The contest map itself—

was trying to kill them.

Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Poisonous mushrooms.

Poisonous fruit.

Contaminated water.

Venomous insects.

Heat.

Crushing humidity.

Vines and leaves that cut—

stabbed—

and struck.

A jungle so dense—

hours of simple movement became exhausting.

Sweat poured off students standing still.

Dehydration accelerated.

Fatigue piled up.

Hunger followed.

Then—

the desperate ate the wrong thing.

More HP vanished.

The special tournament suits linked HP to the wearer’s physical condition and stress.

Healthy—

HP rose or stabilized.

Exhausted.

Injured.

Sick.

Terrified.

HP fell.

Zero—

and the contestant was forcibly teleported to the waiting room.

No exceptions.

◆◆◆

“…Hah. Hah. Hah.”

Granola Leviathan—

Hot Department vice representative.

Member of the Noble Faction.

Heir of one of the Seven Great Families.

Was suffering.

**HP — 74%.**

He had not fought anyone.

Not once.

Yet twenty-six percent was already gone.

“This is insane.”

Granola wiped sweat from his face.

“This jungle is hell.”

He stared into rainwater collected inside a hollow tree.

Clear—

at first glance.

Then he leaned closer.

Rotten smell.

Tiny worms twisted below the surface.

“…Ugh.”

He backed away.

Drink that—

and his stomach would revolt.

Diarrhea would take even more water from his body.

Then his HP would collapse—

before he had done anything.

His food was no better.

A fish hung from his waist.

He had cleaned it quickly—

tried to dry it—

but the air was too wet.

It already smelled spoiled.

“No water.”

“No food.”

“What am I supposed to do?”

Now he understood why older students called this the worst map.

Granola had studied poisons his entire life.

Books.

Specimens.

Lessons.

Yet the real wilderness refused to look like a book.

Plants could be larger—

smaller—

dust-covered—

torn—

upside down—

withered—

half-hidden in shadow.

Variants.

Subspecies.

Unknown species.

Things no illustration showed.

“If even I’m struggling…”

Granola swallowed.

“What are normal people supposed to do?”

He turned—

expecting to see his teammates suffering too.

Instead—

“Oh. This is good.”

“Good.”

“Good.”

Highbro.

Middlebro.

Lowbro.

The three Baskerville brothers sat around a roaring fire—

happily eating fish stew from coconut-shell bowls.

In the center—

Vikir sat expressionless.

Completely at home.

◆◆◆

*It has been a while.*

Vikir felt—

almost nostalgic.

Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Ballak hunting ground.

Baskerville training ground.

Leaves sharp enough to cut skin.

Insects whose stings raised swelling.

Heat.

Humidity.

All familiar.

He found flint.

Built a fire with little smoke.

Used hot, pungent ash to fight the damp.

Caught fish from a nearby river.

Selected mushrooms and fruit he recognized.

Roasted what needed roasting.

Steamed what needed steaming.

For water—

charcoal powder.

Baked earth.

Then boiling.

The triplets—

for all their madness—

followed instructions perfectly.

“Vikir.”

Highbro held up a container.

“Can we drink this?”

“No.”

Middlebro frowned.

“Why? Looks clean.”

“I checked upstream.”

Vikir answered flatly.

“There’s a dead wildcat in the water.”

The brothers immediately put it down.

Lowbro held up a cluster of berries.

“These?”

“Steam them first.”

“Why?”

“Raw, they can make you blind.”

That was enough.

Granola stared.

*Those three Baskervilles…*

*They’re actually obeying him.*

His opinion of Vikir shifted—

slightly.

Then his eyes found the stew.

Red broth.

White fish.

Vegetables.

Steam.

Spice.

Granola salivated.

*No.*

*I am not begging a commoner for food.*

He touched the rotting fish at his belt.

Pride—

hurt.

His stomach—

hurt more.

“Hey.”

Granola looked up.

Vikir held out a coconut-shell bowl.

Hot red soup.

“Eat.”

Granola’s eyes stung.

*This commoner…*

*Maybe he’s not as bad as I thought.*

One sip.

“…!”

His eyes flew open.

*Delicious.*

Absurdly delicious.

Maybe because he was dehydrated.

Maybe because he was starving.

Maybe because food tasted better outdoors.

Whatever the reason—

Vikir’s stew seemed better than food prepared by the Leviathan estate’s finest chefs.

*I should ask if he wants to work in our kitchen later.*

Granola emptied the bowl.

Vikir added more mushrooms.

Fish.

A few dried insects.

Granola watched.

Drooling.

“Um…”

“Have more.”

“There’s plenty.”

“Th-Thanks.”

One bowl.

Another.

Another.

By the end—

Granola had eaten fifteen additional bowls.

His HP—

which had fallen as low as **41%**—

recovered to **92%**.

He patted his swollen stomach.

And noticed—

the pot was still full.

“Hey, commoner…”

He corrected himself.

“…Vikir.”

Vikir looked over.

“Why did you make so much?”

Highbro.

Middlebro.

Lowbro.

All turned too.

Vikir stirred the stew with a broad leaf.

“Fishing.”

Five heads tilted.

Granola looked at the fish in the pot.

“You caught fish to make the soup.”

“So why are you fishing again?”

Vikir gave the rare extra explanation.

“I’m waiting for prey to bite the bait.”

The broad leaf—

was not merely stirring.

It fanned the steam.

Warmth.

Spice.

Food.

The scent drifted through the darkening jungle.

Firelight followed.

Then—

rustle.

Something moved.

Closer.

Closer.

The triplets half-drew their blades.

Granola gripped his grimoire and staff.

Only Vikir remained calm.

He slipped behind the brush—

eyes on the bait.

Crack.

A figure pushed through the leaves.

Exhausted.

Clothes torn.

Covered in dirt and foliage.

The triplets froze.

Granola froze.

Vikir—

also looked surprised.

A tired but clear voice came from the darkness.

“Whew…”

“What is that smell?”

Dolores L. Quovadis stepped into the firelight.
