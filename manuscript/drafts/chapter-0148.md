# Chapter 148: Volunteering for the Golden Week (5)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/148.txt` — `0f28089dcd2bd2e4dd3fe18c175b186f0c6689b6`. Recovered English Chapter 147 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Quilt Rune Indulgentia, Geronto, Pedo, Hebe, Ephebo, Humbert L. Quovadis, Old Testament Faction, Madam Eight-Legs, Ballak, Peri, Dolores L. Quovadis, and Night Hound.

The noon sun burned overhead.

Vikir was pulling weeds from the playground.

Even while doing manual labor—

his eyes and ears remained alert.

He could not afford to miss anything connected to Quilt.

So while he worked—

he gathered information.

An ordinary volunteer had little chance to meet the orphanage director directly.

Most could only watch Quilt from a distance—

or piece together his routine from the people around him.

That alone was inconvenient.

His four constant escorts made it worse.

Geronto.

Pedo.

Hebe.

Ephebo.

*All four are strong.*

Vikir's attention lingered on Geronto.

*Especially that woman.*

She was the smallest of Quilt's shadows.

A female mage.

The magical power coming from her was enormous—

at least around the fifth-circle level.

Her body looked roughly the same age as Vikir's.

The amount of power packed into it made no sense.

There was another disturbing detail.

She carried a black sword hilt in a reverse grip.

It gave off a foul, unpleasant odor.

Old.

Unnatural.

Like an ancient relic.

Whatever it was—

it felt ominous.

In any case—

Quilt could not be reached while those four remained around him.

*For now—focus on what I can learn.*

Vikir reviewed the information he had gathered so far.

Quilt's official work was almost disappointingly ordinary.

He met visiting nobles and merchants.

Sold indulgences and certificates of remission.

Then periodically sent investment reports and sales ledgers back to House Quovadis.

Most of those reports went to one person.

**Cardinal Humbert L. Quovadis.**

Leader of the Old Testament Faction.

At night—

Vikir quietly crossed the orphanage walls.

Then he watched Quilt's study.

Whenever a carrier pigeon or owl departed—

Vikir shot it down and intercepted the message.

The records were illuminating.

> **Indulgence Sales Record**
>
> **Viscount Beckoin** — Murdered his parents after they chose to donate their estate rather than leave it to their children. Disguised the deaths as an accident and seized the inheritance.  
> **Payment for absolution: 1.5 billion gold.**
>
> **Baron Lageso** — Raped and murdered twin sisters, then disposed of their bodies in the sewers.  
> **Payment for absolution: 200 million gold.**
>
> **Lord Finikig** — Murdered a business partner and seized the investment capital. The victim's family fell into ruin and died by collective suicide.  
> **Payment for absolution: 800 million gold.**
>
> **Count Eisel** — Suspected of massive tax evasion approaching 100 billion gold.  
> **Payment for absolution: 300 million gold.**
>
> **CEO of Quika Company** — Manipulated stock prices, contributing to the suicides of more than ten thousand retail investors.  
> **Payment for absolution: 5 billion gold.**

Tax evasion.

Sexual violence.

Murder.

Fraud.

Theft.

Human misery turned into a price list.

Vikir read the stolen correspondence while pulling weeds.

Then reached a simple conclusion.

“…I don't need to know any of this.”

The people in those records were criminals.

Some were monsters in every ordinary sense of the word.

But that was not enough to make them prey for the Night Hound.

They had not—

so far as these records showed—

collaborated with demons and betrayed humanity itself.

Vikir was about to discard the documents.

Then he paused.

Names.

Crimes.

Payments.

Connections to the Old Testament clergy.

Every secret transaction had been written down.

“Someone might appreciate this as a gift.”

Politically—

the list was a powerful weapon.

Vikir folded the letters and set them aside.

He would hide them somewhere safe.

Then—

Wiggle.

Something moved inside his clothing.

“…?”

Vikir looked down.

His eyes widened.

That alone was rare.

A large black egg shifted inside the pocket where he carried it.

The egg of **Madam Eight-Legs**.

He had rescued it during the two years he was stranded deep within Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Since then—

he had kept it with him.

Why was it moving now?

“…?”

Vikir followed the direction of its movement.

Several children were playing nearby.

A strange thought entered his mind.

*Could it be…?*

The egg had once spent time among the wolf pups of the Ballak Tribe.

The pups had rolled against it.

Licked it.

Climbed over it.

Slept around it.

Perhaps—

somehow—

the egg remembered that noisy life.

Was it reacting to the orphanage children because they reminded it of the pups?

Madam Eight-Legs's egg had no parent.

Neither did the children here.

Was that merely coincidence?

*No.*

*That's ridiculous.*

*It's a spider egg.*

*It can't possibly be sentimental.*

Then again—

this was the egg of a Danger Rating S monster.

Madam Eight-Legs had never been ordinary.

Bam!

A sharp sound came from the playground.

Every child nearby froze.

“Ah!?”

Had someone kicked the ball too far?

No.

The ball had not moved.

Its worn leather had finally split.

The air inside burst out.

“Ah…”

“That was our last ball…”

The children gathered around it.

The orphanage earned enormous sums through the sale of indulgences.

Yet the children's toys were ancient donations.

Worn down.

Patched again and again.

The ball had been repaired so many times that barely any original leather remained.

Now—

it was finished.

The child who had kicked it last stood rigid.

Vikir recognized her.

**Peri.**

Usually expressionless.

Usually distant.

Now—

her face looked close to tears.

She stared at the other children with helpless apology.

Then something impossible happened.

Pop!

Madam Eight-Legs's egg shot out from Vikir's clothing.

Thump!

Thump!

Thump!

It bounced lightly across the playground.

Almost as though inviting the children to chase it.

“Huh?”

“A ball!”

The children brightened immediately.

The “ball” moved strangely.

Its bounces were not always natural.

But the children did not care.

They ran after it—

kicked it—

threw themselves into the game.

“…Absurd.”

Vikir stared.

The egg seemed to be enjoying itself.

Whenever a hand or foot struck it—

whenever it flew high into the air—

it bounced back almost eagerly.

Ordinary impacts could not crack the shell.

Instead—

it rebounded like rubber.

As though the heavens had designed it specifically to be kicked around a field.

But no matter how convenient it was—

it remained a monster's egg.

Not a toy for children.

Vikir stepped forward to retrieve it.

“Ahem!”

A cough sounded behind him.

Vikir ignored it.

He took another step.

“Ahem!”

“Ahem!”

The coughing became deliberately louder.

“…?”

Vikir finally turned.

Dolores stood behind him.

Her face was faintly red.

Vikir immediately picked up the papers he had left nearby—

Quilt's intercepted records—

and tucked them inside his clothes.

Dolores narrowed her eyes.

“Studying for a written exam?”

“You're studying even while volunteering?”

“…You're surprisingly diligent.”

“What is it?”

Vikir's voice was flat.

Dolores coughed again.

Her eyes wandered.

“Um.”

“Ahem.”

“I just wanted to ask how the volunteer work was going.”

“I asked some of the others.”

“They said you've been working very hard.”

“Is it going well?”

“Yes.”

“…”

“…”

“…That's it?”

“Yes.”

“…I see.”

Dolores seemed completely unprepared for how little conversation Vikir gave her.

Her entire life—

people had responded to one question with ten answers.

Sometimes a hundred.

She twirled the end of her hair around one finger.

Then forced herself to continue.

“Actually…”

“I came because I wanted to talk about yesterday.”

“…?”

Vikir frowned slightly.

*Yesterday?*

The matter had been so unimportant to him—

he had almost forgotten it.

Dolores hesitated.

Then continued.

“I think the situation with my family made me overreact.”

“You were trying to help me.”

“And I shouted at you.”

“So…”

“I wanted to say I'm sor—”

“…!”

Vikir stopped listening.

Something far more urgent had happened.

Bam!

Peri kicked the black egg.

It flew high.

This time—

its path was wrong.

Plop!

The egg dropped directly into a deep drainage channel at the edge of the playground.

The children screamed.

“No!”

“That's our last ball!”

“We can't play without it!”

“Get it! We have to get it back!”

The wastewater in that channel flowed toward the orphanage's underground sewage-treatment facility.

And what had just fallen into it—

was not a ball.

Gurgle—

The black egg was already being swept away.

The drain was too deep for the children.

The current was too fast.

Vikir moved immediately.

Dolores's unfinished apology died in her throat.

“I'm sorr—huh?”

Vikir ran past her.

He reached the children gathered at the drain.

“It's deep.”

“Move.”

They backed away.

Vikir jumped.

Splash!

Without hesitation—

he plunged into the filthy wastewater.
