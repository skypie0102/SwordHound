The opening ceremony ended.

Eventually.

Mage Tower’s headmaster had spoken for more than two hours.

Tudor dragged his feet toward the lodging district.

“He said ‘one last thing’ over three hundred times.”

Sancho groaned.

“Even a trainer counting repetitions doesn’t count like that.”

Pigi sighed.

“The tournament hasn’t started and I’m exhausted.”

Bianca looked around.

“Where is our principal, anyway?”

“Absent again,” Sinclaire said. “Professor Banshee was recently promoted to vice principal, so he came as acting representative.”

Then they saw the lodgings.

Fatigue vanished.

“Whoa.”

Buildings shaped like enormous hollow mushrooms clustered at different heights.

Soft, elastic walls.

Narrow winding lanes.

Fireflies drifting like natural street lamps.

A village from a fairy tale.

Inside—

the rooms were spacious and warm.

Long wooden tables were loaded with food.

Grilled local silver fish.

Scallop stew.

Roast pork.

Hard cheese.

Low-alcohol white and red wine.

Unfortunately—

the wine was not unlimited.

Colosseo’s hundred competitors sat by year.

Twenty first-years.

Thirty second-years.

Fifty third-years.

Five students to a table.

Tudor looked around.

“Where’s Vikir?”

Pigi pointed diagonally across the hall.

“There.”

Vikir sat with Granola and the Baskerville triplets.

“Will he be okay?”

Sancho snorted.

“You saw his midterm result.”

Sinclaire nodded.

“And he just pulled me out of trouble with Temisquira. Worry about ourselves.”

They began analyzing the tournament.

All four academies used the same allocation.

Twenty first-years.

Thirty second-years.

Fifty third-years.

One hundred per academy.

Four hundred total.

Historically—

third-years dominated.

Second-years sometimes broke into the middle.

First-years mostly participated for experience.

The top ten—

including every past champion—

had always been third-years.

But last year—

four second-years had broken into the top twenty.

Tudor counted them.

“Bakilaga Juragio.”

“Oraorus Hohenheim.”

“Merelini Lovegood.”

“And President Dolores.”

Now all four were third-years.

Obvious names to watch.

The Colosseo freshmen were less ambitious.

Beat the other first-years.

Build experience.

Carry a good ranking into later years.

“We’re only twenty people selected from the whole first-year class.”

“First stage is the survival contest.”

“Top ten survivors move to individual tasks.”

“And successful individual tasks can lead to artifacts.”

“Which means we avoid the monsters.”

“Bakilaga. Hohenheim. Lovegood.”

Sinclaire remembered the train.

*Lovegood’s pressure alone almost stopped my breathing.*

She began with Bakilaga.

“Twenty-five. Greatsword user. He defeated fourth-years as a first-year.”

Previous tournaments had gone badly for specific reasons.

One year—

same-school seniors had attacked him.

Last year—

more experienced third-years from other schools brought him down.

Even so—

he placed fourteenth.

“At the time, his public level was around the peak of Sword Expert,” Sinclaire said. “Now it’s uncertain. Some rumors say he’s reached Sword Graduator.”

Tudor frowned.

“If his own seniors attacked him, doesn’t that mean people in Varangian hate him?”

“No.”

Sinclaire shook her head.

“That was when he was a first- or second-year. He hates unfair treatment and bad customs. He protected peers and juniors, so he clashed with seniors.”

Now—

as the highest-year class and student council president—

Bakilaga had both power and trust.

Experience.

Teamwork.

The two weaknesses he had once lacked.

Pigi took the next profile.

“Oraorus Hohenheim. Twenty-two.”

Mage Tower student council president.

Baptismal name—

Snub-nosed.

The academy’s strongest current student.

Possibly its best student in a century.

Last year—

thirteenth place as a second-year.

Nearly everyone above him had been third-year.

“President Dolores lost to him in the previous two Leagues,” Pigi said. “Partly matchup.”

Monstrous talent.

Monstrous pride.

Then Bianca brightened.

“I researched Merelini Lovegood.”

Tudor waited.

“She’s beautiful.”

“…And?”

“Very beautiful.”

“Bianca.”

“I want to know how she does it.”

Tudor took over.

“Merelini Lovegood. Twenty-three. Mage. Specializes in plant, earth, poison, and insect magic.”

Eldest daughter of an enormously wealthy family.

Rumored—

not confirmed—

to have reached the fourth circle.

Last year—

sixteenth.

Now a third-year.

Aiming for the championship.

Sancho added—

“She’s also known as an actor in a famous troupe. Might make a formal debut next year.”

He paused.

“I’m a fan.”

The table stared at him.

Then the discussion returned to Dolores.

Last year—

fifteenth.

Could she win now?

Tudor believed she could.

Others pointed toward Bakilaga, Hohenheim, and Lovegood.

For the freshmen—

the answer was simpler.

Learn.

Survive.

Prepare for next year.

And the year after.

Across the room—

one first-year held a different standard.

Vikir.

*No need to overdo it.*

*Top ten is enough.*

The Seventh Corpse.

Decarabia.

The only object-type demon among the Ten Corpses.

At this moment—

it should still be dormant somewhere in the human world.

Waiting to be awakened.

Vikir knew the approximate location.

The treasure vault jointly administered by the four academies.

That was why demonic forces had attacked the academies so repeatedly in his previous life.

If Vikir reached Decarabia first—

and removed it before those forces could—

the future would change.

Students who should have died—

might live.

*The Age of Destruction will be delayed another step.*

One fewer demon.

One less source of power feeding the path toward the Demon World.

Then—

“Excuse me.”

The innkeeper approached.

Deeply apologetic.

“The inn next door has suffered a water-pipe failure.”

His younger brother operated it.

No water meant no guests.

But every other inn was full for the holiday season.

Colosseo had reserved one hundred and twenty single rooms.

The innkeeper bowed repeatedly.

“Would you be willing to double up and use sixty rooms instead?”

Compensation—

only seventy percent of the original lodging charge.

A thirty-percent discount.

Breakfast and lunch included.

Students did not care much about saving school money.

Faculty did.

“We can redirect the budget.”

“Buy teaching materials.”

“I need a new laboratory specimen.”

Banshee considered.

“Only if at least ninety percent of the students agree.”

A secret ballot followed.

Ninety-three percent—

yes.

The innkeeper nearly folded in half bowing.

“Thank you!”

Then he opened the doors.

The displaced guests entered.

Colosseo’s students looked up.

And saw a group—

unexpected.

Or perhaps—

exactly the group they should have expected.
