Seth had been talking to Vikir in a gentle, friendly voice for some time.

“How does it feel to be home after so long? Are you adjusting? Do you need your older brother's help?”

He smiled sympathetically.

“Father and Brother Osiris are rather cold, aren't they? Though they're already treating you much more gently than they treat me.”

A self-deprecating laugh.

“You're young and talented. Someone useless like me gets treated more like a block of ice.”

He pushed a dish toward Vikir.

“Try this. My favorite chef prepared it especially well tonight. It's armored-shark roe baked with cream.”

Then another subject.

“I'll visit your room later. You have to tell me about the Depths. My health is poor, so I'm usually in the medical wing or my closed-training grounds.”

His eyes brightened.

“My training grounds aren't far from Underdog City, where you served as Deputy Magistrate.”

Seth kept talking.

“Speaking of which, the city became much more peaceful because of you. That position was originally supposed to be mine, but with my weak body and lack of talent, an important office like that is a burden.”

He laughed.

“If I had a capable younger brother like you helping with administration, life would be much easier.”

Seth filled every silence so Vikir would not feel awkward.

He even greeted each servant who brought food.

Most Baskerville hounds would have been moved by that kindness.

Before regression—

Vikir had been.

Now he only watched Seth's smiling face.

“…”

Seth had always seemed good-hearted.

Always attentive to the people around him.

That softness earned him little respect from Hugo or Osiris.

The younger Vikir had liked him for exactly that reason.

But the Vikir who returned from the Age of Destruction had learned to see with different eyes.

**Seth Le Baskerville is suspected of not being human.**

Cindywendy's line returned to him.

He did not actually need the report to remember it.

A revolting stench had been pouring from Seth's mouth since he sat down.

Rotting flesh.

Dormant organs.

A corpse left too long in a sealed room.

Vikir, who could eat almost anything without losing his appetite, had to control his expression.

No one else reacted.

They could not smell it.

Vikir knew why.

*Demonic scent.*

Only someone who had lived through the Age of Destruction would recognize it immediately.

Before regression, the stench of demons had become so common that Vikir barely registered it.

Here, in a comparatively peaceful age—

it was unmistakable.

Thrum—

Even Beelzebub stirred inside his blood.

The soul-remnant of Madam Eight-Legs sealed within the weapon's orb seemed disturbed as well.

*Why does Seth smell like this?*

Vikir kept his face calm.

He lifted his wineglass slightly.

Its surface reflected the table.

Hugo.

Osiris.

Seth.

No.

Only two people.

Hugo.

Osiris.

Seth's reflection was absent.

*There it is.*

Vikir's thoughts sharpened.

Creatures from the demonic realm were broadly divided into two kinds.

Demonic beasts were monsters.

Demons were higher, intelligent beings.

The distinction was similar to that between beasts and humans.

Ordinary demonic beasts appeared in mirrors and water.

Higher demons did not naturally cast such reflections.

This was knowledge humanity would eventually purchase at a terrible price during the Age of Destruction.

For now—

almost no one knew it.

Vikir did.

*You became careless, Seth.*

He drained the wine.

Whatever stood before him wore Seth's body.

What remained inside was another question entirely.

Vikir recalled old testimonies.

*Young Master Seth? He's a good person. As a child he used to be cold and blunt like the other Baskervilles, but he changed after… that unfortunate incident.*

*What incident? Well, don't repeat this. The night the Patriarch harshly scolded him for his lack of talent, Young Master Seth tried to take his own life.*

*They found him soon enough to save him. Afterward, his personality became much gentler. Maybe facing death changed him.*

Long ago, Vikir had accidentally started a small fire in Baskerville's great library.

He had protected the panicked servants from blame.

Many of them trusted him afterward.

Those stories had come from them.

Now the pieces arranged themselves differently.

*The real Seth may have died that night.*

A demon could have entered the empty shell.

Another piece followed.

Demons valued the blood of human children.

Cindywendy's investigation into Underdog's disappearances had gone far beyond ordinary kidnapping.

Children vanished from orphanages and charitable foundations.

On paper, many had been adopted.

Officially or unofficially.

Then they disappeared completely.

Records said they existed.

Reality did not.

Cindywendy used money and information networks to trace the adoption routes.

Every trail eventually converged near one place.

Seth Le Baskerville's closed-training grounds.

Not far from Underdog City.

Cindywendy had even taken control of a child-care foundation and used donations as an excuse to inspect adoption records.

Some of what she uncovered required decidedly illegal methods.

Vikir had listened carefully.

Because this was tied directly to his death before regression.

Demons drank the blood of children.

In Vikir's previous life, while he served Seth, a cache of children's bloodstained bones had been discovered in a cellar near Vikir's room.

The evidence had been turned against him.

He was accused of colluding with demons.

Executed.

Then sent back into his past.

*Maybe…*

A thought Vikir would once have rejected entered his mind.

*Maybe Hugo truly did not know.*

The possibility remained small.

But it was no longer zero.

Perhaps Hugo had acted on brutal instinct and false evidence.

Perhaps Seth had manipulated the entire situation.

Vikir's expression hardened.

*The real question is how much of Seth's soul remains.*

A stench this strong suggested almost nothing.

Resentment.

Hatred.

Inferiority.

A sense of being unloved.

Self-destruction.

If the true Seth had died drowning in those emotions, they could have opened his soul to something from the other side.

Vikir touched his chin.

Before regression, Seth had been the only master he willingly served.

Now Vikir was considering that Seth may have orchestrated the very betrayal that led to Vikir's execution.

The realization did not shake him.

It made his blood feel cold.

Seth kept talking, unaware.

“So, little brother, which knight order do you like?”

He smiled.

“I've always thought the Wolfhound Knights looked good. Of course the Pit Bulls and Mastiffs are strong too.”

Another grin.

“When I was young, I personally liked the Doberman Knights. Their uniforms looked wonderful.”

“…”

“Now that you're home, what will you do? Enter the Academy? Brother Osiris did.”

Seth leaned closer.

“Or will you return to Underdog as Deputy Magistrate? I heard the citizens even demonstrated to have you appointed permanently.”

His smile widened.

“When I return to the Magistrate's position, perhaps we can work together.”

Vikir stabbed a piece of meat with his fork.

The stench from Seth's breath made his brow tighten.

Then—

“Enough.”

Osiris cut him off.

Seth fell silent.

Osiris's voice remained cold.

“You haven't even finished eating.”

“…”

“Stop distracting our brother with idle chatter. Eat your meal.”

Seth lowered his head.

Vikir could finally breathe without the demonic stench washing directly over him.

He leaned back slightly and reconsidered the two brothers.

Before regression, he would have seen only cruelty.

Cold, arrogant Osiris bullying gentle Seth.

The servants around the table still seemed to see exactly that.

Their eyes showed sympathy for Seth.

But now Vikir understood another possibility.

Osiris kept an instinctive distance from Seth.

He repeatedly pushed other siblings away from him as well.

Whenever Seth approached, Osiris frowned as though sensing something foul.

Perhaps his extraordinary instincts recognized what his conscious mind could not.

Hugo behaved similarly.

*That would also explain why they disliked me so much before regression.*

Vikir had served Seth.

He looked at Osiris again.

Cold.

Blunt.

Awkward.

But beneath it—

human.

More human than Vikir had remembered.

Osiris was also helping Vikir survive his first Great Banquet in his own severe way.

“Vikir. You're holding the third knife incorrectly.”

A little later—

“Tilt the plate in the other direction. Don't scrape the utensils.”

Then—

“Your table manners need work. The napkin folds the other way.”

They sounded like scoldings.

In practice, Osiris was correcting mistakes before Hugo or one of the senior relatives could notice them.

He criticized only what was actually wrong.

Once Vikir fixed something, Osiris never mentioned it again.

A normal child might still have shrunk beneath that tone.

Vikir was not a normal child.

After three corrections, he reproduced the etiquette perfectly.

Osiris finally gave a faint, satisfied smile.

“Better.”

He looked Vikir over.

“Now you look like a noble.”

That clumsy warmth seemed a little clearer.

◆◆◆

The Great Banquet finally ended.

Its appetizers alone had taken two hours.

The entire meal lasted nearly five.

Midnight was approaching when Vikir prepared to leave.

“…Wait.”

Hugo called him back.

“I have something important to tell you, son.”

“Yes, Father.”

Vikir stood before him.

Hugo studied him.

Then changed his mind.

“No.”

A pause.

“It is late. Come see me tomorrow morning.”

“Do you have another engagement after midnight?”

“No.”

Hugo answered simply.

“You may be tired.”

Vikir stared.

Hugo was postponing business because *Vikir might be tired*?

The same Hugo who, if he considered something necessary, would light the entire castle and drag everyone from bed at midnight or dawn?

Several servants quietly drew the curtains over the western windows.

Apparently they wanted to be prepared in case the sun rose from that direction tomorrow.

Even Vikir struggled not to react.

“…”

He bowed calmly.

After Hugo left, only Vikir and Barrymore remained.

The old butler smiled.

“I believe my lord wishes to discuss your Academy admission.”

Barrymore bowed his head.

“Congratulations in advance, Young Master.”

“How do you know?”

“The Academy sent its official notice for the incoming class last night.”

Colosseo Academy.

A new road was opening.
