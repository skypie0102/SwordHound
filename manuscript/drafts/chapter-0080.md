# Chapter 80: The Saintess (3)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/080.txt` — `207f6ca71403141a65fa7b56706adab81cc1d37d`. Recovered English Chapter 79 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Medical and sanitation material remains source-world fiction. Dolores is sixteen; descriptions of admiration and social attention remain nonsexual.

Time passed.

The number of patients continued rising.

Dolores sat alone for a few minutes and thought.

*Healing one person at a time is only a temporary solution.*

Divine power could cure existing patients.

It could not stop new cases while the source remained contaminated.

The slum officially contained forty-two wells.

There were also unregistered wells dug privately by residents.

Most connected to the same groundwater.

Purifying one bucket at a time would accomplish nothing.

Quovadis needed to cleanse the source itself.

One sufficiently deep access point.

One massive concentration of divine power.

A purification essence compressed down to the size of a pill and dissolved into the water.

If it worked, underground flow would carry the blessing throughout the district.

Quovadis priests began trials immediately.

Phase 1a.

Then onward.

All the way through Phase 3c.

While they gathered power and tested the purification essence, Dolores continued treating the sick.

*This disease is horrible.*

Even a cured patient could relapse.

Incubation was short.

Death came slowly.

The design seemed almost deliberate—

as though someone had taken the cruelest qualities of historic plagues and combined them.

Long suffering.

Maximum pain.

Pure malice.

Dolores stood again.

The room tilted for an instant.

She caught herself.

Even her naturally immense reserves were being exhausted.

Her mild anemia did not help.

*I can still move.*

She waited until a little divine power returned.

Then left the tent.

There were always more patients.

◆◆◆

Men were waiting outside.

Young nobles.

Heirs of rich families.

Several were the same visitors Quovadis had refused earlier.

“The Saintess!”

“We came to help!”

“You no longer need to worry about anything!”

“Runmen!”

Dolores stopped.

“Why are you here?”

The answer was obvious.

They wanted her attention.

Some had brought three wagons of relief supplies.

Others loudly announced that they would enter even the poorest district if Dolores were there.

The competition escalated.

They spoke about the slum as though visiting a foreign exhibition.

“I've only read about places like this.”

“Interesting. They still live almost normally.”

“The children are dressed better than I expected. Are these people really that poor?”

“Perhaps giving too much aid destroys their willingness to help themselves.”

Dolores said nothing.

She kept walking.

They took her silence as permission to follow.

At the medical tents, she entered immediately.

The young men stopped.

“There are plague victims in there.”

“I am my family's only heir.”

“The Saintess can heal us if something happens.”

“The brave man wins the beauty. I'm going.”

Some turned around.

Others forced themselves inside.

The smell struck first.

Sweat.

Blood.

Vomit.

Urine.

Feces.

Unwashed bodies.

Fever-heated air.

Groans came from every direction.

The relief tent felt like a living grave.

Then a nearby patient lost control of his bowels.

Liquid waste struck the floor and splashed onto expensive shoes and trousers.

The visitors broke.

“Disgusting!”

“My shoes!”

“Priest! Heal me first!”

“Move! I'm leaving!”

They fled before they had spent more than moments inside.

Dolores watched them go.

“……As expected.”

This happened often.

Her appearance attracted attention at the academy and outside it.

Men found excuses to accompany her volunteer work.

Then they met actual suffering.

Disease.

Filth.

Fear.

People who could not repay them.

And the performance of compassion disappeared.

Dolores distrusted admiration that lasted only while she was clean, distant, and beautiful.

A relief ward was not a stage for courtship.

It was a place where people were fighting for their lives.

Absolute good.

Selflessness.

Sacrifice.

Service.

Kindness.

Mercy.

Those were the things she believed brought a person closer to divine grace.

She went deeper.

The smell worsened.

So did the heat.

The cries.

The suffering.

At the deepest quarantine area, even veteran priests struggled to continue.

Dolores expected despair.

Instead she heard shouting.

“More saline!”

“Divine power cannot solve every problem. Bring the scalpel!”

“……How is he doing that?”

The priests here were exhausted—

but alive with purpose.

At the center moved one black-clad man.

Filth covered him.

He did not care.

Patients cursed at him through fever and pain.

He kept working.

Checking symptoms.

Giving emergency care.

Directing priests.

Moving to the next patient.

The **Hound of the Night** had reached the hardest part of the ward before she had.
