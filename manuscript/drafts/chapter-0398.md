The box opened.

A pillar of silver light rose from inside.

A rank badge.

Silver embroidery—

a Rose of Sharon.

The insignia of a **Major**.

“…This?”

Vikir looked up.

Lieutenant Colonel Bastille smiled warmly.

“Your construction progress has exceeded one hundred percent every month.”

“And last month—

you finally reached two hundred percent.”

“Thanks to my colleagues.”

“Hahahaha!”

“Still humble.”

“I like that.”

“But I cannot leave a subordinate who produces two or three times more than everyone else—

as a mere junior guard.”

A guard was judged by the ability to control prisoners.

Assign labor.

Deliver results.

Vikir had proven all of it.

And Bastille had reasons of his own.

He belonged directly to D'Ordume’s line.

D'Ordume—

at this moment—

was pouring everything into Nouvelle Vague’s great expansion project.

**Level Ten.**

Bastille’s own prospects were tied to its success.

Vikir had removed a major disruption.

And his work output dwarfed everyone else’s.

Of course Bastille favored him.

“You are not someone who should spend his career directing only twelve low-level prisoners.”

“Even the great commanders who once conquered half the continent during the Warring States Era—

began by protecting grain warehouses.”

“….”

“I’m curious.”

“What will happen when you command many guards—

and the many prisoners beneath them?”

“Perhaps my decision today will bring Level Ten’s completion date forward dramatically.”

Bastille was an experienced political operator.

He saw Vikir as a useful key to finishing the project early.

That suited Vikir perfectly.

“I will give everything I have—

and change Nouvelle Vague.”

For once—

the statement was completely honest.

Bastille smiled broadly.

CLINK.

He pinned the silver-and-black insignia to Vikir’s chest.

Thus—

**Captain Garam Nord** became **Major Garam Nord**.

The rise had taken less than a year—

excluding the original Garam’s junior-service period.

“Hahaha.”

“I wasn’t promoted this quickly when I was young.”

“In recent decades—

only D'Ordume and Souaré advanced at comparable speed.”

“And that was early in their careers.”

“I’ll work harder.”

“You should.”

“I trust you now.”

“I’m watching you.”

“You must have suffered—

never having the opportunity to show your ability before.”

“If you think about it—

perhaps that dead prisoner was lucky for you.”

“No.”

“I never thought that.”

“Hahaha!”

“I did.”

“He was lucky for **me**.”

“He gave me the chance to discover a capable subordinate.”

Vikir looked down at the insignia.

The badge itself was valuable.

A piece of BDISSEM—

silver-plated.

But the real value was what the rank meant.

Major.

The end of the junior officer class.

The threshold of senior-guard authority.

A Major received privileges unavailable below that rank.

**1. Within Level Three, freely requisition up to 108 prisoners and assign labor at personal discretion.**

**2. Within Level Seven, summarily execute one prisoner guilty of severe insubordination—such as refusing labor or attacking a guard.**

**3. Enter and leave any labor zone at any time without separate orders or reporting to a superior.**

**4. With superior approval, enter the Breeding Grounds, Execution Grounds, and Punishment Rooms.**

Vikir cared most about the first and third.

Prisoners—

at his discretion.

Labor zones—

whenever he wanted.

*These are ideal conditions for activating Poseidon.*

There was no need to climb to Lieutenant Colonel.

Major was enough.

All essential permissions.

Less scrutiny.

More freedom.

Bastille’s expression became serious.

“A Major’s privileges come with Major responsibilities.”

Authority meant obligations.

Construction targets.

Number of prisoner incidents.

Suppression times.

Ratings from superiors.

Ratings from subordinates.

Vikir did not care.

*There isn’t much time left anyway.*

The countdown had already begun.

His second-stage plan—

conducted from inside the guard hierarchy—

was approaching completion.

Then Bastille said:

“Now that you’re a Major—

you may select new adjutants.”

“Up to three commissioned officers.”

The surrounding guards immediately looked interested.

No one saw Garam as an idiot anymore.

Vikir—

however—

did not hesitate.

“Kirko Grimm alone is enough.”

Silence.

“…?”

Vikir looked around.

Then at Kirko.

Her head was lowered.

Her ears—

red.

Bastille laughed.

“Hahahaha!”

“A prison romance.”

“That’s not necessarily bad.”

“Just be careful about certain complications.”

He looked at Kirko strangely.

Then murmured—

almost to himself:

“…A prison romance.”

“Strange fate.”

“Very strange.”

“Children really do resemble their parents.”

There was something like pity in his voice.

Then he shook his head—

as though he had said nothing.

“Lieutenant Kirko Grimm.”

“A Lieutenant is a little too junior to serve as a Major’s adjutant.”

“I’ll work twice as hard!”

“You already do.”

“Your monthly evaluations have always been excellent.”

“You showed courage during the Level Eight riot.”

“And you performed well assisting Major Garam during the execution.”

Bastille patted Kirko’s shoulder.

“So I prepared a reward for you too.”

Another box.

Inside—

three diamonds.

The insignia of a **Captain**.

“You gained valuable experience through this incident.”

“You learned a great deal.”

“A capable superior paired with a capable subordinate.”

“That’s good.”

“Work well together.”

“And remember—

promotions like yours are extremely unusual.”

“Yes!”

Vikir and Kirko answered together.

**Major Garam.**

**Captain Kirko.**

They had both advanced.

Around them—

junior guards watched with admiration.

Envy.

Respect.

Then—

“…Lieutenant Colonel Bastille.”

A Major nearby spoke nervously.

Bastille turned.

“What?”

“An order came down from above.”

“Above?”

“D'Ordume?”

“Souaré?”

“…Neither.”

Bastille’s face stiffened.

“Is this about the exceptional promotions?”

“Yes.”

“Lieutenant Colonel **Black Tongue** has taken an interest.”

Bastille covered his face with one hand.

“…What strange idea does that lunatic have now?”

“Good fortune rarely comes without complications.”

“Hm.”

“Militarily—

there is nothing improper.”

“Still.”

“Unusual promotions often attract inspection.”

Vikir listened.

*Black Tongue.*

A Lieutenant Colonel with no known personal name.

Only the unpleasant title.

One of Nouvelle Vague’s Five Wardens.

A member of the **Audit Team**.

Except—

the Audit Team consisted of exactly one person.

Black Tongue.

Another nickname followed him.

**The guard who punishes guards.**

Nouvelle Vague was too remote for ordinary surface law.

So the prison maintained its own laws.

Its own punishments.

For prisoners—

and for guards.

Black Tongue was unusual because his work focused not on prisoners—

but on prison staff.

Corruption.

Discipline violations.

Internal misconduct.

He investigated.

Judged.

Punished.

Among the guards—

he was uniquely feared.

Yet ordinary junior personnel rarely encountered him.

In that sense—

he was even less known than Flubber.

Vikir thought.

*Black Tongue.*

*Good.*

*I wanted to meet him anyway.*

Unlike BDISSEM and Flubber—

Black Tongue was not operationally essential to Vikir’s escape plan.

Vikir’s reason for wanting to meet him—

was personal.

Then—

grind.

A faint sound.

Teeth.

Vikir turned.

Kirko stood beside him.

Face blank.

But faint veins had risen along her neck and jaw.

She had been the one grinding her teeth.

Before Vikir could ask why—

“Major Garam.”

“Captain Kirko.”

Bastille spoke again.

“You must report to the interrogation room immediately.”

“Your promotions happened too quickly.”

“The audit division has questions.”

“Your identities are clear.”

“Your records are clean.”

“Your achievements are substantial.”

“So there should not be a major problem.”

He hesitated.

“But…”

His voice lowered.

“Lieutenant Colonel Black Tongue.”

“Personally—

I advise extreme caution around him.”

“Remember that.”

The good mood vanished.

Guards who had looked envious moments earlier—

now watched Garam and Kirko with sympathy.

Unease.

Vikir remained calm.

“As ordered.”

“We’ll go now.”

The hound’s eyes did not show fear.

If anything—

they showed interest.
