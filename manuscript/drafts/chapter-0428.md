Sinclaire.

Among Colosseo Academy’s twentieth class—

only Vikir had stood clearly above her.

In the history Vikir originally knew—

Sinclaire vanished from the world after graduation.

Now—

she had returned to her classmates.

“Move!”

Sinclaire shouted at Bianca.

Then raised one hand.

CRACK!

The mountain itself split.

A buried gold vein tore out of the earth.

Stone strata broke free and floated into the air.

Gold ore melted inside a field of intense magic.

Then changed shape.

One hand.

Ten.

A hundred.

A thousand.

Countless golden fists formed behind Sinclaire.

A great wheel of mana spun at her back.

Above her head—

the **Money Hat** finally showed what it could really do.

The artifact increased its wearer’s magical power according to the amount of wealth available to them.

And Sinclaire—

now formally acknowledged as a Bourgeois successor—

had access to a fortune beyond ordinary imagination.

She had also become substantially stronger through the Hell Tree ordeal.

Earth magic.

Metal magic.

Both answered her at once.

BOOM!

The golden fists hit the Invincible Cavalry.

Horses fell.

Knights rolled across the ground.

The encirclement loosened immediately.

Bianca did not waste the opening.

She lifted Tudor.

Ran.

Then leapt toward safer ground.

Sinclaire followed—

staying behind them.

SPEARS flew toward Bianca.

Golden fists knocked them away.

For several moments—

Sinclaire held the entire pursuit force back.

Bianca finally looked at her.

“…Your family?”

“I left it to Uncle Damien.”

Sinclaire lowered her head.

“This isn’t the best time to say it…”

“But I was worried about all of you.”

There had once been a time when Sinclaire and Bianca were inseparable.

Roommates.

Classmates.

Friends.

Sinclaire’s original plan had been simple.

Graduate from Colosseo with overwhelming results.

Prove herself.

Then compete with her cousin Juliet for the Bourgeois headship.

But Juliet died.

And Sinclaire found herself increasingly drawn toward Vikir.

Everything became confused.

So she withdrew.

Left the Academy.

Cut contact.

Tried to remove relationships that made her hesitate.

Then the Hell Tree happened.

A place where classmates were told that only one might survive.

A place where Sinclaire had expected betrayal—

and instead received trust.

Her friends had been prepared to die so the most talented among them could live.

No accusations.

No punishment.

Only acceptance.

Sinclaire had been ashamed.

She remembered another voice too.

Vikir.

**Decide for yourself.**

**What you see.**

**What you hear.**

**What you believe.**

Those words returned after the vault.

After Bartolomeo.

After Belial.

The father whose recognition she had chased all her life—

had already become a demon’s puppet.

Her goal of becoming House Bourgeois’s head—

once tied so tightly to earning that father’s recognition—

lost its meaning.

So what had she truly wanted?

What should she believe?

What should she pursue now?

Sinclaire thought for a long time.

Then chose.

And that choice—

had brought her here.

“I’m sorry.”

“I misunderstood you.”

“Please…”

“Let me be your friend again.”

Her voice trembled.

The golden wall did not.

Bianca looked back.

Sinclaire was sweating heavily.

Blood ran from the corner of her mouth.

The spell was tearing at her body.

“If you can forgive me—

I’ll do anything—”

She never finished.

“OH, COME ON!”

Bianca grabbed Sinclaire by the collar—

and yanked her backward.

BOOM!

The golden barrier collapsed.

The Invincible Cavalry surged through.

Sinclaire had still bought several priceless seconds.

SPEARS followed.

Bianca carried her across the rocks.

One spear cut strands from Bianca’s hair.

Another missed by inches.

Sinclaire looked at her.

“Bianca…”

Bianca shouted back.

“Idiot!”

“We were already friends!”

Tears filled Sinclaire’s eyes.

The two had shared a room from their first year.

Then parted bitterly.

Sinclaire had hidden her family.

Vanished without explanation.

Ignored old friends.

For a young friendship—

it had felt like betrayal.

Now—

none of that mattered as much.

They clasped hands.

Stronger than before.

“Sorry I’m late.”

“….”

“Sorry I left school without telling you.”

“….”

“Sorry I deliberately ignored you.”

“….”

“Sorry I hid that I was Bourgeois.”

“….”

“And inside the Hell Tree—”

“Enough!”

“I can’t breathe if I have to answer every apology!”

Bianca’s usual arrogant composure disappeared.

Both girls were crying.

Neither looked dignified.

Then a roar came from behind.

“How dare you go after my friend!”

“I won’t forgive that!”

“I’ll pay you back double!”

Tudor—

after emergency treatment from Dolores, Sancho, and Pigi—

had returned to his feet.

“Bianca!”

“Tudor!”

The two clasped hands for a moment—

then immediately turned toward the pursuit force.

Sancho and Pigi smiled at Sinclaire.

“So the **Wealthy of the Night** finally arrived?”

“Wouldn’t **Golden King of the Night** sound better?”

Sinclaire laughed through tears.

Together.

That was what she had been missing.

Then Pigi muttered:

“…Still.”

“It’s a shame one person is missing.”

Everyone froze.

Only briefly.

Tudor answered first.

“He’ll come back.”

Sancho nodded.

“He’s the last person I can imagine dying.”

Pigi swallowed.

“…Right?”

Bianca snorted.

“He’s probably healthier than any of us.”

Sinclaire clenched one fist.

“We’ll bring Bro back.”

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Sinclaire.

All turned toward the senior they trusted most.

Dolores.

“Ready!”

“The prayer is complete!”

White holy power spread outward like a dome.

The Donquixote knights shuddered.

Cloudiness disappeared from some eyes.

Their movements slowed.

Tudor shouted.

“It works!”

Dolores frowned.

“No.”

“This is wrong.”

“The control is too strong.”

“This is power at the level of the **Ten Corpses**.”

The culprit channeling that influence—

stood in front of her.

Pedro.

“Kahahaha!”

“As long as I’m here—

that lord’s influence will not disappear!”

Demonic energy spilled from him.

As Pedro approached—

the cavalry’s eyes clouded again.

Dolores bit her lip.

“…Then there’s no choice.”

She brought both hands together.

Prayed.

Light gathered.

Warm.

Gentle.

Pedro laughed.

“Prayer?”

“Holy spells?”

“Light can only make people feel warm—”

A huge shadow covered his face.

“…What?”

Dolores reached behind herself.

And pulled out—

a hammer.

Large.

Beautiful.

Extremely heavy.

One of Pope Nabokov I’s favorite possessions.

A weapon considerably closer to **physical force**—

than divine intervention.
