**Dolores.**

The anti–Night Hound column made her think of Vikir again.

> **Evil Beyond the Line — ‘Night Hound’**
>
> The Night Hound has crossed the line.
>
> His crimes against the Imperial House and Seven Great Houses have reached the heavens.
>
> Surrender now.
>
> It may be the last chance for a peaceful end.

Reading the attacks on the Night Hound—

made Dolores feel sick.

Hearing Vikir repeat the same condemnation—

made it worse.

“A villain like the Night Hound will be punished someday.”

The words hurt more than hearing someone insult Dolores herself.

*You don't know him.*

The thought came hot and immediate.

Then—

she stopped herself.

Ordinary people could not be blamed for misunderstanding something they had never seen.

Long ago—

Rune himself had been condemned by the people he tried to save.

The Night Hound—

in Dolores's eyes—

was enduring the same kind of misunderstanding.

*True heroes don't force the public to bleed for them.*

Dolores bit her lip.

She could walk down the stairs.

Scold her juniors.

Defend the Night Hound.

But—

that was probably not what he wanted.

*It's all right.*

*Even if no one else knows what he sacrificed—*

*I do.*

*I can remember.*

A genius the world did not understand.

A prophet no one recognized.

Dolores felt something almost like a duty—

because she believed she alone truly understood him.

And as public hatred—

fear—

disgust—

toward the Night Hound grew—

Dolores's own feelings became more complicated.

Attachment.

Respect.

Longing.

A painful emotion she could not name.

They intensified enough—

that lately—

sleep did not come easily.

Every day brought a feeling she had never experienced before.

“….”

Dolores released the railing.

Turned away.

She had come to speak with Vikir.

But if she looked at him now—

she might become angry.

And she had no right to be angry at him for not knowing the truth.

Not a good moment.

So she left.

◆◆◆

After showering—

Dolores sat at the desk in her dormitory room.

*Still.*

*I need to talk to Vikir.*

Among Academy students—

Vikir was supposedly the only one who had personally seen the Night Hound at Indulgentia.

She needed to know what he saw.

There was another reason too.

“…I need to apologize for the accident.”

Vikir had helped her.

When Dolores lost control while drunk—

he had accepted the humiliation publicly.

Everyone had believed *he* was the one who wet himself.

Admitting that in front of a crowd—

must have been difficult.

Dolores did not know why he had done it.

But afterward—

he asked for nothing.

Never boasted.

Never demanded repayment.

That made the gesture seem—

to her—

like simple kindness.

“…If only he would stop insulting the Night Hound.”

“He really is a good junior.”

Her feelings toward Vikir were complicated too.

At first—

she had barely cared about him.

Then—

disliked him.

An arrogant junior.

At the freshman talent show—

his military song had moved elderly professors to tears.

That made him seem unusual.

Then came the tardiness.

Demerits.

Sleeping during lectures.

Restricted zones.

Warning after warning.

Dolores opened his discipline record.

> **Vikir — Conduct / Attitude Demerits**
>
> Used emergency exit on the third-year dormitory floor — **-1**
>
> Entered the fourth-year-only section of the practice grounds — **-1**
>
> Used the central stairs on the first floor of the poison laboratory building — **-1**
>
> Entered the controlled zone of the experimental monster-breeding facility — **-1**
>
> Used the central stairs on the sixth floor of the faculty research building — **-1**
>
> Used the central stairs on the third floor of a Hot Department-only practical-training building — **-1**
>
> Entered the physical training room outside operating hours — **-1**
>
> Entered a restricted zone beside the cafeteria food warehouse — **-1**

Dolores stared at the list.

It was still astonishing.

How did someone accumulate this many demerits immediately after entering school?

For Dolores—

who had always followed rules—

it was almost unimaginable.

*Especially the monster-breeding control area.*

*That should have been three points.*

She had reduced it to one.

A small act of favoritism—

because Vikir was her junior in the same club.

Even Dolores had a tendency to protect people closer to her.

Still—

as the violations accumulated—

Vikir's reputation had settled firmly into *problem student*.

But—

*It would be unfair to see only that.*

At Indulgentia—

he worked harder than almost anyone.

Toilets.

Dining hall.

Plumbing.

Laundry.

Playroom.

Playground.

Dirty work.

Boring work.

Work no one praised.

While many privileged Academy students struggled with even one exhausting task—

Vikir quietly performed the work of five or six.

No boasting.

No demand for recognition.

Then—

at the end—

he jumped into sewage to retrieve what everyone thought was a child's toy.

Returned it covered in filth.

Who willingly did that for someone else?

And that was not the only time Vikir sacrificed himself.

*People make mistakes when they're drunk.*

The words returned to Dolores.

What Vikir had said when he took the blame for her.

He had said it—

clearly—

for her.

Dolores's face heated.

“Even when I explain it—”

“no one believes me.”

She had repeatedly insisted that *she* had caused the accident.

Everyone laughed.

Impossible.

Instead—

they praised Dolores for trying to sacrifice her own reputation to protect Vikir.

The more she corrected them—

the better Dolores looked.

And the worse Vikir looked.

The guilt had become unbearable.

“Haa…”

She exhaled.

She needed to apologize.

Thank him.

For the work at the orphanage.

For taking the humiliation onto himself.

Yet—

every time Vikir insulted the Night Hound—

anger returned.

Because to Dolores—

the Night Hound had become one of the noblest people she had ever known.

“But…”

“I can't defend him recklessly in front of someone who doesn't know anything.”

She pressed a hand to her forehead.

Then reached a conclusion.

The Night Hound—

and Vikir—

were separate matters.

She owed Vikir an apology.

And gratitude.

She also needed to ask him what he knew.

*Public is public.*

*Private is private.*

*Apologize where I should apologize.*

*Thank him where I should thank him.*

Dolores stood.

Then hesitated.

“I can't show up empty-handed.”

“And I'm asking for information.”

“What does Vikir need?”

Dolores rarely owed anyone favors.

She discovered—

to her frustration—

that she had no idea how to repay one.

Then—

a familiar round figure approached.

**Pigi.**

The junior who was almost always near Vikir.

“Pigi.”

“Do you know where Vikir is?”

“Ah!”

“President!”

Pigi straightened.

“Vikir should be at the practice range.”

“He's training for the practical evaluation.”

“Why…?”

Dolores immediately looked away.

“No particular reason.”

“I was going to check on the club juniors before the exams.”

She handed him a packet.

A first-year written-exam study guide—

notes Dolores had prepared by hand when she herself was a freshman.

Pigi looked moved.

“I'll study it with Vikir!”

“Thank you!”

“No need.”

Dolores pushed another copy toward him.

“Give Vikir his own.”

“I made plenty.”

“Oh.”

“All right.”

Pigi pointed.

“Cross that wooded path.”

“Go over the hill.”

“There's a practice range.”

“Vikir should be there.”

It was used by the archery class.

At this hour—

students should be in free practice.

*Vikir uses a bow?*

Dolores nodded.

Then started toward the range.

All the way there—

she rehearsed what she should say first.
