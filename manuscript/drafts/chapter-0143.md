# Chapter 143: Identity of the Villain (3)

Canonical/project terminology controls Vikir, Pigi, Tudor Donquixote, Sancho Barataria, Dolores L. Quovadis, Professor Banshee Morgue, Cold Department, Hot Department, Quovadis, Cindywendy, and Quilt Rune Indulgentia. Another morning arrived.

At six—

the wake-up call sounded through the dormitories.

Students rubbed sleep from their eyes and shuffled toward the field in front of the residence halls.

The field had been divided in half.

Girls on one side.

Boys on the other.

Everyone wore the same gray training clothes.

An instructor stood at the front.

The students followed the morning calisthenics one movement at a time—

shaking the last traces of sleep from their bodies in the cold air.

When the exercises ended—

the crowd scattered.

Some students went back to bed.

Some headed directly for the bathhouse.

Others went straight to breakfast.

A number of girls immediately began the long process of getting ready for the day.

Colosseo's bathing facilities were extensive.

Cold pools.

Hot pools.

Even saunas.

After passing through a changing room thick with the smell of damp clothes and sweat—

sinking into hot water warmed the body enough to chase away both the morning chill and lingering drowsiness.

It also brought back the appetite.

As roommates—

Vikir and Pigi went straight to the bathhouse after morning exercise.

“Ugh… the air is freezing at this hour.”

Pigi looked half-dead.

“I wish they'd stop scheduling calisthenics at dawn. If they let us sleep a little longer, our brains would probably work better…”

Pigi loved sleep.

Every morning exercise session left him complaining as though his body were falling apart.

Vikir listened without much reaction and walked beside him.

Then two familiar students spotted them.

“Hey!”

Tudor and Sancho waved.

The two of them shared a room as well.

“Vikir, Pigi. Straight to the bath again today?”

“Yeah.”

Pigi nodded.

“You have to come immediately after exercise. If you wait until after breakfast, the showers are packed and you have to stand in line.”

Tudor and Sancho followed them inside.

After washing—

all four entered the hot pool.

Tudor leaned back.

“What are you doing after this? Going straight to breakfast?”

Pigi shook his head.

“I don't really have an appetite today. I might skip breakfast and go finish the assignment I haven't done yet.”

Sancho had a different plan.

“I was thinking of sleeping a little more and eating just before the dining hall closes. They give you more leftovers around then.”

He glanced around.

“What are they serving today, anyway?”

Pigi, Tudor, and Sancho began debating the best use of the morning.

Then Vikir spoke.

“I'm going straight to breakfast.”

That was Vikir.

He followed a fixed routine.

Other people's plans did not change his own.

Pigi, Tudor, and Sancho thought about it.

Then—

“I'll eat too.”

Pigi changed his mind first.

“Hmm. If I skip breakfast, I'll be starving by lunch.”

Tudor nodded.

“Fine. I'm eating too!”

Sancho shrugged.

“Then I'll go as well. Food tastes better when everyone eats together.”

Without meaning to—

Vikir's habits were becoming a standard for the people around him.

Exactly ten minutes after entering the bath—

Vikir stood.

He rinsed off.

Changed.

Left.

Pigi, Tudor, and Sancho followed.

On the way out—

Tudor tilted his head.

“That's strange.”

“What is?”

Sancho asked.

Tudor grinned.

“When I'm around Vikir, I naturally end up standing next to him.”

“Really?”

“Yeah.”

Tudor glanced at Vikir's back.

“It's like having a reliable guide.”

“Or maybe an older brother.”

“Maybe it's because I don't have one at home.”

Sancho considered that.

“I can see it.”

“He moves on schedule like a machine.”

“If I move with him, my own routine stays organized too.”

“That's worth learning from.”

Tudor laughed.

“I'd like to get closer to him, but he's so serious that he's hard to approach.”

“Time will solve that.”

Sancho shrugged.

“We have four years together.”

Walking behind them—

Pigi had been listening.

He felt the same way.

*Vikir really is amazing.*

Tudor came from the prestigious Donquixote family.

Sancho was a scholarship student backed by the Northern Mercenary Guild Alliance.

Yet both of them wanted to become closer to Vikir.

He also ranked at the top academically.

He attracted attention from girls.

And when Pigi had frozen during the freshman talent show—

Vikir had helped him without hesitation.

*I'm lucky I got him as my roommate.*

Pigi clenched a fist.

*Then I should become the kind of roommate who makes Vikir think he was lucky to meet me too.*

Just as he silently made that resolution—

“Hey, Pigi.”

Vikir stopped ahead.

He turned.

Tudor and Sancho stopped with him.

“H-Huh?”

Pigi looked up.

Vikir gestured for him to hurry.

“Why are you walking so slowly?”

“Come on.”

“We said we'd work on the morning assignment together.”

Pigi's face brightened instantly.

“Right! I'm coming!”

He hurried forward.

“My leg just hurt for a second…”

Vikir's eyes moved down.

“Your leg?”

“Did you injure it?”

“No! It's fine now! It doesn't hurt at all!”

Pigi ran over with a satisfied smile and took his place beside Vikir.

Tudor and Sancho immediately joined in.

“Did you hurt it during exercise? Where?”

“Don't ignore even a small injury.”

Sancho spoke seriously.

“I brought medicinal herb extract from the north. I'll give you some. The salve is good enough to help even with broken bones.”

Pigi spent the next minute insisting to his two new friends that nothing was wrong.

He could not stop smiling.

A strange confidence had begun to grow inside him.

Maybe—

he really could handle the next four years.

◆◆◆

After bathing—

the four headed to the dining hall.

Breakfast was already laid out.

Bread.

Cereal.

Roasted corn.

Pineapple.

Blanched vegetables.

There were heavier dishes too.

Grilled sausages.

Chicken.

Fried eggs.

Grilled octopus tentacles.

But not many students ate heavily first thing in the morning.

The dining hall itself did not separate students by gender.

Even so—

the first-year boys and girls tended to sit some distance apart.

Vikir finished breakfast and immediately headed toward the lecture buildings.

Pigi, Tudor, and Sancho walked with him.

Then—

Tudor suddenly stopped.

“Wait.”

He looked ahead.

“Hey, Vikir!”

“This leads to the central staircase.”

“Only professors and fourth-years are allowed through there. We can't use it!”

“…”

Vikir already knew that.

That was precisely why he had come this way.

*If I'm going to leave and re-enter the Academy at night—*

*I need to identify the routes with the least traffic.*

For strategic reasons—

Vikir had been inspecting every passage through every Academy building one by one.

The semester had barely begun.

Yet he already knew most of Colosseo's internal structure.

Naturally—

that habit came with consequences.

“Stop! Freshman!”

A sharp voice rang out.

“Why are you using the central staircase!?”

Vikir stopped.

This was the source of many of his conduct problems.

The person approaching him was—

**Dolores L. Quovadis.**

Student council president.

Her thick bangs hung over the frames of her horn-rimmed glasses.

The moment she recognized Vikir—

she put both hands on her hips.

“Vikir.”

A weary look crossed her face.

“You again?”

“I'm sorry.”

Vikir bowed slightly.

“I have a terrible sense of direction.”

Dolores stared at him.

“This central staircase leads to areas where professors and senior students conduct research.”

“It needs to stay quiet.”

“Some of the laboratories are confidential.”

“Others are dangerous.”

“How many times have freshmen been told that this route is forbidden?”

“I'm sorry.”

“And don't expect special treatment because you're my junior in the newspaper club.”

Dolores opened her magical record book.

“Demerit.”

She added another entry.

The page already contained a considerable list.

> **Vikir — Conduct Score**
>
> **Demerit Reasons**
>
> Dormitory third-floor emergency exit — **−1**
>
> Fourth-year-only section of the training hall — **−1**
>
> First-floor central staircase of the toxicology laboratory — **−1**
>
> Smoking area of the experimental monster breeding facility — **−1**
>
> Sixth-floor central staircase of the faculty research building — **−1**
>
> Third-floor central staircase of the Hot Department practical-training building — **−1**
>
> Physical training room outside permitted hours — **−1**
>
> Restricted passage beside the dining-hall food warehouse — **−1**

And those were only the violations already recorded.

Dolores stared at the list.

Her mouth fell open slightly.

“…You really are a badly behaved student, aren't you?”

Pigi, Tudor, and Sancho panicked.

They stepped forward almost at once.

“S-Sorry! We should have warned him earlier!”

“He really is terrible with directions.”

“We'll make sure he follows us from now on. Hey, Vikir—how did you even collect this many demerits?”

All three looked back at him.

Their expressions clearly said—

*Lower your head.*

Dolores was not persuaded.

“No.”

She shut the record book.

“This is too many.”

“Vikir, you're doing mandatory service this weekend.”

Vikir had already performed on-campus service several times.

For reasons he still found irritating—

Professor Banshee Morgue seemed particularly willing to assign him extra corrective work.

But Dolores's next words went beyond what Vikir expected.

“If your demerits were only a little high, I'd end this with another round of campus service.”

She pointed at the record.

“But you've gone far beyond that.”

“A lazy student with conduct this poor isn't going to correct himself by cleaning a classroom or helping in the dining hall for a few hours.”

“…Then?”

“What do you mean, ‘then’?”

Dolores's voice rose.

“You're doing **off-campus service**.”

Pigi, Tudor, and Sancho simultaneously covered their faces.

Vikir had crossed a line none of them wanted to cross.

They looked at him with pity.

Off-campus service.

This was different from spending a weekend cleaning toilets—

or helping serve meals in the cafeteria.

Students assigned to off-campus service were sent to outside institutions.

Hospitals.

Monasteries.

Orphanages.

They spent the holiday there doing whatever work the institution required.

And this weekend was no ordinary weekend.

A regular holiday—

the Academy's founding anniversary—

and the Rune Church's Resurrection Festival—

had aligned to create a **ten-day Golden Week**.

Vikir had just been sentenced to spend it working.

Dolores looked down at him coldly.

“You have more than a week.”

“Use it to reflect on your conduct while you volunteer.”

“If you're lucky—”

“and the person in charge of the institution gives you a good evaluation on the final day—”

“your demerits may be cleared.”

She turned and walked away.

Tudor and Sancho immediately moved closer.

“Looks like she really marked you after that newspaper-club argument.”

Tudor patted Vikir's shoulder.

“Hang in there, friend.”

Sancho nodded solemnly.

“Was it because you said the Night Hound was a villain?”

“For what it's worth, I agree with you.”

“I don't understand the Saintess's reaction either.”

Pigi looked genuinely sorry for him.

“Vikir.”

“I can at least tell you where they're sending you.”

“If you know in advance, you can prepare yourself.”

Pigi had always been unusually well informed.

Naturally—

he knew where the Academy sent troublemakers and students with failing conduct scores.

“This time, you're going to the **Indulgentia Orphanage**.”

Vikir's eyes shifted.

“Indulgentia Orphanage?”

Tudor and Sancho repeated the name as well.

Pigi nodded.

“It's an orphanage managed by the Indulgentia family, a branch family of Quovadis.”

“The director is also the founder of the childcare foundation.”

“And he's an outside lecturer here at the Academy.”

Vikir's expression sharpened.

“The director?”

“Who is he?”

Pigi tilted his head.

Then answered.

“**Quilt Rune Indulgentia.**”

“Head of the Indulgentia family.”

“A collateral house of Quovadis.”

At that name—

Vikir's eyes narrowed.

**Quilt.**

Head of House Indulgentia.

He had seen the name many times.

It appeared repeatedly in the reports Cindywendy had sent him.

Vikir's next assassination target.
