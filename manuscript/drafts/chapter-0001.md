# Chapter 1: Hellhound (1)

> Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/001.txt`. English MTL Chapter 1 was independently aligned by title, opening/closing events, proper nouns, and scene sequence, then used only as a secondary reference.

The river of blood had finally run dry.

The war between the human world and the Demon Realm had raged for decades, leaving behind a terrifying entry in the history books: the ‘Age of Destruction’.

And then came the day humanity’s victory was carved into stone.

A severed head was hung above the main gate of House Baskerville, renowned as the Iron-Blooded Sword House.

Vikir. ‘Vikir Van Baskerville.’

The illegitimate son of Hugo Le Baskerville, patriarch of House Baskerville.

For years, his hands had been drenched in blood as he worked in the shadows of the family.

He was the hound of House Baskerville.

Vikir struggled.

His entire life had been a struggle.

A bastard. The son of a concubine.

That was why his middle name was ‘Van’.

Unlike his brothers within the family, he had not been granted a name such as ‘Le’ or ‘Re’. Because of that, he had been forced to work hundreds of times harder than everyone else.

But the result was anything but good.

Espionage, assassination, kidnapping, threats, smuggling… For the sake of the family, he had survived more than five hundred trials that brought him to the brink of death. In the end, however, fate abandoned him.

When he was framed as a spy for the demons and faced execution under a false charge, one thought burned fiercely in his mind.

He wanted to live.

He wanted to live again.

◆◆◆

Waa—waa—waa—

The cries of babies filled the enormous room.

House Baskerville, the Iron-Blooded Sword House, had been blessed with one happy event after another.

The head of the family, Marquis Hugo Le Baskerville, swept an indifferent gaze over the children born that year.

“Doesn’t look like there’s a single useful one among them.”

For the children born that year, it was a remarkably harsh first assessment to receive from their father.

… At that moment.

As Hugo passed the cribs in the nursery, his steps came to a halt beside one of them.

Vikir. Vikir Van Baskerville.

An illegitimate child who, by all rights, should not have been there.

Unlike his brothers, who were already six to eight months old, he had only just reached one hundred days when he was brought into this room.

The baby was not crying.

He simply lay there with his eyes closed, motionless as though he were dead.

“…”

Hugo looked down at Vikir, and his expression softened slightly.

It was not because he was pleased that Vikir seemed more gifted than the other children.

He merely thought that, in a room full of trash, a piece of trash that was a little quieter was preferable.

Before long, Hugo spoke to the dozens of wet nurses standing behind him.

“Take the children to the Cradle of Swords.”

The children of House Baskerville were subjected to trials from the moment they were born until the day they died.

From the cradle to the grave.

And their very first trial was about to begin.

… What was the Cradle of Swords?

It was a rite of passage unique to House Baskerville.

Countless blades were planted across a small, round hill, forming a maze, and an infant was placed at its center.

The infant would be trapped inside the labyrinth of blades.

The slightest movement could slice the child open, leaving wounds all over the body.

In such an environment, the infant had to crawl through the needle-like maze of blades and escape.

Only after plunging into the River Styx that encircled the maze would the child truly become a member of House Baskerville.

Hugo Le Baskerville thought, ‘The waters of the Styx only work on infants under a year old. Once a child soaks in this river, their entire body becomes as hard as steel.’

Of course, the river’s power was not limitless.

If one infant absorbed a great deal of that power, there would be correspondingly less left for the others.

Just like a mother’s milk.

The children of House Baskerville were now cast into the Cradle of Swords.

Whoever escaped the labyrinth first would be the first to enter the river. That would determine the futures of these children.

Thirty-two children born that year—including legitimate heirs, illegitimate children, and cousins—competed against one another inside the Cradle of Swords.

The densely packed blades spiraled across the hill, guiding the children toward a long, razor-sharp path of thorns.

Waa—waa—waa—

Some babies wailed loudly for their wet nurses. Some were already crawling around with surprising alertness. Others panicked as the blades cut them and drew blood, while still others lay on the ground sucking their fingers, seemingly with no intention of escaping at all… That was more or less how the children behaved.

After all, none of them was even a year old. Such a result was only natural.

However.

“Useless.”

Hugo Le Baskerville’s assessment was cold and merciless.

Weak little life-forms. Creatures incapable of surviving without someone else’s help.

Hugo regarded the human infants with naked contempt.

Even his own children were no exception.

He was one of the foremost swordsmen on the continent, a man said to have possessed iron blood from the day he was born.

“How are they supposed to fight the fiends of the Demon Realm like this? How long will it take before I can raise these brats to watch my back?”

Hugo stood above them, gazing at the waters of the Styx that surrounded the Cradle of Swords, and let out a sigh.

The mysterious river flowed only through House Baskerville’s sacred grounds, one of the holy places of the continent. Simply soaking in its waters increased mana, purified aura, and made the entire body as tough as steel.

If its effects had not been limited to children under a year old, Hugo would have immersed himself in it long ago.

As Hugo gazed at the river with a look of regret—

“… Hey, over there!”

“Ah! How is that possible?!”

“Unbelievable!”

Cries of astonishment erupted from all around.

What could possibly have shocked the family’s guardian knights so badly?

Hugo raised his head, puzzled.

And at that moment—

“…!”

A strange light flashed through eyes that had, until then, held only boredom, contempt, impatience, and disappointment.

The Cradle of Swords.

A single child was forcing his way through that dense forest of blades, heading straight for the River Styx.

Scrape—scrape—slash—

The child’s body was already covered in blood.

Astonishingly, he was crossing directly through the spiral-shaped maze of blades.

Rather than taking the longer, relatively safer path, he squeezed straight through the narrow gaps between the blades.

Blade marks streaked his soft, pale cheeks.

His plump waist and arms were cut and torn.

Both knees had been skinned raw, and his tiny hands were already a bloody mess.

The child crawled through the countless blades, carving a path forward in blood.

The sheer presence radiating from a child not yet a hundred days old stunned everyone in the family.

Even Marquis Hugo Le Baskerville, patriarch of the Iron-Blooded Sword House, was no exception.

Before long, the child made it all the way through the maze.

His brothers were still trapped in the center of the Cradle of Swords, unable to find their way out.

Plop!

The instant the child emerged from the maze, he threw himself into the River Styx.

The water gradually turned red.

In a rare display of emotion, Hugo stepped forward and gripped the railing tightly with both hands.

The attendants’ eyes widened when cracks spread through the marble beneath his fingers.

They had never seen the normally impassive man so shaken.

“The child! What happened to the child?”

Hugo raised his voice.

The infant who had fallen into the River Styx still had not resurfaced.

Before long, several of the family’s guardian knights approached the river and peered into the water.

Then the knights cried out in unison.

“Y-young master is drinking the water!”

The answer left Hugo staring with his mouth wide open.
