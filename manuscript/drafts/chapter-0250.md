At last, the long University League was over.

**<University League Rankings>**

1. Dolores L. Quovadis  
2. Merelini Lovegood  
3. Bakilaga Juragio

Dolores and Lovegood, both expected to place around third, had taken first and second.

Meanwhile, Mage Tower’s ace, Hohenheim, had performed far worse than expected.

Those two facts alone were enough to dominate conversation.

…But they still were not the biggest story.

**<University League Rankings>**

4. Highbro Le Baskerville  
5. Middlebro Le Baskerville  
6. Lowbro Le Baskerville  
7. Vikir  
8. Sinclaire  
9. Granola de Leviathan  
10. Oraorus Hohenheim

Six first-years had broken into the top ten.

That sent shockwaves through the entire League.

Until now, first-years entering the competition had been treated as people there to “gain experience.”

In practice, that usually meant becoming easy points for the upperclassmen.

This year shattered that assumption.

Vikir’s group had fought alongside Dolores and somehow placed every one of its members inside the top ten.

Granola, meanwhile, had become a particular target.

Because he had eliminated Hohenheim, the Mage Tower students and Hohenheim’s supporters had hunted him relentlessly until he became the first member of Vikir’s side to be knocked out.

Granola himself felt deeply wronged.

Still—

he was secretly rather pleased that he had managed to eliminate Hohenheim.

Another first-year had also drawn attention by carrying her team to an unusually high score almost entirely on her own.

Sinclaire.

A commoner with no family name attached to her.

Almost everything about her background remained mysterious.

Yet she had independently secured eighth place.

Considering that Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca, who had been grouped with her, all finished outside the top twenty—

which was already impressive for first-years—

Sinclaire’s result was extraordinary.

Riding that momentum, Colosseo’s first- and second-years defeated large numbers of third-years from the other schools and flooded the upper rankings.

Dolores ultimately took first place overall.

Mage Tower, on the other hand, had hosted the tournament—

yet not a single one of its students besides its ace Hohenheim entered the top ten.

And even Hohenheim had barely managed tenth.

He had fought brilliantly before his early elimination.

But for someone who had entered the League aiming for first place, tenth was an intolerable result.

By tradition, the final moments of every major match were recorded and permanently preserved inside image stones.

Which meant Hohenheim’s last moments would remain in the League’s hall of honor forever.

Humiliating.

Hohenheim would be suffering from that result long after the tournament ended.

◆◆◆

After the first round, Colosseo held a small celebration at its inn.

The plumbing problem at the lodging reserved by Varangian Academy had finally been repaired, so only Colosseo students were present.

“For President Dolores, first place in this year’s rankings!”

“Cheers!”

“Cheers!”

Cups rose high throughout the hall.

Amid the noise, Tudor lifted his own drink and turned toward Vikir.

“Congratulations, my friend! Seventh place as a first-year! I still can’t believe one of my friends is this incredible!”

“You’ve said that a hundred times already, Tudor.”

Sancho laughed from beside him.

Pigi leaned in from the front.

“But Vikir, how did you end up grouped with the Baskerville triplets this time?”

Everyone had been curious about that.

Vikir’s answer was simple.

“We worked together during the Nadelai competition and again during the midterms. It went unexpectedly well. After that, they started coming to me whenever they needed help.”

Bianca spoke without thinking.

“Maybe it’s also because you’re a commoner, so they don’t feel pressured around you.”

“Bianca!”

“Huh? No, that’s not what I meant! I just thought that might be how they see it. If it sounded insulting, I’m sorry. I didn’t mean it that way…”

Tudor’s rebuke made Bianca panic.

Vikir waved it off.

“She isn’t wrong. Children from high-ranking noble houses can make one another feel pressured or uncomfortable.”

There was nothing inherently shameful about being a commoner anyway.

And Vikir was not the only commoner in the group.

Sinclaire.

Another first-year commoner.

And the honor student who had finished directly behind Vikir in eighth place.

“…”

For some reason, whenever a conversation involved Vikir, Sinclaire normally found a way to jump into it.

On any other day she might have said something like—

“What!? Why are you discriminating against commoners! You nobles are terrible!”

or—

“Big Brother Vikir! We commoners have to work hard together!”

Today, however, she had been strangely quiet.

Everyone was beginning to wonder why.

“Keep it up.”

Vikir spoke first.

“Ah—ah… right. Keep it up! Let’s keep it up!”

Only then did Sinclaire seem to come back to herself.

She raised both fists toward Vikir in a small cheer.

Whenever the subject of family background came up, Sinclaire often grew unusually quiet.

The others quickly changed the subject.

“So that settles the first stage.”

Pigi brought up the schedule for the next part of the League.

“Only the top ten from the first stage can enter the second stage.”

The first stage had been the survival competition.

The ten survivors selected from it would now receive individual assignments.

Those who completed those assignments would finally earn the right to claim one of the priceless artifacts stored in the treasure vault jointly managed by Colosseo, Mage Tower, Varangian, and Temisquira.

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca began explaining the second stage to Vikir and Sinclaire.

“The second stage follows an apprenticeship system.”

Everyone nodded as Pigi explained.

Each of the ten survivors would leave the academies and visit a higher-level working organization, then complete a commission for that organization.

The organizations in question were the Empire’s Seven Great Houses and the Imperial Family.

Students were expected to enter the service of powers like these after graduation, so the system gave them practical experience in advance.

In other words, the ten survivors would each travel to one of the great powers—

the Iron-Blooded Sword House Baskerville,

the Mage House Morgue,

the Faithful House Quovadis,

the Divine Archer House Usher,

the Spear House Donquixote,

the Poison House Leviathan,

the Tycoon House Bourgeois,

or the Imperial Family—

and complete whatever assignment that organization gave them.

Once the task was complete, the house would provide an official certificate bearing its seal.

The content and difficulty varied wildly.

Some assignments were straightforward:

hunt a dangerous high-ranking monster.

Collect a rare material.

Others were far more abstract:

solve poverty in a specific district.

Answer a difficult philosophical problem.

Design a political institution.

Propose a diplomatic strategy.

“If a student belongs to one of the houses, tradition says they should avoid taking that house’s assignment.”

Pigi continued.

“It isn’t an absolute rule, but it’s strongly recommended.”

Everyone agreed.

For example, Dolores could not reasonably choose an assignment from House Quovadis.

Doing so would immediately raise questions about fairness.

“Vikir and Sinclaire aren’t publicly affiliated with any house, so the restriction doesn’t matter for them.”

Pigi looked between the two.

“If anything, you should pick something comparatively easy. The assignments from the Seven Great Houses all have different difficulty ratings.”

House Quovadis assignments, for example, were usually rated around three and a half stars.

Moderately difficult.

House Morgue’s assignments, on the other hand, were usually around four and a half stars.

They had a reputation for demanding extremely difficult work.

Before long, everyone was arguing over which house offered the best choice.

“I’d avoid Morgue. I heard their assignments got much harsher after they changed evaluators this year.”

“I’d take Donquixote. It’s perfect if you like the sea and adventure.”

“Your family would probably assign something ridiculous like ‘develop an uninhabited island.’”

“Historically, Bourgeois has the easiest second-stage assignments.”

“Right. Bourgeois looks like a good choice.”

“Which means everyone else will think the same thing. The competition will be awful.”

At that moment, Sinclaire timidly raised one hand.

“Um… I already have a house in mind.”

Everyone stared.

Sinclaire rarely stated her preferences so clearly.

“I’m thinking about taking either an Imperial Family assignment or one from House Quovadis.”

Their expressions turned puzzled.

Both were considered harder than many of the other options.

“Why those two?”

Bianca tilted her head.

Sinclaire gave an awkward smile.

“Well… there’s a reason.”

While the others focused on her—

Vikir quietly stood.

He slipped away from the crowd and stepped onto a secluded terrace.

*The second-stage assignment.*

One last hurdle.

Once he cleared it, he would gain access to the treasure vault jointly controlled by the four academies.

And inside that vault—

the Seventh Corpse.

Decarabia.

At last, the fourth of the Ten Corpses would be within reach.

Vikir sat alone on the dark terrace, thinking through what came next.

Then—

a calm, clear voice called his name.

“Vikir.”

The curtain at the edge of the noisy, brightly lit party shifted like a ghost.

A composed face appeared from behind it.

Dolores.

The winner of the University League’s first stage had come looking for him.

Quietly.

Almost secretly.
