The long National University League—

had finally reached the end of its first stage.

And the results—

shook all four academies.

**<National University League Rankings>**

1. Dolores L. Quovadis  
2. Merelini Lovegood  
3. Bakilaga Juragio  

Dolores and Lovegood—

both expected to contend for the top three—

finished first and second.

Hohenheim—

Mage Tower’s representative ace—

performed far worse than expected.

That alone—

would have caused endless discussion.

But it was not the real shock.

**<National University League Rankings>**

4. Highbro Le Baskerville  
5. Middlebro Le Baskerville  
6. Lowbro Le Baskerville  
7. Vikir  
8. Sinclaire  
9. Granola Leviathan  
10. Oraorus Hohenheim  

Six first-years.

Six—

inside the top ten.

Until now—

first-years entered the League to gain experience.

In practice—

most people treated them as stepping stones for upper-years.

That assumption—

had just been shattered.

Vikir’s group fought through the Survival Contest—

and, after joining forces with Dolores—

every member finished inside the top ten.

Granola’s path was the strangest.

Because Hohenheim believed Granola had poisoned him—

the Mage Tower ace’s followers hunted Granola relentlessly.

Granola became the first member of Vikir’s group to be eliminated.

He considered the whole thing deeply unfair.

At the same time—

a small part of him remained absurdly proud.

After all—

everyone believed *he* had eliminated Hohenheim.

Then there was Sinclaire.

A commoner.

No family name.

A background still shrouded in mystery.

She placed eighth—

on her own merits—

entirely separate from Vikir’s group.

Tudor.

Sancho.

Pigi.

Bianca.

Her teammates all finished outside the top twenty.

Even that—

was an excellent result for first-years.

But Sinclaire had gone much farther.

Colosseo’s first- and second-years surged through the rankings—

beating many third-years from other academies.

Dolores took first.

Mage Tower—

despite hosting the League—

placed nobody in the top ten except Hohenheim.

And he barely held tenth.

His early combat record had been overwhelming.

His elimination—

embarrassingly fast.

For someone who had entered aiming for first—

the result was unbearable.

Worse—

League tradition required the elimination moment to be recorded and permanently preserved on an image stone.

Hohenheim’s final moments—

would remain in the Hall of Fame forever.

*Humiliating.*

His suffering—

would outlive the tournament.

◆◆◆

After the first stage—

Colosseo held a small celebration at the inn.

Varangian had already moved back to its original lodging after the broken water pipes were repaired.

So this party—

belonged to Colosseo alone.

“To President Dolores, first place in the League!”

“Cheers!”

“Cheers!”

Cups rose.

Voices filled the room.

Tudor turned toward Vikir.

“Congratulations, buddy!”

He slapped the table.

“Seventh place as a first-year! I still can’t believe one of my friends did that!”

Sancho laughed.

“That’s the hundredth time you’ve said it.”

Pigi leaned forward.

“Vikir.”

“There’s something everyone wants to know.”

Vikir looked over.

“How did you end up on a team with the Baskerville triplets?”

A reasonable question.

Vikir’s answer—

was ready.

“We worked together once during the Naphtali game and again during the midterm.”

“The results were better than expected.”

“After that, they sometimes asked me for help.”

Bianca nodded.

“Maybe being a commoner made it easier too.”

Tudor frowned.

“Bianca.”

“What?”

She realized how it sounded.

“No! I didn’t mean commoners are inferior.”

“I just mean heirs of major noble families can make each other tense.”

“If that came out badly, sorry.”

Vikir waved it off.

“She’s right.”

“High-ranking nobles can feel pressure around one another.”

To Vikir—

common birth was not an insult.

There was another commoner at the table.

Sinclaire.

Ranked eighth—

directly behind him.

Usually—

whenever conversation turned toward Vikir—

she joined immediately.

Today—

she was quiet.

Especially—

when the subject became birth.

Everyone noticed.

Before anyone could make it more awkward—

Vikir spoke first.

“Do your best.”

Sinclaire blinked.

Then raised both fists.

“Ah—yes!”

“I will! You too, brother!”

The others quickly changed subjects.

Pigi took the opportunity.

“With that, the first stage is over.”

“Only the top ten can enter the second.”

The Survival Contest had been the first stage.

The ten highest-ranked contestants—

would now receive individual practical assignments.

Complete the assigned task—

and the student earned the right to proceed toward an artifact from the treasure vault jointly administered by the four academies.

Tudor, Sancho, Pigi, and Bianca began explaining the system to Vikir and Sinclaire.

“The second stage is based on apprenticeship.”

The ten rankers would leave the academies—

visit one of the Empire’s major practical organizations—

and complete a real assignment.

The Seven Great Families.

Or—

the Imperial Family.

Academy graduates would eventually work with—

or for—

powers like these.

The League gave them practical experience in advance.

Baskerville.

Morgue.

Quovadis.

Usher.

Donquixote.

Leviathan.

Bourgeois.

And the Imperial Family.

Each offered different work.

Hunt a dangerous high-ranking monster.

Retrieve a particular object.

Address poverty in a region.

Answer a difficult philosophical problem.

Design a political institution.

Propose a diplomatic method.

The range—

was enormous.

Pigi added one warning.

“If the student already belongs to a family, it’s customary to avoid that family’s task.”

“It isn’t an absolute rule.”

“But it keeps the evaluation objective.”

Dolores—

for example—

would normally avoid Quovadis.

Vikir and Sinclaire—

publicly affiliated with no Great Family—

had no such restriction.

“The families also have different average difficulty levels,” Pigi said.

“Quovadis assignments usually sit around three and a half stars.”

“Morgue is closer to four and a half.”

That started another argument.

“Avoid Morgue.”

“Their assignments became much harsher after the leadership change.”

“Donquixote sounds good if you like the sea.”

“Your family would probably tell someone to develop a deserted island.”

“Historically, Bourgeois tasks are the easiest.”

“Which means everyone will want them.”

Then—

Sinclaire raised one hand.

Shyly.

“I already have a family in mind.”

Everyone turned.

Sinclaire rarely stated a preference so clearly.

“I’m considering either the Imperial Family—”

“—or Quovadis.”

Confusion.

Those were not easy choices.

Bianca tilted her head.

“Why those two?”

Sinclaire smiled awkwardly.

“Because…”

Before she could finish—

Vikir stood.

Quietly.

He left the table—

and moved toward a secluded terrace.

*The second-stage assignment.*

Only one more gate.

Complete the task.

Enter the four-academy treasure vault.

Reach the artifact inside.

**Decarabia.**

The Seventh Corpse.

If Vikir destroyed it—

it would become the fourth of the Ten Corpses he had removed from the board.

He sat alone in the dim terrace light—

considering what came next.

Then—

a calm voice called his name.

“Vikir.”

A curtain moved like a pale ghost in the light spilling from the party.

Dolores stepped through.

The winner of the first stage—

had come to find him.

Alone.
