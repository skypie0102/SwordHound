Vikir entered the deep reaches of Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

Following the low ridges inland from Hill 207 on the western front—

he eventually reached a familiar place.

Ballak Village.

He had once spent a long stretch of his life there.

But now—

there was nothing.

After the Ah’Heman incident, the Ballak warriors had moved even deeper into the jungle.

Vikir had not seen them since.

Even Aiyen’s occasional letters had stopped at some point.

He had no way of knowing how they were doing.

*According to Cindywendy, Ballak hasn’t appeared for trade either.*

*Did something happen?*

The other jungle tribes regarded Vikir as a hero.

They cooperated actively with Cindywendy’s trade network.

But Ballak was absent.

Cindywendy herself had expressed confusion over the recent loss of contact.

Vikir chose not to dwell on it.

“…They aren’t people who go down easily.”

Ballak had many exceptional warriors.

Akwilla, the Night Fox who led them, was terrifyingly strong.

And her daughter Aiyen—

despite her youth—

had already climbed to a realm few warriors ever reached.

They were not people who needed Vikir’s constant protection.

*If they deliberately cut contact and hide, no one on this side will find them.*

No news could be good news.

For now—

Vikir decided to trust that they would contact him again eventually.

“First things first.”

“I need to find the Wraith Tree.”

◆◆◆

The Wraith Tree.

An ancient artifact—

and a form of magical alchemy now surviving only in legend.

It took root not in ordinary soil—

but in a mage’s inner world.

It fed on the karma of souls.

It consumed things that were abstract and metaphysical—

then projected its harvest back into the material world.

It was an existence almost impossible to understand.

The tree appeared in the mythological text **The Return of the Magic Hound**—

the story of Morgue Tzersi, one of House Morgue’s ancient ancestors.

No one knew exactly when the myth had first been written.

It was believed to predate the unification of the continent—

back when countless small states still fought across the land.

The tale had been passed down within Morgue for so long that almost nobody questioned whether it had ever been real.

Vikir knew better.

Because in the Age of Destruction—

someone had truly used the Wraith Tree.

*Snake.*

*King of the Dead.*

*Contractor of Seere.*

*The Wraith Tree should originally have fallen into his hands.*

In the future Vikir remembered, Snake had become its owner.

Seere had used Snake’s body to control the Wraith Tree—

and through it, wielded terrifying power.

At first glance—

the Wraith Tree looked like nothing more than a dead, twisted tree.

But it grew by devouring the will of the dead.

Once it absorbed enough death and enough wandering souls—

its trunk and branches could become as enormous as the World Tree of myth.

Skull-shaped fruit would hang from those branches.

The seed required one thing above all else to germinate.

**Mass death.**

The Wraith Tree absorbed countless dead souls.

Then it bore fruit containing the powers they had possessed in life.

A human who ate such fruit could inherit those powers and skills.

That was why any mage who planted a Wraith Tree within their soul was inevitably drawn toward black magic.

Its growth demanded blood.

Lives.

Corpses.

And more death.

Before regression—

Snake had used it to turn the corpses of those he killed into an army.

Then he had transformed their souls into fruit—

and consumed them.

*His total mana eventually rivaled Camus Morgue.*

*A once-in-a-millennium genius who built her power through talent and relentless effort.*

Their clashes had been catastrophic.

Sky-shattering.

Earth-rending.

At the time—

Vikir had been only a low-ranking officer.

He had not even dared to look directly at battles on that scale.

*One of their fights erased an entire island.*

In peaceful times—

the Wraith Tree could remain a seed.

Or a small shrub.

In war—

fed by enough death—

it could become something vast enough to resemble the World Tree itself.

This time—

Vikir intended to bring that power to his own side before the Age of Destruction arrived.

*Pomeranian’s aptitude must really be extraordinary.*

Ordinary black mages would never feel such instinctive fascination for an artifact like this.

Yet Pomeranian had become obsessed with it the moment she saw the illustration.

Perhaps—

when ancient artifacts were involved—

fate really did matter.

Vikir descended from a great tree using Baby Madam’s silk.

“Here.”

His destination was a barren region deep in the jungle.

An impossible-looking desert of white sand.

The **Yuuni Desert**.

A salt desert.

One of the driest places in the world.

Almost no moisture could survive there.

White salt sand.

Withered vegetation.

Nothing else.

Vikir crouched and pressed a finger into the ground.

When he withdrew it—

the skin on his fingertip had already become rough and shriveled.

The salt drew moisture from everything it touched.

Legend said that long ago—

the tears of an evil god had dried here and created the vast desert.

Whether that story was true or not—

the Yuuni Desert had claimed countless lives.

Mummified monsters lay scattered across the white expanse as proof.

Vikir walked for some time.

Then—

“…!”

He found something enormous.

A monster corpse.

Almost nothing remained but bones and dried skin.

**<Dragon of the Saha River — Basilisk>**

**Danger Rating:** S  
**Size:** 44 m  
**Discovery Location:** Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain, 10th Ridge  
**Alias:** ‘Dragon of the Saha River’

A terrifying being believed to be a hybrid of dragon and demon.

Its breath could make birds fall dead from the sky.

Where its scaled body crawled—

grass would not grow for half a century.

Its existence alone was a disaster.

For reasons unknown, it had made its lair on the Tenth Ridge and remained there.

If it ever began moving in earnest—

humanity would need to prepare.

Neither ordinary blades nor magic could easily kill it.

One of the three great disasters beyond common measure.

Adonai.

Madam Eight-Legs.

And lastly—

the Basilisk, Dragon of the Saha River.

*After Madam disappeared, I thought this thing might start rampaging.*

*So why is it dead here?*

The Basilisk and Madam Eight-Legs had long been bitter enemies.

Their mutual pressure had restrained both of them.

Once Madam vanished—

Vikir had expected the Basilisk’s influence to expand.

Instead—

it was dead.

*I was planning to hunt it anyway.*

*This saves me the trouble.*

The carefully prepared hunting plan had become unnecessary.

Vikir examined the corpse.

The Basilisk had not died naturally.

Compared to the specimens Vikir remembered from the Age of Destruction—

this one was smaller.

And the body was covered in sword wounds.

Cuts through hide.

Piercing damage through flesh.

Bones shattered by blades.

Many wounds had partially healed due to the Basilisk’s monstrous regeneration—

leaving deep scars behind.

There were also burn marks from aura.

The thick scales bore splashed traces of dense liquid-like aura.

The dead swordsman who had fought it—

had clearly reached the upper end of the Sword Graduator realm.

Vikir traced the attack patterns.

A familiar structure emerged.

*Fourth Fang.*

A Baskerville hound had fought here alone long ago.

Vikir searched the area.

Not far from the Basilisk—

a torn black cloak fluttered in the wind.

A skull had been eroded by years of salt.

A Baskerville uniform lay in rags across the white sand.

The thick cloak identified the dead man as a member of the Pit Bull Knights.

A knight whose name had vanished with time.

Judging by the sword traces—

he had only reached the Fourth Fang.

His lineage was probably not particularly distinguished.

Yet he had honed that one Fang for so long—

and to such a terrifying degree—

that he had slain the Dragon of the Saha River alone.

Vikir stood silently before the remains.

For a moment—

it felt like looking at his own bones from his previous life.

Another hound.

Another nameless death.

He bowed his head.

“…Go somewhere better.”

Then Vikir carefully removed the gold badge symbolizing his new rank as Senator.

He placed it beside the remains.

The gesture was the highest respect he could offer.

Then—

“…?”

Something caught his eye.

Between the dead knight’s ribs—

a bundle of old parchment had survived.

A letter.

The seal was red.

Highest military secrecy.

And the mark belonged to the Head of House Baskerville.

“…Hugo?”

The dry salt had preserved the letter remarkably well.

Vikir broke the seal without hesitation.

He unfolded the parchment.

Then—

he began to read.

The contents were far more surprising than he expected.
