# Chapter 49: The Husband Hunt (4)

> Restarted Chinese-first reconstruction. Primary source: `source/chinese/chapters/049.txt`. **Documented raw-gap exception:** the Chinese file jumps directly from the campfire description to “They especially welcome women,” omitting the immediately necessary setup that identifies **Aiyen / Ahun** and explains the Ballak “husband hunt.” The independently aligned recovered English Chapter 49 preserves that connective block, and the Chinese resumes in the middle of the same explanation. That limited block is restored here as a source-gap repair; recovered English does not otherwise override Chinese semantics. Canonical forms **Aiyen**, **Ahun**, **Ballak Tribe**, **Low Sword Graduator**, **Camus Morgue**, **Adolf Morgue**, and **Staffordshire Baskerville** are used. Camus's captivity/coercion is kept non-graphic and non-eroticized.

The forest drowned in darkness was unnaturally quiet.

The silent forest was unnaturally dark.

Stars lay across the black sky like coarse salt scattered over a carpet, yet not even their faint light could penetrate the dense vines and leaves to reach the flooded jungle floor.

There were no insects chirping anywhere.

CRACKLE. CRACKLE.

Only the faint fire gnawing at the wood broke the silence.

Camus Morgue sat bound and gagged among the Ballak hunters.

Even while being dragged through the jungle, her sharp memory and powers of observation had allowed her to pick up several names and positions.

The young woman leading the hunting party was **Aiyen**.

She appeared to be about seventeen, with dark hair streaked by silver, pointed ears, and a face smeared with charcoal.

The young man who had fired the paralytic dart and thrown the noose was **Ahun**.

Most of the other hunters looked seventeen or eighteen as well.

They had built only a small fire, covering much of it with ash so that it would give warmth without throwing much light.

As Camus listened, she began to understand what kind of expedition she had been caught by.

At this time of year, Ballak youths left the tribe on what could be called a **husband hunt**—or, depending on who was doing the hunting, a wife hunt, son-in-law hunt, or daughter-in-law hunt.

They searched other tribes and sometimes even the Empire's frontier for potential spouses.

The reason was practical: bringing in outsiders prevented the hereditary problems that came from repeatedly marrying within a tiny population and introduced new blood into the tribe.

Naturally, the hunters wanted strong candidates.

Those they judged suitable could be taken as mates. Others might instead be enslaved or killed.

Women were especially valued because, for a small tribe trying to increase its population, each woman represented more future children than each man.

That explained why Ahun had been staring at Camus with an ugly smile.

“The men of the tribe will be pleased. We caught such a strong woman.”

Camus understood enough Ballak to know what he meant.

She clenched her teeth.

“You expect me to bear children for you barbarians? I'd rather die!”

Camus tried to bite down on her own tongue, but Ahun reacted faster and forced the gag back into place.

He grinned and pressed an arrowhead beneath her chin.

“This arrogant slave needs to learn manners.”

He had barely moved toward her when—

CLACK!

Someone stepped between them.

Aiyen.

The young leader of the Ballak hunting party pressed the end of her bow against Ahun's chest.

“Back off.”

Her order was short.

Ahun scowled.

“What? Captain, I caught her.”

“Shut up. Keep your voice down.”

“What are you talking about? *I* shot the dart. *I* threw the noose.”

“With whose help?”

Aiyen's cold question silenced him.

“If I hadn't stopped that ‘boy with the sword,’ you'd already be dead.”

She meant Vikir.

The reminder made Ahun visibly flinch.

He raised his voice in frustration.

“So this woman belongs to you now?”

“Of course.”

“But you're a woman too!”

“So?”

Ahun had no answer.

A moment later, cold killing intent rolled from Aiyen's body.

Silver aura shimmered along the bow in her hand.

Camus's eyes widened.

Liquid aura.

The unmistakable sign of a Sword Graduator.

It was faint, but there was no mistaking it: Aiyen possessed the strength of a **Low Sword Graduator**.

A seventeen-year-old Graduator.

Camus had never imagined another genius of that level existed at their age besides Vikir and herself.

Aiyen did not care about her surprise.

Ahun, meanwhile, immediately gave ground before her strength.

“If you want to make trouble, come at me anytime. I'll beat you into paste.”

“...I won't.”

“You won't?”

“...Fine. Whatever. I give up. Do what you want with her.”

Ahun turned away, furious and humiliated.

Only then did Aiyen look back at Camus.

Camus did not mistake the intervention for rescue.

“Good eyes. Strong body. Good blood.”

Aiyen inspected her with the same detached gaze someone might use on merchandise.

“I was captured by people of your Empire too. Especially because of Morgue. Sold as a slave.”

“...”

“You will become the same.”

Aiyen closed her eyes for a moment as though remembering someone.

“But you won't be as lucky as I was.”

When her eyes opened again, her expression had hardened.

“I'll take my time. Break your pride little by little. Train even your soul until obeying me—even licking my feet—is ordinary to you—”

She never finished.

BOOM!

A violent explosion erupted from the bushes behind the camp.

“Die, you beasts.”

The voice sounded like a death sentence.

Ice spears flew through the darkness and struck the hunters in the rear.

Several Ballak warriors fell in less than a second.

Behind them stood Adolf Morgue, red lines of mana spreading from his hands.

Then the rest of the Morgue and Baskerville pursuit force charged into the camp.

“I'll repay what you did earlier.”

Staffordshire and the Baskerville triplets drew their blades and cut down several hunters.

Morgue's mages attacked at the same time.

Spells streaked through the darkness, filling the jungle with brilliant light and explosive noise.

Yet something was wrong.

The Ballak hunters did not respond efficiently.

Even with the difference in numbers and firepower, they should have been able to minimize their losses by abandoning the fight and focusing entirely on escape.

Instead, both warriors and wolves looked increasingly agitated.

Strangely, they seemed more afraid of the *light and noise* of the surprise attack than of the attack itself.

“Are these lunatics really making this much noise in the flooded jungle at night!?”

Ahun jumped backward in alarm.

His eyes were no longer focused on the blades and magic around him.

They were fixed on the darkness beyond the camp.

Aiyen reacted the same way.

“...”

She moved immediately.

Her first action was not to counter Morgue's magic or Baskerville's swords.

She kicked dirt over the campfire and extinguished it.

Only after the remaining light vanished did the Ballak hunters begin moving in earnest.

They mounted their wolves, firing arrows while retreating deeper into the flooded jungle.

“They're running! Don't let a single one escape!”

Staffordshire drove the Baskerville hounds after them.

Amid the pitch-black chaos, a shadow slipped toward Camus.

Vikir.

His sword cut through the ropes and noose binding her to the stake in a single flash.

Still weakened by the paralytic poison, Camus smiled the moment she recognized him.

“...You! You came!”

“Shh.”

Vikir lifted her into his arms and began withdrawing.

Or tried to.

A silver shadow appeared directly in front of him.

“I knew you'd come, ‘boy with the sword.’”

Aiyen faced Vikir head-on.

Her eyes ran over him from head to toe.

“A body strong enough to cross this distance so quickly.”

“...”

“A will strong enough to pierce the darkness of the flooded jungle.”

“...”

“And the courage to risk your life to save your woman.”

“...?”

Vikir frowned.

Why was this Ballak woman evaluating him?

He started to move past her.

Aiyen pointed at Camus in his arms.

“I won't ask about the past.”

The corner of her mouth curved upward.

“Passed.”
