# Chapter 377: The Hounds of Nouvelle Vague (1)

> Chinese-primary reconstruction from `source/chinese/chapters/377.txt`. Recovered English Chapter 375 is the verified shifted secondary witness. Established repo terminology controls Nouvelle Vague, Rune, Sakkuth de Leviathan / Plague Leper, Vikir, Cindywendy, Isabella, Professor Banshee Morgue, Mozgus Quovadis, Lovebad Bourgeois, Madeline, Passamonte, Hobbs, Ten Corpses, Gate of Destruction, and Night Hound.

Nouvelle Vague.

A place abandoned by the world.

Ten thousand meters beneath the sea—

even here—

one day each year carried a little warmth.

Christmas.

Rune’s birthday.

Out of all three hundred sixty-five days—

this was the only day prisoners received a holiday.

The food changed too.

No stale salted fish.

No black bread hard as brick.

Instead—

dried pork.

Soft white bread.

For once—

the prisoners ate in a proper dining hall instead of on the labor-site ground.

Candles burned in lead holders.

The gloomy stone chamber filled with the smell of bread and meat.

Even the guards simply stood at a distance—

weapons on their backs—

and did not interfere.

The pork was tough.

A little fishy.

The bread was already half-dry.

The prisoners ate as though they possessed the whole world.

“I survived the entire year for this.”

“Good thing you’re alive.”

“Poor bastards who died yesterday.”

“Hehehe.”

Then—

“Kurururu…”

“Move.”

A Level Eight prisoner entered.

**Sakkuth de Leviathan.**

The **Plague Leper**.

The other prisoners immediately stood and moved away.

Three full decontamination procedures on entry had failed to remove all traces of his poison.

People feared him.

“He can’t even control his plague.”

“Touch his blood or saliva and you get infected.”

“Look at those spots.”

“Poison or plague—

either way he’s disgusting.”

Sakkuth ignored every stare.

“Kurururu!”

“You forced trash don’t understand!”

“I came into this place on my own!”

“And one day I’m leaving!”

“My Queen will make sure of it!”

He had been saying the same thing for two years.

At first—

the words had sounded like genuine contempt for everyone else.

Over time—

they had changed.

Now there was obsession in them.

Madness.

Almost like a curse repeated too many times.

Prisoners grimaced.

Or looked afraid.

Either way—

nobody wanted to stand near him.

Soon—

an empty circle formed around Sakkuth.

Satisfied—

he sat.

Chewed his pork and bread.

Then paused.

Someone was still behind him.

One prisoner.

One person who had not moved.

Vikir.

Sakkuth frowned.

“Brat.”

“You’re not afraid of my plague?”

“….”

Vikir did not answer.

If Sakkuth knew what kind of venom flowed through Vikir’s body—

something far worse than ordinary plague—

he might lose consciousness on the spot.

Crunch.

Crunch.

Vikir finished his meal quietly.

Very different from Christmas at Colosseo Academy.

Then—

he heard several prisoners whispering at the next table.

“…Is it real?”

“I’m telling you.”

“The guards already searched it.”

“It’s torn to hell, though.”

“Damn.”

“You really got one?”

“Hehehe.”

“You’d be surprised what falls into the toilet purification tanks.”

“Good stuff too.”

A prisoner assigned to cleaning had found something.

Vikir assumed cigarettes.

A watch.

Gambling pieces.

Cheap drugs.

Then—

“Holy shit.”

“You found a **newspaper**?”

Vikir’s attention sharpened immediately.

A newspaper.

Inside Nouvelle Vague—

that meant news from the surface.

And newspapers were almost impossibly rare.

A few sheets of paper had to survive the entire journey down.

Unless an outside-duty guard carried one in—

regular access was impossible.

Still—

some guards and prisoners desperately wanted surface news.

Vikir was one of them.

Scrape.

He pushed back his chair.

Walked over.

“Hey.”

The Level One prisoners nearly jumped.

“Y-yes!”

Light sentences.

Three years—

if they survived that long.

Most did not.

They were too weak.

Too fragile.

Many died within one year.

And to them—

a Level Nine prisoner like Vikir was a disaster waiting to happen.

Vikir kept his voice low.

“A newspaper like that will only cause trouble for you.”

“Treasure beyond your ability to protect becomes a curse.”

“….”

“Trade it.”

He held out a bag of black bread—

a recent reward.

The prisoners’ eyes shook.

“You already read it, right?”

“Uh…”

“Actually…”

“I can’t read.”

“Then let me read it.”

“I’ll tell you what it says afterward.”

There was no reason to refuse.

The prisoner reached into his clothes.

Pulled out a carefully folded yellow sheet.

“I rescued it from the shit pit.”

“Smells a little.”

“Hehehe.”

Vikir handed over the bread.

Took the paper.

It was badly damaged.

Most pages were gone.

Only the front and second page survived at all.

Even those had blurred letters and ripped sections.

Barely enough remained to read the headlines.

Worst of all—

the date was six months old.

*Once—

I read newspapers before they were even published.*

During his time in Ryukeion—

Colosseo’s newspaper club—

newspapers had been everywhere.

Now even a filthy six-month-old scrap felt priceless.

Rustle.

Vikir read carefully.

**[Breaking…] …palace consort attacked…**

**—Usher Family… mysterious assault… severe losses… authorities identify one suspect… victim Madeline… seriously injured but life not endangered…**

**[Exclusive] Successive attacks… victims…**

**—Donquixote Family… another mysterious attack… Count Passamonte… minor injuries… authorities identify one suspect… believed same attacker…**

**[Exclusive] Suspected highly poisonous assassin arrested…**

**—Leviathan Family… Hobbs attacked… recent surge in monsters blamed… mysterious gates and strange dungeons appearing throughout Leviathan territory… Empire-wide investigation launched… Night Hounds… suspicious organization… unrest spreading…**

The fragments were incomplete.

But the picture was enough.

Usher.

Donquixote.

Leviathan.

All attacked.

Possibly by one person.

*Who?*

Then one line pulled Vikir’s attention away from everything else.

**Mysterious gates and strange dungeons appearing repeatedly.**

The strange phrase **Night Hounds** appeared too.

But Vikir barely cared about that.

*The Ten Corpses have begun moving openly.*

The Gate of Destruction—

already showing signs of opening.

And this newspaper was six months old.

*I need to hurry.*

The Age of Destruction was arriving early.

Possibly because of the butterfly effect created by Vikir’s own actions.

The Corpses he had killed.

The instructions he had left with Cindywendy.

The people who had begun questioning his conviction before he was sent away.

He remembered voices.

*I watched you during your Academy life.*

*You are not evil.*

*I know that much.*

*After seeing the demonized men with my own eyes—

I finally understand.*

*You have to live.*

*Why did I realize this so late?*

*I’ll request a retrial immediately.*

*Even if we have to sink the ship—

the Head of House ordered us to free you.*

Professor Banshee.

Mozgus Quovadis.

Lovebad Bourgeois—

a man Vikir had barely known.

Isabella Baskerville.

Given the final attitudes of the Morgue—

Quovadis—

Bourgeois—

and Baskerville escorts—

Vikir could imagine what had happened among the Seven Great Families after his imprisonment.

*Now I understand why the Ten Corpses are rushing the Gate.*

Political pressure.

Strategic pressure.

They were using demonic common sense—

not human common sense.

Snap.

Vikir folded the newspaper.

Then washed his hands with water dripping from the ceiling.

*I need more outside information.*

Finding another newspaper could take months.

There was only one reliable way.

*Go outside myself.*

He had found Poseidon.

That alone meant more than ninety percent of his reason for entering Nouvelle Vague was complete.

Vikir looked down at the heavy restraints around his wrists and ankles.

*No reason to keep wearing prison clothes anymore.*

The prison break—

was beginning.
