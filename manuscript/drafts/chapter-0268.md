A cute black puppy’s face.

That was the mask Vikir was wearing.

A cheap children’s mask bought from a street vendor.

Emergency Night Hound equipment.

Dolores stood in front of him—

still looking as though she could not decide whether this was real.

“…Mr. Night Hound?”

Vikir nodded.

Thump.

Thump.

Thump.

Dolores’s heart raced.

A moment ago—

she had been wishing he were there.

Now he had appeared as if he could read her thoughts.

*If that isn’t a supernatural power…*

*then what is it?*

Her mind wandered somewhere even more dangerous.

*…Fate?*

The kind of word that appeared in cheap romance novels.

Dolores had always considered that sort of thing childish.

But now—

standing here—

the idea became harder to dismiss.

Destined people.

Inevitable bonds.

All those embarrassing things that made one’s heart race.

*Come to think of it…*

She remembered the moment when her soul had briefly resonated with Night Hound’s.

A cold road.

Lonely.

Painful.

A pilgrim walking through thorns while carrying everything alone.

That night—

she had felt his pain.

His grief.

His numb loneliness.

She knew almost nothing about the man beneath the mask.

Yet she had become convinced that some invisible connection existed between them.

“Focus.”

Night Hound’s hand waved in front of her face.

Dolores snapped back.

“Ah.”

“Sorry.”

“I just… blanked out for a moment.”

She flushed.

Then looked at the absurd little dog mask.

“Something feels different.”

“The atmosphere.”

“And the mask.”

“…”

Vikir did not answer.

Dolores interpreted the silence herself.

*This meeting wasn’t planned.*

*He must have been too rushed to prepare his normal mask.*

“So we happened to run into each other.”

“…”

“Do you live nearby?”

“…”

Again—

silence.

Again—

Dolores treated it as an answer.

*Weekend.*

*Casual clothes.*

*He knows the Colosseo grounds extremely well.*

Until now—

she had suspected Night Hound had an informant inside the Academy.

Maybe—

he was inside the Academy himself.

Then another thought struck her.

*Wait.*

*Why did I say Vikir’s name earlier?*

It had slipped out unconsciously.

Was it because of the hand over her mouth?

Because it reminded her of the earlier corridor incident with Quilt?

Something about the sensation felt familiar.

Dolores could not identify why.

Only that she had missed something important.

Meanwhile—

Vikir was watching Humbert’s adjutant leave.

There was a faint demonic smell on him.

*Dantalian is dead.*

*Yet Quovadis still reeks of demons.*

The same odor had been present around the indulgences.

And Humbert himself—

when Vikir observed him from a distance—

carried a particularly strong trace.

*New Testament priests have none of it.*

*Then the Old Testament Faction is definitely connected to the Sixth Corpse.*

Humbert was especially suspicious.

His carriage.

His mansion.

The precious metals and artwork.

The constant luxury.

He had once received regular hospitality and tribute from Quilt Rune Indulgentia.

Yet he was also a veteran politician—

the kind who knew how to cut off a poisoned tail before it dragged him down.

*But he isn’t a demon.*

Vikir had checked repeatedly.

Humbert was closely connected to something demonic—

but was not himself possessed.

*Then I need to find his new source of money.*

House Indulgentia was gone.

Yet Humbert remained enormously wealthy.

Following the money might reveal the link between the Ninth Corpse and the Sixth.

Dolores gave him the next clue herself.

“The conflict between the Old and New Testament factions has allowed heresies and cults to spread everywhere.”

“That’s why I came here.”

Vikir’s attention sharpened.

*Heresies.*

*Cults.*

*Who benefits most when the two Quovadis factions fight each other?*

He remembered Edward Bourbon Jr.

Saint Bourbon.

Mid Sword Graduator.

High priest of the Ohm Cult.

A future traitor who would join the Demon Army and seriously wound Tudor and Bianca.

Vikir had already removed him.

And on his corpse—

an Old Testament indulgence.

**<Indulgence>**

**‘All sins of this faithful believer are forgiven.’**

**—Issued and guaranteed by the Old Testament Faction. Counterfeiting is punishable.—**

Vikir’s investigation had suggested something important.

*The Old Testament’s new money may be coming from cults and heresies.*

Perhaps—

in exchange for religious autonomy—

the Old Testament Faction was selling them legitimacy through indulgences.

Maybe Martin Luther had realized the same thing.

Dolores covered her mouth.

“…Oh my.”

“Cardinal Luther knew?”

“I’ve been completely blind.”

“This isn’t the time to blame yourself.”

The cults and heresies were probably not the real enemy.

Only intermediaries.

Money movers.

Money launderers.

Small fish.

The true hand was behind them.

Old Testament Quovadis.

Cults and heresies across the Empire.

And some unknown force connecting the money between them.

If Quovadis was going to be cleansed—

that filthy flow of money had to be cut.

“First we hit the intermediaries.”

Dolores nodded.

What outside force was using something as mundane as money—

quietly manipulating Quovadis?

Her expression hardened.

“…When cults spread, the people hurt most are always the poor and desperate.”

She continued.

“I don’t care whether someone believes in Rune.”

“If helping others becomes a habit and gives them peace, then salvation doesn’t have to come through religion.”

“But these people…”

Money.

Food.

Bodies.

Blood.

Appetite.

Sex.

Pleasure.

The demands changed.

The exploitation did not.

“The man I killed yesterday was the same kind.”

Dolores flinched.

As she expected—

Night Hound had never stopped fighting.

Still alone.

Still carrying pain by himself.

“…Let me help.”

She grabbed his hand.

Hard.

Her determination had always been strong.

Now—

Respect.

Affection.

Neither hidden particularly well.

“I can help.”

“The buff you used during the Dantalian fight.”

“Can you do it again?”

“…That.”

*Right.*

“Fine.”

“Then we start with the cults and heresies tied to the Old Testament.”

“H-how?”

“Are we going to visit every one across the Empire?”

“No.”

“That would take too long.”

“There’s a way to gather them all at once.”

Dolores’s eyes widened.

A method to wipe out the dangerous cults and heresies in one sweep.

Something the younger New Testament priests had debated repeatedly—

then abandoned as impossible.

Night Hound said it as though the answer were obvious.

“What is it?”

Dolores had already pulled out a notebook.

And a pen.

Vikir spoke in the same flat voice as always.

“I need your body.”
