Oraorus Hohenheim.

Mage Tower’s prodigy.

Eldest son—

of the Aureolus family—

whose specialty was alchemy.

A student whose magical studies could stand beside House Morgue.

Whose astrology was spoken of in comparison with the Imperial court.

Whose academic record throughout Mage Tower’s advanced curriculum—

was nearly flawless.

Third-year.

Student council president.

Early graduation already in sight.

Graduate school after that.

Then—

a faculty position.

Possibly—

the youngest professor in Mage Tower history.

And perhaps—

one day—

Mage Tower Master.

That impossible elite—

made a sound no dignity could survive.

Pfffft.

It did not come from his mouth.

Hohenheim froze.

Then—

another sound.

Wet.

Violent.

Also—

not from his mouth.

“G-Ghhh!?”

He grabbed his stomach.

His whole body curled—

like a shrimp thrown onto a hot plate.

Cold sweat soaked him.

His insides moved without permission.

*Healing magic.*

He needed it.

But healing was high-level work.

Difficult enough under calm conditions.

Nearly impossible—

while his body revolted and his mana was already disordered from fighting Bakilaga.

“…Poison?”

Hohenheim stared at the spilled stew.

“But I checked the ingredients.”

“Nothing there was poisonous.”

Then—

his mind caught up.

*Combination.*

In Hohenheim’s own reasoning—

some foods said to be harmless alone could cause trouble when paired.

His memory raced through every such warning he had ever heard.

Honey and black tea.

Eel and peach.

Beer and peanuts.

Crab and persimmon.

Spinach and tofu.

Carrot and cucumber.

Cheese and beans.

Seaweed and salt.

Old food-pairing lore—

most often associated, in his mind, with stomach trouble.

Then he identified two suspicious ingredients in the stew.

**Chacachaca Catfish.**

A man-eating fish from Le Rouge et Le Noir Mountain.

And—

**Sabac tree bark.**

Harmless enough separately.

Together—

according to the survival knowledge behind this trap—

severe abdominal pain.

*Who knows a combination like this?*

Hohenheim’s furious gaze swept the area.

Then stopped.

Granola Leviathan.

The heir of the Empire’s great poison clan.

A talented—

but young—

first-year.

Hohenheim’s genius brain reached the wrong conclusion instantly.

*It was him.*

*Who else would make poison like this?*

Granola stared back.

Terrified.

“Why are you looking at me like that?”

“What did I do?”

A hand landed on Granola’s shoulder.

Vikir.

“Congratulations.”

Granola blinked.

“Group leader.”

“Thanks to your poison, we caught Mage Tower’s third-year ace.”

“…Me?”

Granola pointed at himself.

“My poison?”

“Yes.”

Vikir’s expression did not change.

“Your achievement.”

“Your poison.”

Granola scratched his head.

Completely lost.

He did not see—

Hohenheim’s hatred deepening behind him.

Then—

thud.

Hohenheim collapsed.

Vomiting.

Diarrhea.

Both ends—

in open rebellion.

His HP began falling.

**9.**

**5.**

**4.**

**3.**

**2.**

Bakilaga stood nearby.

Stunned.

“…Are you all right?”

No answer.

“…Right.”

He hesitated.

Then—

very carefully—

patted Hohenheim’s shoulder.

A gesture so gentle—

it was almost reverent.

**1.**

Bakilaga jerked his hand away.

“S-Sorry!”

“I didn’t mean to—”

Silence.

“…Right.”

A tear slipped from Hohenheim’s eye.

Bakilaga understood.

They had been rivals for years.

But this humiliation—

required no rivalry to understand.

Some childhood disasters—

were universal.

Bakilaga scratched his head.

“…Tch.”

Then left.

The final duel he had anticipated—

had ended in the most absurd way possible.

Rustle.

Another person appeared.

Hohenheim looked up.

“…You.”

Vikir.

Expressionless.

Hohenheim’s fury weakened into a bitter smile.

There was almost nothing left to protect.

“So.”

“In the end, some nameless Colosseo first-year gets the honor of defeating me.”

Vikir said nothing.

“Fine.”

Hohenheim raised his chin.

“How many chances will someone like you ever have to face a genius like me?”

“Take my head.”

“Use your best weapon.”

“Your best technique.”

“Sword.”

“Axe.”

“Bow.”

“Anything.”

His HP remained—

**1.**

“You may break my body.”

“But you will never break my pride.”

“My will.”

Vikir drew—

nothing.

No sword.

No bow.

No axe.

No magic.

Empty hands.

Hohenheim frowned.

Instinct—

warned him.

Something terrible—

was aimed at him.

But where?

No steel.

No arrow.

No spell.

For the first time—

a small, unfamiliar fear appeared.

Vikir’s lips moved.

One weapon—

finally emerged.

“Shit-pants.”

Hohenheim froze.

Cruelty.

Pure—

merciless—

fact-based cruelty.

His pride—

which had survived sword and magic—

cracked.

**0.**

Stress.

Humiliation.

Rage.

The final point vanished.

Oraorus Hohenheim—

was eliminated.

◆◆◆

News spread quickly.

Mage Tower’s ace—

gone.

Not by greatsword.

Not by spell.

Not by arrow.

A stew.

A stomach.

And one final insult.

Dolores stared at Vikir.

“…Vikir.”

“Mm.”

“That stew I ate earlier.”

She swallowed.

“Was it the same kind Hohenheim ate?”

“Yes.”

A chill ran down her spine.

“…Were you planning to do that to me too?”

“Yes.”

Vikir nodded.

Dolores turned white.

Same school—

but still rivals for ranking.

Her terror had nothing to do with betrayal.

It was far more primal.

*No.*

*Absolutely not.*

*Please.*

She covered herself front and back—

and began edging away.

Vikir watched her.

For an instant—

her desperation reminded him of the Dolores from the Age of Destruction—

facing demons without end.

He decided to correct the misunderstanding.

From his pocket—

he took several dried mushrooms.

“Boil these with it.”

Dolores stopped.

“They neutralize the effect created by combining Chacachaca Catfish and Sabac bark.”

Her eyes widened.

She remembered—

the small mushrooms Vikir had added directly to her bowl.

“Then…”

Her voice shook.

“What you just said?”

“Joke.”

“….”

Dolores stared at him.

Her HP—

had fallen by roughly ten percent—

from mental damage alone.
