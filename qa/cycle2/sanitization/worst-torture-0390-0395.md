## Chapter dispositions

### Chapter 390 — FAIL

The source says Garam/Vikir strikes prisoners' vital/body points chosen specifically to **maximize pain without impairing labor**, with the three-section baton swinging **like chopping meat**. The draft reduces this to understanding how to use pain without ruining mobility and omits the meat-chopping image.

More materially, the Chinese explicitly says disobedient prisoners are **starved and whipped**, and that Starvation Drought plus torture techniques learned in the Age of Destruction can make their **souls submit**. The draft generalizes this into “tools to make them reconsider” and “understanding what broke resistance,” then states he does not need elaborate cruelty. That materially sanitizes the stated coercion.

### Chapter 391 — FAIL

Several source-explicit plague/body-horror details are generalized:

- Sakkuth's engorged eye vessels writhe **like centipedes trying to crawl out across the eyeball surface**; the draft reduces this to eyes that look ready to burst.
- after biting his tongue, the Chinese says his blood-filled mouth sprays **like a fountain**; the draft reduces this to “spat blood.”
- guards previously sent toward another dangerous warden are said to have apparently been **“eaten”**; the draft changes this to the abstract “do not feed more men into that problem.”
- Sakkuth's spit hits the ground with a **nauseating sound and stench** while melting the soil; the draft retains the ground reaction but removes the disgust/odor detail.

The hostage violence, blood from Kirko's nose/mouth, face stepping, plague-spit threat, and rape-origin “garbage” degradation are otherwise retained.

### Chapter 392 — FAIL

Sakkuth's hostage threat is softened. The Chinese says that if Garam is lying, he will break Kirko's neck and **dismember her by five-horse tearing**; the draft reduces this to “tear her apart.”

Later, Sakkuth says cowardly Garam might become **urinary and fecally incontinent and suffer heart paralysis** from fear, making him worthless as a hostage. The draft changes this to **“piss yourself and die of fright,”** omitting the defecation and replacing the source's heart-paralysis wording.

### Chapter 393 — FAIL

Kirko's injuries are source-explicitly **broken ribs and a broken collarbone**, with near-suffocation and pallor. The draft weakens this to **“Her ribs hurt. Her collarbone felt fractured.”** This changes confirmed fractures into pain/uncertainty.

The blood coughing, near-strangulation, immediate recovery stance, sacrificial misreading, and later combat escalation are otherwise retained.

### Chapter 394 — FAIL

The source states that even if Sakkuth endures **torture/beating and solitary confinement**, Vikir already has another ending prepared for him. The draft changes this sequence to **“interrogation—solitary—labor,”** replacing explicit torture with the materially softer “interrogation.”

The execution/guillotine threat, burn-scar recreation, Kirko's ointment, false escape hope, and Vikir's intent not to save Sakkuth because of Red Death are otherwise retained.

### Chapter 395 — FAIL

During Sakkuth's final agitation, the Chinese explicitly identifies what he is scattering as a **poison mixture** and specifies its source components. The draft reduces this to **“foul-looking substances from the cell,”** removing the fact that the material is poison and generalizing the source description.

The death-sentence protest, foaming mouth, Rune Bible, hidden D-7 message, tears, false hope, certain execution, and “torture of hope” conclusion are otherwise retained.
