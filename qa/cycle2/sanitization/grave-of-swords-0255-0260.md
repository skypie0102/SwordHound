## Review standard

Chinese source is the semantic authority. Drafts were checked directly for unsupported softening, euphemization, omission/generalization, mistranslation affecting explicit force, and unsupported intensification. Existing family QA was used only for continuity/navigation.

## Chapter dispositions

### Chapter 255 — FAIL

The draft preserves the Wraith Tree's death-fed nature, corpse army, swallowed dead-soul fruit, salt-desert mummies, Basilisk wounds, dead Pit Bull knight, and body/remains imagery.

However, the Basilisk information window is materially softened. The Chinese states that **no blade or magic can kill** the creature. The draft changes this to:

> Neither ordinary blades nor magic could easily kill it.

Adding **ordinary** and **easily** weakens the source's absolute monster-profile claim.

### Chapter 256 — FAIL

The salt-storm danger is retained, including the crow's snapped neck and broken wings and its rapid mummification.

But the source goes further: after falling, the crow's **entire body/bones are shattered**, while clarifying that it had already **died from rapid dehydration before the impact**. The draft omits both the full-body bone-shattering aftermath and the explicit dehydration-as-cause-of-death statement.

### Chapter 257 — PASS

The draft retains entire demon legions vanishing, later being found torn to pieces, scattered remains, the prior mass deaths that fed the Wraith Tree, blood strengthening it, skull-shaped fruit hanging like corpses, blood-colored juice, agonized screams, and dying undead being restored by the fruit. No material sanitization or unsupported intensification was found.

### Chapter 258 — PASS

The draft preserves the lethal pressure of Cane Corso's attack, Vikir's neck being seized, Sword-Master aura failing against the armor, the real possibility of death, and the landing injury where the skin of Vikir's palm is torn away to avoid catastrophic neck injury. No material sanitization was found.

### Chapter 259 — PASS

The draft preserves the destructive nine-fang sequence semantically, Vikir's body being ripped apart, terror and helplessness at the edge of death, the diagonal near-bisection wound, regeneration beyond ordinary monsters, the Gargoyle immortality slot, and Cane Corso's corpse-like hands and lonely death. English consolidation of closely overlapping cutting verbs does not materially reduce the sequence's destructive force.

### Chapter 260 — FAIL

Two fidelity issues remain:

- the Chinese explicitly states that Vikir **had never truly loved anyone in his life** before describing how he discarded emotion to reach the Sixth Fang and later regained emotion through Camus; the draft generalizes this to **“Vikir had spent one life rejecting emotion”**, removing the source's direct love characterization;
- Cane Corso's list of concepts rejected by the Ninth Fang includes understanding, empathy/resonance, acceptance, belief, common sense, and causality. The draft adds **“Probability”**, which is not present in the Chinese source.

The Death Knight profile, death/awakening requirements for Ninth/Tenth Fang, corpse-state explanation, Grave of Swords philosophy, tower disappearance, and Vikir's Eighth-Fang advancement are otherwise retained.

## Required Phase-4 remediation

- **255:** restore the Basilisk profile's source-level absolute invulnerability claim.
- **256:** restore the crow's source-explicit full-body bone-shattering aftermath and rapid-dehydration cause of death.
- **260:** restore the source's direct “never truly loved anyone” characterization and remove the unsupported “Probability” addition.

No manuscript change is made during Phase 1 discovery.

## Family result

**FAIL — Chapters 255, 256, and 260 require Phase-4 remediation.**
