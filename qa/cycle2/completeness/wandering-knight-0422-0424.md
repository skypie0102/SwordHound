## Chapter dispositions

### Chapter 422 — FAIL

The Wakewack drought/famine, wildfire and dried-lake devastation, dungeon/monster expansion, wandering-knight arrival, tavern scarcity, Imperial succession factions, Night-Hound retrial dispute, Seven-House civil war, collapsed trade, Night Walkers, their titles/slogan, and the monster-attack alarm are all retained.

However, the chapter materially summary-compresses source-present dialogue and political/social detail:

- the tavern debate separately argues that the First Prince has appeared publicly for years and leads from the front, uses a household eldest-son inheritance analogy, and answers with the counterpoint that the family's second son is smarter; the draft reduces these exchanges to a short faction summary;
- the source explicitly discusses the small direct-line populations of Donquixote and Usher and the resulting importance of collateral relatives; this contextual explanation is omitted;
- the Night Walkers rumor exchange includes a concrete report that **roughly half the population of a neighboring city followed them**, followed by the villagers asking why such heroes have not come to Wakewack and speculating that the disaster zone is simply too broad for their manpower. The draft generalizes this to whole neighborhoods following them and removes the separate complaint/explanation exchange;
- the source lists the public's mixed response as hope, admiration, curiosity, anxiety, vigilance, fear, and daily ridicule from skeptics; the draft compresses this into a shorter miracles/scams/fear/mocking summary.

Under the Cycle-2 completeness rule against summary-style condensation of distinct dialogue and explanatory beats, Chapter 422 fails despite passing Phase 1.
