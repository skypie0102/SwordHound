### Chapter 69 — FAIL

The Camus-search disclosure, Aiyen's reluctance and eventual honesty, Vikir's “hell” answer, decision not to return yet, Leviathan dagger identification, rainy-season signs, lungfish kill/dissection, half-digested Rokoko hunter, harpoon-from-inside reconstruction, weakness hypothesis, struggle marks, reverse tracking, and dead Rokoko camp are all present.

One source-specific numeric/age detail is lost. From the condition of the remains, the Chinese identifies the swallowed Rokoko hunter as **just over twenty years old**, explicitly emphasizing that he was in his physical prime. The draft keeps only **“a young adult in physical prime.”** Phase 2 requires retention of source numbers/quantitative details, so the precise age estimate is incomplete.

### Chapter 70 — FAIL

The draft retains the corpse heat/stink, flies, vomit, diarrhea, red skin lesions, chills, dead woman and children, abandoned sick/dead villagers, Red Death identification, Aiyen's plague terror, survivor migration, ritual-book recovery, Pomeranian's slave/kitchen-helper clues, Baskerville pendant, and young-Hugo portrait reveal.

Two disease-mechanic details are materially altered or omitted:

- the Chinese says the infected person's **metabolism becomes extremely slow**, making the dying process take a very long time; the draft retains only a “drawn-out collapse” and omits the slowed-metabolism mechanism;
- the Chinese presents the contagion as so extreme that merely **making eye contact with a sick person can infect someone**. The draft changes this into something frightened people merely **believed**, weakening an asserted source mechanic into rumor/perception.

### Chapter 71 — FAIL

The pendant/family deduction, softened re-questioning of Pomeranian, Vikir's awkward smile, Hugo's first-wife/first-daughter history, frontier relocation, Pomeranian's direct-granddaughter relationship, her mother's grave, Aiyen's stars explanation, uncle/niece bond, Oxbear-hide carrier, Chihuahua/Morgue planning, Red Death council, curse-versus-plague dispute, and Vikir's closing claim that he knows how to stop the disease are retained.

A source-explicit Hugo characterization is missing. The Chinese says Hugo habitually regarded **boys as more useful in combat**, sought sons/grandsons rather than daughters, and effectively treated children as **tools for battle**. The draft keeps the male-heavy household and his general ruthlessness but omits this concrete child-as-combat-tool attitude.
