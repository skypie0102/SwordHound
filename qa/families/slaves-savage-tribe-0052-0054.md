## Cross-chapter semantic / canonical decisions

- **Tough Life continuity:** Ch. 52 attributes Vikir's survival/recovery to River Styx protection and the Murcielago-derived ability now stored in Beelzebub. Recovered-English corrupted ability wording is rejected.
- **Ballak duality:** communal generosity and freedom toward recognized insiders coexist with extreme brutality toward outsiders/prisoners. Neither side is flattened or moralized away.
- **Thorn-Tree Punishment:** retained descriptively from Chinese; not promoted to a Fandom-canonical proper noun.
- **Aiyen captivity dynamic:** Vikir's leash/slave status, Aiyen's debt repayment, and the husband-hunt distinction are preserved without romanticizing coercion.
- **Akwilla reveal chronology:** Chapters 50–52 keep the Night Fox unidentified. Chinese Ch. 53 itself reveals **Akwilla** as Ballak chieftain, Aiyen's mother, and the current **Night Fox**; only from this chapter forward is that identity allowed.
- **Akwilla strength:** she is source-linked to Hugo's nose scar, Adolf's severe wound, the 16 Sword Graduator / 10 Fourth-Circle / 6 Fifth-Circle kills, and likely Cerberus arrow scars. No unsupported formal rank is invented.
- **Reproductive/social material:** Ch. 53–54 contains breeding-role, reproductive-health, nudity/body, and sexual-custom context. It is retained clinically/factually and non-erotically, with no added sexual detail.
- **Toilet-culture joke:** Aiyen presents one attitude toward public bodily exposure; later Ballak villagers mock Vikir for relieving himself openly. Her smile/averted gaze preserves the joke without explicitly asserting motive.
- **Hunting economy:** Ch. 54 preserves the near-300 population and roughly 200 kg boar-equivalent daily meat requirement when supplemented with gathered foods.
- **Age-of-Destruction skills:** Vikir's construction, brickmaking, drainage, laundry, pickpocketing, and field survival are treated as established survival-era competence rather than newly invented talents.
- **Slave punishment:** Aiyen punishes Vikir herself under Ballak ownership rules, then makes clear Ahun may not privately punish her slave. The violence is explicit; the final “Did it hurt?” is not expanded into added romance narration.
